## 2. Reconciliation Issues

### Issue 1: Section 1.5.3 "(T1)" Labeling Error

**Problem**: Section 1.5.3 in Document A is labeled "(T1)" but its content addresses temporal synthesis and continuity of consciousness — topics belonging to the culmination of the T-stage sequence, not to T1 (aisthesis/first sensation).

**Evidence**:
- T1 is already covered in section 1.3 ("(T1) Aisthesis as the First Motion")
- Section 1.5.3 discusses how phantasia provides "continuity of consciousness," synthesizes the temporal moments, and transitions to Heidegger's ecstatic temporality
- The content logically follows T4-T5 and precedes the Chapter 2 transition

**Proposed correction**: The label should be "(T4/T5) Temporal Synthesis" or simply "Temporal Synthesis." The "(T1)" is likely a vestigial label from an earlier numbering scheme. **Flagged for author revision.**

### Issue 2: T7 Placeholder in Notes

**Problem**: Document B lists T7 in its skeleton outline but assigns no content.

**Resolution**: T7 does not appear in the primary text (Document A) and has no developed content in any document. It is treated as an **abandoned placeholder** in this v1.0 schema. If later chapters develop a T7 stage, the schema can be versioned to v1.1 or v2.0.

### Issue 3: T-Stage Coverage Gap (T5-T6)

**Observation**: T5 (deliberation) and T6 (action/pursue-avoid) are described in the outfielder walkthrough (sec. 1.1) but do not receive dedicated sections comparable to T0-T4. They appear as embedded elements within sections 1.5.1 (emotion as motion → T6 connection) and 1.5.2 (doxa formation → T5 connection).

**Resolution**: This is an authorial choice, not an error. T0-T4 receive individual treatment because they build the phantasia mechanism. T5-T6 are covered functionally within the emotion/doxa/pathos sections because deliberation and action depend on phantasia rather than constituting it.

### Loop Structure

The T-stages are NOT purely sequential. Document A (pp. 3-4) describes an explicit feedback loop:

```
T1-T3: continuous (new percepts arriving)
T4: runs in background (phantasmata available for recall/recognition)
T5: updating (recalculates as new T1-T3 data arrives)
T6: feeds back to T1 (action changes perceptual field, generating new T1)
```

The outfielder example demonstrates this: as the ball moves (continuous T1-T3), the player's phantasia activates recognition of trajectory patterns (T4), which enables real-time recalculation of the catch angle (T5), directing locomotion (T6), which changes what the outfielder sees (back to T1).

### Chapter 2 Extension: Stimmung's Temporality

Chapter 2 does not add new T-stages but introduces a **pre-condition prior to T0**: *Stimmung* as the existential horizon within which the entire T-sequence operates. The aisthesis-vs-Stimmung table (A p. 16) shows:

| Aisthesis (T1) | Stimmung |
|----------------|----------|
| Occurs only at the instant of contact | Pervades temporal existence |
| Disappears instantly when object is gone | Not tied to a single instant |
| Has no retention | Shapes entire horizon of meaning |
| Cannot carry temporality by itself | Colors past, future, and present simultaneously |

This means Stimmung is not a T-stage but the **temporal horizon** within which T0-T6 unfold. The 4-step schema (Stimmung → Phantasia → Doxa → Pathos) overlays the T-sequence rather than replacing it:

- Stimmung = pre-temporal horizon (prior to T0)
- Phantasia = T3-T4 mechanism (temporal trace)
- Doxa = T5 moment (judgment on the appearance)
- Pathos = T6 affective culmination (structured emotional response)
