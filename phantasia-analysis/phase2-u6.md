## 2. Hierarchical Outline

- **1.5.1 Emotion is Motion** (pp. 9-11)
  - Etymological argument: "emotion" = e-motion = motion out
  - Three kinds of motion in emotional response: affective (pathetic), locomotive, intellectual
  - Phantasia is the condition for all three: it provides the images that drive emotion
  - The "affective architecture": phantasia structures how emotions are organized
  - Connection to *De Motu Animalium*: emotion literally moves the body
- **1.5.2 The Affective Image** (pp. 11-12)
  - **Taking-as**: Phantasia presents X "as" Y — the wolf appears "as dangerous"
  - This "as" structure is prior to judgment (*doxa*) — the appearance precedes and conditions the opinion
  - **Phantasia→Doxa→Pathos chain**:
    1. Phantasia presents an appearance (wolf "as" dangerous)
    2. Doxa assents: "this is dangerous" (opinion/judgment)
    3. Pathos responds: fear arises, attended by pain
  - **Voluntary vs involuntary phantasia**
    - Deliberative: "we can call up a picture at will" (DA III.3, 427b17-20)
    - Non-deliberative: "in forming opinions we are not free" — the appearance compels
  - **Sensitive vs deliberative phantasia distinction**
    - Sensitive: shared with animals, tied to immediate appearances
    - Deliberative: unique to rational animals, enables comparison and planning (T5)
  - **Rhetoric's dependence on phantasia**
    - Orators create phantasmata in the audience through *enargeia* (vivid description)
    - Rhetorical phantasia = producing appearances that stir pathos via doxa
    - Rhetoric is the art of managing the phantasia→doxa→pathos chain

**Critical observation**: This unit treats phantasia as **both** faculty and appearance simultaneously. The wolf example shows the faculty (phantasia presents) producing an appearance ("as dangerous") that drives judgment (doxa) and emotion (pathos). This is the fullest demonstration of Tension #1.

## 6. Temporal Structure (T0-T6)

- T4-T6 are densely interwoven in this unit, not clearly sequential
- Taking-as (T4) shades into judgment (T5) without a clear boundary
- The phantasia→doxa→pathos chain suggests a temporal sequence (T4→T5→T6), but the text also describes cases where phantasia directly triggers pathos without doxa (the involuntary case)
- This creates a **branching temporal structure**:
  - Path A: phantasia → doxa → pathos (deliberative, rational)
  - Path B: phantasia → pathos (non-deliberative, immediate)

## 7. Tables and Schemas

```
Phantasia presents X "as" Y
    ↓
Doxa assents: "X is Y" (judgment/opinion)
    ↓
Pathos responds: emotion arises (attended by pleasure/pain)
    ↓
Orexis: pursue or avoid
```

**Variant (non-deliberative)**:
```
Phantasia presents X "as" Y
    ↓ (directly)
Pathos responds: emotion arises
    ↓
```

## 9. Revision Notes

- **What is clear**: The phantasia→doxa→pathos chain is the unit's strongest contribution. The emotion-as-motion etymology is effective. The rhetoric connection is compelling.
- **What is repeated**: The faculty/appearance duality appears repeatedly without explicit reconciliation. Document B's "Resolution" (phantasia is BOTH) should be imported here.
- **What is gestured at but under-developed**: The branching temporal structure (with/without doxa) deserves explicit treatment. When does phantasia bypass doxa? Is this the sensitive/deliberative distinction? The text doesn't cleanly map these.
- **What contradicts**: The rattlesnake example (U5) showed phantasia→pathos directly (no doxa), but this unit's central argument is the phantasia→doxa→pathos chain. The relationship between the two paths needs reconciliation.
- **What's missing**: Document C's P1-P5 propositions offer a richer propositional analysis of the emotion-phantasia relationship. The Gibsonian "perception is for action" framework from C could strengthen the pursue/avoid discussion.
