## 2. Hierarchical Outline

- **2.3 Resonant Motion and Resonant Affect: The Dual Trace of Perception** (pp. 17-18)
  - **Resonant motion** (author's term): the *kinesis* that remains in the sense organ after perception
    - "For the sake of clarity I will call the kinēsis that remains in the sense organ 'resonant motion'"
    - Kinetic dimension: the physical trace of the perceptual encounter
    - Maps to T2-T3 in Ch 1's framework
  - **Resonant affect** (author's term): the persistent subjective-affective coloration
    - "and the persistent subjective-affective coloration 'resonant mood'" (later revised to "resonant affect")
    - Affective dimension: how the encounter colors the perceiver's felt sense
    - Not yet structured emotion (pathos) — a pre-emotional affective residue
  - **Dual trace**: Every perceptual encounter leaves BOTH a kinetic trace AND an affective trace
    - The kinetic trace is what Aristotle describes (apoleipomene kinesis → phantasma)
    - The affective trace is what Heidegger's framework reveals (Stimmung colors the encounter)
    - Phantasia operates on both traces simultaneously
  - **Antichesis** (p. 15/17-18): Author's coined term for "internal resonance"
    - The mechanism whereby sensible form reverberates in the soul
    - Bridges the Aristotelian kinetic account and the Heideggerian existential account
    - The resonance is both physical (kinesis) and existential (Stimmung)
- **2.4 Mood as Ontological Ground, Emotion as Particularized Affect, Phantasia as Bridge** (pp. 18-19)
  - **2.4.1 Mood (Stimmung) as Ontological Ground of Disclosure** (p. 18)
    - Mood discloses the world as meaningful before any particular encounter
    - Mood is not caused by objects but is the condition for objects to show up as mattering
  - **2.4.2 Emotion (Pathos) as Particularized, Structured Affect** (pp. 18-19)
    - Emotion is particular: directed at THIS object, arising from THIS encounter
    - Emotion is structured: organized by phantasia into a coherent affective response
    - Emotion requires phantasia's "taking-as" to have determinate content
  - **2.4.3 Phantasia as the Bridge Between Mood and Emotion** (p. 19)
    - **4-step temporal schema**:
      1. Stimmung (Mood) → Opens a temporal horizon of meaningfulness
      2. Phantasia → Supplies internalized appearances (phantasmata) attuned within this mooded horizon
      3. Doxa → Assents to the appearance as bearing determinate evaluative significance
      4. Pathos (Emotion) → Organizes the appearance into a structured emotional response attended by pleasure or pain
    - **Tripartite temporal formula**:
      - Stimmung = pre-temporal resonance (before temporal sequence begins)
      - Phantasia = temporal trace (retains before/after)
      - Pathos = structured motion in time (kinetic response)

**Critical observation**: This unit definitively positions mood as PRIOR TO phantasia. The 4-step schema makes the order explicit: Stimmung first (opens horizon), then Phantasia (supplies appearances within that horizon), then Doxa (assents), then Pathos (responds). Phantasia does not generate significance — it operates within a horizon of significance already opened by Stimmung.

## 6. Temporal Structure (T0-T6)

- The 4-step schema overlays the T-stage sequence:
  - Stimmung = prior to T0 (temporal horizon)
  - Phantasia = T3-T4 (mechanism)
  - Doxa = T5 (judgment)
  - Pathos = T6 (affective culmination)
- The tripartite temporal formula adds a temporal character to each:
  - Stimmung: pre-temporal (before the before/after sequence)
  - Phantasia: temporal (bridges before/after)
  - Pathos: in-temporal (structured kinetic response within time)
- **Ch 2 extension**: Stimmung introduces a temporal dimension that CANNOT be captured by the T-stage sequence — it is the horizon within which T0-T6 unfold, not a T-stage itself

## 8. Tensions and Open Questions

| # | Tension | Poles | Description | Location |
|---|---------|-------|-------------|----------|
| 10 | Resonant motion vs resonant affect | kinetic trace / affective trace | Are these really separable? The author distinguishes them, but both are traces of the same perceptual encounter. Can you have resonant motion without resonant affect, or vice versa? | pp. 17-18 |
| 3 | Phantasia vs mood | phantasia discloses / Stimmung discloses | Partially resolved here: Stimmung discloses the horizon; phantasia discloses particular content within that horizon. But: does phantasia ever disclose INDEPENDENTLY of Stimmung? | pp. 18-19 |

**Open question about antichesis**: The term "antichesis" is introduced as "internal resonance" but its relationship to Aristotle's own terminology is unclear. Is this an Aristotelian term being repurposed, or an entirely novel coinage? The Greek *antithesis* (ἀντίθεσις) means "opposition" — is "antichesis" (ἀντίχησις) from *anti-* + *ēchos* (echo)? The etymology should be made explicit.

## 9. Revision Notes

- **What is clear**: The dual trace argument (resonant motion + resonant affect) is the chapter's most original contribution. The 4-step schema is precisely formulated. Antichesis as a coined term is memorable and conceptually rich.
- **What is repeated**: The mood-as-prior-to-phantasia point has been stated in U8 and is reinforced here with more precision.
- **What is gestured at but under-developed**: The etymology and philosophical rationale for "antichesis" needs more development. Why this term? What is its relationship to Greek philosophical vocabulary? Also: the dual trace claim (every perception leaves both kinetic and affective traces) is asserted but not argued from Aristotelian or Heideggerian texts. What evidence supports the claim that EVERY perception has an affective trace?
- **What contradicts**: The 4-step schema (Stimmung→Phantasia→Doxa→Pathos) implies doxa is always involved, but U5's rattlesnake example showed phantasia→pathos without doxa. The schema needs a non-deliberative variant.
- **What's missing**: A worked example showing the 4-step schema in action (analogous to the outfielder example in Ch 1). The schema is abstract; a concrete illustration would make it vivid.
