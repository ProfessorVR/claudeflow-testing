## 1. Formulations Differing from A's Chapter 2

### 1a. "As If" (*hōs*) Structure of Phantastic Presentation

**E's formulation** (Part I): Phantasia presents things "as if" they are present when absent, "as if" they are fearful before judgment occurs. The *hōs* structure is phenomenological: phantasia produces a seeming, a quasi-perceptual presentation that is experienced as if it were veridical.

**A's formulation**: A uses "taking-as" language — phantasia presents X "as" Y. This is more aligned with Heidegger's as-structure (*Als-Struktur*) than with Aristotle's phenomenology.

**Assessment**: These are distinct framings:
- E's "as if": Aristotelian phenomenology — the phantasma appears "as if" present (even when the object is absent)
- A's "taking-as": Heideggerian hermeneutics — the phantasma is taken "as" something meaningful

E's version is more faithful to Aristotle's own language (DA III.3, 428a1-3: "phantasia is that in virtue of which we say a phantasma occurs to us"). A's version better serves the Heidegger bridge. **Recommendation**: Use BOTH in A — E's "as if" for the Aristotelian exposition (Ch 1), A's "taking-as" for the Heidegger transition (U7) and Ch 2.

### 1b. "Orienting Faculty" Framing

**E's title**: "Orienting Faculties: Phantasia and Stimmung as World-Disclosing Structures"

**A's framing**: "The Soul's Temporal Medium" (Ch 1 title) and "The Ontological Ground of Affective Disclosure" (Ch 2 subtitle)

**Assessment**: E treats both faculties as ORIENTING — they orient the creature in its environment. A treats them asymmetrically — phantasia is a "medium" (passive, transmissive) while Stimmung is a "ground" (foundational, prior). E's symmetric treatment reflects the earlier "conditions of possibility" framing; A's asymmetric treatment reflects the later "subsumption" thesis.

## 3. "Both as Conditions of Possibility" Framing

**E (Part III)**: Phantasia and Stimmung are **parallel conditions of possibility**:
- Phantasia is a condition of possibility for perceptual experience (Aristotle)
- Stimmung is a condition of possibility for existential disclosure (Heidegger)
- They operate in different domains (perception vs existence) but are structurally analogous
- Neither subsumes the other; both are necessary conditions in their respective domains

**A's framing**: Stimmung "subsumes" phantasia by providing its "ontological completion":
- Phantasia provides the mechanism; Stimmung provides the significance
- This implies a hierarchy: Stimmung is ontologically prior
- Phantasia is "completed" rather than independently sufficient

**Philosophical difference**:

| Dimension | E's "Conditions" | A's "Subsumption" |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Relationship | Parallel, symmetric | Hierarchical, asymmetric |
| Phantasia's status | Independently sufficient in its domain | Incomplete without Stimmung |
| Stimmung's role | Parallel condition alongside phantasia | Ontological ground that completes phantasia |
| Philosophical claim | Conservative (both have their place) | Bold (Stimmung subsumes/completes) |

**Assessment**: A's revision from "conditions of possibility" to "subsumption/completion" represents a deliberate philosophical strengthening. The subsumption thesis is a bolder, more original claim. However, the "conditions of possibility" language is more defensible philosophically. This is Tension #11.

**Recommendation**: A should acknowledge the "conditions of possibility" reading (possibly in a footnote or in the tensions discussion) and explain WHY the subsumption/completion thesis is preferred. This would pre-empt objections from readers who might argue that Aristotle's account is complete on its own terms.

## 4. "Key Differences: Perception versus Existence" Schema

**E (Part III)** contains an explicit comparative schema:

| Dimension | Aristotle (Perception) | Heidegger (Existence) |
|-----------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Domain | Faculty psychology | Fundamental ontology |
| Method | Empirical / observational | Phenomenological / hermeneutic |
| Causation | Mechanistic (efficient + material) | Existential (conditions of possibility) |
| Object | Sensible form | Being-in-the-world |
| Temporality | Sequential (before/after of motion) | Ecstatic (past/present/future as co-equiprimordial) |
| Key faculty | Phantasia | Befindlichkeit/Stimmung |
| Disclosure | Through images (phantasmata) | Through attunement (mood) |

**Status in A**: **Absent as a formal schema**. The content is implicit across Ch 2 but never consolidated into a table.

**Assessment**: This schema is valuable because:
1. It makes the Aristotle/Heidegger comparison explicit and systematic
2. It clarifies the "mechanism vs significance" division that A's conclusion relies on
3. It would help readers who are unfamiliar with one framework understand the comparison

**Recommendation**: Consider adding this schema to A sec. 2.2 (temporal divergence) or as an appendix.
