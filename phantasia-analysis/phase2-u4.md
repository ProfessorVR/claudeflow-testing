## 2. Hierarchical Outline

- **T1: Aisthesis as the First Motion** (sec. 1.3)
  - Hylomorphism: form received without matter (wax analogy)
  - Sensation as *alloiosis* (qualitative change)
  - *Dynamis* → *energeia*: perceptual capacity passes from potential to actual
  - Aisthesis is instantaneous: occurs only at the moment of contact
  - When the sensory object withdraws, aisthesis ceases
- **T2: Perceptual Actualization** (sec. 1.4, first part)
  - The sense organ undergoes *kinesis* — the motion is "in" the perceiver
  - Transition from T1 to T2: the actualization is not just passive reception but an active process
  - The perceiver is changed by the encounter
- **T3: Resonant Motion and Phantasma Generation** (sec. 1.4, second part)
  - *Apoleipomene kinesis*: the "left-behind motion" that persists after the object withdraws
  - This residual kinesis is the raw material of phantasia
  - The phantasma crystallizes as an inner likeness from this residual motion
  - White on *phantasma* as "sensory residue"
  - The phantasma is NOT a copy of the object but a trace of the perceptual encounter

## 6. Temporal Structure (T0-T6)

- T1 is **instantaneous**: the "now" of perceptual contact
- T2 is **transitional**: the moment passes from present contact to residual motion
- T3 is **retentive**: the residual motion persists, introducing the "after" into the soul
- The T1→T2→T3 sequence is the mechanism by which phantasia internalizes the before/after structure established at T0
- Key temporal claim: Without T2-T3 (the persistence of kinesis), aisthesis would be a series of disconnected "nows" — phantasia would have no material to operate on

## 9. Revision Notes

- **What is clear**: The T1-T3 sequence is the mechanistic backbone of the chapter. The argument from aisthesis through perceptual actualization to phantasma generation is tightly constructed.
- **What is repeated**: The wax analogy is standard and doesn't need more development.
- **What is gestured at but under-developed**: The claim that phantasma is a "trace, not a copy" deserves more argument. What exactly is preserved? Just the form? The affect? The temporal ordering?
- **What contradicts**: Nothing internally. Document D's *koinon aistheterion* (common sense synthesis) is absent — A treats aisthesis as if it's a single faculty, but Aristotle distinguishes special and common sense.
- **What's missing**: The *koinon aistheterion* from Document D. If the common sense synthesizes sensory data from multiple modalities, then T1-T2 is more complex than A presents — the outfielder hearing the crack AND seeing the ball requires cross-modal synthesis before phantasia enters.
