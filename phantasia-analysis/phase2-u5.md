## 2. Hierarchical Outline

- **Phantasma activation**: Phantasia activates stored resonant motion to produce internal images
  - The soul can operate on phantasmata in the absence of the original sensory object
  - This is what distinguishes phantasia from aisthesis: aisthesis requires the object's presence
- **Phantasia vs memory distinction**
  - Phantasia and memory have "distinct temporal phases"
  - Memory (*mneme*) is directed toward the past: it re-presents what was
  - Phantasia presents images that may refer to past, present, or future (or to nothing actual at all)
  - The distinction is temporal directionality, not content
- **Rattlesnake example**
  - Hearing a rattle → phantasia activates stored image of snake → fear arises
  - No snake is present to perception; phantasia operates on auditory residue
  - The phantasma functions as *eikon* (likeness): it stands in for the absent object
  - Fear (pathos) is generated through phantasia, not through direct perception
- **Temporal awareness through phantasmata**
  - Phantasmata enable the soul to be aware of temporal duration
  - Without stored images, there would be no sense of "how long ago" or "what comes next"

**Key observation**: The rattlesnake example shows the phantasia→pathos connection WITHOUT the intermediate doxa step. The hearer doesn't form an opinion ("that is a snake"); the phantasma directly triggers fear. This suggests the phantasia→doxa→pathos chain (U6) may not always apply — sometimes phantasia→pathos is direct. This is Tension #9 (voluntary vs involuntary).

## 6. Temporal Structure (T0-T6)

- T4 is the first stage where the soul operates **independently of the present sensory object**
- The temporal character of T4 is "extended": phantasia bridges past perception (T1-T3) to present thought
- The phantasia/memory distinction is temporal: memory is past-directed; phantasia is temporally flexible (past, present, future, or fictive)
- T4 is where the "temporal medium" thesis becomes most explicit: phantasia is what gives the soul access to temporal breadth

## 9. Revision Notes

- **What is clear**: The phantasia/memory distinction and the rattlesnake example are well-presented.
- **What is repeated**: The phantasia/aisthesis contrast (phantasia works without object presence) was established in U4 and is restated here.
- **What is gestured at but under-developed**: The phantasia/memory interface. "Distinct temporal phases" is asserted but the mechanism of distinction is unclear. Document C's meta-note ("Make sure to clarify the difference between the storage capabilities of phantasia and memory proper") indicates the author was aware of this gap.
- **What contradicts**: The rattlesnake example shows phantasia→pathos directly (no doxa), but U6 will argue for a phantasia→doxa→pathos chain. This needs reconciliation.
- **What's missing**: More on what *eikon* means in this context. Is the phantasma a resemblance to the original object, or to the original perceptual experience? This distinction matters for the temporal medium thesis.
