## Cross-Framework Terminology Map

Key pairings between Greek (Ch 1) and German (Ch 2) vocabularies:

| Greek (Aristotle) | German (Heidegger) | Relationship in Chapter |
|-------------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| phantasia | — | No German equivalent. The chapter argues Heidegger lacks a concept of phantasia; this is the gap his account has. |
| aisthesis | — | Heidegger doesn't use aisthesis directly; the aisthesis/Stimmung comparison (Table 2) is the chapter's construction. |
| pathos | Befindlichkeit (existential) / Gefühl, Affekt, Leidenschaft (ontic) | Heidegger reads Aristotle's pathos as anticipating Befindlichkeit (BCAP). But pathos is also ontic (Gefühl/Affekt). |
| kinesis | — | No direct equivalent. Heidegger's temporality is not kinetic but ecstatic. |
| chronos | Zeitlichkeit | Chronos = physical time (sequential). Zeitlichkeit = existential temporality (ecstatic). |
| doxa | — | Heidegger doesn't use doxa. The chapter places doxa within the 4-step schema between phantasia and pathos. |
| — | Stimmung | No Greek equivalent. The chapter argues this is the gap in Aristotle's account. |
| — | Geworfenheit | No Greek equivalent. Thrownness is an existential structure without Aristotelian precedent. |
| — | Bedeutsamkeit | No Greek equivalent. Significance as an existential structure is absent from Aristotle. |

**Key insight**: The cross-framework map reveals an asymmetry. Aristotle has concepts Heidegger lacks (phantasia, phantasma, aisthesis, doxa) and Heidegger has concepts Aristotle lacks (Stimmung, Geworfenheit, Bedeutsamkeit). The chapter's thesis is that BOTH sets are needed: phantasia provides the mechanism (Greek), Stimmung provides the horizon (German).
