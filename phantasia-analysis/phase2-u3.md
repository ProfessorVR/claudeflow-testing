## 2. Hierarchical Outline

- **Motion (*kinesis*) as ontological ground**
  - Motion is prior to and more fundamental than time
  - *Kinesis* encompasses locomotion, qualitative change (*alloiosis*), growth, generation/corruption
  - All psychic activity is ultimately a form of motion
- **Time (*chronos*) as "number of motion with respect to before and after"**
  - Physics IV definition: time is not motion itself but the countable aspect of motion
  - The "before and after" (*proteron kai husteron*) structure is what phantasia internalizes
- **Asymmetric ontological dependency**
  - Time depends on motion: without motion, no before/after
  - Motion does not depend on time: motion can occur without being counted
  - This asymmetry grounds the entire T-stage sequence in kinesis
- **The soul-time question**
  - Does time require a soul that counts? (Physics IV.14, 223a21-29)
  - If time is the number of motion, and numbering requires a counter (soul), then time is soul-dependent
  - This connects T0 to phantasia: the soul's capacity to retain before/after IS phantasia

## 9. Revision Notes

- **What is clear**: The Physics IV analysis is precise and well-argued. The asymmetric dependency claim is the strongest philosophical move in this section.
- **What is repeated**: The soul-time question appears here and is echoed in the temporal synthesis section (U7).
- **What is gestured at but under-developed**: The soul-time question could be pressed harder — if time requires soul, and phantasia is the soul's temporal faculty, then phantasia partially constitutes time itself. This would strengthen the "temporal medium" thesis.
- **What contradicts**: Document D's reciprocal dependency vs A's asymmetric dependency.
- **What's missing**: The *nun* (now) analysis from Documents B and D is largely absent from A. The paradoxical nature of the "now" (boundary between past and future, both dividing and uniting) would enrich the argument.
