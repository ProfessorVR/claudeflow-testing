## 2. Hierarchical Outline

- **Transition paragraph** (p. 15)
  - Aristotle's account leaves unanswered: what provides the horizon of significance within which phantasmata acquire meaning?
  - Phantasia furnishes the mechanism but not the phenomenological horizon
  - Heidegger's *Stimmung* provides the answer
- **2.1 Befindlichkeit: The Primordial Structure of Attunement** (pp. 15-16)
  - *Befindlichkeit* = "how one finds oneself" = the existential structure of affective disposition
  - It names the a priori condition for the possibility of any particular mood
  - Befindlichkeit is NOT a psychological state but an existential structure (*Existenzial*)
  - Stimmung is the ontic manifestation; Befindlichkeit is the ontological structure
  - Three aspects: (1) discloses thrownness (*Geworfenheit*), (2) discloses Being-in-the-world as a whole, (3) is the condition for encountering things that matter (*Bedeutsamkeit*)
- **2.2 The Temporal Divergence: Aisthesis and Stimmung** (pp. 16-17)
  - Central argument: aisthesis and Stimmung have fundamentally different temporal structures
  - **Table 2**: Aisthesis vs Stimmung comparison (see Section 7)
  - Aisthesis: instantaneous, disappears when object withdraws, no retention, cannot carry temporality
  - Stimmung: pervades temporal existence, not tied to a single instant, shapes the entire horizon of meaning, colors past/present/future simultaneously
  - This temporal divergence is why aisthesis alone cannot explain meaningful experience

## 6. Temporal Structure (T0-T6)

- Stimmung operates at a level prior to T0: it is the **temporal horizon** within which the T-stage sequence unfolds
- The aisthesis/Stimmung comparison shows that T1 (aisthesis) captures only the instantaneous "now," while Stimmung captures the full temporal field
- This means the T-stage sequence, while mechanistically correct, is phenomenologically incomplete without Stimmung
- **Ch 2 extension**: Stimmung does not add a new T-stage; it provides the existential context for all T-stages

## 7. Tables and Schemas

### Table 2: Aisthesis vs Stimmung Comparison (p. 16-17)

| Aisthesis (Aristotle) | Stimmung (Heidegger) |
|------------------------|---------------------|
| Occurs only at the instant of contact | Pervades temporal existence |
| Disappears instantly when object is gone | Is not tied to a single instant |
| Has no retention | Shapes our entire horizon of meaning |
| Cannot carry temporality by itself | Colors past, future, and present simultaneously |

**Architectonic significance**: This table is central to Ch 2's argument. It shows why aisthesis alone cannot explain temporal experience (the gap identified in U7) and why Stimmung can. Phantasia bridges the two: it inherits aisthesis's content (phantasmata from sensory encounters) and operates within Stimmung's horizon (giving those phantasmata meaningful significance).

## 8. Tensions and Open Questions

| # | Tension | Poles | Description | Location |
|---|---------|-------|-------------|----------|
| 3 | Phantasia vs mood | phantasia as disclosure / Stimmung as disclosure | Both disclose the world, but in different registers: phantasia discloses through images, Stimmung discloses through attunement | pp. 15-17 |
| 4 | Form-reception vs meaning-constitution | Aristotle: form received without matter / Heidegger: meaning constituted through attunement | The aisthesis/Stimmung table crystallizes this opposition | pp. 16-17 |

**Open question**: Does the aisthesis/Stimmung comparison do justice to Aristotle? Aristotle was not asking about "horizons of meaning" — he was asking about how the soul receives sensory form. The comparison may impose Heideggerian questions on Aristotelian material. The chapter would benefit from acknowledging this asymmetry.
