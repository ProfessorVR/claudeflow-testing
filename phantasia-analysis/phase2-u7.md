## 2. Hierarchical Outline

- **Continuity of consciousness** (pp. 12-13)
  - Without phantasia, experience would be a series of disconnected "nows"
  - Phantasia provides temporal continuity: it bridges past perception to present thought
  - This is the chapter title's justification: phantasia IS the soul's temporal medium
- **Frede's melody insight** (p. 13)
  - Hearing individual notes is aisthesis; hearing a MELODY requires retention and synthesis
  - The melody doesn't exist in any single note — it exists in the temporal synthesis performed by phantasia
  - Phantasia unifies discrete perceptual instants into a coherent temporal gestalt
- **Phantasia as temporal medium** (pp. 13-14)
  - The "temporal medium" thesis reaches its culmination: phantasia is the faculty that makes temporal experience possible
  - All higher cognitive functions (deliberation, memory, opinion formation) presuppose this temporal synthesis
- **Heidegger BCAP §§26-28 references** (pp. 14-15)
  - Heidegger reads Aristotle's *pathos* as anticipating *Befindlichkeit*
  - BCAP analysis of *dioxis*/*phyge* (pursuit/avoidance) connects to orexis and T6
  - Ecstatic temporality: Heidegger's extension of Aristotle's temporal framework
  - The transition: Aristotle's account leaves open the question of the HORIZON OF SIGNIFICANCE
- **Transition to Chapter 2** (p. 15)
  - The gap: aisthesis is instantaneous and cannot carry temporality by itself
  - Phantasia provides the mechanism but not the meaning — what gives phantasmata their significance?
  - Heidegger's answer: *Stimmung* (mood/attunement) provides the horizon
  - The ***Important*** meta-note (p. 15): author flags this as a key transition point

## 6. Temporal Structure (T0-T6)

- This unit synthesizes across ALL T-stages: it's the argument that phantasia makes temporal experience possible
- The Frede melody analogy is the clearest illustration: a melody is a temporal object that requires phantasia to be heard as a unity
- The Heidegger transition introduces a temporal dimension BEYOND the T-stage sequence: ecstatic temporality (past/present/future as structural features of Being, not just as before/after of motion)
- **Key gap identified**: The T-stage sequence explains HOW temporal experience works mechanistically, but not WHY it matters — what gives phantasmata their significance? This is the gap Chapter 2 fills.

## 9. Revision Notes

- **What is clear**: The melody analogy is brilliant and should be expanded. The transition to Heidegger is well-motivated: the "gap" (horizon of significance) is clearly identified.
- **What is repeated**: The claim that aisthesis is instantaneous has been stated in U4 and is restated here. Necessary for the argument.
- **What is gestured at but under-developed**: The BCAP references (§§26-28, dioxis/phyge) are mentioned but not developed. Document D has significantly more BCAP material (pp. 205-206 analysis). The transition would be stronger with more explicit engagement with Heidegger's own reading of Aristotle in BCAP.
- **What contradicts**: The section numbering "(T1)" — **flag for author revision**.
- **What's missing**: The transition could acknowledge that the gap (horizon of significance) is not a failure of Aristotle's account but a question Aristotle didn't need to ask within his framework. The gap arises only when we impose Heideggerian questions on Aristotelian material. Making this explicit would strengthen the cross-framework argument.
