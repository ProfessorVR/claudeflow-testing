## 3D. Contested / Ambiguous Nodes

### 1. Bedeutung (Meaning)

- **German**: *Bedeutung*
- **Units with conflicting usage**: U-F3, U-M1, U-M4, U-M6
- **Nature of ambiguity**: In U-F3, Bedeutung appears as a relational property -- the biological significance an object holds for an organism ("relations of meaning are the only certain guides in Umwelt research"). In U-M1, it becomes an ontological category: objects without a subject-relation are "meaningless" (beziehungslos), and meaning is conferred through the act of entering a functional cycle. By U-M4-M6, Bedeutung has become a cosmic principle: "meaning ruled them all" (U-M6, p. 200), binding organs to media, food to consumers, predators to prey. The concept inflates from epistemological guide to ontological bond to quasi-theological principle across the two books.
- **Translation issue**: "Meaning" risks overintellectualizing; Gadamer preferred "significance." In the Theory of Meaning, the word arguably means something closer to "functional purposiveness" than semantic content.

### 2. Gegengefuge / Kontrapunkt (Counterstructure / Counterpoint)

- **German**: *Gegengefuge* (U-F0), *Kontrapunkt* (U-M4-M6)
- **Units with different usage**: U-F0 vs. U-M4, U-M5, U-M6
- **Nature of ambiguity**: In U-F0, Gegengefuge is the object-internal structural link between perception-mark carrier and effect-mark carrier within a single functional cycle. It is a local, mechanical concept. In U-M4-M6, Kontrapunkt describes the macro-level structural correspondence between entire organisms across Umwelten (the flower is bee-like, the bee flower-like). Both terms describe "structural fit," but the scale shifts from intra-object to inter-organismic. Uexkull does not explicitly connect the two terms or acknowledge the shift. In U-M1, the Gegengefuge is also reframed as Komplement (complement), inverting its orientation from object-side to subject-side.
- **Translation issue**: O'Neil preserves the distinction (counterstructure vs. counterpoint). Some secondary literature collapses them.

### 3. Planmassigkeit / Naturplan (Conformity to Plan / Nature's Plan)

- **German**: *Planmassigkeit, Naturplan, Plan*
- **Units with different usage**: U-F3, U-F4, U-F7, U-M4, U-M6
- **Nature of ambiguity**: In U-F3, Planmassigkeit is introduced as a methodological replacement for goal-attribution (Ziel) -- a deflationary heuristic. By U-F4, Naturplane are "supraindividual" and replace instinct; they are "neither force nor material substance." By U-M4-M6, the "composer" of these plans possesses "supersensory knowledge not bound to time." The concept migrates from heuristic to metaphysical postulate. Uexkull never specifies whether plan-talk is regulative (Kantian) or constitutive (ontotheological).
- **Translation issue**: "Planlike quality" (O'Neil) domesticates the quasi-teleological force. "Conformity to plan" is more neutral but loses the agency implied in Naturplan.

### 4. Ton (Tone) -- Polysemy Across the System

- **German**: *Ton, Wirkton, Suchton, Bedeutungston, Merkton, Eigenton, Ich-Ton, Organton, Lebenton*
- **Units with different usage**: U-F4, U-F6, U-F7, U-M1, U-M2, U-M3, U-M5, U-M6
- **Nature of ambiguity**: Uexkull's most prolific and unstable term spans at least six distinct registers: (a) perceptual affordance quality (Wirkton, U-F4: sitting tone, feeding tone), (b) functional search-orientation (Suchton, U-F6: any seat, not a particular chair), (c) cellular vital response (Eigenton/Ich-Ton, U-M2), (d) organ-level directive (Organton, U-M2-M3), (e) organism-level composite (Lebenton, U-M3), and (f) environmental meaning-valence and compositional element (Bedeutungston, U-M5). Uexkull explicitly acknowledges stretching the term (U-M5, p. 192) but treats the polysemy as productive.
- **Translation issue**: "Tone" in English is primarily acoustic. Uexkull's usage is closer to "quality" or "valence" in most contexts, but the musical metaphor is deliberate and load-bearing. No single English word captures the full range.

### 5. Bauplan (Construction Plan / Body Plan)

- **German**: *Bauplan, Bauplanregel, Bauplanmelodie*
- **Units with different usage**: U-M1, U-M2, U-M3, U-M4
- **Nature of ambiguity**: In Part I (implicitly) and U-M1, Bauplan refers to an organism's static structural plan. In U-M2, Bauplanmelodie (form development order) becomes a dynamic developmental melody. In U-M3-M4, Bauplanregel (form development rule) is subordinated to the Bedeutungsregel (meaning rule). The concept thus operates as: (a) static body plan, (b) dynamic developmental melody, and (c) a rule subordinate to meaning. Standard biology uses Bauplan for body plan; Uexkull extends it to environmental structure and subordinates it to his meaning framework.
- **Translation issue**: O'Neil renders Bauplanmelodie as "form development order," losing the musical metaphor (Melodie).

### 6. Stimmung (Mood / Attunement)

- **German**: *Stimmung*
- **Units with different usage**: U-F4, U-F6
- **Nature of ambiguity**: In U-F4, Stimmung determines which effect image activates for a given perception image (hermit crab's three moods vis-a-vis the same sea anemone). By U-F6, Stimmung operates in the search-image framework (feeding tone forces perception image without stimulus). Uexkull uses Stimmung in a biosemiotic sense closer to "dispositional state" or "tuning," but the term carries heavy philosophical freight from Heidegger (Befindlichkeit/attunement) and Dilthey (Erlebnis). Whether Uexkull intends a biological mechanism or a phenomenological structure is undetermined.
- **Translation issue**: "Mood" (O'Neil) is adequate but loses the etymological connection to Stimme (voice) and stimmen (to tune), which Uexkull exploits through the musical metaphor.

### 7. Merkmal (Perception Mark) vs. Standard German "Feature"

- **German**: *Merkmal*
- **Units with different usage**: throughout (U-F0 through U-M6)
- **Nature of ambiguity**: In standard German, Merkmal means "feature" or "characteristic" -- an objective property. Uexkull redefines it as the quality of an object *as perceived by a subject* -- an external mark existing only within a functional cycle. This terminological reappropriation creates persistent ambiguity: when Uexkull says an object has certain Merkmale, the standard-German resonance of "objective features" lingers despite the technical meaning.
- **Translation issue**: O'Neil's "perception mark" is correct for the technical sense but loses the productive double meaning.

## 3E. Book Trajectory

The argumentative arc of Uexkull's combined text traces a path from empirical biology to speculative natural philosophy, progressively expanding a single structural insight -- that organisms and their environments are mutually constituting -- into a comprehensive theory of meaning.

**Part I: A Foray into the Worlds of Animals and Humans** opens with a polemical declaration against mechanism and a founding example. The Foreword and Introduction (U-F0) establish the tick as paradigm case: an organism whose entire world consists of three stimuli (butyric acid, warmth, tactile contact), processed through three functional cycles. From this impoverished but "perfect" world, Uexkull derives the book's foundational concepts -- Umwelt, Merkwelt, Wirkwelt, Funktionskreis, Merkmal, Wirkmal, Gegengefuge -- and states the principle that all organisms are equally well-fitted to their environments. He also announces the Kantian epistemological commitment that structures the entire Foray: "Without a living subject, there can be neither space nor time."

The middle chapters of the Foray (U-F1 through U-F4) build out the Umwelt's spatial, temporal, and qualitative dimensions. U-F1 demonstrates that there is no universal space but three subject-constituted spaces (effect, tactile, visual), each bounded by the organism's sensory apparatus and enclosed by the farthest plane. U-F2 extends this to time: perception time (Merkzeit) is species-specific, and the fighting fish's faster moment makes motion appear slower in its world than in ours. U-F3 marks a pivotal turn: it introduces Bedeutung (biological meaning) as "the only certain guide in the investigation of environments" and replaces teleological goal-attribution with Planmassigkeit (conformity to plan). The perception mark is no longer just a stimulus-response unit but a meaning-bearing quality selected by the organism's structural plan. U-F4 deepens this with the effect image (Wirkbild) and mood (Stimmung): the hermit crab assigns three different meanings to the same sea anemone depending on its internal state. The concept of Wirkton (effect tone) emerges here, establishing that objects are encountered not as bare things but as carriers of action-affordances perceived "with the same assurance" as shape or color.

The later Foray chapters (U-F5 through U-F7) extend the Umwelt from perceptual to social and ecological scales. Territory, home, and the familiar path are shown to be "exclusively subjective products" invisible in objective surroundings (U-F5). The search image and search tone reveal that the subject actively generates perceptual content without external stimulus, and "magical environments" push the Umwelt concept beyond stimulus-response into free subject-productions (U-F6). The Foray culminates in U-F7's oak paradigm: a single tree is ten different objects in ten different Umwelten -- the forester's cordwood, the girl's demon, the fox's shelter, the bark beetle's food-nursery, the ichneumon wasp's soft-as-butter oviposition site. Each environment "cuts out" a portion of the oak; the oak-in-itself carries and shelters all environments but is "never known by all the subjects." The conclusion extends the perspectivism to science itself: the astronomer, the deep-sea researcher, the chemist, and the atomic physicist each inhabit their own Umwelten, and "no harmony" obtains among their worlds.

**Part II: A Theory of Meaning** reopens the argument at a deeper register. Where the Foray described organisms in their environments, the Theory of Meaning asks what constitutes the organism-environment relationship as such. The answer is meaning (Bedeutung), and the text systematically reworks every Foray concept through this lens.

U-M1 introduces the carrier of meaning (Bedeutungstrager) as the replacement for "object": no animal ever encounters a mere object, only entities already bearing meaning conferred by the subject's functional cycle. The Scholastic essentia/accidentia distinction is reinterpreted as meaning-relative: properties acquire rank (essential or accidental) only within a meaning-relation. The dwelling-shell (Wohnhulle) extends the framework to plants, which lack functional cycles but are enveloped by meaning factors (wind, rain, sunlight). U-M2 develops the biological infrastructure of meaning through self-tones (Ich-Ton / Eigenton), melodic induction, and the distinction between meaning factors (for plants) and carriers of meaning (for animals). Functional cycles are reframed as meaning cycles (Bedeutungskreise) whose task is the "utilization of meaning." The priority of the meaning question over causal investigation is stated as a methodological imperative.

U-M3 introduces the primal image (Urbild): the spider's web conforms to the archetype of the fly, not to any particular fly, because the spider has never encountered one. Behind the "curtain of appearances," primal scores (Urpartituren) of different species are connected by a meaning plan (Bedeutungsplan). The distinction between meaning rule (Bedeutungsregel) and form development rule (Bauplanregel) is established through Spemann's grafting experiments: the same general meaning order ("become a mouth") is realized by different species-specific form melodies. The sensory organ is revealed as a double-fronted structure -- outer front (physicochemical sieve) and inner front (living carillon of self-tones) -- grounding the musical metaphor in cellular physiology.

The theoretical crescendo arrives in U-M4-M5 with the composition theory of Nature (Kompositionslehre). Meaning factors and meaning utilizers stand in contrapuntal relation like voices in a duet. The oak's canopy is contrapuntal to rain drops; the octopus's swimming sac is contrapuntal to seawater's incompressibility; the bat's frequency is contrapuntal to the moth's hearing organ. The bee-flower reciprocity is stated as a fundamental principle: "Were the flower not bee-like, and were the bee not flower-like, the consonance could never work." Goethe's dictum -- "Were the eye not sun-like, it could never gaze upon the sun" -- is completed by Uexkull: "Were the sun not eye-like, it could not shine in any sky." Sufferance of meaning (Bedeutungsduldung) accounts for the passive side: organisms that receive meaning against their interests serve species-level equilibrium. Biology's task is now to write the "score of Nature" (Naturpartitur), a comprehensive contrapuntal notation of all meaning-relations in a habitat.

U-M6 brings the argument to its conclusion. Progress is explicitly rejected: "every animal had its own life stage," each populated with meaningful things and fellow players, and "meaning ruled them all." Ranke's dictum ("every age is immediate to God") is applied to Umwelten: each is a complete composition, not a stepping-stone to the next. Materialism is refuted through the inconstancy of matter across Umwelten: no property of an object remains constant when we pass from one environment to another. The formative melody (Bildungsmelodie), the immaterial principle directing morphogenesis, is declared "completely inscrutable" -- "genes = clavier, protoplasm = material, but only the melody is lacking." The final concept, the meaning-germ (Bedeutungskeim), posits that each organism crystallizes from an originary meaning-nucleus as a painting crystallizes from an artistic idea: the acorn is the meaning-germ of the oak.

Across both texts, the conceptual trajectory follows a pattern of systematic deepening: from empirical observation (tick, paramecium, sea urchin) to structural analysis (functional cycle, perception/effect marks) to meaning-theory (carriers of meaning, tones, counterpoint) to speculative natural philosophy (composition theory, world-score, meaning-germ). The Funktionskreis of the Introduction becomes the Bedeutungskreis of the Theory of Meaning; the perception mark becomes the carrier of meaning; the counterstructure (Gegengefuge) expands into counterpoint (Kontrapunkt); and the subject-constituted Umwelt becomes one voice in a cosmic compositional score. What begins as a biologist's protest against mechanism ends as a music-theoretical ontology of Nature in which meaning -- not matter, not mechanism, not progress -- "ruled them all."
