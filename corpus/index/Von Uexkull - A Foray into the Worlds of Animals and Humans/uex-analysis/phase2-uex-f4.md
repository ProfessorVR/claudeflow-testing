## 2. Hierarchical Outline

### Goal and Plan — tail (p. 95)
1. Mother hen and black chick experiment (Figure 27): acoustic vs. optical perception marks yield contradictory functional cycles (pp. 95)
   - Same chick (black) triggers two incompatible responses: rushing toward peeping, attacking on sight
   - Perception marks have not "coalesced into one unit" in the hen's Umwelt (p. 95)

### Perception Image and Effect Image (pp. 96–101)
1. **Nature's plan vs. instinct** (pp. 96–97)
   - Instinct dismissed as a "stopgap product" — replaced by Nature's supraindividual plans (Naturpläne) (p. 96)
   - Plans are neither force nor material substance; concrete analogy: hammer without plan only hits fingers (p. 96)
   - Without plans there would be "no orderly Nature, only chaos" — crystals, Bohr's atomic model as examples (p. 96)
2. **Hermit crab and mood-dependent meaning** (pp. 97–98)
   - Same sea anemone acquires three different meanings depending on crab's mood (Stimmung):
     - Protective tone (pink): anemone placed on shell for squid defense
     - Dwelling tone (orange): crab tries to crawl into anemone (shell removed)
     - Feeding tone (green): starving crab eats the anemone
   - Perception image (Wahrnehmungsbild) completed and altered by effect image (Wirkbild) (p. 97)
3. **Dog experiments: sitting tone** (pp. 97–98)
   - Dog trained to "chair" command jumps on boxes, shelves, footstools — all acquire "canine sitting tone"
   - "Dog chairs" differ from "human chairs": species-specific effect images (p. 98)
4. **The ladder anecdote and effect tone** (pp. 98–99)
   - Man from African interior sees only "bars and holes" until climbing is demonstrated
   - Once the act is learned, perception image acquires "climbing tone" — the effect image complements the perception image (p. 98)
   - Generalization: we alloy effect images with perception images to create meaning; this meaning announces itself as an "effect tone" (Wirkton) (p. 99)
5. **Multiple effect images and mood** (pp. 99)
   - Same object can carry multiple effect images (chair as weapon → "battering tone")
   - Subject's mood (Stimmung) determines which effect image gives tone to perception image
   - Requirement: central effect organs (Wirkorgane); purely reflexive animals (sea urchin) excluded
6. **Effect tones confer environmental security** (pp. 100–101)
   - Number of objects in Umwelt = number of actions available to animal
   - Fewer actions → fewer objects → impoverished but safer environment
   - Paramecium: one action → all objects bear same "obstacle tone" → maximal security
   - Experience increases objects: dogs learn "human" objects but with fewer effect tones
7. **Comparative room illustrations** (pp. 100–101)
   - Same room depicted for human, dog, and fly environments (Plates 2–4)
   - Human: sitting shade, food shade, eating/drinking tones, walking shade, reading shade, writing shade, obstacle shade, light tone
   - Dog: only eating, sitting, obstacle shades remain
   - Fly: everything has "running tone" except table objects and lamp

## 6. Cross-Pipeline References

### 6a. Heidegger B&T Connections
- **Stimmung (mood)** as meaning-determinant: Uexkull's "mood" (Stimmung) that determines which effect image activates parallels Heidegger's analysis of Befindlichkeit/Stimmung in B&T Division I (especially sections 29-30), where mood is a fundamental existential that discloses how Dasein finds itself in the world. The structural parallel is strong: in both cases, mood is not a secondary coloring of perception but a prior condition for how entities show up as meaningful. [INTERP-high]
- **Effect tone and readiness-to-hand**: Uexkull's Wirkton — the "sitting" of the chair, the "climbing" of the ladder perceived as immediately as shape or color — resonates with Heidegger's analysis of equipment (Zeug) and readiness-to-hand (Zuhandenheit) in B&T sections 15-18. For Heidegger, the hammer is encountered primarily in its "in-order-to" (Um-zu), not as a present-at-hand thing with properties. Uexkull's effect tone is the biosemiotic analogue of this pragmatic disclosure. [INTERP-high]
- **Environmental poverty vs. richness**: Uexkull's claim that fewer objects yield greater security for the animal connects to Heidegger's later (1929/30) distinction in *Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics* between animal as "world-poor" (weltarm) and Dasein as "world-forming" (weltbildend). The Foray provides the biological material Heidegger reinterprets. [INTERP-high]

### 6b. BCAP Connections
- Aristotle's analysis of phantasia (De Anima III.3) as an image that mediates between perception and action has a structural parallel to Uexkull's Wirkbild: both are non-sensory representations that guide motor activity. The connection is textual-hook level only; no direct citation.
