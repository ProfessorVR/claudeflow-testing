## 2. Hierarchical Outline

### Foreword (pp. 41–43)
1. Anti-mechanist declaration: these worlds are invisible to machine-theorists (p. 41)
2. Animals as subjects, not objects: aids to perception (Merkzeuge) vs. aids to effects (Werkzeuge) (pp. 41–42)
3. Discovery of the Umwelt: Merkwelt + Wirkwelt = closed environment (p. 42)
4. The soap-bubble metaphor: each animal surrounded by a bubble containing its accessible features (p. 43)
5. Acknowledgments: Kriszat (illustrations), Lorenz (jackdaw images), Eggers (nocturnal butterflies), Huth (watercolors) (p. 43)

### Introduction (pp. 44–52)
1. **The tick paradigm** (pp. 44–48)
   - Life cycle of Ixodes ricinus: egg → nymph → adult → blood meal → death
   - Three stimuli only: butyric acid (olfactory), warmth (thermal), hair/skin (tactile)
   - Three functional cycles executed in sequence
   - 18-year starvation survival: time as subject-controlled
2. **Physiologist vs. biologist debate** (pp. 45–47)
   - Physiologist: tick is a machine with receptors, effectors, and reflex arc
   - Biologist: every cell is a "machine operator" (Maschinenführer), not a machine part
   - Johannes Müller's principle: all external effects produce the same specific sensation (perception sign)
3. **The functional cycle (Funktionskreis)** (pp. 49–50)
   - Schema (Figure 3): Subject ↔ Object linked through perception organ / effect organ
   - Perception-mark carrier and effect-mark carrier connected through Gegengefüge (counterstructure)
   - "The effect mark extinguishes the perception mark" (p. 49)
   - Multiple functional cycles link subjects to objects
4. **Fundamental principle**: "All animal subjects, from the simplest to the most complex, are inserted into their environments to the same degree of perfection" (p. 50)
5. **Time as subject-relative** (p. 48)
   - Human moment = 1/18th second
   - Tick's waiting period: time stops for years, restarts at butyric acid signal
   - "Without a living subject, there can be no time"
6. **Kantian connection** (p. 48)
   - "Without a living subject, there can be neither space nor time"
   - Biology connects with Kant's epistemology; subject constitutes its world
