## 2. Hierarchical Outline

### The Companion (pp. 113–117)
1. Jackdaw attack pattern: black moving objects trigger attack regardless of identity (pp. 113–114)
   - Swimming trunks substituted for cat; white jackdaw not attacked
   - Perception image of "black object carried past" sets off fixed reaction
2. Comparison with sea urchin: general perception images cause systematic confusion (p. 114)
3. Tschock the jackdaw: companion relations across life stages (pp. 115–116)
   - Mother-companion (Lorenz), love-companion (maid), adoptive-companion (young jackdaw), flight-companion (crows)
   - No single perception image corresponds to "companion" — the role changes
4. Innate vs. acquired perception images: mother-signs engraved after hours/days (pp. 115–116)
   - Lorenz quoted: acquired signs become indistinguishable from inborn ones
5. Exclusivity of companion tones: once love tone conferred on substitute, true companion becomes impossible (pp. 116–117)
   - Bittern at Amsterdam Zoo: male preferred zoo director over female conspecific
6. Jackdaw/non-jackdaw distinction: boundary drawn individually, not by perception image alone but by effect image of the individual's own attitude (p. 117)

### Search Image and Search Tone (pp. 117–121)
1. Search image concept introduced via two personal anecdotes (pp. 117–118)
   - Water pitcher replaced by glass carafe: search image "wipes out" perception image
   - 100-mark banknote standing on edge: saleswoman cannot perceive bill that does not match her search image
2. Neurological schema of perception processes (pp. 119–120)
   - Physical (airwaves) → physiological (nerve impulses) → quasi-mental (perception signs transposed into environment)
   - Search image as an invisible optical perception image connected to non-visual stimuli
3. Search image–perception image conflict: if the two differ too greatly, search image excludes perception image (pp. 120–121)
4. Dog's search image: strictly determined (fetching a stick) (p. 121)
5. Toad's hunger-driven search image: worm-shape vs. spider-shape (p. 121)
6. **Search tone** distinguished from search image (p. 121)
   - Searching for a function (Leistung), not a specific object: any seat, not one particular chair
   - Hermit crab revisited: different moods relabelled as search tones (protection, dwelling, feeding)
   - Hungry toad begins with unspecific feeding tone, acquires determinate search image only after eating

### Magical Environments (pp. 119–125)
1. Opposition between human Umgebung and animal Umwelt restated (p. 119)
2. Exceptions to stimulus-driven perception: search image, familiar path, territory — all "free productions of the subject" (p. 119)
3. Definition of magical environments: very effective phenomena visible only to the subject, not bound to repeated experience (pp. 119–120)
4. Child's magical world: Frobenius's anecdote — girl playing Hansel and Gretel sees the witch "in the flesh" (p. 120)
5. Primitive peoples and cultivated Europeans both exhibit magical formations (p. 120)
6. Dogs: magical character of their connection of experiences; master grasped "magically, not divided into cause and effect" (p. 121)
7. Starling and the imaginary fly: bird with no experience of flies performs complete flycatching sequence on an unseen object (pp. 121–122)
   - Environment "laden with feeding tone" forces the perception image without sensory stimulus
8. Pea weevil larva: bores exit channel through still-tender pea flesh for future use (p. 122)

## 6. Cross-Pipeline References

### 6a. Heidegger B&T Connections
- **Free productions of the subject** (p. 119) → Heidegger's analysis of projection (Entwurf) in B&T §31: Dasein projects possibilities onto entities before encountering them perceptually. The search image as "free production" resonates with the priority of understanding over perception. [INTERP-high]
- **Magical environment / phenomena visible only to the subject** → Parallels Heidegger's account of mood (Stimmung, B&T §29): mood discloses the world before any determinate perception, and what shows up is not reducible to stimulus-response. The "feeding tone" that generates an imaginary fly is structurally similar to Befindlichkeit making entities manifest within an attunement. [INTERP-high]
- **Effect image determines perception** (p. 117: "the effect image of the individual's own attitude" decides which companion tone a perception image receives) → Parallels the primacy of practical engagement (Zuhandenheit) over theoretical inspection (Vorhandenheit) in B&T §§15–16. The object is constituted by the subject's orientation, not by detached observation. [INTERP-high]
- **Search tone over search image** (function before form) → Resonates with the in-order-to (Um-zu) structure of equipment in B&T §15: we encounter things first through their function, not their sensory profile. [INTERP-high]

### 6b. BCAP Connections
- The starling's imaginary fly as innate behavior enacted without experience resonates with Aristotle's distinction between phantasia and perception proper (DA III.3): the starling's "magical" image functions like phantasia operating independently of current aisthesis.

### 6c. Rickert Connections
None in this unit.

## 7. Tensions

| Tension | Node A | Node B | Type | Description |
|---------|--------|--------|------|-------------|
| Search image vs. perception image | Suchbild | Merkbild | contrasts_with | The search image can actively suppress the perception image; yet the search image is itself a species of perception image (invisible optical image). The boundary is unstable |
| Innate vs. acquired | angeborene Zeichen | erworbene Zeichen | contrasts_with | Lorenz reports that acquired signs become indistinguishable from innate ones within hours. Uexkull treats this as remarkable but does not resolve the ontological status of the distinction |
| Magical vs. stimulus-driven | magische Umwelt | reizgetriebene Umwelt | contrasts_with | The magical environment contradicts the earlier stimulus-response model of the Funktionskreis; perception images arise without any external stimulus. Uexkull expands his framework but does not formally reconcile the two |
| Tone vs. image | Suchton | Suchbild | refines | Search tone is more general (function-oriented); search image is more specific (form-oriented). Toad example suggests tone precedes image, but the priority is not systematically argued |
| Subject's attitude vs. object's features | Wirkbild | Merkbild | contrasts_with | Whether an entity counts as companion or enemy depends on the subject's effect image, not the object's perception marks. This inverts the earlier chapters' emphasis on stimulus properties |
