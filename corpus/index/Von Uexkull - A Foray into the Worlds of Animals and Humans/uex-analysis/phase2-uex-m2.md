## 2. Hierarchical Outline

### Environment and Dwelling-Shell (pp. 150–153)
1. Carrier of meaning as complement of animal subject; perception-mark and effect-mark carriers linked by counterstructure (p. 150)
2. Animal vs. plant dwelling: animal house = mobile + nervous system + functional cycles; plant house = immobile + no perception/effect organs (pp. 150–151)
3. Both organisms make a precise selection from external effects (p. 151)
4. Melodic induction vs. mechanical induction: nerve-to-nerve progression follows a melody, not electrical causation (pp. 151–152)
5. Johannes Muller's vital energy: living tissue (muscle) responds to any stimulus with its specific vital activity, unlike a dead bell (p. 152)
6. The living carillon (*Ich-Ton*): each living bell responds to any stimulus with its subjective self-tone; a living carillon is controlled by a melody, not a mechanism (pp. 152–153)
7. Arndt's slime mold film: amoebas self-organize into zones, form stalk and fruiting body; free cells obeying a melody (pp. 153–154)
8. Slime mold as animal-to-plant transition: amoeba phase has Umwelt with bacteria as meaning carriers; adult fungus has only a dwelling-shell with meaning factors (wind) (pp. 153–154)

### Utilization of Meaning (pp. 154–160)
1. Dwelling-world transforms into Umwelt (animal) or dwelling-shell (plant) when observed from the subject's standpoint (p. 154)
2. Meaning-utilization (*Bedeutungsverwertung*): functional cycles reinterpreted as meaning cycles; perception + effectuation = utilization of carriers of meaning (pp. 154–155)
3. Clouds vs. dandelion parachutes: wind as cause (clouds) vs. wind as meaning factor utilized by planned form (dandelion, maple key, linden fruit) (p. 155)
4. Thesis: "The meaningful form that lasts is always the product of a subject and never of a planlessly worked-on object" (p. 155)
5. Meaning factors of the plant: rain → drip grooves; sunlight → chlorophyll; form owes itself to meaning, not to causation (p. 155)
6. Priority of the meaning question: "Only once it is solved does it make any sense to research causally conditioned processes" (p. 155)
7. Growth melody (*Wachstumsmelodie*): controls self-tones of germ cells; form development order arranges zones with technical central points (p. 156)
8. Driesch and sea urchin germ cells: original equality in value of germ cells (p. 156)
9. **Spemann's grafting experiments** (pp. 156–158): tissue transplanted between gastrula-stage embryos develops according to location, not origin; meaning order "mouth" given to tadpole tissue in triton host → tadpole mouth, not triton mouth
10. Meaning order (*Bedeutungsregel*) vs. form development order (*Bauplanmelodie*): general meaning command ("become a mouth") + species-specific form melody → can produce dissonance/malformation (pp. 158–159)
11. Flatfish analogy: ray (dorso-ventral flattening) vs. plaice (lateral flattening) — same meaning order, different form orders (p. 159)
12. Sea urchin venom pincers: same meaning task (chase enemy), different mechanical solutions across species; one species uses crossbow mechanism (pp. 159–160)
13. Centripetal (mechanism/clock) vs. centrifugal (organism/triton) construction: machine assembled from pre-made parts inward; organism grows outward from germ (p. 160)
14. Organs vs. parts: living beings consist of organs (living cells with self-tones), not parts; organ tone = meaning tone controls cell self-tones (p. 160)

## 6. Cross-Pipeline References

### 6a. Heidegger B&T Connections
- **Meaning order vs. form development order** → Resonates with Heidegger's analysis of the *as-structure* (Als-Struktur) of understanding (B&T §32): an entity is encountered "as" something (meaning order) before its specific material constitution matters. The general prompt "mouth" that is then realized species-specifically parallels the hermeneutic "as" preceding ontic determination. [INTERP-high]
- **Priority of the meaning question over causal investigation** → Parallels Heidegger's claim that existential analysis must precede any positive science (B&T §§3–4). Uexküll's "only once it is solved does it make any sense to research causally conditioned processes" (p. 155) maps onto Heidegger's foundational-ontology argument. [INTERP-high]
- **Centripetal (mechanism) vs. centrifugal (organism) construction** → Connects to Heidegger's critique of the present-at-hand (Vorhandenheit) as derivative from the ready-to-hand (Zuhandenheit): the clock-parts model treats organs as present-at-hand components assembled externally, whereas the organism grows from within as an equipmental totality. [INTERP-high]
- **Organ tone / meaning tone** → The concept of *Bedeutungston* as the holistic directive that controls parts resonates with Heidegger's notion of *Bewandtnisganzheit* (totality of involvements) in B&T §18, where the whole determines the significance of each piece of equipment. [INTERP-high]

### 6b. BCAP Connections
- Uexküll's distinction between living beings and mechanisms (p. 160: "each living being, unlike all mechanisms, consists not of parts but of organs") echoes Aristotle's *Physis*-analysis where natural beings have their principle of motion within themselves, unlike artifacts. Potentially relevant to BCAP's treatment of Aristotle's critique of mechanistic explanations.
