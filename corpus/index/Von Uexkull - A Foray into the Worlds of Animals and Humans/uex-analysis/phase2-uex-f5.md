## 2. Hierarchical Outline

### The Familiar Path (pp. 102–106)
1. Human familiar path: local guide follows invisible route through unmarked terrain (p. 102)
2. Three kinds of perception marks in path description: visual, directional planes of coordinate system, directional steps (p. 102)
3. The walking step as fixed directional unit and historical measure of length (p. 103)
4. Animal familiar paths: olfactory and tactile perception signs as structuring elements (p. 103)
5. Critique of American maze experiments: researchers missed the Umwelt problem entirely (pp. 103–104)
   - Never studied perception marks, coordinate systems, or directional steps
   - Never considered that left/right is a problem in itself
6. Blind man and guide dog: introducing alien perception marks into the dog's Umwelt (pp. 104–105)
   - Dog must learn marks irrelevant to its own Umwelt (mailbox, open window, curbstone)
7. Jackdaw's familiar path: uses outbound path for return flight, fails to recognize home from unfamiliar direction (p. 105)
8. Rats persist in familiar detour even when direct path is available (p. 105)
9. Fighting fish experiments (pp. 105–106)
   - The unfamiliar has a repelling effect
   - Fish uses familiar hole even when unfamiliar hole is closer to food
   - Visual and directional perception marks plus directional steps involved
10. Summary metaphor: familiar path as "a streak of a more fluid medium in a more viscous one" (p. 106)

### Home and Territory (pp. 106–111)
1. Stickleback territory: male builds nest, defends invisible border; always wins in own territory (pp. 106–107)
2. Territory as purely an Umwelt problem: "an exclusively subjective product" inexplicable from knowledge of surroundings alone (p. 107)
3. Distinction: housefly's repeated flight path is not territory; spider's web-nest is home + territory (p. 107)
4. Mole: home (padded cave), territory (tunnel system), hunting ground overlap (pp. 107–108)
   - Scent organ detects food at 5–6 meters through solid earth
   - Tunnel system compared to spider's web
   - Defends territory to the death against neighboring moles
5. Mole's spatial orientation without vision: effect space (Wirkraum) + directional steps + tactile perception marks joined in a spatial schema (Raumschema) (pp. 108–109)
   - Can reproduce destroyed passage system via projected schema
6. Bees: have home but no territory (hunting ground not defended) (p. 109)
7. Magpies: home + territory (tolerate no free-roaming magpies) (p. 109)
8. Territory as political map: conspecific defense transforms hunting ground into territory (p. 109)
9. Raptor neutral zone: prey-free buffer between nest and hunting ground protects young from parental predation (pp. 109–110)
   - Nestling → branchling progression in protected area
   - Songbirds exploit raptor neutral zone for nesting safety
10. Dog territorial marking via odor marks: Hamburg Zoo map experiment (pp. 110–111)
    - Urinating contest when dogs walked simultaneously
    - Spirited dog over-marks stranger's scent; timid dog passes silently
11. Bear territorial marking: rubbing bark at full height signals body size to rivals (p. 111)

### The Companion (p. 112)
1. Duckling raised among turkeys: avoids water and other ducks (p. 112)
2. Wild duck follows Uexkull's boots; black dachshund also triggers following — black moving object substitutes for mother-image (p. 112)
3. Grey goose hatchlings from incubator: must be placed with goose family immediately or they reject conspecifics permanently (p. 112)
4. Explanation: confusion of perception images (Verwechslung der Merkbilder), especially common in birds (p. 112)
5. Jackdaw attacks cat carrying prey but not empty-mouthed cat: perception image for "attackable enemy" requires teeth out of action (p. 112)

## 6. Cross-Pipeline References

### 6a. Heidegger B&T Connections
- **Territory as subjective product** → Resonates with Heidegger's analysis of space as existentially constituted (B&T §§22–24). Territory cannot be found by inspecting Umgebung (objective surroundings), just as Dasein's spatiality cannot be derived from geometric space. [INTERP-high]
- **Projected spatial schema (Raumschema)** → The mole's ability to rebuild destroyed passages from an internal schema parallels Heidegger's notion that Dasein projects its spatial orientation in advance of encountering particular entities (B&T §23, de-severance and directionality). [INTERP-high]
- **Companion / Merkbild confusion** → The imprinting phenomenon raises the question of how the "as-structure" (B&T §32) operates in animal perception: the duckling sees the turkey *as* mother. Whether animals genuinely have an "as-structure" is precisely the question Heidegger addresses in GA 29/30. [INTERP-high]

### 6b. BCAP Connections
- **Territory and the Aristotelian topos** → Territory as species-specific, defended, and invisible to outsiders contrasts with Aristotle's treatment of place (topos) as the innermost boundary of the containing body (Physics IV.4). Uexkull's territory is constituted by the subject; Aristotle's topos is constituted by the surrounding body. Potential cross-text hook.
