## 2. Hierarchical Outline

### Form and Movement as Perception Marks (pp. 79–85)
1. **Sea urchin lacks form and movement as perception marks** (p. 79)
   - Reflex persons have local signs but no inter-place connection
   - Form and movement require joining together of several places
2. **Moving form vs. resting form as independent perception marks** (pp. 79–80)
   - Jackdaw and grasshopper: cannot see motionless grasshopper, only snaps at hopping one (p. 79)
   - "Playing dead" explained: resting form absent from predator's Umwelt entirely (pp. 79–80)
   - Fly-angling experiment: swinging pea imitates flying female; males only respond to movement (p. 80)
   - Moving female and resting female are two different perception marks (p. 80)
3. **Movement without form as perception mark** (pp. 80–82)
   - Scallop and starfish: starfish form is irrelevant; only slow movement triggers tentacle response (pp. 80–82)
   - Speed-specificity: scallop's eyes tuned to starfish's speed, not form or color (pp. 81–82)
   - Two functional cycles in sequence: movement triggers tentacles (olfactory), then flight cancels perception marks (p. 82)
4. **The earthworm and the problem of form-perception** (pp. 82–84)
   - Darwin's claim: earthworms distinguish leaves and pine needles by form (p. 82)
   - Refutation: gelatin-stick experiments show earthworms distinguish by taste, not shape (pp. 82–84)
   - "Nature knows how to deal with difficulties that to us seem entirely insuperable" (p. 84)
5. **Bees and the schema of open vs. closed form** (pp. 84–85)
   - Bees prefer open forms (stars, crosses) over closed forms (squares, circles) (p. 84)
   - Biological meaning (Bedeutung): blooms = open forms, buds = closed forms (p. 84)
   - Perception cells arranged in two groups: "opened" and "closed" schemata (p. 84)
   - Schemata filled with colors and odors when transposed outward (p. 84)
   - Key claim: "Relations of meaning are... the only certain guides in the investigation of environments" (p. 84)
6. **Hierarchy of perception capacity** (p. 85)
   - Earthworm, scallop, tick lack true perception images (Merkbilder)
   - Bees possess schemata that enable genuine form-perception

### Goal and Plan (pp. 86–91)
1. **Against goal-attribution in animal life** (pp. 86–87)
   - Humans project goal-directedness onto animals — "a fundamental mistake" (p. 86)
   - Even "lying in wait" (tick) smuggles human teleology into description (p. 86)
   - Methodological imperative: "extinguish the will-o'-the-wisp of the goal" (p. 86)
2. **Plan (Planmäßigkeit) vs. Goal (Ziel)** (pp. 86–87)
   - Vital expressions must be viewed "from the point of view of the plan" (p. 86)
   - Only highest mammals might exhibit goal-oriented action; even then, within Nature's overall plan (p. 86)
3. **Moths and sound: same perception mark, opposite effects** (pp. 86–87)
   - Brightly colored moths flee at high-pitched sound; protectively colored moths alight (p. 86)
   - No goal or distinction possible: no moth has seen its own coloration (p. 87)
   - Hearing organ built solely for the bat's frequency — totally deaf to all else (p. 87)
4. **Fabre's emperor moth experiment** (pp. 87–88)
   - Female leaves chemical trace on paper; males crowd paper, not the bell-jarred female (p. 87)
   - Functional cycle activated by perception mark; normal object eliminated, so effect mark never produced (p. 88)
5. **Grasshopper/cricket loudspeaker experiment** (pp. 87–88)
   - Mates respond to amplified sound, ignore visually present but silenced individual (p. 88)
   - Optical image has no effect — auditory perception mark alone activates functional cycle (p. 88)
   - Both experiments prove: not goal-pursuit but planlike activation of functional cycles (p. 88)
6. **Mother hen and chick: dismantling "maternal care" as goal** (pp. 88–91)
   - Bound chick peeps → hen rushes toward sound even without seeing chick (p. 88)
   - Bell-jarred chick visible but inaudible → hen entirely undisturbed (p. 91)
   - Sound, not sight of distress, is the operative perception mark (pp. 88–91)

## 6. Cross-Pipeline References

### 6a. Heidegger B&T Connections

- **Perception marks as "as"-structure**: Uexküll's claim that the scallop perceives movement-as-enemy (not starfish-as-form) and the jackdaw perceives motion-as-prey structurally parallels Heidegger's hermeneutical "as" (apophantisches Als, B&T §33). The organism always already encounters the object *as* something within its functional cycle. [INTERP-high]
- **Planmäßigkeit and Bewandtnisganzheit**: Uexküll's "Nature's plan" (Naturplan) as the structural totality within which functional cycles are embedded resonates with Heidegger's "totality of involvements" (Bewandtnisganzheit, B&T §18). Both are non-teleological structural wholes that precede any particular encounter. [INTERP-high]
- **Against goal-projection**: Uexküll's methodological imperative to "extinguish the will-o'-the-wisp of the goal" parallels Heidegger's critique of theoretical comportment as derivative of pre-theoretical engagement. Both reject the projection of human purposive reason onto non-human modes of being. [INTERP-high]
