## 2. Hierarchical Outline

### Transition from Magical Environments (pp. 126–130)
1. Inborn path (magischer Weg) vs. familiar path: same structural elements (perception signs + effect signs), but given as innate melody rather than experience-triggered (pp. 126–128)
2. Weevil larva, birch-leaf roller, migratory birds as instances of the inborn path (pp. 126–127)
3. Magical phenomena: the hen and the guinea pig shadow — environment haunted by irrecoverable experience (p. 128)
4. Purely subjective realities in environments vs. objective realities of surroundings (pp. 129–130)
5. Foundational principle restated: "each and every subject lives in a world in which there are only subjective realities" (p. 130)

### The Same Subject as Object in Different Environments (pp. 130–136)
1. Programmatic statement: how does the same subject appear as object in different Umwelten? (pp. 130–131)
2. **The oak paradigm** — single object, multiple environments (pp. 131–136):
   - Forester: oak = cords of wood (use tone / Nutzton) (p. 132)
   - Little girl: oak = demon with wicked face (danger tone / Gefahrton in magical environment) (p. 132)
   - Russian workers: apple tree fungus = miraculous icon (magical tone) (pp. 132–133)
   - Fox: oak roots = protective roof (protection tone / Schutzton) (p. 133)
   - Owl: oak branches = protective wall (protection tone) (p. 133)
   - Squirrel: branches = springboards (climbing tone / Klettertönung) (p. 134)
   - Songbirds: twigs = nest supports (carrying tone / Tragtönung) (p. 134)
   - Ants: bark surface = hunting ground (pp. 134–135)
   - Bark beetle: beneath bark = food + nursery (pp. 135–136)
   - Ichneumon wasp: oak wood = soft as butter (penetration for oviposition) (p. 136)
3. Each environment "cuts out" a portion of the oak; the oak-in-itself carries and shelters all environments but is "never known by all the subjects" and "never knowable for them" (p. 136)

### Conclusion (pp. 137–139)
1. Oak paradigm scaled to Nature: "Nature's tree of life" harbors millions of environments (p. 137)
2. Four scientific Umwelten as further perspectival cuts:
   - Astronomer: suns and planets at solemn pace; light takes millions of years (pp. 137–138)
   - Deep-sea researcher: fantastic fish, phosphorescent organs (p. 138)
   - Chemist: 92 elemental "letters" composing Nature's "matter-words" (p. 138)
   - Atomic physicist: electrons circling, bombardment of particles — "no harmony among the worlds" (p. 138)
3. Implied final thesis: even the scientific observer's environment is a "tiny excerpt from Nature, tailored to the capacities of the human subject" (p. 137)

## 6. Cross-Pipeline References

### 6a. Heidegger B&T Connections
- **Nature as unknowable totality sheltering all Umwelten** (p. 136) --> Heidegger's distinction between entities "within-the-world" and the world itself (B&T Section 14); the oak-in-itself is structurally analogous to Heidegger's critique of presence-at-hand as failing to capture Being. [INTERP-high]
- **Each Umwelt "cuts out" its object** --> Resonates with Heidegger's analysis of the ready-to-hand (Zuhandenheit): equipment appears only within a referential totality, not as bare object (B&T Section 15–16). The forester's oak-as-cordwood is a paradigmatic case of something showing up within a practical context. [INTERP-high]
- **Scientific environments as perspectival excerpts** (pp. 137–138) --> Parallels Heidegger's critique of theoretical attitude as derivative and world-impoverishing (B&T Section 69b). The astronomer's Umwelt is still an Umwelt, not a view from nowhere. [INTERP-high]
- **Subjective realities as sole content of environments** (p. 130) --> Connects to Heidegger's insistence that Dasein is always already "in" a world constituted by significance, not by bare sensory data (B&T Sections 12–13). [INTERP-high]

### 6b. BCAP Connections
- Aristotle's distinction between to pragma (the thing) and to phainomenon (how it appears) provides a classical frame for the oak's multiple appearances. [INTERP-low]

### 6c. Rickert Connections
- Uexkull's claim that summing all perspectival features yields "chaos" rather than knowledge resonates with Rickert's argument in *Die Grenzen* that the manifold of intuition requires value-selection to become knowledge. The environment-cut functions analogously to Rickert's concept-forming selection. [INTERP-medium]
