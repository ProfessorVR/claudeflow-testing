## 2. Hierarchical Outline

### Counterpoint as Motif/Motive of Form Development — final section (pp. 195–199)
1. **Meaning of use-objects** (pp. 195–196)
   - Coffee cup: counterpoint to coffee + human hand; function = motive of production
   - Chair: seat, back, arms, legs each build bridges to counterpoints (body, ground)
   - "The theory of meaning culminates in the uncovering of this connection" (p. 195)
   - Human technology as progressive detachment from Nature; bridges upon bridges
2. **Nature's form-development** (pp. 196–198)
   - Slime mold: free amoebas → tissue cells via new counterpoint (wind as spore-spreader)
   - Vertebrate organ buds: meaning fixed through relation to the whole; Spemann's graft experiment
   - Pea weevil larva: future form (Gestalt) as present motif—goal-pursuing quality (von Baer)
   - Spider web: web is goal, fly is counterpoint/motive
   - Birch-leaf roller: "constant path" as motive for its own emergence—Nature's compositional secrets
3. **History of natural technology** (pp. 198–199)
   - Lamarck as first researcher of Naturtechnik (giraffe/palm contrapuntal relation)
   - Haeckel's ancestor speculation rejected
   - Driesch's sea urchin experiment: halved germ → two whole urchins; melody cannot be cut

### Progress (pp. 199–201)
1. **St. Matthew Passion analogy** (p. 199): drama of Nature as single composition, not progressive improvement
2. **Rejection of progress** (pp. 199–200)
   - No imperfection in simplest animals; material always used in best possible way
   - "Every animal had its own life stage"—each populated with meaningful things and fellow players
   - Masterful hand: composition after composition, "never showing any transitions from the imperfect to the perfect"
3. **Meaning as universal bond** (p. 200)
   - "Meaning ruled them all": meaning binds organs to medium, food to consumer, predator to prey, male to female
   - "Everywhere there was a progression, but nowhere progress in the sense of the survival of the fittest"
4. **Ranke on history** (pp. 200–201)
   - "Every age is immediate to God, and its value consists not in that which comes of it later, but in its own existence—in its own self"
   - Age = group of human Umwelten within a limited period; each immediate to God = same composition
5. **Refutation of materialism** (pp. 201–202)
   - Inconstancy of objects: same flower stalk = four different objects in four environments
   - Inconstancy of matter: bee sees non-yellow flower; ichneumon wasp drills fir as butter
   - "No single property of matter remains constant as we course through the series of environments"
   - Constancy of subjects better founded than constancy of objects

### Summary and Conclusion (pp. 204–208)
1. **House-and-garden analogy** (pp. 204–205)
   - Body = house; Umwelt = garden; sensory organs = windows (light, sound, olfactory, taste, tactile)
   - Garden surrounded by horizon with house at center; each house under its own canopy
   - Revisits tick: of all things we discover around the tick, "none enters the tick's environment"
2. **Objects as perception-sign constructs** (pp. 205–206)
   - "All the properties of things are nothing other than the perception signs imprinted upon them by the subject"
   - Living cells form a living carillon (Glockenspiel); each cell responds with a "self-tone" (Eigenton)
3. **The functional cycle restated** (p. 206)
   - Perception organ → brain carillon → effect organ → carrier of meaning → back to subject
   - "A sort of musical process" linking carrier and recipient of meaning
4. **Formative melodies and counterpoint** (pp. 206–207)
   - Bumblebee and snapdragon: mutual motif-exchange in formative melodies
   - "If its body were not flowerlike, its structure would never work"
   - This "cardinal principle of natural technology" decides the question of progress negatively
5. **Inscrutability of the formative melody** (p. 207)
   - Natural technology's successes plain, but melody formation inscrutable
   - Analogy: we see the painter's hand, not the formative melody that moves it
   - Music box analogy: we understand how it plays, never how a melody constructs its music box
   - Genes = clavier, protoplasm = material, but "only the melody is lacking"
6. **Meaning-germ (Bedeutungskeim)** (pp. 207–208)
   - Music box: score crystallized out of the melody; melody = meaning-germ of the box
   - Arosenius painting Jul: meaning-germ "Madonna" → all objects result automatically
   - Acorn = meaning-germ of oak; egg = meaning-germ of chicken
   - Living protoplasm: the most pliable material; yields to any form development from self-tones
   - Each organ bud contains its own meaning-germ

## 6. Cross-Pipeline References

### 6a. Heidegger B&T Connections
- **"Every age is immediate to God"** (Ranke, via Uexküll, p. 200) → Resonates with Heidegger's critique of vulgar time-concept in B&T Division II; each moment (Augenblick) has its own authentic temporality, not a stepping-stone to the next. [INTERP-high]
- **Formative melody as inscrutable** (p. 207) → Parallels Heidegger's withdrawal of Being: that which gives beings their being is not itself a being and cannot be grasped as present-at-hand. The melody that "constructs its music box" is structurally analogous to the ontological difference. [INTERP-high]
- **Subject bound to its species-nature** (p. 203: "the human being remains bound to its human nature") → Anticipates Heidegger's Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics (GA 29/30) distinction: animal as "poor in world" (weltarm), human as "world-forming" (weltbildend). Uexküll here allows degrees (tools broaden the compass) but maintains an enclosure. [INTERP-high]
- **World-score (Partitur)** → Resonates with Heidegger's "worldhood of the world" (B&T §§14–18): the totality of meaning-relations that constitutes a world. For Uexküll this totality is composed by Nature; for Heidegger it is disclosed by Dasein. [INTERP-high]
- **Rejection of progress / linear time** → Connects to Heidegger's critique of leveled-off time (Nivellierung) in B&T §81; authentic temporality is not a succession of now-points improving toward a telos. [INTERP-high]

### 6b. BCAP Connections
- **Naturtechnik / inscrutability of formative melody** → Aristotle's distinction between techne and physis in Physics II: natural things have their principle of motion in themselves, unlike artifacts. Uexküll's position that Nature's technique is inscrutable aligns with Aristotle's claim that physis is prior to techne.

### 6c. Rickert Connections
- **Inconstancy of matter / subject-dependence of properties** → Rickert's neo-Kantian distinction between natural-scientific concept formation (generalizing) and historical concept formation (individualizing). Uexküll's argument that properties change per Umwelt undermines the natural-scientific presupposition of stable matter.
