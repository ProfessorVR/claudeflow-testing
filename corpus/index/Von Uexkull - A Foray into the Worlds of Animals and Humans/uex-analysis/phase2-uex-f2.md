## 2. Hierarchical Outline

### A. Perception Time (pp. 70-72)

1. **Time as subjective product** (p. 70)
   - Credit to Karl Ernst von Baer for making time "intuitively understandable as a product of the subject"
   - Definition of moments as "smallest indivisible vessels of time," expressions of *Momentzeichen* (moment signs)
   - Human moment = 1/18th of a second, uniform across sensory modalities

2. **Cinematographic analogy: slow motion and time-lapse** (pp. 70-71)
   - Slow motion stretches rapid processes into human perception time (bird wings, insect wings)
   - Time-lapse compresses slow processes into human perception time (blooming flower)

3. **Animal experiments on perception time** (pp. 71-72)
   - Fighting fish: requires 30 images/sec to recognize mirror image (vs. human 18/sec) — faster moment, slower-appearing motion
   - Fish trained with rotating discs: black-and-white sectors fuse to gray above 1/50th sec threshold
   - Snail (*Helix pomatia*): stick moving 4+/sec perceived as stationary; moment = 3-4 per second — all motion appears faster in snail's Umwelt

### B. Simple Environments (pp. 73-78)

1. **Spatiotemporal framework as secondary** (p. 73)
   - Space and time "of no immediate use to the subject" — needed only when multiple perception marks must be distinguished
   - Simple Umwelten harbor only one *Merkzeichen* (perception sign)

2. **Paramecium: single functional cycle** (pp. 73-74)
   - One perception mark: *Hindernis* (hindrance)
   - Single effect mark cancels perception mark via flight response (backward + lateral turn)
   - Feed (putrefactive bacteria) defined negatively: sends out no stimulus

3. **Rhizostoma (medusa): self-sustaining reflex loop** (pp. 75)
   - One functional cycle governs swimming, eating, breathing simultaneously
   - Eight bell-shaped organs produce the medusa's own effect mark, which generates the next perception mark — loop ad infinitum
   - "Reflex animal" (*Reflextier*): same reflex runs from bell to muscle band

4. **Sea urchin: the reflex republic** (pp. 76-78)
   - Multiple autonomous "reflex persons" (*Reflexpersonen*): quills, tube feet, four kinds of pedicellariae
   - Dog vs. sea urchin maxim: "When a dog runs, the animal moves its legs. When a sea urchin runs, its legs move the animal" (p. 76)
   - Civil peace without central direction, maintained by *Autodermin* — chemical substance blocking receptors when skin contacts itself
   - Perception marks remain "completely isolated" because functional cycles are isolated

5. **Tick contrasted with sea urchin** (pp. 77-78)
   - Tick possesses a "common perception organ" integrating three stimuli into a single prey-unit
   - Sea urchin cannot unify its isolated perception marks
   - Shadow response: sea urchin reacts identically to cloud, ship, or fish — no visual space, shadow = tactile-like stimulus on light-sensitive skin

## 6. Cross-Pipeline References

### 6a. Heidegger B&T Connections

- **Temporality as subject-constituted**: Uexkull's claim that time is "a product of the subject" (p. 70) resonates with Heidegger's analysis of temporality (*Zeitlichkeit*) as constitutive of Dasein's being, though Uexkull's framework is biological-perceptual rather than existential-ontological. [INTERP-high]
- **World-poverty and the animal**: The graduated scale from paramecium (one functional cycle) through sea urchin (reflex republic) to tick (integrated perception) anticipates the *Grundbegriffe der Metaphysik* (1929/30) lectures where Heidegger directly engages Uexkull, placing the animal as "poor in world" (*weltarm*) between stone (*weltlos*) and Dasein (*weltbildend*). The paramecium's single-perception-mark world is an extreme case of this poverty. [INTERP-high]

## 7. Tensions

| Tension | Node A | Node B | Type | Description |
|---------|--------|--------|------|-------------|
| Mechanistic description vs. holistic Umwelt theory | Reflex arc (mechanistic) | Functional cycle (biosemiotic) | Methodological | Uexkull uses reflex-arc language ("reflex animal," "reflex republic") yet insists these are functional cycles with perception and effect marks — semiotic, not merely causal. The tension is between borrowed mechanistic vocabulary and the vitalist-semiotic framework. |
| Central vs. decentral organization | Centrally directed organisms (dog, human) | Reflex republic (sea urchin) | Ontological | Uexkull presents central direction as the norm for "higher animals" but shows the sea urchin achieves coordinated behavior without it. The boundary between these organizational types and what counts as "higher" remains under-theorized. |
