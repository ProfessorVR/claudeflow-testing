## 2. Hierarchical Outline

### Form Development Rule and Meaning Rule (pp. 171–172, tail)
1. Bat-moth example: bat's peep as dual carrier of meaning — friend to bats, enemy to moths (p. 171)
2. Meaning rule intervenes in form development rule (embryogenesis), not in active development itself (p. 172)
3. Form development as a whole depends on the meaning rule; grafting disrupts irreversibly (p. 172)

### The Meaning Rule as the Bridging of Two Elementary Rules (pp. 172–176)
1. **The acorn riddle**: future effects cannot causally influence present germ; no causal chain from seedling to environment (pp. 172–174)
   - Rejects phylogenetic-mechanical explanation as a blind alley (p. 173)
2. **Rule of Nature**: sun, moon, seasons, storms follow a common natural order; only certain factors penetrate the oak's dwelling-shell (Wohnhülle) (pp. 173–174)
3. **Meaning rule selects** from elementary natural factors, making them "sound in the living carillon" of cells (p. 174)
4. **Arndt's film evidence**: amoeba formation — free amoebas oriented to "food" reorient to "wind" as new meaning factor; a unified melody replaces cacophony (p. 174)
5. **Subjectification hierarchy**: cell tone → organ melody → organism symphony = primal image (Urbild); contradicts mechanical object-on-object causation (p. 175)
6. **Counterpoint thesis**: meaning factors stand in contrapuntal relation to meaning utilizers, as voices in a duet; this yields a composition theory (Kompositionslehre) of Nature (pp. 175–176)

### The Composition Theory of Nature (pp. 176–184)
1. **Methodological disclaimer**: Nature teaches no "Lehre"; the theory generalizes observed compositional rules (pp. 176–177)
2. **Musical model**: at least two tones for harmony; duet voices composed point for point (p. 176)
3. **Subject-environment schema**: subject as meaning utilizer/receiver; object as carrier of meaning; contrapuntal composition when both join in the same meaning (p. 176)
4. **Oak-rain contrapuntal table** (p. 177): canopy roof-tile arrangement vs. physical drop-formation rule → common meaning rule of capturing/distributing liquid
5. **Octopus-seawater** (pp. 177–178): incompressibility of water as counterpoint to muscular swimming sac; meaning rule = swimming
6. **Generalization across media** (p. 178): all effector organs (running, jumping, climbing, flying, etc.) built contrapuntally to the properties of water/land/air
7. **Receptorial relations** (p. 178): sensory organs also contrapuntally built — eye for light, ear or tactile organ for darkness
8. **The gender functional cycle** (pp. 179–180): males and females composed for each other; love duet in a thousand variations
   - Emperor moth scent experiment (Fabre): olfactory mark overrides visual (pp. 179–180)
   - Bitterling-mussel triangle: secondary carrier of meaning inserted into sexual circle (pp. 179–180)
9. **Meaning-switch without carrier change** (pp. 180–181): brown ground beetle females switch from "friend" to "food" after mating; curbstone analogy (path → projectile)
10. **Imprinting as meaning-conferral** (pp. 180–181): Lorenz's grey geese imprint first seen creature as "mother"; Uexküll's reinterpretation via meaning-role assignment
11. **Eagle owl fostering ducks** (p. 181): owl's fosterlings carry meaning "offspring" while conspecific ducks carry meaning "prey"
12. **Span of the meaning rule** (p. 181): short in sex/offspring (same species), unlimited in enemy/food cycles
13. **Bat-moth revisited as formal schema** (pp. 181–182): same tone → two different hearing organs via two different meanings
14. **Tick-mammal contrapuntal table** (pp. 182–183): four-point schema — butyric acid/olfaction, hair/tactile, warmth/temperature, soft skin/stinger; common meaning rule = recognizing and attacking prey
15. **Mammal-in-itself** (p. 183): in human environment, mammal-in-itself is only a concept (notional abstraction); in tick's environment, it is a thoroughly intuitable object with few properties
16. **Hermit crab and snail shell** (pp. 183–184): gripping tail contrapuntally composed to snail shell, as rowing tail to water; no mechanical gradual adaptation
17. **Bee shape-perception** (p. 184): Mathilde Hertz's discovery — bees distinguish open vs. closed shapes; interpretation rule controls honey-gathering; two schemata contrapuntal to two flower shapes
18. **Earthworm taste-substitution** (p. 184): worm cannot form shape schemata but distinguishes leaf tips from bases by taste (Mangold); taste substitutes for absent visual capacity

## 6. Cross-Pipeline References

### 6a. Heidegger B&T Connections
- **Nature as composer vs. Dasein as world-discloser**: Uexküll's claim that Nature composes organisms contrapuntally to their environments parallels (and diverges from) Heidegger's analysis of worldhood. For Heidegger, Dasein discloses world through care-structure; Uexküll posits a suprasubjective compositional agency ("Nature"). [INTERP-high]
- **Meaning-switch (Bedeutungswechsel)**: The curbstone shifting from path-element to projectile (p. 180) closely parallels Heidegger's analysis of the "as-structure" (Als-Struktur) in B&T §32-33, where the same entity shows up differently depending on the subject's practical engagement. [INTERP-high]
- **Mammal-in-itself as intuitable vs. concept**: The tick's mammal-in-itself being "thoroughly intuitable" rather than a mere concept (p. 183) inverts the Kantian Ding-an-sich and resonates with Heidegger's rejection of the thing-in-itself in favor of beings as they show themselves within a world. [INTERP-high]
- **Subjectification (Zellton → Organismusymphonie)**: The ascending hierarchy of meaning-constitution from cell to organism echoes Heidegger's layered structure of Being-in-the-world, though Uexküll locates constitution in biological organization rather than existential structures. [INTERP-high]

### 6b. BCAP Connections
- The bat-moth hearing example and the tick-mammal schema may connect to Aristotle's analysis of perception (aisthesis) as a reception of form without matter (De Anima II.12), though Uexküll's framework is explicitly anti-Aristotelian in its rejection of final cause language while functionally reintroducing teleological structure via "meaning rule."

### 6c. Rickert Connections
- Uexküll's explicit distinction between "Nature teaches no lessons" (Lehre disclaimer, p. 176) and the generalizing observational method resonates with Rickert's distinction between nomothetic and idiographic approaches. Uexküll's Kompositionslehre occupies an ambiguous position: it generalizes rules but from individual cases, not subsumptive laws.

| Meaning rule as supra-temporal vs. particular | Bedeutungsregel (eternal) | empirical examples (historical) | contrasts_with | The meaning rule is said to operate identically "one million years ago" as today (p. 174), yet all evidence is drawn from contingent present-day organisms. | Subject-dependence of meaning vs. Nature's composition | Subjekt (meaning-switch) | Natur (composes) | contrasts_with | Meaning-switch examples (ground beetle, imprinting) show meaning is subject-constituted; yet the Kompositionslehre posits Nature as the composing agency. | Intuitable mammal-in-itself (tick) vs. conceptual mammal-in-itself (human) | Tick Umwelt | Human Umwelt | contrasts_with | Uexküll claims the tick encounters a genuine intuitable mammal-in-itself, while humans only have a concept. This reverses the usual hierarchy of cognitive complexity and raises questions about whether "intuitable" and "abstract" map onto richer/poorer Umwelten. |
