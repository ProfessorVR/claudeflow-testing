# Phase 0 — Volume Overview: *The Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics*

*Boredom-focused comprehensive index entry. This overview orients the whole book; the deep analytic spine is the boredom analysis (Prelim §18c + Part One §§16–38 + Part Two Ch.1–2 §§39–44).*

## 1. Bibliographic identity

Martin Heidegger, *The Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics: World, Finitude, Solitude* — the Freiburg lecture course of winter semester **1929–30**, published as *Die Grundbegriffe der Metaphysik. Welt — Endlichkeit — Einsamkeit* (GA 29/30, Klostermann, 1983/1992), trans. **William McNeill & Nicholas Walker**, Indiana University Press, 1995 (ISBN 0-253-21429-7 pbk). Dedicated in memory of Eugen Fink. The English text prints the German GA pagination in its running heads, which is the citation form most boredom scholarship uses.

## 2. The course's overall shape

The book has three large movements plus end matter:

- **Preliminary Appraisal (§§1–15)** — what philosophy/metaphysics *is*: not science, not worldview-proclamation (§1); philosophizing as driven by a fundamental attunement, taking its lead from Novalis — philosophy as *homesickness*, an urge to be at home everywhere (§2); comprehensive ("world-, finitude-, individuation-") questioning (§3); the ambiguity of philosophizing (§§4–7); and a long etymological-historical reconstruction of *physis*, *logos*, and the word "metaphysics" (§§8–15). §18c, late in the appraisal's bridge into Part One, **first names profound boredom** as the concealed fundamental attunement of the contemporary situation (book p.74).

- **Part One: Awakening a Fundamental Attunement (§§16–38)** — the boredom analysis proper. Methodology of *awakening* (not observing) an attunement (§§16–18); then the **three forms of boredom** worked in ascending depth (Ch.2 first form §§19–23; Ch.3 second form §§24–28; Ch.4 third/profound form §§29–36; Ch.5 the particular profound boredom of contemporary Dasein §§37–38).

- **Part Two: Really Asking the Metaphysical Questions — What Is World? (§§39–76)** — the three questions released by profound boredom, with **time as their root** (§39); then the comparative thesis-triad *the stone is worldless / the animal is poor in world (*weltarm*) / man is world-forming (*weltbildend*)* (§42), pursued through animality (§§45–48), the organism and capacity (§§49–57), captivation/*Benommenheit* with Driesch & Uexküll (§§58–63), and world-formation via the apophantic *logos* and the "as"-structure (§§64–76).

- **End matter** — Appendix for Eugen Fink (p.367), Editor's Epilogue (p.370), Postscript to the 2nd ed. (p.374), Glossary (p.375).

## 3. The governing thesis about attunement

A *Stimmung* (mood/attunement) is not a subjective coloring laid over a neutral world. A **fundamental attunement (*Grundstimmung*)** is *disclosive*: it makes manifest beings as a whole and how we stand amid them. Hence attunement cannot be "ascertained" like something present-at-hand; it must be **awakened** — let be awake — and it is precisely what everyday busyness keeps asleep. This is the methodological hinge (§§16–17) that licenses treating boredom not as a feeling-state to be measured but as an attunement to be interpreted from within.

## 4. Why boredom

Heidegger chooses boredom (*Langeweile*, literally "long-while") as the *Grundstimmung* whose structure he can exhibit, because in it time itself becomes oppressive and beings as a whole withdraw into indifference — which is exactly the condition that, pressed to its depth, forces the metaphysical questions. The three forms are a controlled descent from a boredom *caused by a determinate situation* to a boredom that *rises from Dasein itself* and finally to a *profound, impersonal* boredom in which "it is boring for one."

## 5. The three forms (summary; deepened in fcm-04/05/06)

1. **Becoming bored *by* something** (*Gelangweiltwerden von etwas*) — the train-station: a determinate boring thing leaves us empty (*Leergelassenheit*) and holds us in limbo (*Hingehaltenheit*) as time drags; we *pass the time* (*Zeitvertreib*) to drive it off.
2. **Being bored *with* something** (*Sichlangweilen bei etwas*) — the dinner-party: outwardly we are engaged, even say we are not bored at all; yet a boredom arises *from ourselves*, a "casualness" that leaves us empty in a self-formed emptiness; the passing-the-time has become the very thing we are bored with.
3. **Profound boredom** (*es ist einem langweilig*, "it is boring for one") — passing-the-time is no longer permitted; a single threefold temporal horizon entrances us (*Gebanntheit*); beings as a whole give a "telling refusal," and in that refusal the *Augenblick* (moment of vision) is announced as what could make Dasein possible.

## 6. The structural moments (deepened in the manual)

Two recur and transform across all three forms: **being left empty** (*Leergelassenheit*) and **being held in limbo** (*Hingehaltenheit*). Their unity is temporal: profound boredom is the **entrancement (*Gebanntheit*) of the single threefold horizon of time**, against which the *Augenblick* stands as rupture. The word *Langeweile* — the "lengthening of the while" — names this temporal stretching directly.

## 7. From boredom to the three questions (the Part One→Two hinge; fcm-08)

Profound boredom, interpreted to its ground, *gives to be questioned* the three "metaphysical questions": **world, finitude, individuation** — and §39 identifies **the essence of time as their common root**. This is why Part Two ("What is world?") is not a new topic but the cashing-out of the boredom analysis.

## 8. Part Two in brief (comprehensive map; fcm-10…13)

The world-question is pursued comparatively: stone (*weltlos*) / animal (*weltarm*) / human (*weltbildend*). The animal's "poverty" is not lesser quantity of world but a *deprivation* — a being-open in **captivation** (*Benommenheit*) that has *no access to beings as beings*. Driesch (vitalism) and Uexküll (*Umwelt*) supply the biological interlocutors (§61b). World-formation is finally located in the human capacity to take beings *as* beings — the "as"-structure — read off the apophantic *logos* of Aristotle's *De Interpretatione* (§§72–76). *(For the animal/Uexküll strand the existing asset `tmp/heidegger-uexkull-bt-report/Heidegger-on-Uexkull-in-BT-and-FCM.md` is reused, not re-derived.)*

## 10. Interlocutors

Novalis (homesickness); Spengler, Klages, Scheler, Ziegler, Nietzsche (the four "philosophy of culture" diagnoses, §18a–b); Aristotle (*physis*/*logos*/*De Interpretatione*); Thomas Aquinas, Suárez (history of "metaphysics," §§13–14); Hans Driesch, Jakob von Uexküll (biology, §61b).
