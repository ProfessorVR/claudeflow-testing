# FCM Unit fcm-14 — End Matter: Appendix, Editor's Epilogue, Postscript, Glossary

**Work.** Heidegger, *The Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics: World, Finitude, Solitude* (*GA* 29/30), trans. William McNeill & Nicholas Walker (Indiana UP, 1995). Freiburg winter semester 1929–30.

**Unit.** End Matter (the non-lecture apparatus following Part Two, Ch. 6 §76): the **Appendix** (Heidegger's Fink tribute), the **Editor's Epilogue** (F.-W. von Herrmann), the **Postscript to the Second Edition**, and the **Glossary**. Book pp. 367–376 · pdf 195–200.

**Locus convention.** Triple locus `(p.NN / GA pp.NN / pdf NN)`, with `pdf = floor((book_page + 24) / 2)`. **GA caveat:** the running heads on pp. 367–376 carry *no* bracketed *GA* page (the last visible bracket in this cache is p.366 = *GA* [531–32]). All *GA* values below are therefore **derived/approximate** from that anchor and marked with `~`. Quotations are held to ≤ ~20 consecutive words; German/Greek italicized.

This is the condensed **"Map"** unit: apparatus, not argument. Its value to the boredom corpus is twofold — the Epilogue gives *external, author-confirmed* evidence that boredom is the course's centerpiece, and the Glossary fixes the canonical German↔English lexicon on which every other unit's quotations rest.

## §1. Appendix — *For Eugen Fink on His Sixtieth Birthday* (p.367 / GA pp.~533 / pdf 195)

A peculiarity of the 2-up scan: the Appendix *begins already on p.366*, set in the right column of the 366/367 spread while the left column still runs the close of §76 (the Nietzsche/*Zarathustra* "intoxicated song," "*The world is deep* / *Deeper than day can comprehend*," p.366 / pdf 195). The tribute proper opens "Dear Eugen Fink."

Heidegger recounts three "signs" of the decades-long friendship: the writing-desk Fink helped move 37 years earlier; Fink's name entered "at number 14" on the participant list for the WS 1928–29 upper seminar, with "**Both names are underlined in red**" — the red line meaning "These students are likely to achieve something" (p.367 / pdf 195); and shared upper-Swabian origins plus the same Greek and Latin teachers at the Constance grammar school. Fink, "already known as a recognized and privileged student of Husserl's," is said to have "entered another, and yet the same school of phenomenology" (p.367 / pdf 195).

The speech then turns to a Nietzsche saying — "One repays a teacher badly if one always remains merely a student" ("Of the Bestowing Virtue," *Thus Spoke Zarathustra*) — and to an end-of-philosophy meditation (p.368 / GA pp.~534 / pdf 196). Philosophy "is dissipating into independent sciences" (logistics, semantics, psychology, anthropology, sociology, etc.) and is "being superseded" and "overpowered" by what "attempts to consolidate itself under the title of cybernetics." Nietzsche's *Will to Power* No. 466 supplies the diagnosis: "the victory of method over science." Yet the decisive thesis is that **"the end of philosophy is not the end of thinking"** (p.368 / pdf 196), and that thinking "must in future first open the time-play-space for poetising, so that … there may again be a wording world" (p.369 / pdf 196). The gift: Valéry's "The Young Parca," translated by Celan. The speech-text is dated "Freiburg i. Br., 30 March 1966" and accompanied by a cover note "Yours, Martin Heidegger."

For the boredom corpus the Appendix is *context*, not doctrine — but the poetising-and-thinking / "wording world" motif and the *Welt*-as-"deep" *Zarathustra* lines rhyme with the course's world-thesis vocabulary, and the dedication explains why the editor placed this text in the volume at all (see §2).

## §2. Editor's Epilogue (p.370 / GA pp.~536 / pdf 197)

This is the bibliographically load-bearing section. Von Herrmann establishes that the manuscript bore the title "*The Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics: World, Finitude, Solitude*," delivered "four hours per week during the winter semester of 1929–30" at Freiburg (p.370 / pdf 197). Two attendees — Walter Brocker and Heinrich Wiegand Petzet — report that the **noticeboard subtitle read "World, Finitude, Individuation," not "Solitude"** (p.370 / pdf 197); since Heidegger never deleted *Einsamkeit* in the manuscript, the published subtitle retained "Solitude." The volume, listed as Vol. 30 in the prospectus, "now appears as the double issue 29–30" (p.371 / pdf 197).

On the physical text: the manuscript "comprises 94 sides in horizontal folio format," and — crucially for this whole extraction method — "the main text is written on the left-hand side of the page," while "the right-hand side contains the numerous textual insertions, textual improvements, and abbreviated remarks" (p.371 / pdf 197). The editor drew on Ute Guzzoni's early-1960s typescript (prepared with a DFG grant), Simon Moser's bound shorthand record (which Heidegger "regarded as his personal record" and annotated), and Heidegger's "Directives for preparing a text adequately suited for print." Editorial labor included deciphering the German-script hand, supplementing from authorized notes and recapitulations, dividing continuous prose into paragraphs, and supplying "consistent punctuation," since Heidegger "only indicated the syntactic caesurae within sentences by dashes" (p.371 / pdf 197).

The structural facts matter for cross-referencing the other units. Only three titles are Heidegger's own: Part One bears "**Awakening a Fundamental Attunement in Our Philosophizing**," Part Two "**What Is World?**," and one clearly separated section, "**Summary and renewed introduction following the vacation**" (the Christmas-holidays §44) (p.372 / pdf 198). "The remaining structuring … as well as the formulation of all titles except the three mentioned, was undertaken by the editor," who selected titles "solely by using phrases formed by Heidegger" (p.372 / pdf 198). Heidegger "firmly rejected" a subject index, so the detailed table of contents stands in its place. Two minor source notes: the Leibniz sentence "ascribed to him on page 24 of the German text" could not be located despite "intensive and wide-ranging inquiries" (p.372 / pdf 198); and the occasional *Sein und Zeit* references key to the 15th separate edition (Niemeyer 1979) and *GA* Vol. 2 (p.372 / pdf 198).

The **key boredom evidence** sits in the final third (p.373 / GA pp.~539 / pdf 198). At the time of preparing the *Gesamtausgabe*, Heidegger "gave the editor the present lecture course in person for editing," and during working visits "repeatedly directed the conversation toward this lecture course, and **emphasized the detailed analysis of boredom, in addition to the significance of the course for the concept of world**" (p.373 / pdf 198). Von Herrmann then ties Part One to the inaugural lecture: shortly before elaborating this course, "on 24 July 1929, Heidegger had given his inaugural Freiburg lecture, 'What Is Metaphysics?,' in which he **dealt with the attunement of boredom for the first time**," and "the comprehensive analysis of boredom and its three forms, which constitutes the first part of the present lecture course, must be read … in the context of this inaugural lecture" (p.373 / pdf 198). The Part Two task — determining the essence of life toward the concept of world — is described as one Heidegger "merely mentions in *Sein und Zeit* (Section 12 …) but does not undertake." The section closes with the rationale for dedicating the volume to Fink (d. 25 June 1975) and is signed "F.-W. v. Herrmann, Freiburg i. Br., December 1982."

## §3. Postscript to the Second Edition (p.374 / GA pp.~540 / pdf 199)

A short corrigendum. After noting that "some minor typographical errors in the first edition have been corrected," von Herrmann retracts the first edition's verdict on the SS 1929 course "Introduction to Academic Study" (planned Vol. 29). The Epilogue had stated the course "not only was not held, but was not even worked out in manuscript form"; but "just after publication of the volume … two written transcripts came to light, one by Herbert Marcuse, the other by Alois Siggemann," so "the conclusion arrived at by the editor … proved to be incorrect" (p.374 / pdf 199). He reconstructs the three earlier lines of inquiry (a negative archive search; two attendees "able to remember the other academic activities … but had no recollection of this particular lecture course"; and Heidegger's own list "**Lectures and Seminars since the Publication of *Sein und Zeit* (all those fully worked out)**," which omitted the course). The two transcripts — "each comprising only nine and seventeen typed pages" — show Heidegger "concerned with grasping the genuine essence of science and philosophy," interpreting "Plato's allegory of the cave from Book VII of the *Republic*" (p.374 / pdf 199); but they are "impossible to reconstruct and to edit" as a text. Signed "F.-W. v. Herrmann, Freiburg i. Br., March 1992."

## §4. Glossary — German↔English equivalences (p.375 / GA pp.~541 / pdf 199–200)

The Glossary is the corpus's terminological key. Entries run *English-headword: German-equivalent*. I group the legible entries by cluster.

**Boredom cluster (the spine):** "(profound) boredom: *(tiefe) Langeweile*"; "to be bored with …: *sich langweilen mit/bei …*"; "to become bored by …: *gelangweiltwerden von …*"; "boring: *langweilig*" (p.375 / pdf 199). The temporal stem surfaces as "the while: *die Weile*"; "to drag: *zögern*" with "time as it drags: *der zögernde Zeitverlauf*"; "to endure: *währen*" and "during: *während*" (p.376 / pdf 200). These pin the three forms' idioms: *sich langweilen mit* (first form), *gelangweiltwerden von* (second form), and the *Weile*/*zögern* duration the third form arrests.

**Attunement cluster:** "(fundamental) attunement: *(Grund)stimmung*"; "to attune: *stimmen*"; "(mood of) melancholy: *Schwermut*"; "(mood of) courage: *Mut*" (p.375 / pdf 199). *Schwermut* and *Mut* confirm the §44 melancholy-of-philosophizing word-family.

**Captivation / animal cluster:** "captivation: *Benommenheit*" / "captivated: *benommen*," doubled by "benumbment: *Benommenheit*" / "benumbed: *benommen*"; "to disinhibit: *enthemmen*" / "disinhibition: *Enthemmung*"; "encircling ring: *Umring*," "ring: *Ring*," "to encircle: *umringen*"; "poor in world: *weltarm*" / "poverty in world: *Weltarmut*"; "(instinctual) drive: *Trieb*" / "driven activity: *Treiben, Umtrieb*" / "drivenness: *Getriebenheit*" (pp.375–376 / pdf 199–200). This fixes the animal-thesis vocabulary of the second part.

**Structural moments of profound boredom:** "(being) held in limbo: *Hingehaltenheit*"; "(being) left empty: *Leergelassenheit*"; "(telling) refusal: *Versagen*" / "to refuse: *versagen*"; "to entrance: *bannen*" / "entrancement: *Bann*"; "withholding: *Genommenheit*"; "being taken: *Hingenommenheit*"; "to oppress: *bedrängen*" / "oppressiveness: *Bedrängnis*"; "compulsion, compelling force: *Zwang*" / "to compel: *zwingen*" (pp.375–376 / pdf 199–200). These are exactly the moment-names the third-form analysis builds on.

**Temporal cluster:** "the standing 'now': *das stehende Jetzt*"; "standing time: *die stehende Zeit*"; "to temporalize: *sich zeitigen*"; "the 'now': *das Jetzt*"; "temporality: *Zeitlichkeit*"; and the liberating "moment of vision: *Augenblick*" (pp.376 / pdf 200). The *stehende Jetzt* / *Augenblick* pairing is the temporal hinge of the whole boredom analysis.

**World / Dasein cluster:** "world: *Welt*" / "world-forming: *weltbildend*" / "worldless: *weltlos*" / "(world-)formation: *(Welt-)bildung*"; "individuation: *Vereinzelung*"; "solitude: *Einsamkeit*"; "finitude: *Endlichkeit*"; "proper authenticity: *Eigentlichkeit*"; "resolute disclosedness: *Entschlossenheit*"; "moment of vision: *Augenblick*" (pp.375–376 / pdf 199–200).

**OCR gaps flagged.** The legible glossary text does **not** contain a headword entry for *Dasein* or for *Befindlichkeit* — both central to the conceptual frame but absent from this two-page list as captured. (*Befindlichkeit* belongs to *Sein und Zeit*'s lexicon and may simply not be a glossary headword here; *Dasein* is used untranslated throughout.) These are noted as gaps rather than silently omitted.

## §5. Synthesis for the boredom corpus

Three takeaways. **(1)** The Epilogue is the single strongest piece of *external* evidence that boredom is FCM's deliberate centerpiece: Heidegger personally "emphasized the detailed analysis of boredom" and tied it to "What Is Metaphysics?" (1929), the first treatment of the "attunement of boredom" (p.373 / pdf 198). This lets the bridge cite a non-circular warrant. **(2)** The Glossary is the lexicon of record — every quotation in the deep units (*Langeweile*, *Stimmung*, *Benommenheit*, *Hingehaltenheit*, *Leergelassenheit*, *Augenblick*, *weltarm*) traces to these fixed equivalences. **(3)** The bibliographic facts (94-folio manuscript, left-text/right-insertions layout, three Heidegger-authored titles, rejected subject index, the 29-30 renumbering and the Postscript's Marcuse/Siggemann correction) are exactly what a corpus bibliography entry needs.

**Bridge relevance.** This end-matter unit is the corpus's ground truth: it supplies the author-confirmed warrant that boredom — not some arbitrary mood — is FCM's privileged attunement, and the fixed German↔English lexicon on which the King–Salvo bridge's mapping from lab constructs (boredom stimulus, disengagement, captivation/flow) onto Heideggerian terms entirely depends.
