# fcm-02 — Preliminary Appraisal, Chapter Three: Origin and History of the Word "Metaphysics" (§§8–15)

*Unit of* Martin Heidegger, *The Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics: World, Finitude, Solitude* (GA 29/30, trans. McNeill & Walker, Indiana UP 1995). Book pp.25–58; GA [37–87]; pdf 24–41. Locus form: `(p.NN / GA pp.NN / pdf NN)`, with the GA bracket read from the running heads (the Klostermann pagination drifts non-linearly against the English). This is a **map** (condensed) unit, and a **Greek-heavy** one: the OCR garbles the Greek badly (e.g. *physis* prints as `(pwig`/`cpuoiq`, *logos* as `Xoyoq`, *aletheia* as `aXp0£ta`), so Greek is reconstructed and transliterated throughout; uncertain reconstructions are flagged.

Chapter Three closes the Preliminary Appraisal by answering a single embarrassing question: with what right does Heidegger keep calling his comprehensive questioning about world, finitude, and individuation "metaphysics," when he simultaneously rejects metaphysics as a fixed scholastic discipline `(p.25 / GA pp.37 / pdf 24)`? The answer is a history of the word — physis/logos/aletheia at the Greek dawn, the post→trans shift of *meta*, and the descent through Aristotle, Aquinas, and Suárez — that ends by repurposing the title itself. For the boredom index the unit carries little direct boredom content; its value is that it grounds the book's title-concept and hands, at §15–§16, straight into Part One's awakening of a fundamental attunement.

## §1. "Metaphysics" is not a primal word (§8 opening)

Heidegger opens negatively: the word "metaphysics" is "not a primal word [*Urwort*]" `(p.25 / GA pp.37-39 / pdf 24)`. A primal word is enunciated out of "an essential and originary human experience"; "metaphysics," by contrast, "goes back to the Greek wording which reads, when broken down" as *ta meta ta physika*, a phrase that "later became amalgamated into the expression 'metaphysics'" and "serves to designate philosophy" `(p.25 / GA pp.37-39 / pdf 24)`. The methodological point is decisive and recurs throughout: "we are first giving this word its meaning on the basis of an understanding of philosophy," not taking philosophy's essence from the word `(p.25 / GA pp.37-39 / pdf 24)`. The elucidation therefore begins with the last term, *physika*, and within it *physis*.

## §2. *physis* as the self-forming prevailing of beings as a whole (§8a)

*physis* (*physika*) is "customarily translate[d] as nature," via Latin *natura/nasci*, "to be born, to arise, to grow" `(p.25 / GA pp.37-39 / pdf 24)`. But Heidegger pushes past "growth" to render it "the **self-forming prevailing of beings as a whole**" `(p.25 / GA pp.37-39 / pdf 24)` — the unit's keystone phrase (German *Walten*, prevailing/holding-sway). This is "not... the object of natural science," "neither... a broad, pre-scientific sense, nor... Goethe's sense" `(p.26 / GA pp.39-40 / pdf 24)`. Procreation, birth, maturing, aging, death "are not events in the narrow, present-day sense of a... biological process"; they "belong to the general prevailing of beings, which comprehends within itself human fate and its history" `(p.26 / GA pp.39-40 / pdf 24)`. *physis* is "this whole prevailing that prevails through man himself, a prevailing that he does not have power over … but which precisely prevails through and around him" `(p.26 / GA pp.39-40 / pdf 24)`. It is "intended more originally than" the modern nature/history pair, "encompass[ing] both, and even in a certain way includ[ing] divine beings" `(p.26 / GA pp.39-40 / pdf 24)`.

## §3. *logos* as taking the prevailing out of concealment (§8b)

Because man exists, "to exist as man already means: to make whatever prevails come to be spoken out" `(p.26 / GA pp.39-40 / pdf 24)`. The spoken-out prevailing is the *logos*; speaking is *legein*. What does *legein* accomplish? Heidegger reads it off its **opposite**, taken from Heraclitus (Frgm. 93): the Delphic master "neither speaks out, nor does he conceal, but gives a sign" `(p.27 / GA pp.41 / pdf 26)`. The opposite of *legein* is therefore *kryptein*, "keeping concealed and in concealment" (German *Verbergen*) — from which "it necessarily follows that the fundamental function of *legein* is to **take whatever prevails from concealment**," "revealing [*Entbergen*]" `(p.27 / GA pp.41 / pdf 26)`. "In the *logos* the prevailing of beings becomes revealed, becomes manifest" `(p.27 / GA pp.41 / pdf 26)`. And since prevailing is "torn from concealment in the *logos*," it "must, as it were, try to conceal itself" — confirmed by the second Heraclitus fragment (Frgm. 123), which Heidegger renders "**The prevailing of things has in itself a striving to conceal itself**" (*physis kryptesthai philei*, the famous "*physis* loves to hide") `(p.27 / GA pp.41 / pdf 26)`.

## §4. *aletheia* as truth torn from concealment (§8c)

The revealing accomplished by the *logos* is then named through *aletheia*. Heraclitus Frgm. 112 gives the gloss: "wisdom [lucidity] is to say and to do what is unconcealed as unconcealed, in accordance with the prevailing of things" — to act *kata physin*, "listening out for them" `(p.28 / GA pp.42-43 / pdf 26)`. Our "colourless expression 'that which is true'" loses what the *a*- carries: read as *a-letheia*, "**un-concealment**," it parallels German *Un-schuld* (in-nocence), *Un-endlichkeit* (in-finity) — "that which is not concealed" `(p.29 / GA pp.43-44 / pdf 26)`. The *a*- is "the *a-privativum*"; "the Greeks thus implicitly understand something negative in the innermost essence of truth" `(p.29 / GA pp.43-44 / pdf 26)`. Hence the anchor claim: "**Truth is understood by the Greeks as something stolen, something that must be torn from concealment**, in a confrontation in which precisely *physis* strives to conceal itself" `(p.29 / GA pp.43-44 / pdf 26)`. Truth "is in part rooted in the fate of human Dasein"; it "has nothing to do with the business of proving propositions at the writing desk" `(p.29 / GA pp.43-44 / pdf 26)`. Heidegger explicitly links this to *Being and Time*, where he "emphatically pointed to this primal meaning of the Greek concept of truth" — "not... merely in order to provide a better and more literal translation," nor "artificially playing around with etymologies," but to make "**the fundamental position of man in antiquity**" visible `(p.30 / GA pp.44-46 / pdf 27)`. The early thinkers are accordingly the *physiologoi* — "neither 'physiologists'... nor... philosophers of nature," but "the genuine primordial title for a questioning about beings as a whole," those "who bring it to revealedness (truth)" `(p.29 / GA pp.43-44 / pdf 27)`.

## §5. The two meanings of *physis* (§8d → §9)

*physis* is "already ambivalent in itself" `(p.31 / GA pp.46-48 / pdf 27)`. **First meaning**: that "which of itself is always already at hand, continually forming and passing away of its own accord, as distinct from... *techne*" `(p.30 / GA pp.44-46 / pdf 27)` — the *physei onta* opposed to the *techne onta*, "a regional concept," "a distinctive region of beings" (heavens, stars, ocean, earth) `(p.30 / GA pp.44-46 / pdf 27)`. Here Heidegger plants the Heraclitus *kosmos* fragment (Frgm. 30): "this *physis* always was, always is, and always will be an ever-flaming fire" `(p.31 / GA pp.46-48 / pdf 27)`. **Second meaning**: "prevailing as such, which lets everything that prevails be as that which it is" — no longer a region but "the nature of beings," "the innermost essence... the inner law of a matter" (the nature of spirit, soul, the work of art) `(p.31 / GA pp.46-48 / pdf 27)`. Crucially, "**one of these two concepts... does not... suppress the other**, but... they both continue alongside one another," and both "express something equally essential" for "that questioning which in principle questions concerning the prevailing of beings as a whole: philosophy" `(p.31 / GA pp.46-48 / pdf 27)`. ("We barbarians... think that such things happened overnight" — it "took centuries" `(p.32 / GA pp.48-49 / pdf 28)`.)

§9 locates both meanings **in Aristotle**. Beings "impose themselves as a whole" and "attract investigation"; out of philosophizing "there grow individual philosophies, which we later call sciences" — "**philosophy is not a science**," sciences are "ways and kinds of philosophizing" `(p.33 / GA pp.49-51 / pdf 28)`. *episteme* (*epistasthai* = "to stand before a matter, to know one's way around in it") takes on "the decided meaning of 'science'" only in Aristotle `(p.33 / GA pp.49-51 / pdf 28)`. The *episteme physike* relating to *physis* is "physics, not yet in the narrow meaning of contemporary modern physics, but physics that also comprises all the disciplines of biology," questioning up to "the Prime Mover," the "*theion*, the divine, without yet associating this with any particular religious view" `(pp.33-34 / GA pp.49-51 / pdf 28)`. For the **second** meaning, "that which makes beings beings" is "the essence of beings, their being" — *ousia* — which "for Aristotle... is still called *physis*" `(p.33 / GA pp.49-51 / pdf 28)`. Aristotle thus "**explicitly amalgamated**" the two orientations — "questioning concerning beings as a whole and questioning concerning what the being of beings... is" — as *prote philosophia*, First Philosophy, "philosophizing proper" `(p.33 / GA pp.49-51 / pdf 28)`. But "**Aristotle says nothing... about how he thinks these two orientations in their unity**," so "this question is open and is open to this day, or rather is not even posed any more today" `(p.33 / GA pp.49-51 / pdf 28)` — the live thread the rest of the chapter tracks through its scholastic burial.

## §6. The scholastic decline (§10)

"What Aristotle achieved... has been handed down to us in individual lecture courses and treatises," with "nothing of an Aristotelian system... such as was first invented later" `(p.35 / GA pp.52-54 / pdf 29)`. "Aristotle died around 322–21 b.c.," and "its descent and proper decline begins with him" `(p.35 / GA pp.52-54 / pdf 29)`. With school-formation "**Living questioning dies out**": what was "spoken out... is taken on its own and made into a useful result, something... for everyone to learn and repeat," so the inheritance "is uprooted and is no longer comprehended as something rooted" `(p.35 / GA pp.52-54 / pdf 29)`. To physis (the produced opposed to the self-prevailing) and to *ethos* — "man's stance... from which our expression 'ethics' comes" `(p.36 / GA pp.54-55 / pdf 30)` — is added a prior concern with the *logos* itself; hence "there ensue three disciplines of philosophy scholastically conceived: **logic, physics, ethics**" `(p.36 / GA pp.54-55 / pdf 30)`. The historical evidence is Sextus Empiricus (*Adversus Mathematicos* VII.16): the division was "prefigured in Plato" and "first explicitly introduced by those around **Xenocrates** and the pupils of Aristotle... as well as the Stoics" `(p.36 / GA pp.54-55 / pdf 30)`. This "scholastic structuring prefigures the conception of philosophy... for the following period," so that philosophy becomes "an affair of schooling and learning" `(p.36 / GA pp.54-55 / pdf 30)`.

## §7. The post→trans changeover of *meta* (§11)

Here is the unit's etymological hinge. When first-century-b.c. editors "gathered and order[ed]... the entire corpus of Aristotelian treatises" by the three disciplines, the First-Philosophy treatises "were unable to [be] accommodate[d]" — "there arose the embarrassment of its not belonging in any of the disciplines" `(p.38 / GA pp.57-58 / pdf 30)`. The only way out: file it "alongside, behind physics... after physics. Behind, following after, is *meta* in Greek," so "philosophy proper was placed behind physics: *ta meta ta physika*" `(p.38 / GA pp.57-58 / pdf 30)`. This is "a purely technical title which says nothing at all as regards content" — "the title for an embarrassment" `(p.38 / GA pp.57-58 / pdf 30)`. **First meaning of *meta*: after (post)** — as in *methodos*, "the way in which I go after a matter" `(p.38 / GA pp.58-60 / pdf 30)`. But *meta* "has a further meaning... connected with the first": in *metabole* (changeover, *Umschlag*), "away from something toward something else" `(p.39 / GA pp.58-60 / pdf 31)`. When *ta meta ta physika* compressed into Latin "*metaphysica*," "the *meta* has altered its meaning": it "now no longer means that which comes after the doctrines on physics, but that which deals with whatever turns away from the *physika* and turns toward... beings in general and toward that being which properly is" `(p.39 / GA pp.58-60 / pdf 31)`. **Second meaning: over/beyond (trans)** — "post in Latin, the second means *trans*"; "Metaphysics becomes the title for knowledge of... the suprasensuous" `(p.39 / GA pp.58-60 / pdf 31)`. Heidegger insists "**this changeover is by no means as inconsequential and harmless as it may appear**": "Something essential is decided by it — **the fate of philosophy proper in the West**" `(p.39 / GA pp.58-60 / pdf 31)`. (He notes the title's offspring: meta-logic, meta-geometry, "metapoliticians," even "a meta-aspirin," the "metastructure of protein" `(p.40 / GA pp.60-61 / pdf 32)`.)

## §8. The three incongruities (§12)

The traditional concept makes "three assertions": "[1.] it is trivialized; [2.] it is intrinsically confused; [3.] it is unconcerned about the real problem" `(p.41 / GA pp.61-63 / pdf 32)`. **(a) Trivialized**: the popular metaphysical — "God, immortal soul" — is taken as "a being that is at hand, albeit a higher one" `(p.41 / GA pp.61-63 / pdf 32)`. Christian dogma "took control of ancient philosophy and reinterpreted it"; Descartes' *Meditationes de prima philosophia* makes "the proof of the existence of God and of the immortality of the soul" First Philosophy's objective `(p.43 / GA pp.65-66 / pdf 33)`. So "the metaphysical is itself one being among others"; the *meta* "reveals nothing at all of that peculiar turnaround which philosophizing... entails" `(p.44 / GA pp.66-68 / pdf 33)`, "a complete misinterpretation of the *theion* which, in Aristotle, is at least left to stand as a problem" `(p.44 / GA pp.66-68 / pdf 33)`. **(b) Confused**: it combines "two separate kinds of lying out beyond" — the *suprasensuous* being (the *theion*, beyond the senses as a being in its own right) and the *unsensuous* determinations of the being of beings (unity, multiplicity, otherness — "things that I cannot taste or weigh") `(p.45 / GA pp.68-69 / pdf 34)`. Aquinas "straightforwardly took over this second direction... from Aristotle" (the *on he on*, *ens commune*), so "the intrinsic concept of metaphysics is in itself confused" `(p.45 / GA pp.68-69 / pdf 34)`. **(c) Unproblematic**: because trivialized and confused, "it simply cannot come about that... the *meta* in its proper sense is made a problem" — until "Kant... attempted in one particular direction to make metaphysics itself a problem" (cf. *Kant and the Problem of Metaphysics*) `(p.45 / GA pp.68-69 / pdf 34)`.

## §9. Aquinas (§13)

§13 supplies "**historical evidence**" for the three features. Aquinas (Prooemium to his commentary on Aristotle's *Metaphysics*) "**straightaway equates** prima philosophia, metaphysica, and theologia or scientia divina" `(p.46 / GA pp.69-71 / pdf 35)`. The grounding is "brilliant... yet completely covering over the problem": highest knowledge is the *scientia regulatrix* (the science governing all others — the lineage runs to Descartes and to "Fichte's Science of Knowledge"), "most intellectual" because it "deals with what is most knowable," the *maxime intelligibilia* `(pp.46-47 / GA pp.69-72 / pdf 35)`. These divide threefold: (1) *ex ordine intelligendi* — the highest causes, *causa prima* = "God as creator," so "*prima philosophia* is knowledge of the highest cause... completely alien to Aristotle in this form" `(p.47 / GA pp.71-72 / pdf 35)`; (2) *ex comparatione intellectus ad sensum* — the *universalia*, the categories (*unum, multa, potentia, actus*), which Aquinas strikingly calls the *transphysica*, "found by way of a regressive resolution," assigned to *metaphysica* (de ente — "synonymous with the expression ontology") `(pp.48-49 / GA pp.72-74 / pdf 36)`; (3) *ex ipsa cognitione intellectus* — "that which is free of matter," God and the intelligences, the *scientia divina sive theologia* `(p.49 / GA pp.74-75 / pdf 36)`. Thus "First Philosophy deals with the first causes (*de primis causis*), metaphysics with beings in general (*de ente*) and theology with God (*de Deo*)" `(p.49 / GA pp.74-75 / pdf 36)`. The verdict: this holds "three directions of questioning... systematically... together in a quite different way, one essentially determined by faith," never "oriented toward the intrinsic problematic itself" `(p.49 / GA pp.74-75 / pdf 36)`. The Aristotelian disharmony — questioning "equality, difference, and opposition" vs. "the ultimate ground of beings" — is in the Middle Ages "raised to a principle," with "the whole of metaphysics... explicitly assigned to the knowledge of God" `(pp.49-50 / GA pp.74-77 / pdf 36)`.

## §10. Suárez and modern metaphysics (§14)

"Direct influence on the development of modern metaphysics was exercised by... the Spanish Jesuit **Franz Suárez**" (1548–1617), whom Heidegger places "even above Aquinas in terms of his acumen and independence of questioning" `(p.51 / GA pp.77-78 / pdf 37)`. His *Disputationes metaphysicae* (1597) is the first to give "the metaphysical problem" "an independent development," "of particular influence... for Descartes," who "studied in the Jesuit school of La Flèche" and "consulted [Suárez] time and again" `(pp.52-53 / GA pp.78-81 / pdf 37)`. Suárez surveys metaphysics' names (*sapientia*/sophia, *prudentia*/phronesis, *prima philosophia*, *naturalis theologia*/theologike, and finally *metaphysica*) `(p.53 / GA pp.80-81 / pdf 37)`, and "gives the expression a different meaning from that of Aquinas": where "Aquinas uses the expression *metaphysica* insofar as it treats *de ente in communi*," "Suárez... says it is called metaphysics **because it is theology**," dealing with God `(p.53 / GA pp.80-81 / pdf 38)`. Suárez "stresses the *meta* in the sense of *post*" — "the stages of knowledge proceeding from the sensuous to the suprasensuous" — while "at the same time... bring[ing] into play the interpretation in terms of content" (*meta* = "that which... exceeds the sensuous") `(p.53 / GA pp.81-83 / pdf 38)`. Modern metaphysics is then "determined by the fact that the entirety of the traditional problematic comes under the aspect of a new science... mathematical natural science" `(p.55 / GA pp.83-84 / pdf 39)`: knowing "must itself be absolutely certain," so "**the problem of absolute certainty is... the fundamental problem of modern philosophy**," seen "most clearly... in Descartes, but especially in Fichte" `(p.55 / GA pp.83-84 / pdf 39)`. The I/consciousness "is precisely placed at the basis as the most secure and unquestioned foundation," so modern metaphysics includes the questioner only "in the negative sense" — "the I is precisely **not** put in question" `(p.55 / GA pp.83-84 / pdf 39)`.

## §11. Metaphysics as the fundamental problem of metaphysics itself (§15)

The chapter's payoff. Surveying the whole, "this expression 'as a whole' is a term which contains the real problem," so "we cannot simply take the title 'metaphysics' in its traditional meaning" `(p.56 / GA pp.85-86 / pdf 39)`. Instead: "We are taking over the expression 'metaphysics'... **as a title for the fundamental problem of metaphysics itself which lies in the question of what it, metaphysics, itself is**" `(p.56 / GA pp.85-86 / pdf 39)`. "This question: What is metaphysics, what is philosophizing?, remains inseparable from philosophy and is its constant companion," "whereas the question of what mathematics, physics, or philology is can fundamentally be neither posed nor solved by these sciences" `(p.56 / GA pp.85-86 / pdf 39)`. Heidegger then concedes the result is "**thoroughly negative**": "Although we dealt directly with philosophy itself... we have shirked in the face of philosophy," speaking "about it" but "not directly and concretely from out of it" `(p.56 / GA pp.85-86 / pdf 39)`. The remedy reaches forward into the boredom spine: we must "**take action within metaphysics itself**," letting the questions "What is world? What is finitude? What is individuation?" `(p.57 / GA pp.86-87 / pdf 40)` arise "in their necessity and possibility from out of a **fundamental attunement**" — "**we properly undertake such questioning whenever we set about awakening a fundamental attunement of our philosophizing**," "the first and proper fundamental task of this lecture course" `(p.57 / GA pp.86-87 / pdf 40)`. (The very next page opens Part One §16, "letting what is asleep become wakeful" `(p.59 / GA pp.87 / pdf 41)`, the threshold of fcm-03.)

## §12. Structural summary for the index

Three index-relevant functions. (a) It fixes the **Greek triad** *physis* / *logos* / *aletheia* — prevailing, revealing-from-concealment, and truth-as-something-torn — as the originary stratum the later word buries. (b) It narrates the **post→trans changeover of *meta*** as the decisive Western event ("the fate of philosophy proper in the West") and the **three incongruities** of the inherited concept (trivialized / confused / unproblematic), with **Aquinas** and **Suárez** as the documentary keystones of metaphysics' theological capture. (c) At **§15** it converts "metaphysics" into the name of its own fundamental question and, at the chapter seam, issues the **awakening-an-attunement** demand that launches the boredom analysis. The most quotable nodes: "the self-forming prevailing of beings as a whole," "truth is something stolen," "the fate of philosophy proper in the West," and "awakening a fundamental attunement of our philosophizing."

## Boredom-bridge relevance

This is the title-grounding map, not a boredom analysis, but it licenses two moves the King–Salvo bridge relies on: the *aletheia*-as-unconcealment reading — truth wrested from a self-concealing *physis* `(p.29 / GA pp.43-44 / pdf 26)` — underwrites the bridge's surface/depth thesis that a phenomenon's truth must be *torn* from a concealment rather than read off the engaged surface; and §15–§16's redefinition of metaphysics as the question of what metaphysics itself is, demanding that one first *awaken a fundamental attunement* `(p.57 / GA pp.86-87 / pdf 40)`, is the doorway from this map directly into the boredom spine (fcm-03+) on which the empirical bridge actually turns.
