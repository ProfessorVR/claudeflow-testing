# fcm-00 — Preliminary Appraisal, Chapter One: The Detours toward Determining the Essence of Philosophy, and the Unavoidability of Looking Metaphysics in the Face (§§1–3)

*Unit of* Martin Heidegger, *The Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics: World, Finitude, Solitude* (GA 29/30, trans. McNeill & Walker, Indiana UP 1995). Book pp.1–10; GA [1–13]; pdf 12–17. Locus form: `(p.NN / GA pp.NN / pdf NN)`, GA read from the running heads (the Klostermann pagination drifts non-linearly against the English), pdf = floor((book_page+24)/2). This is a **map (condensed)** unit and the opening unit of the boredom spine: it installs the *Grundstimmung* apparatus on which the entire later analysis of profound boredom depends, but it contains no boredom analysis as such.

## §1. The incomparability of philosophy — neither science nor worldview (§1)

Heidegger opens by treating the course title "The Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics" as if it announced "a fixed discipline" on the model of "Fundamental Features of Zoology" or "Fundamental Elements of Linguistics" `(p.1 / GA pp.1 / pdf 12)`. On that reading metaphysics would be "the central discipline" of philosophy, philosophy "the universal science", and the course "a condensed report" rounding off one's studies — whereupon "the business of the university can begin" `(p.1 / GA pp.1 / pdf 12)`. He immediately suspects this picture: something may have "shattered at the very heart of the machinery", held together only by "the obtrusiveness and banality of organisation" `(p.1 / GA pp.1 / pdf 12)`. The governing suspicion is that it is "a prejudice that metaphysics is a fixed and secure discipline" and "an illusion that philosophy is a science that can be taught and learned" `(p.1 / GA pp.1 / pdf 12)`.

**§1a — not science.** Since Descartes, "the beginning of modernity", philosophy has vainly tried "to raise itself to the rank of a science, of absolute science" `(p.2 / GA pp.2-3 / pdf 13)`. But to posit absolute science as philosophy's goal "is itself fundamentally an error and a misunderstanding of the innermost essence of philosophy"; indeed to judge philosophy "according to the idea of science is the most fateful debasement of its innermost essence" `(p.2 / GA pp.2-3 / pdf 13)`. The alternative — philosophy as "the proclamation of a worldview", the "personal conviction of an individual thinker" drumming up supporters, philosophy as "some great marketplace" — is "just as mistaken" `(p.2 / GA pp.2-3 / pdf 13)`. The first verdict is therefore negative and double: **philosophy (metaphysics) is neither science nor the proclamation of a worldview**, and so "can be determined only from out of itself and as itself", "comparable with nothing else" `(p.2 / GA pp.2-3 / pdf 13)`.

**§1b — not via art and religion.** One might think philosophy could be grasped by comparison with "art and with religion" `(p.2 / GA pp.2-3 / pdf 13)`. Comparison with art and religion is "a justified and necessary determination of their essence as equal" — though "equality here does not mean identity" `(p.3 / GA pp.3-5 / pdf 13)`. Yet even so we shall "never grasp the essence of philosophy through these comparisons" unless "we have already managed to look philosophy in the face to begin with", for only then "can we differentiate art and religion from it" `(p.3 / GA pp.3-5 / pdf 13)`. So "this route too is closed". Being "repeatedly rebuffed" by comparison drives us "into a corner" with the question of what metaphysics itself is `(p.3 / GA pp.3-5 / pdf 13)`.

**§1c — the historical escape route as illusion.** A final route remains: "we shall inquire in the realm of history" `(p.3 / GA pp.3-5 / pdf 13)`. We could ask where the word 'metaphysics' comes from, what discipline it names, and push through "to the matter itself". But "in all this we shall fail to experience what metaphysics itself is unless we already know this beforehand" — "all reports on the history of philosophy remain mute", acquainting us with "opinions about metaphysics, but not with metaphysics itself" `(p.3 / GA pp.3-5 / pdf 13)`. This route "contains the greatest illusion, because it always pretends" that historical knowledge gives us "what we are looking for" `(p.3 / GA pp.3-5 / pdf 13)`. All three detours fail.

## §2. Determining philosophy from out of itself — the word of Novalis (§2)

**§2a — withdrawal into the obscurity of the essence of man.** The detours nonetheless yield "an important and perhaps essential insight": that "we ourselves avoid confrontation" with metaphysics, "steal away" and "take to detours" `(p.4 / GA pp.5-6 / pdf 14)`. The negative result is that philosophy "does not permit itself to be grasped or determined by way of detours or as something other than itself"; it must be apprehended "from out of itself" `(p.4 / GA pp.5-6 / pdf 14)`. Hence the pivotal identification: philosophy "is only whenever we are philosophizing. Philosophy is philosophizing" `(p.4 / GA pp.5-6 / pdf 14)`. Apparently uninformative, this "says something essential": it names the direction "in which metaphysics withdraws from us" — into "the obscurity of the essence of man" `(p.4 / GA pp.5-6 / pdf 14)`. Because philosophizing is "our own human activity", the question of metaphysics turns into the question of what we ourselves are, and our own essence proves "alien to us" `(p.4 / GA pp.5-6 / pdf 14)`. Metaphysics is "something to do with the whole, something extreme, where an ultimate pronouncement and interlocution occurs on the part of human beings" `(p.4 / GA pp.5-6 / pdf 14)`.

**§2b — homesickness as fundamental attunement, and the triad.** Here Heidegger takes "direct and apparently arbitrary recourse to a word of Novalis" `(p.5 / GA pp.6-8 / pdf 14)`: *"Philosophy is really homesickness, an urge to be at home everywhere"* `(p.5 / GA pp.6-8 / pdf 14)`. (Translator's note: *Trieb*, "urge / drive", and its cognates recur "both in the analyses of boredom and in the discussions of animal life" — the two major themes of the course.) He raises and waves off the obvious objection — Novalis is "merely a poet", and Aristotle says *"πολλὰ ψεύδονται ἀοιδοί"*, "Poets tell many a lie" (*Metaphysica* A 2, 983a3f.) — answering that "art… is the sister of philosophy" while "all science is perhaps only a servant" `(p.5 / GA pp.6-8 / pdf 14)`. *Heimweh* is then unpacked: philosophy can be such an urge "only if we who philosophize are not at home everywhere" `(p.5 / GA pp.6-8 / pdf 14)`. To be at home everywhere means neither "here or there" nor every place "one after the other", but "to be at once and at all times within the whole" — and "this 'within the whole' and its character of wholeness" Heidegger names **the world**: "What is that — world?" `(p.5 / GA pp.6-8 / pdf 14)`.

From the same homesickness the other two questions are derived. Driven "to being as a whole", we find that "our very being is this restlessness": we are "driven on" yet "simultaneously torn back", "this underway, this transition, this 'neither the one nor the other'" — and "the unrest of this 'not' we name **finitude**": "What is that — finitude?" `(p.6 / GA pp.8-9 / pdf 15)`. Finitude "is not some property merely attached to us, but is our fundamental way of being"; "finitude only is in truly becoming finite" `(p.6 / GA pp.8-9 / pdf 15)`. In that becoming-finite "there ultimately occurs an **individuation** of man with respect to his Dasein" — not the "frail little ego" puffing itself up, but "that solitariness in which each human being first… enters into a nearness to… world" `(p.6 / GA pp.8-9 / pdf 15)`. The triad is thus posed together: "world, finitude, individuation", each of which "inquires into the whole" `(p.6 / GA pp.8-9 / pdf 15)`. Pursuing them requires "the hammer of conceptual comprehension [*Begreifen*]", concepts "of a primordial kind" that "remain eternally closed off" to "indifferent and noncommittal scientific acumen" `(p.6 / GA pp.8-9 / pdf 15)`.

**The attunement claim proper.** The decisive formulation arrives next: we shall never have grasped such concepts "unless we have first been gripped [*ergriffen*] by whatever they are supposed to comprehend"; "all such being gripped… comes from and remains in an attunement [*Stimmung*]" `(p.7 / GA pp.9-10 / pdf 15)`. Because philosophizing "happens in the ground [*Grunde*] of human Dasein", the attunements out of which it arises "are always necessarily fundamental attunements [*Grundstimmungen*] of Dasein" — so that "philosophy in each case happens in a fundamental attunement" `(p.7 / GA pp.9-10 / pdf 15)`. The grounding is layered: "conceptual philosophical comprehension is grounded in our being gripped, and this is grounded in a fundamental attunement" `(p.7 / GA pp.9-10 / pdf 15)`. And it is exactly here that Novalis is vindicated: "Does not Novalis ultimately mean something like this when he calls philosophy a homesickness?" — then "this poet's word would not be at all deceptive, if only we extract what is essential" `(p.7 / GA pp.9-10 / pdf 15)`. The whole movement closes by transforming the guiding question: "What is metaphysics? has transformed itself into the question: What is man?", with man rendered "more enigmatic", yet "in the essence of this mysterious being, philosophy happens" `(p.7 / GA pp.9-10 / pdf 15)`.

## §3. Metaphysical thinking as comprehensive thinking (§3)

§3 retrospectively names the genre. The detours are "really false trails [*Holzwege*], paths which suddenly stop, which lead up a blind alley" `(p.8 / GA pp.10-12 / pdf 16)`; the lesson is "that we may by no means shirk the task of grasping philosophy and metaphysics itself immediately, directly" `(p.8 / GA pp.10-12 / pdf 16)`. As soon as we inquire, "philosophy withdraws from us into a peculiar obscurity of its own where it properly resides … as human activity in the ground of the essence of human Dasein" `(p.8 / GA pp.10-12 / pdf 16)`. Metaphysics, then, "is not some discipline of knowledge"; it is "a fundamental occurrence within human Dasein" `(p.9 / GA pp.12-13 / pdf 16)`. Its concepts are not the "determinative representation[s]" of science (the lectern, the house) but "anchored in our being gripped" `(p.9 / GA pp.12-13 / pdf 16)`. Hence the double sense of **comprehensive concept** (*Inbegriff*): metaphysical concepts "comprehend the whole within themselves", and "in a second sense which is equally essential" they "comprehend within themselves the comprehending human being and his or her… Dasein" `(p.9 / GA pp.12-13 / pdf 16)`. The reciprocity is strict — "No concept of the whole without the comprehending of philosophizing existence" `(p.9 / GA pp.12-13 / pdf 16)`. The unit's summary thesis: "Metaphysical thinking is comprehensive thinking in this double sense. It deals with the whole and it grips existence through and through" `(p.9 / GA pp.12-13 / pdf 16)`, the questioner "thereby also included in the question, placed into question" `(p.9 / GA pp.12-13 / pdf 16)`.

## §4. Structural summary for the index

Three index-relevant moves. (a) §1 **closes every external route** to philosophy — science, worldview, art/religion, history — establishing that philosophy "can be determined only from out of itself". (b) §2 re-determines philosophy as **philosophizing in the ground of human Dasein**, and, via the Novalis fragment, names *Heimweh* the **fundamental attunement of philosophizing**, deriving from it the **world / finitude / individuation triad** and the layered grounding *Grundstimmung* → *Ergriffenheit* → *Begreifen*. (c) §3 names metaphysical concepts **comprehensive concepts (*Inbegriffe*)** and metaphysical thinking the **double-sense comprehensive thinking** that grips existence through and through. The single most load-bearing node for the rest of the course is "philosophy in each case happens in a fundamental attunement" `(p.7 / GA pp.9-10 / pdf 15)`: it is the licence the later boredom analysis exercises.
