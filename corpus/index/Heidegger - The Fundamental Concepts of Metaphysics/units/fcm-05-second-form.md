# fcm-05 — The Second Form of Boredom: Being Bored *with* Something (*Sichlangweilen bei etwas*)

**Unit:** Part One, Chapter Three, §§24–28 · **book pp.106–131** · **GA 161–198** · **pdf 65–77**
**Attunement (*Stimmung*) under analysis:** the second of the three forms of *Langeweile* — the surface-occupied, depth-hollow form, in which we are outwardly engaged (even deny being bored) yet a boredom rises *from us*. This is the keystone form for the King–Salvo bridge.

Citations use the triple locus `(p.NN / GA pp.NN / pdf NN)`; GA brackets are read directly from the running heads (the Klostermann pagination drifts non-linearly against the English). Quotations are held to short anchor-phrases; the argument is paraphrased.

---

## §1. Method: why a *more original* grasp is needed (§24a)

The chapter opens not with a new phenomenon but with an unfinished problem inherited from the first form. Heidegger has extracted two structural moments of *becoming bored by* something — being held in limbo by time as it drags, and being left empty by the beings that "refuse" themselves — and has begun to see that they are not externally bolted together. Leaving-empty does not mean something being absent but "a particular kind of being present at hand" in which things refuse us something we spontaneously expect (p.106 / GA pp.161-63 / pdf 65). Crucially, holding-in-limbo "itself determines and sustains leaving empty," so time's "strange and enigmatic power" reaches into *both* moments, not just the first (p.107 / GA pp.161-63 / pdf 66).

This yields the chapter's governing demand: the intrinsic connection cannot be "an external bond." There must be a "peculiar structural link [*Fuge*]" determined in advance "from the whole and original essence of boredom" (p.107 / GA pp.161-63 / pdf 66). The two moments are not "simply stuck together, but tailored to one another." We will grasp their unity "only by grasping boredom more originally than hitherto" — and that requires envisaging "a more original *form* of boredom," one in which we become *more* bored (p.107 / GA pp.161-63 / pdf 66). [**C1**]

A decisive methodological caveat blocks the obvious reading of "more": the *duration* of boredom "decides nothing about its extent." We can be more bored "in someone's company for five minutes than during the whole four hours" at the station; the objective span "is not decisive for the extent and degree or … for the *depth* of boredom" (p.108 / GA pp.163-64 / pdf 66). [**C2**] The depth sought "points in the direction of … the ground of our essence, from which the possibility of every form of boredom will evidently spring" (p.108 / GA pp.163-64 / pdf 66). The earlier linguistic distinction between *becoming bored by* and *being bored with* now reveals itself as substantive: it "lies precisely in boredom's becoming more profound" (p.108 / GA pp.163-64 / pdf 66). Interpreting the second form is therefore "of decisive methodological significance," not a taxonomic addition — "we are not interested in that" (p.108 / GA pp.163-64 / pdf 66).

## §2. The example: the evening invitation (§24b)

Where the first form had the railway station, the second has the dinner-party. Heidegger first notes that here the boundary "fundamentally cannot be drawn precisely at all, because the limits merge together" (p.109 / GA pp.164-66 / pdf 67) — the second form shades into the third, which matters for the bridge's "surface/depth split" hypothesis.

The scene is deliberately everyday and inconspicuous. We have been invited out; we did not need to go but had time, so we went. "There we find the usual food and the usual table conversation," everything "not only very tasty, but tasteful as well"; afterward a "lively discussion," perhaps music, "witty and amusing." The ladies assure us "that it really was very nice" (p.110 / GA pp.166-67 / pdf 68). "There is nothing at all to be found that might have been boring about this evening … neither the conversation, nor the people, nor the rooms." We come home satisfied — and only then, glancing at the interrupted work and looking ahead to tomorrow, "then it comes: *I was bored after all this evening*" (p.110 / GA pp.166-67 / pdf 68).

The phenomenological puzzle is sharp. We can find nothing that bored us, yet "I myself was bored. With what, then?" We were not occupied with ourselves "for a moment" — "On the contrary, I was totally involved." We do not even say we were bored *with ourselves* but "bored *with the evening*" (p.110 / GA pp.166-67 / pdf 68). Heidegger forecloses two dismissals: that the talk of boredom is "merely an illusion" arising from an ensuing bad mood over a lost evening; and that this is mere *blasé* conduct. "No: it is quite clear that we were bored, even though it was all so pleasant. … Or perhaps it was precisely the pleasantness of this evening with which we were bored?" (p.110 / GA pp.166-67 / pdf 68). The question is not whether the boredom was justified, nor its causal genesis, but "what properly belongs to this having been bored with the evening" (p.111 / GA pp.167-69 / pdf 68).

## §3. The transformed passing-the-time (§24b continued)

Following the standing guideline — never investigate "free-floating boredom in itself" but always boredom within the *passing-the-time* (*Zeitvertreib*) associated with it (p.111 / GA pp.167-69 / pdf 68) — Heidegger looks for the passing-the-time and at first "searches in vain." We even had to hide a "repeated, though suppressed yawning" and resist drumming our fingers (p.111 / GA pp.167-69 / pdf 68). The tempting inference — that this second form simply *lacks* passing-the-time, so it would be a "free-floating, unimpeded boredom" (p.111 / GA pp.167-69 / pdf 68) — is rejected, because it would describe only a *consequence* and leave the proper essence dark.

The correct path is to see the yawning and finger-drumming as a "flaring up" of the familiar passing-the-time, then to read that flaring-up "in the context of the whole situation" (p.111 / GA pp.167-69 / pdf 68). The cigar is the pivotal instance: "we take a cigar … not to become more sleepy, nor to be stimulated by the nicotine, but because smoking itself is a socially ideal way of passing the time" — an "inconspicuous possibility of passing the time" that "plays right into our hands" precisely because "it presents itself in such a public manner" (p.112 / GA pp.169-70 / pdf 68). But smoking is not an *isolated* occupation against boredom: "while smoking we precisely participate in the conversation." Boredom is not "a fly that we can chase away"; passing-the-time, "however much it fights against boredom, … also firmly captures it at the same time" (p.112 / GA pp.169-70 / pdf 68).

This is the decisive transformation: "It is not smoking as an isolated occupation, but our entire comportment and behaviour that is our passing the time" — "the whole evening of the invitation itself" (p.112 / GA pp.169-70 / pdf 68). [**C3**] Hence "passing the time was so difficult to find." And a strange reciprocity surfaces: "The evening is that with which we are bored, and simultaneously, what we are bored with here is passing the time" — boredom and passing-the-time "become intertwined." Boredom becomes "more and more concentrated on us, on our situation as such," the individual details being "only coincidentally that with which we ourselves are bored, they are not that *which bores us*" (p.113 / GA pp.170-72 / pdf 68).

## §4. General contrast: determinate vs indeterminate boring things (§25a)

Heidegger now contrasts the two forms from the angle of "that which is boring." In the first, what is boring is "this or that, this railway station, the street, the region." In the second, "we find *nothing* that is boring" — more precisely, "we are not able to say *what* is boring us." What bores us "has this character of '*I know not what*'," held "quite clearly before us … without any explicit reflection." We "let ourselves slip into this strange casualness regarding this 'I know not what'" (p.114 / GA pp.172-73 / pdf 69). [**C4**] So the contrast is: form I, "a determinate boring thing"; form II, "something *indeterminate* that bores us" (p.114 / GA pp.172-73 / pdf 69).

He then dismantles the apparent absence of *both* structural moments. First, **time does not press**: "in accepting the invitation … we have given ourselves time; we have time for it and leave ourselves time for it," whereas at the station "we do not wish to lose any time." So we do not look at the clock, "not even furtively," and "yet we are bored with the evening" (p.115 / GA pp.173-75 / pdf 69). The earlier interpretation — being held in limbo *by time as it drags* — "fits only the first form" (p.115 / GA pp.173-75 / pdf 69). Second, **the beings do not refuse themselves**: "Things and people at this evening do not leave us standing … the evening is full," and we even "do not expect or demand any result" (p.115 / GA pp.173-75 / pdf 69). Provisionally, then, "in this second form of boredom … we find absent both being held in limbo by the dragging of time and being left empty by those beings surrounding us" (p.115 / GA pp.173-75 / pdf 69). This *apparent* double-absence is the foil the rest of §25 overturns.

## §5. Obstructive casualness and self-forming emptiness (§25b)

The comparison's positive result is methodological: we "must interpret the second form of boredom purely on its own terms, without glancing sideways at the structure of the first" (p.116 / GA pp.175-76 / pdf 70). Passing-the-time has expanded to "the situation itself as a whole," and to this is added an "inconspicuousness … insofar as passing the time does not specifically occupy us ourselves as such." We "allow ourselves to slip into it, just as though it were already lying in wait"; it "lacks that fluttering unease," being "peculiarly casual and assured" (p.116 / GA pp.175-76 / pdf 70). Heidegger blocks the inference that inconspicuous passing-the-time implies a faint boredom: we may not "draw 'conclusions' about the supposed features of the boredom" from surface features of passing-the-time (p.116 / GA pp.175-76 / pdf 70). The "I know not what" must be *held in its indeterminacy*, not replaced by a determinacy (p.117 / GA pp.176-77 / pdf 70).

The hinge is the German wordplay (flagged in the translators' note at p.118): *Leergelassenheit* (being left empty), *Lässigkeit* (casualness), *sich überlassen* (abandoning oneself), and *Sichzurücklassen* (leaving oneself behind), all turning on *lassen* — "the sense of both leaving and letting" (tr. note, p.118 / GA pp.177-79 / pdf 71). In this casualness "we abandon ourselves [*uns überlassen*] to our being there alongside and part of things." Therefore "any seeking to be satisfied by beings is absent *in advance*." Being-left-empty "does not now first ensue … through the absence of fullness," but "grows from the depths, because its own precondition, namely seeking to be satisfied by beings, is already obstructed in such casualness" (p.118 / GA pp.177-79 / pdf 71). [**C5**] This is "a leaving empty that attacks more profoundly … a preventing of that seeking, and the diffusion of casualness" — so that our apparent satisfaction "manifests itself … as an illusion (a peculiar dissatisfaction!)," a passing-the-time that "does not so much drive off boredom as precisely attest to it and let it be-there [*da-sein*]" (p.118 / GA pp.177-79 / pdf 71).

This is where the boring is first traced to its source: "what is boring can bore us without directly coming toward us from particular boring things." In casualness "there arises a slipping away, away from ourselves toward whatever is happening." "Obstructive casualness as a more profound way of leaving empty" indicates "that the boredom … comes from us" — though this "comes from us" is "still obscure and must remain so" until the first moment is settled (p.118 / GA pp.177-79 / pdf 71).

§25b closes by naming the emptiness positively. The casualness is "twofold": abandoning ourselves to what is going on, *and* "leaving ourselves behind [*Sichzurücklassen*]" — "our proper self." In this casualness "an emptiness can *form*." So here too there is being-left-empty, "an essentially more profound form thereof": not "an emptiness that remains unfilled," but "an emptiness precisely first *forms* itself. … This emptiness is a being left behind of our proper self. This self-forming emptiness is this 'I know not what'" (p.120 / GA pp.180-82 / pdf 72). [**C6**] The evening cannot "satisfy the resolute openness [*Entschlossenheit*] of our whole Dasein"; "the evening does not correspond to what we, without clearly knowing it, properly seek for our own selves" (p.119 / GA pp.179-80 / pdf 71).

## §6. Held in limbo to standing time (§25c)

Heidegger now recovers the *second* moment with the same warning against importing the first form's structure. The question is reframed: "what, in the second form of boredom, corresponds to that first mode of being held in limbo?" (p.120 / GA pp.180-82 / pdf 72). Since "we have in advance left ourselves time for the evening … it cannot hold us in limbo as the dragging of time" (p.120 / GA pp.180-82 / pdf 72). The answer comes via passing-the-time: it "is grounded in casualness, which is the originary way in which we are left empty" (p.121 / GA pp.182-83 / pdf 73). "The evening itself is our passing the time" (p.121 / GA pp.182-83 / pdf 73).

What happens to *time*? Having left ourselves time, "we pay no attention to enduring [*Wahren*]." So "we are here *not* held in limbo by time," but the converse: "time does not bind us to itself. It abandons us entirely to ourselves" (p.121 / GA pp.182-83 / pdf 73). Yet this very having-time is the condition of the deeper holding: "it is ultimately the very fact that we have taken time that gives time the possibility of holding us in limbo … in a more profound way" (p.121 / GA pp.182-83 / pdf 73). The taking is not subtraction — "we have not cut a piece out of this whole like a piece of cake." Rather "We bring it to a stand — yet do not cause it to vanish" (p.121 / GA pp.182-83 / pdf 73). [**C7**]

As "standing," time "spreads a stillness into Dasein" yet "becomes hidden as such" by our joining in (p.122 / GA pp.183-85 / pdf 73). It "shows itself — yet does not flow — it stands"; and "this standing of time is a more originary holding in limbo, which is to say, oppressing" (p.122 / GA pp.183-85 / pdf 73). The everyday "now" is familiar as flowing and determinate; "standing time is indeterminate and unfamiliar," and our chattering "lets time stand and leaves it standing." This not-releasing "announces itself as a more originary being held in limbo than that of a protracted being detained through mere dragging." Tellingly, this binding "happens precisely where we *believe we have time*," whereas thinking we have no time yields merely a "protracted" holding (p.122 / GA pp.183-85 / pdf 73).

The mechanism of standing is the spending of time: we "spend [*verbringen*] it … remove it [*bringen sie weg*]," removing "this *during* … this 'during' in its enduring," closing ourselves off "from this unsettling and paralysing sound of the sequence of nows" (p.124 / GA pp.186-88 / pdf 74). We do not step out of time; "We make time stand," so that the "enduring of the 'during' swallows up … the flowing sequence of nows and becomes a single stretched 'now' which itself does not flow, but stands." We are "entirely present [*ganz Gegenwart*] *for* what is present [*das Anwesende*]" (p.124 / GA pp.186-88 / pdf 74). [**C8**]

This making-present (*gegenwärtigen*, "here understood in the transitive sense") seals off having-been and unbinds the future: "Entirely present for whatever is happening, we are cut off from our having-been and from our future" (p.125 / GA pp.188-89 / pdf 75). This is "not that they are factically removed," but "a peculiar dissolution of the future and having-been into the mere present." With the horizon of the earlier closed off and the future unbound, the "now" loses "its possibility of a transition from not-yet to no-longer, its flowing." "Sealed off and unbound on both sides, it becomes stuck in its abiding standing, and in its being stuck it stretches itself [*dehnt sich*]" (p.125 / GA pp.188-89 / pdf 75). There are "not several nows, but fewer and fewer, indeed merely one, a stretched one." This "stretched 'now' stands into our Dasein" (p.125 / GA pp.188-89 / pdf 75). And the standing 'now' is *ourselves*: "This standing time — this is we ourselves … our self as that which has been left behind with respect to its provenance and future" (p.126 / GA pp.189-91 / pdf 75). Its standing tells us "that we have left it standing," i.e., "it precisely is not releasing us" but "summons us, it sets us in place" — "then we are bored" (p.126 / GA pp.189-91 / pdf 75). [**C9**]

## §7. The structural unity in making-present (§26)

§26 cashes the chapter's opening debt. The standing 'now' that "sets us in place" *is* what bores us, and it is the "I know not what," "indeterminate and unfamiliar" — since the flowing now is the familiar, "the standing 'now' … is something unfamiliar … indeterminate in an emphatic sense. We do not wish to have it determined at all" (p.127 / GA pp.191-92 / pdf 76). The two moments now coincide: being-left-empty "consists in a self-forming emptiness; this self-forming emptiness is what is boring," and so "must … be identical with the indeterminate, unfamiliar standing 'now'." "In its self-forming emptiness, it can leave us empty, and in leaving us empty simultaneously *hold us in limbo*" (p.127 / GA pp.191-92 / pdf 76). [**C10**]

This is "a decisive insight … into the unity of the two structural moments." They are "not two pieces arbitrarily stuck together; rather, letting ourselves go in this peculiar chattering away is a making present." "Wholly present, we bring time to a stand," and "this self-forming emptiness … sets us in place, binds us … holds us in limbo … as our own proper self that we ourselves have left standing" (p.127 / GA pp.191-92 / pdf 76). The conclusion: "The structural unity of the two structural moments is grounded in a making-present that brings the time taken to a stand." The unitary essence "must therefore be sought in time after all" — in "the manner and way in which this time stands into our Dasein and in which our Dasein itself is temporal." "Boredom springs from the temporality of Dasein," arising "from a quite determinate way and manner in which our own temporality temporalizes itself" — confirming "that boredom is possible only because … every Dasein as such has its time" (p.127 / GA pp.191-92 / pdf 76). [**C11**]

## §8. The boring arises out of Dasein itself (§27)

The concluding characterization makes the surface/depth structure explicit. Passing-the-time "is identical with our entire participation in the evening," yet boredom "spreads out beyond" the boring: "the standing 'now' that sets us in place and at the same time lets us go," "diffused in its strange ungraspability throughout the whole enduring of the evening" (p.128 / GA pp.192-94 / pdf 76). Because "the 'during' is what is boring, the whole evening must be organized as a way of passing the time"; and because the boring is "diffused throughout the particular situation as a whole, it is far more oppressive — despite its ungraspability" (p.128 / GA pp.192-94 / pdf 76).

The keystone contrast: "in the first case what is boring comes from outside … so that we become bored *by* …" — "A particular situation … transposes us into boredom." But "in the second case, what is boring does not come from outside: *it arises from out of Dasein itself*" (p.128 / GA pp.192-94 / pdf 76). [**C12**] Hence "the second form of boredom is less situation-bound than the first"; the surrounding beings are "only the occasion on which it comes to arise." "Boredom here means oneself being bored," and "we are held more toward ourselves, somehow enticed back into the specific gravity of Dasein, even though … we leave our own proper self standing and unfamiliar" — whereas form I is "an uneasy fidgeting that is directed outward" (p.128 / GA pp.192-94 / pdf 76).

## §9. More profound than the first — and the seven points (§28)

§28 problematizes "more profound" before affirming it. The two forms differ in their relation to time: in II "we leave ourselves time"; in I "we are oppressed by the dragging of time … we have no time" (p.129 / GA pp.194-95 / pdf 77). On its face the first situation is "the more serious," since "we wish to lose none of *our* time" and are thereby "worried about ourselves," while the second is "less serious, frivolous" (p.129 / GA pp.194-95 / pdf 77). Heidegger refuses to let this settle the matter, since the distinction "is a distinction with respect to their relationship to time, and time itself somehow constitutes the concealed essence of boredom" (p.129 / GA pp.194-95 / pdf 77).

He then *inverts* the surface verdict. Why do we "have no time" in the first form? "Because we need it and wish to use it … For our everyday occupations, to which we have long since become enslaved." This "not having any time is ultimately a *greater being lost of the self*" than the wasting-time that "leaves itself time." The having-time of the second form may harbor "a far greater balance and thereby security of Dasein — a being-alongside-oneself [*Bei-sich-selbst*]." So "having time and having no time is essentially ambiguous": the "'having no time' that looks like the most rigorous seriousness" is "perhaps the way in which we are most lost in the banalities of Dasein" (p.130 / GA pp.195-97 / pdf 77). [**C13**] The lesson: "it is impossible to infer straightforwardly a more serious, more profound Dasein from not having time" — depth "can be read off only from the specific constitution of boredom itself in each case" (p.130 / GA pp.195-97 / pdf 77).

The unit closes with the **seven-point comparison** of form I (*becoming bored by*) and form II (*being bored with*) (pp.130–131 / GA pp.195-98 / pdf 77) [**C14**]: **[1]** left-empty — I, absence of fullness for an emptiness at hand; II, the emptiness first forming itself. **[2]** held-in-limbo — I, detained by what drags in a time we need; II, not released, set in place by standing time as our left-behind self. **[3]** relation to situation — I, bound and stuck fast to a situation limited by extrinsic circumstances; II, not bound to the particular happenings. **[4]** I, a self-conspicuous attempt to pass the time with something arbitrary; II, an inconspicuous passing-the-time "hidden from the self that is bored, in our entire comportment." **[5]** I, "a fluttering unease … a being driven around within boredom"; II, passing-the-time "more of an evasion … boredom itself … more a letting oneself be bored." **[6]** range of resonance — I, "a being forced in between particular boring things"; II, "the floating dissipation of boredom throughout the whole situation." **[7]** I, "the extrinsic arrival and advent of boredom from out of a particular environment," a "fidgeting directed outward"; II, "the arising of boredom within and from out of Dasein," "a being drawn into the specific gravity of boredom" (p.131 / GA pp.197-98 / pdf 77).

A closing warning: the seven points are not a "result" or a "definition … to take home," but a scaffold for "learning and understanding how to move in the depths of Dasein" (p.131 / GA pp.197-98 / pdf 77) — the slide toward the third, profound form is already underway.

## §10. Boredom-bridge relevance (HIGHLY RELEVANT — keystone form)

This unit is the **structural pivot of the entire King–Salvo bridge**. The second form is the surface-occupied / depth-hollow form, and it predicts, almost point-for-point, the anomaly in the author's empirical work: a "clinical immersion" stimulus that reads boredom-like on EEG (low arousal) while reading *engaged* on gaze, with subjects reporting engagement *and* dragging time.

1. **The surface/depth split is the second form's signature.** Outwardly we are "totally involved," chatting, smoking, in "high spirits"; we *deny* being bored and only retrospectively recognize it. Heidegger's claim that "what is boring does not come from outside … it arises from out of Dasein itself" (C12, §27) gives the bridge its core mechanism: engaged surface behaviour (gaze-on-task, social participation) is fully compatible with — indeed *is* — the passing-the-time *of* a boredom rising from the subject. Behavioural engagement is therefore **not** evidence against boredom; in this form it is the very medium of it.

2. ***Lässigkeit* (casualness) operationalizes "engaged yet empty."** Casualness "obstructs in advance" the seeking-to-be-satisfied (C5), so the emptiness "grows from the depths" rather than from any stimulus deficit. This maps to a participant who is absorbed in the immersion (no searching, no fidget) yet self-forms an emptiness — predicting *flat physiological arousal without overt disengagement behaviours*, exactly the EEG-low / gaze-engaged dissociation.

3. **Standing time explains "engaged AND dragging time."** The temporalization analysis (C7–C9) shows that *because* the subject gives themselves over to the present (making-present, *ganz Gegenwart*), having-been and future dissolve into a single stretched "now" that *stands*. Reported time-distortion ("it dragged") is here not a sign of under-stimulation but the felt correlate of standing time — a more originary holding-in-limbo than the first form's clock-watching drag. This dissolves the apparent paradox in the self-report data.

4. **Depth is not read off the time-relation or arousal alone (C13).** Heidegger's inversion — "having no time … the most rigorous seriousness … perhaps the way in which we are most lost" — directly cautions the empirical team against inferring engagement from low time-pressure or from any single surface index. Depth "can be read off only from the specific constitution of boredom itself." This licenses a *multi-moment* coding scheme (left-empty + held-in-limbo as transformed) rather than an arousal-threshold classifier.

5. **Form-mapping for the team's own typology.** The unit's seven-point table supplies the discriminating vocabulary: the team's high-arousal "searching gaze" boredom corresponds to **form I** (fluttering unease, fidgeting directed outward, point 5/7); the low-arousal "zombie stare" corresponds to the **second/third** forms (floating dissipation, letting-oneself-be-bored, arising from Dasein). The "limits merge together" remark (§24b) further warns that immersive stimuli will sit on the II→III boundary, motivating the move into fcm-06 (profound boredom) for the deepest cases.

In short, fcm-05 furnishes the bridge with (a) a mechanism by which engagement and boredom coexist, (b) a temporal account of dragging-time-under-engagement, and (c) an explicit caution against arousal- or time-pressure-based shortcuts — the precise conceptual machinery the surface/depth empirical split requires.
