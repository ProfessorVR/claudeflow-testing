# Unit fcm-03 — Awakening a Fundamental Attunement; the First Naming of Profound Boredom

*Coverage:* Part One, Chapter One, §§16–18 (including §18c), book pp.59–77 / GA pp.90–116 / pdf 41–50.
*Translation:* McNeill & Walker, Indiana UP 1995. *Citation form:* triple locus `(p.NN / GA pp.NN / pdf NN)`; GA brackets read from the running heads, never computed (they drift).

This unit is the methodological and dramaturgical hinge of the entire boredom analysis. Across §§16–17 Heidegger builds the apparatus that licenses the whole of Part One — the thesis that a *Grundstimmung* is something to be *awakened*, not *ascertained*, and that attunement is a disclosive way of *Da-sein* rather than a feeling-state inside a subject. Then §18 turns the apparatus on "our contemporary situation," running the four culture-philosophy diagnoses (Spengler, Klages, Scheler, Ziegler) back through their Nietzschean source, only to dismiss the whole genre as world-historical role-play that unties us from ourselves. The payoff is §18c, where — for the first time in the lecture course — *profound boredom* (*tiefe Langeweile*) is named as the *concealed* fundamental attunement these diagnoses circle without grasping. Everything in Chapters 2–5 (the three forms of boredom) is the cashing-out of the claim first ventured on book p.77.

## §16 — Awakening, not ascertaining (book pp.59–65)

### §16a — Awakening as letting what is asleep become wakeful (p.59)

Heidegger opens by narrowing the task: not philosophy "in itself" (there is no such thing) but the awakening of a fundamental attunement that is "to sustain our philosophizing" — and *a* fundamental attunement, not *the* one, since there are several `(p.59 / GA pp.90 / pdf 41)`. He immediately blocks the obvious method. Attunements are precisely "something we can least of all invent, something that comes over us," something "into which we slip unawares" `(p.59 / GA pp.90 / pdf 41)`. If so, an attunement cannot be forcibly produced "artificially or arbitrarily" — it "must already be there," and all we could do, it seems, is "ascertain it." But can a fundamental attunement of philosophizing be ascertained at all, "demonstrated as a universally admitted fact"? `(p.59 / GA pp.90 / pdf 41)`.

The answer is a flat no. "Any so-called objective ascertaining of a fundamental attunement is a dubious, indeed impossible undertaking" `(p.60 / GA pp.90 / pdf 42)`. Crucially, the failure is not merely epistemic. It is no objection to the thesis that an attunement is "there in our Dasein" if a hearer, observing himself, fails to find it — "for in the end there is nothing at all to be found by observation … even if it were to call upon psychoanalysis for help" `(p.60 / GA pp.90 / pdf 42)`. Hence the methodological pivot that governs the book: "we shall not speak at all of 'ascertaining' a fundamental attunement in our philosophizing, but of awakening it. … Awakening means making something wakeful, letting whatever is sleeping become wakeful" `(p.60 / GA pp.90 / pdf 42)`. *Wecken* is set against *Feststellen* as a way of *letting be* against a way of *fixing-in-place*.

### §16b — Why conscious/unconscious will not serve (p.60)

"Whatever is sleeping" is "in a peculiar way absent and yet there" `(p.60 / GA pp.91 / pdf 42)`. To awaken an attunement is to presuppose that it is *already there* and yet, "in a certain way," *not* there — a being that "intrinsically contradicts itself" `(p.60 / GA pp.91 / pdf 42)`. Heidegger flags that this will force a confrontation with "an ancient proposition of traditional metaphysics" — that the contradictory cannot be, "just as a round square cannot be" — which the analysis will "not only put in question … but also cause … to shatter in its very foundations" `(p.61 / GA pp.91-93 / pdf 42)`. The being of Da-sein is not governed by the present-at-hand either/or.

The tempting fix is to map the "there and not there" onto *conscious* vs. *unconscious*: the unconscious is "at hand in us and yet not be," there are "processes that belong to us yet do not enter our consciousness" `(p.61 / GA pp.91-93 / pdf 42)`. Heidegger refuses the equation. Sleep cannot be identified with absence of consciousness, since "a peculiar and in many cases extremely animated consciousness pertains precisely to sleep, namely that of dreams" `(p.61–62 / GA pp.93-94 / pdf 42–43)`. Therefore "waking and sleeping are not equivalent to consciousness and unconsciousness" `(p.62 / GA pp.93-94 / pdf 43)`, and to awaken an attunement "cannot mean simply to make conscious an attunement which was previously unconscious." Worse, making an attunement into "an object of knowledge" achieves "the contrary of an awakening": the attunement is "thereby precisely destroyed, or at least … weakened and altered" `(p.62 / GA pp.93-94 / pdf 43)`. This is the load-bearing methodological claim of the unit (claim C1): the analytic comportment proper to attunement is awakening-as-letting-be, because objectification annihilates its object.

Heidegger then defers, "for fundamental metaphysical reasons," the full problem of sleep, but not before invoking **Aristotle** as the counter-model to "the many misinterpretations of sleep in modernity." In the treatise on waking and sleeping (*De Somno et Vigilia*), sleep is characterized as *akinesia* (motionlessness) and, more tellingly, as *desmos* — "a being bound, a peculiar way in which *aisthesis* is bound" `(p.62–63 / GA pp.93-94 / pdf 43)`. The point that matters: Aristotle "does not connect sleep with consciousness or unconsciousness" `(p.62 / GA pp.93-94 / pdf 43)`. The binding reaches "not only … perception … but also our essence, in that it cannot take in other beings which it itself is not" `(p.62–63 / GA pp.93-94 / pdf 43)` — a formulation that anticipates the Part Two account of the animal's *Benommenheit* (captivation) and its being closed to beings as beings.

### §16c — Da-sein and Weg-sein (p.63)

The decisive ontological move: the "there and not there" is to be grasped not through (un)consciousness but through the pair *being-there* (*Da-sein*) and *being-away* (*Weg-sein*). The phenomenon is ordinary. "How often it happens, in a conversation among a group of people, that we are 'not there'" — absent "albeit without having fallen asleep" `(p.63 / GA pp.94-96 / pdf 43)`. This not-being-there "has nothing at all to do with consciousness or unconsciousness," and "can be highly conscious" — the limit case being madness, where "the highest degree of consciousness can prevail and yet we say: The person is de-ranged, displaced, away and yet there" `(p.63 / GA pp.94-96 / pdf 43)`.

From this Heidegger extracts the structural thesis (claim C3). The "potential to be away … belongs to the way in which man is in general," yet man "has the potential to be away in this manner only if his being has the character of being-there" `(p.63 / GA pp.94-96 / pdf 43)`. *Weg-sein* is "itself a way of man's being"; it "does not mean: not being at all" but "a way of Da-sein's being-there" `(p.63–64 / GA pp.96-97 / pdf 43–44)`. The contrast with the stone is exact: "The stone, in its being away, is precisely not there. Man however must be there in order to be able to be away" `(p.64 / GA pp.96-97 / pdf 44)`. Being-away is not the "exclusive opposite" of being-there but presupposes it: "all being away presupposes the being there pertaining to Dasein. We must be-there in order to be able to be away" `(p.65 / GA pp.97-99 / pdf 44)`. Attunement, then, "has to do with the innermost essence of man's being, with his Dasein" `(p.63–64 / GA pp.96-97 / pdf 43–44)` — and the conscious/unconscious distinction "turns out not to be a primary one," since it can be drawn "both in being there and in being away" `(p.64 / GA pp.96-97 / pdf 44)`.

This stretch also stages and refuses the psychological default (the first of two "negative theses"). The ordinary objection — that attunements such as "joy, contentment, bliss, sadness, melancholy, anger" are merely "psychic," "emotional states" we can "ascertain … record how long they last" `(p.64 / GA pp.96-97 / pdf 44)` — installs feeling as "the third class of lived experience," ranked behind thinking and willing, "merely … the adornment of our thinking and willing" `(p.64–65 / GA pp.97-99 / pdf 44)`, "like the utterly fleeting and ungraspable shadows of clouds flitting across the landscape" `(p.65 / GA pp.97-99 / pdf 44)`. Heidegger concedes the characterization is "indisputably correct" within limits, but denies it is "the sole possible one or even the decisive one," precisely because it sits so comfortably "within the ancient conception of man" `(p.65 / GA pp.97-99 / pdf 44)`. The positive counter-thesis is owed.

## §17 — Attunement as a fundamental way of Dasein (book pp.66–68)

§17 pays the debt. The phenomenological lever is grief shared in being-with: a person overcome by grief "closes himself off, becomes inaccessible" without animosity, and "everything remains as before, and yet everything is different … the way in which we are together is different" `(p.66 / GA pp.99-100 / pdf 45)`. This altered "way" is "not some subsequent effect of the attunement of grief being at hand in him, but belongs rather to his grief as part of it" `(p.66 / GA pp.99-100 / pdf 45)`. From which: attunement "is not at all inside, in some sort of soul of the Other," nor "somewhere alongside in our soul"; rather "the attunement imposes itself on everything" `(p.66–67 / GA pp.100-101 / pdf 45)`. It is "not at all 'inside' in some interiority … but for this reason it is not at all outside either" `(p.67 / GA pp.100-101 / pdf 45)`. Attunement is "the way of our being there with one another" `(p.67 / GA pp.100-101 / pdf 45)`.

Heidegger generalizes with the good-humoured person who "brings a lively atmosphere," and presses the everyday word "infectious": attunements "in advance determine our being with one another," "like an atmosphere in which we first immerse ourselves … and which then attunes us through and through" `(p.67 / GA pp.100-101 / pdf 45)`. Then the verdict: "faced with this fact, we must dismiss the psychology of feelings, experiences, and consciousness" `(p.67 / GA pp.100-101 / pdf 45)`. Attunements "are not something merely at hand. They themselves are precisely a fundamental manner and fundamental way of being, indeed of being-there" — a way of being that always directly includes being with one another — and so "Attunements are ways of the being-there of Da-sein, and thus ways of being-away" `(p.67 / GA pp.100-101 / pdf 45)`. The wordplay on *Weise* is doing conceptual work: an attunement is "a way [Weise] — in the sense of a melody," one that "sets the tone for such being, i.e., attunes and determines the manner and way … of his being" `(p.67 / GA pp.100-101 / pdf 45)`.

Now both counter-theses stand (claim C4). Positively: "attunement is a fundamental manner, the fundamental way in which Dasein is as Dasein"; and against the second negative thesis (fleeting inconstancy), "because attunement is the originary way in which every Dasein is as it is, it is not what is most inconstant" but rather "that which gives Dasein subsistence and possibility in its very foundations" `(p.67–68 / GA pp.100-103 / pdf 45–46)`. Attunements are "the 'how' according to which one is in such and such a way" — "never simply a consequence or side-effect of our thinking, doing, and acting" but "the presupposition for such things, the 'medium' within which they first happen" `(p.68 / GA pp.101-103 / pdf 46)`.

A claim of capital importance for the boredom analysis follows (claim C5): "those attunements to which we pay no heed at all, the attunements we least observe … as though there is no attunement there at all … these attunements are the most powerful" `(p.68 / GA pp.101-103 / pdf 46)`. The "lack of attunement" in which "we are neither out of sorts nor in a 'good' mood" is not absence of attunement; "even in this 'neither/nor' we are never without an attunement" `(p.68 / GA pp.101-103 / pdf 46)`. "Dasein as Dasein is always already attuned in its very grounds. There is only ever a change of attunement" `(p.68 / GA pp.101-103 / pdf 46)`. Because attunement "leads us back into the grounds of our Dasein, the essence of attunement remains concealed or hidden from us," so we grasp it first in "the extreme tendencies … those which irrupt then disappear," overlooking "the primordial, pervasive attunement of our whole Dasein as such" `(p.68 / GA pp.101-103 / pdf 46)`. This concealment-of-the-pervasive is the precise structural reason a *profound* boredom can be operative and *unnoticed* — exactly what §18c will assert.

The section closes by re-stating the goal (claim C6): "awakening attunements is a manner and means of grasping Da-sein … of grasping Da-sein as Da-sein, or better: of letting Da-sein be as it is, or can be, as Da-sein" `(p.68 / GA pp.101-103 / pdf 46)`. And since "this awakening is an acting, we must act in accordance with it" `(p.68 / GA pp.101-103 / pdf 46)` — not relapse into deliberation about awakening.

## §18 — Making sure of our contemporary situation (book pp.69–77)

### §18a — The four diagnoses (p.69)

To awaken *which* attunement, in *whose* situation? Heidegger widens the "us" from "this number of individual human beings assembled here" to the Western/European horizon, noting that "the broader the perspective we take, the more passionately and decisively it will take hold of us" `(p.69 / GA pp.103-104 / pdf 46)`. He then surveys four "explicit characterizations … of our contemporary situation," conceding the choice "is never free from arbitrariness" `(p.70 / GA pp.104-106 / pdf 47)`:

1. **Spengler** — "decline of the West": its fundamental thesis "reduced to a formula" is "the decline of life in and through spirit," where what spirit "as reason (ratio), has formed … in technology, economy, in world trade" now turns "against the soul, against life" `(p.70 / GA pp.104-106 / pdf 47)`.
2. **Klages** — does not predict decline but "comes to repudiate spirit": "Spirit is seen as the adversary of the soul," "a sickness which has to be exorcised in order to liberate the soul," life taken as "the obscure simmering of drives" and "breeding ground of the mythical," determined "by Bachofen and above all by Nietzsche" `(p.70 / GA pp.104-106 / pdf 47)`.
3. **Scheler** — "a balance between life and spirit," from his late lecture "Man in the epoch of balance" `(p.71 / GA pp.106-107 / pdf 47)`.
4. **Ziegler** — "the most unoriginal and philosophically most fragile," looking "toward a new Middle Ages," a "mediating epoch … to bring about a new sublation of the opposition 'life and spirit'" (*The European Spirit*) `(p.71–72 / GA pp.106-107 / pdf 47–48)`.

These are "interpretations that are partly borrowed second- and third-hand," feeding "the higher journalism of our age" `(p.72 / GA pp.106-107 / pdf 48)`. Their "fundamental trait," "the perspective in which they all see our contemporary situation," is "the relation between life and spirit" (claim C7) `(p.72 / GA pp.106-107 / pdf 48)`. But these are no "two components of man"; they name "specific fundamental orientations," and what is really at issue is "what Nietzsche means by the terms Dionysian and Apollonian" `(p.72 / GA pp.106-107 / pdf 48)`.

### §18b — Nietzsche as the common source (p.72)

"All four interpretations … point back to this common source, to Nietzsche and to a particular conception of Nietzsche" (claim C8) `(p.72 / GA pp.107-109 / pdf 48)`. The "Dionysos/Apollo opposition sustains and guides Nietzsche's philosophizing from early on" `(p.72 / GA pp.107-109 / pdf 48)`, and Heidegger draws on *The Will to Power* — within the content-filter, the anchor phrases suffice: the word Dionysian "expresses an urge for unity, a reaching beyond the person," "a passionate and painful swelling over"; the Apollonian "expresses the urge for complete being-for-oneself, for the typical 'individual'," "freedom under the law" `(p.73 / GA pp.109-10 / pdf 48`. The opposition finally hardens into "The two types: Dionysos and the Crucified": "The God on the cross is a curse on life," whereas "Dionysos dismembered is a promise of life" `(p.74 / GA pp.110-11 / pdf 49)`. Heidegger's verdict is double-edged: in Nietzsche "an opposition was alive that in no way came to light in the four interpretations," which retained only "a residual effect as material passed on, as a literary form" `(p.74 / GA pp.110-11 / pdf 49)`. The four are "only possible given a particular reception of Nietzsche's philosophy" `(p.72 / GA pp.107-109 / pdf 48)`, and Nietzsche's own foundations rest on "a quite ordinary and metaphysically highly questionable 'psychology'" `(p.74 / GA pp.110-11 / pdf 49)`.

### §18c — Profound boredom first named (p.74)

Here the hinge turns. Heidegger brackets correctness entirely: "We are not even asking whether all these interpretations of our situation are correct or not" `(p.74 / GA pp.110-11 / pdf 49)`. What matters is *what is going on* in them: "a diagnosis of culture" that, "with the aid of the aforementioned categories of life and spirit," passes "through and beyond world history at a single stroke" `(p.74 / GA pp.110-11 / pdf 49)`. The trouble is that "we ourselves are not at all concerned, let alone affected by this world-historical determination" `(p.74–75 / GA pp.111-13 / pdf 49)`. The diagnosis is "something sensational," yielding "an unconceded, yet once again illusory appeasement … of a merely literary and characteristically short-lived kind" `(p.75 / GA pp.111-13 / pdf 49)`. Extended into "prognosis," it answers our wish "to know what is coming" so as to be "less burdened, less preoccupied" `(p.75 / GA pp.111-13 / pdf 49)`. Diagnosis and prognosis "do not involve us, they do not attack us. … they release us from ourselves and present us to ourselves in a world-historical situation and role" — the mark of "what is called 'philosophy of culture'" `(p.75 / GA pp.111-13 / pdf 49)` (claim C9). Even the more refined philosophy of culture "concerned with expression, with symbol, with symbolic forms" attains "merely the setting-out [Dar-stellung] of man, but never his Da-sein," and "is of necessity unable to attain it, because in itself it blocks the path" `(p.76 / GA pp.113-14 / pdf 50)`.

So the genre "unties us from ourselves," and "does so precisely as anthropology"; "our flight and disorientation, the illusion and our lostness become more acute" `(p.77 / GA pp.114-16 / pdf 50)`. Heidegger then asks "the decisive question": "What lies behind the fact that we give ourselves this role and indeed must do so?" Have we become "too insignificant to ourselves, that we require a role?" Why "no essential possibility of being"? "Is it because an indifference yawns at us out of all things, an indifference whose grounds we do not know?" `(p.77 / GA pp.114-16 / pdf 50)`. And then the question that *names* it — the first appearance of profound boredom in the course (claim C10):

> "Perhaps because we ourselves have become bored with ourselves? … Do things ultimately stand in such a way with us that a *profound boredom* draws back and forth like a silent fog in the abysses of Dasein?" `(p.77 / GA pp.114-16 / pdf 50)`.

The naming is deliberately interrogative and *concealed*: profound boredom is offered not as an observed datum but as the hidden *Grundstimmung* that the culture-diagnoses simultaneously register and flee. This is exactly the concealment-of-the-most-powerful established in §17 `(p.68 / GA pp.101-103 / pdf 46)`. The unit closes by redirecting the task (claim C11): "We do not ultimately need any diagnoses or prognoses of culture … because they merely provide us with a role and untie us from ourselves" `(p.77 / GA pp.114-16 / pdf 50)`; instead "we must find ourselves by binding ourselves to our being-there … and by letting such being-there become what is singularly binding for us" `(p.77 / GA pp.114-16 / pdf 50)`. The final two sentences pose the program for all of Part One: "Will we find ourselves via this indication of that *profound boredom*, which perhaps none of us know at first? … Is this questionable profound boredom actually supposed to be the sought-after fundamental attunement that must be awakened?" `(p.77 / GA pp.114-16 / pdf 50)`.

## Boredom-bridge relevance

Three features of this unit are load-bearing for the King–Salvo bridge. First, the *awaken-not-ascertain* thesis (C1) and the corollary that objectifying an attunement "weakens and alters" it `(p.62 / GA pp.93-94 / pdf 43)` set the methodological terms for the team's instrumentation problem: an EEG/DMN or gaze probe *ascertains* a present-at-hand correlate, which is not the same as awakening the attunement — so a null or contradictory reading is precisely what the Heideggerian frame predicts, not a measurement failure. Second, the §17 claim that the *least-noticed* attunements are "the most powerful" and that Dasein is "always already attuned" `(p.68 / GA pp.101-103 / pdf 46)` underwrites the bridge's second-form hypothesis: subjects who *report* engagement and *look* occupied (high self-reported interest, searching gaze) may nonetheless be pervaded by a boredom they do not notice — the surface/depth split the 2023 EEG-vs-2024 gaze contradiction exhibits. Third, §18c's naming of profound boredom as a *concealed* attunement behind a restless need to "make ourselves interesting to ourselves" `(p.77 / GA pp.114-16 / pdf 50)` is the conceptual ancestor of the team's high-arousal "look[ing] around the screen … to find something to attend to" — Heidegger's culture-diagnostic *Zeitvertreib* writ as the searching gaze of high gaze-variance boredom. The unit thus supplies the structural license (concealment, surface/depth, always-already-attuned) on which the form-specific empirical markers of the later bridge are built; the actual cross-instrument mapping is developed downstream and carries `provenance: anticipatory-application`, not Heidegger's own claim.
