# fcm-06 — The Third Form of Boredom: Profound Boredom as *Es ist einem langweilig* ("It Is Boring for One")

*Part One, Chapter Four, §§29–36 · book pp.132–159 · GA pp.200–239 · pdf 78–91 · trans. McNeill & Walker, Indiana UP 1995.*

This is the keystone unit of the boredom analysis. Where the first form (*Gelangweiltwerden von etwas*, fcm-04) was bored *by* a determinate situation and the second (*Sichlangweilen bei etwas*, fcm-05) was bored *with* something while outwardly engaged, the third form is the deepest, stillest, most impersonal: a profound boredom in which there is no longer any "I" or "you" who is bored, but only the impersonal verdict *es ist einem langweilig* — "it is boring for one." Heidegger's wager is that this form, pressed to its ground, *is* the disclosure of original time, and so the hinge from Part One ("awakening a fundamental attunement") to Part Two ("really asking the metaphysical question, what is world?"). The whole interpretation is conducted along the now-familiar pair of structural moments — being left empty (*Leergelassenheit*) and being held in limbo (*Hingehaltenheit*) — but both are transformed past recognition.

## §1 — Prerequisites: putting man-as-consciousness in question (§29, p.132)

Returning from vacation, Heidegger recapitulates the seven points under which the first two forms were distinguished (the two structural moments; situation-relatedness; the two kinds of passing-the-time and their relation; the range of oscillation; and the provenance and proximity to the ground of Dasein), noting the decisive result that the first form "comes to meet us from the outside" while the second shows "boredom arises out of Dasein itself" (p.133 / GA p.200 / pdf 78). But this distinction in depth only *indicates* the direction of deepening; it has "not yet penetrated into the depths of its very essence."

The methodological crux follows. One might try to *construct* profound boredom indirectly out of a deeper concept of time — and in principle this must be possible, "for we can see that the more profound it becomes, the more completely boredom is rooted in time" (p.133 / GA p.201 / pdf 78). But "we know precisely nothing concerning this." So the route is the converse: "to press forward to the essence of time through our interpretation of the essence of boredom." This is not obstinacy; the essence of time "cannot be illuminated at all in any other way" — not by "simply speculating about time and thinking up another concept." Crucially, this path demands a prior demolition: the conception of man "as consciousness, as subject, as person, as a rational being" must be "put in question" — and not merely the Cartesian *access* to consciousness but "the initial positing of man as consciousness in general" — because it is "the chief obstacle preventing our access to original time" (p.134 / GA p.201 / pdf 79). The depths will open only if "profound boredom bores as such," attuning us "through and through" (p.134 / GA p.203 / pdf 79).

## §2 — No longer permitting any passing-the-time; the overpowering (§30, p.134)

The deeper the boredom, the "more silent, the less public, the quieter, the more inconspicuous and wide-ranging" — and correspondingly the harder its passing-the-time is to recognize; perhaps, Heidegger suspects, "there is no passing the time at all for this profound boredom," and this absence is "distinctive of it" (p.134 / GA p.203 / pdf 79). The form is named in the impersonal: profound boredom bores "whenever we silently know, that it is boring for one."

Heidegger dwells on the grammar of the "it" and the "one." The "it" is the impersonal of *es donnert*, *es regnet* — "the title for whatever is indeterminate, unfamiliar." The "for one" is not "for me as me, not for you as you… but for one" (p.135 / GA p.203 / pdf 79): "name, standing, vocation, role, age and fate as mine and yours disappear." What remains is *not* "a universal ego in general" — this is no abstraction or generalization — but rather, and this is "what is decisive: that here we become an undifferentiated no one" (p.135 / GA p.203 / pdf 79). Where the earlier procedure looked for an *example*, here "there is none to be found," not because it does not happen but because "it is not at all relative to a particular situation." It can occur "out of the blue, and precisely whenever we do not expect it at all." The one non-binding occasion offered: "it is boring for one' to walk through the streets of a large city on a Sunday afternoon" (p.135 / GA p.203 / pdf 79).

The treatment of passing-the-time is the section's pivot. In the second form passing-the-time had already shrunk to "an evasion in the face of …"; now "we no longer even attain this evasion." Passing-the-time is *missing* — but not by forgetting or oversight. Rather "we have already understood that all passing the time is powerless against this boredom"; the boredom "wishes to tell us something," and "not something arbitrary or contingent" (p.135 / GA p.204 / pdf 79). To not-permit passing-the-time "means to let this boredom be overpowering" (p.136 / GA p.205 / pdf 80). Whereas in the first form we "shout down the boredom" so as not to listen, and in the second we exhibit "a not wanting to listen," here there is "a being compelled to listen" — compelled by "that kind of compelling force which everything properly authentic about Dasein possesses," related to "Dasein's innermost freedom." The "it is boring for one" "has already transposed us into a realm of power over which the individual person, the public individual subject, no longer has any power" (p.136 / GA p.205 / pdf 80).

## §3 — Being left empty: the telling refusal of beings as a whole (§31a, p.136)

The concrete interpretation proceeds along the two moments, taken again as "non-binding" directives that may transform. The emptiness here is neither the first form's unfilled situational lack nor the second form's "self-forming" emptiness of letting-oneself-go (p.137 / GA p.207 / pdf 80). It is "an emptiness precisely where, as this person in each case, we want nothing from the particular beings" — and that we want nothing "is already due to the boredom." For with this attunement we are "not merely relieved of our everyday personality… but simultaneously also elevated beyond the particular situation… and beyond the specific beings surrounding us." It "makes everything of equally great and equally little worth," taking us back "to the point where all and everything appears indifferent to us" (p.137 / GA p.207 / pdf 80).

The indifference is total and instantaneous, not cumulative: "each and every thing at once becomes indifferent… all of a sudden everything is enveloped and embraced by this indifference" — not leaping "from one thing over onto another like a fire" (p.138 / GA p.207 / pdf 80). "Beings have… become indifferent as a whole, and we ourselves as these people are not excepted." We no longer stand as subjects opposite beings but "find ourselves in the midst of beings as a whole, i.e., in the whole of this indifference"; yet "beings as a whole do not disappear… but show themselves precisely as such in their indifference" (p.138 / GA p.208 / pdf 80).

Heidegger then resolves an apparent contradiction (if we too are indifferent, how can we be "left empty," which presupposes a claim to fulfilment?). The answer reaches the formula of the section: it is boring not for me as me but "for one as this particular *Da-sein*" — and "the indifference of beings as a whole manifests itself for Da-sein, but for Da-sein as such" (p.139 / GA p.210 / pdf 81). In this boredom "the beings that surround us offer us no further possibility of acting"; there is "a telling refusal [*Versagen*] on the part of beings as a whole with respect to these possibilities." Hence the section's thesis: "Being left empty in this third form of boredom is Dasein's being delivered over to beings' telling refusal of themselves as a whole" (p.139 / GA p.210 / pdf 81). We are "left entirely in the lurch… not only not occupied with this or that being… but as a whole." And the decisive qualification: "The emptiness is not a hole between things that are filled, but concerns beings as a whole and yet is not the Nothing" (p.140 / GA p.210 / pdf 81).

## §4 — Being held in limbo: the refusal that tells; impelling toward the *Spitze* (§31b, p.140)

This profound boredom "does not have the character of despair"; being-left-empty "alone does not constitute boredom," but is bound up with a being-held-in-limbo with which "it first constitutes boredom" (p.140 / GA p.210 / pdf 81). The derivation is linguistic and ontological at once: "All telling refusal [*Versagen*] is in itself a telling [*Sagen*], i.e., a making manifest" (p.140 / GA p.212 / pdf 82). What do beings "tell us in refusing to tell"? They tell of "the very possibilities of [Dasein's] doing and acting" — but indeterminately, "it merely tells of them indirectly and in general." There occurs "the dawning of the possibilities that Dasein could have, but which are left unexploited"; this "telling announcement [*Ansagen*] of possibilities left unexploited" is the seed of being-held-in-limbo (p.141 / GA p.212 / pdf 82).

The being-held-in-limbo of the first form was "being held up by the dragging of time," of the second "being set in place by standing time" — but "here in the third form… there is nothing to be found here of time"; one "feels timeless… removed from the flow of time" (p.142 / GA p.213 / pdf 83). Heidegger refuses to explain away this remoteness; instead he resolves to continue the interpretation "without regard to the initially concealed temporal essence," rather than "forcibly dragging in the time-structure externally" (p.142 / GA p.215 / pdf 84).

The refusal is radicalized: the "it is boring for one" "explodes the situation and places us in the full expanse of whatever is in each case manifest as a whole" — beings refuse "altogether in prospect and in retrospect" (p.143 / GA p.215 / pdf 83). The self whose name and status become irrelevant "does not thereby lose its determinacy, but rather the reverse"; this "peculiar impoverishment… first brings the self in all its nakedness to itself as the self that is there and has taken over the being-there of its Da-sein" (p.143 / GA p.216 / pdf 83). What is refused is "whatever makes it possible" — "whatever is utmost and primary in making possible all possibilities of Dasein." So the telling announcement "is a calling [*Anrufen*]… that which properly makes possible the Dasein in me" — "an utterly unambiguous pointing" toward what makes possible all essential possibilities, "for which we apparently have no content" (p.143 / GA p.215–216 / pdf 83). This impels Dasein "toward the singular extremity [*Spitze*] of whatever originarily makes possible" (p.144 / GA p.216 / pdf 84). The being-held-in-limbo of the third form is thus "being impelled toward the originary making-possible of Dasein as such." The structural unity is then stated as one oscillation: "this leaving empty that takes us into an expanse together with a holding us in limbo … that intensifies extremity is the originary manner in which the attunement that we call boredom attunes us" (p.144 / GA p.217 / pdf 84).

## §5 — The temporal core, I: *Gebanntheit* of the threefold horizon (§32a, p.144)

Time was elaborated "without reference to time" — but precisely "in this 'it is boring for one' that the clock does not play any part"; "looking at the clock here loses all meaning," and yet "we move just as deeply within the essence of time" (p.144 / GA p.217 / pdf 84). The breakthrough: when beings refuse themselves "as a whole," they "withdraw from us without exception in every respect" — in *respect* (*Hinsicht*), *retrospect* (*Rücksicht*), and *prospect* (*Absicht*), which are "not… mere perception" but "the perspectives of all doing and activity of Dasein" (p.145 / GA p.219 / pdf 85). These three "proceed to distribute" themselves into "present, having-been, and future"; they are "not lined up alongside one another, but originarily simply united in the horizon of time as such": "there is the single and unitary universal horizon of time." The "as a whole" of beings' refusal "is evidently possible only insofar as beings are enveloped by the single yet simultaneously threefold horizon of time" (p.145 / GA p.219 / pdf 85).

Heidegger marks the abyssal difficulty here — the horizon-character of time is itself unclarified; "never yet… has the case been heard of in philosophy where a bland triviality did not conceal behind it the abyssal difficulty of the problem" (p.146 / GA p.220 / pdf 86). He presses a battery of questions about whether the horizon is mere "scenery on a stage," a neutral "vessel," or "belongs to time itself" — invoking the Greek *horizein* (ὁρίζειν, "to delimit," p.147 / GA p.220 / pdf 86) — but defers them. What he secures is the temporal sense of being-left-empty: beings withdraw, "yet not at all in such a way that Dasein is left alone"; the withdrawal (*Entzug*) "is possible only if Dasein… is entranced [*gebannt*] as Da-sein, and indeed as a whole" (p.147 / GA p.222 / pdf 84). "Time entrances [*bannt*] Dasein" — "not as the time which has remained standing as distinct from flowing, but… the time which in each case Dasein itself as a whole is. This whole time entrances as a horizon" (p.147 / GA p.222 / pdf 84). What entrances "is nothing other than the temporal horizon." This *Gebanntheit* "is the temporal character of being-left-empty." What entrances is not a "determinate time-point" (the "now" "sinks at a stroke") nor "a more stretched 'now'"; the boredom "can take hold of us in an instant like a flash of lightning … precisely in this instant the whole expanse of the entire time of Dasein is there" — in "their unarticulated unity in the simplicity of this unity of their horizon all at once" (p.148 / GA p.222 / pdf 85).

## §6 — The temporal core, II: impelled toward the *Augenblick* (§32b, p.148)

Time "is that which, in this boredom, strikes Dasein into time's entrancement," and through that entrancement gives beings "the possibility of a telling refusal" (p.148 / GA p.223 / pdf 86). But "this entrancing power of time… is that which also calls and tells of what is properly refused" — namely Dasein's possibility — and "this entrancing time is itself this extremity that essentially makes Dasein possible" (p.149 / GA p.223 / pdf 86). What time "gives to be free in its telling announcing is nothing less than the freedom of Dasein as such," a freedom that "only is in Dasein's freeing itself." Self-liberation happens "only if Dasein resolutely discloses [*sich entschließt*] itself to itself" — and to do so amid these beings means "to bring these beings together into an extremity," resolving "for action here and now." "This resolute self-disclosure is the moment of vision [*Augenblick*]" (p.149 / GA p.224 / pdf 87).

The *Augenblick* is defined precisely: "nothing other than the look of resolute disclosedness [*Blick der Entschlossenheit*] … in which the full situation of an action opens itself and keeps itself open" (p.149 / GA p.224 / pdf 87). Being impelled into the extremity is "a being impelled through entrancing time into that time itself… toward the moment of vision as the fundamental possibility of Dasein's existence proper." The entranced whole-time "announces and tells of itself as that which is to be ruptured and can be ruptured solely in the moment of vision" (p.150 / GA p.224 / pdf 87). Heidegger then credits the concept: "What we here designate as 'moment of vision' is what was really comprehended for the first time in philosophy by Kierkegaard" — opening "the possibility of a completely new epoch of philosophy" — while noting that fashionable "literature about Kierkegaard" has obscured "this decisive point" (p.150 / GA p.225 / pdf 87). He cross-refers to his own treatment of the Augenblick's "rootedness in the essence of temporality… in *Being and Time*, §65," as "an external aid," the problem there "not solved… but merely grasped in its nucleus" (p.150 / GA p.225 / pdf 87).

Two reasons make this the hardest stretch of the lecture: the boredom "conceals its temporal character," and the path "leads via boredom to time, without our having in advance adequately assured ourselves of the essence of time" — "straight into the dark, without our having a light that could illuminate the path before us" (p.150 / GA p.226 / pdf 87). The Augenblick "ruptures the entrancement of time… insofar as it is a specific possibility of time itself"; "it is not some now-point that we simply ascertain, but is the look of Dasein in the three perspectival directions… present, future, and past" (p.151 / GA p.226 / pdf 88). The phenomenon is "one unitary phenomenon" — expanse and extremity "which are not two, but one" — in which "the Dasein in us oscillates out into the expanse of the temporal horizon… and thus is able only to oscillate into the moment of vision pertaining to essential action" (p.151 / GA p.227 / pdf 88). Yet in the entrancement of Dasein the Augenblick is "now… not thus given" (p.152 / GA p.227 / pdf 88).

## §7 — *Langeweile* as the lengthening of the while (§33, p.152)

Only now can the word receive "a more essential meaning. In boredom, *Langeweile*, the while [*Weile*] becomes long [*lang*]" (p.152 / GA p.229 / pdf 88). Not "any short while," but "that while whilst Dasein is as such," "the while that measures out that tarrying awhile [*Verweilen*] which is allotted to Dasein." This while "is at first and for the most part concealed from Dasein." That the while "becomes long" does not mean short time "appears longer": "what is at issue is not the time of the clock or chronology, but the lengthening or shortening of time proper" — for "Human Dasein can become essential in terms of the brevity of objective time … it can remain inessential even if it reaches seventy years old or more" (p.152 / GA p.229 / pdf 88). The lengthening "is the expansion of the temporal horizon, whose expansion does not bring Dasein liberation… but precisely the converse in oppressing it with its expanse" (p.153 / GA p.229 / pdf 88).

The lengthening is also "a vanishing of the shortness of the while" — and shortness here means "the sharpness and extremity of a moment of vision pertaining to action." This vanishing "precisely does not make the moment of vision vanish, however; … rather only the possibility vanishes here, whereby the possibility of whatever is possible is precisely intensified." Even in vanishing, "the moment of vision still presses itself upon us as that which is properly and tellingly refused in time's entrancing" (p.153 / GA p.229 / pdf 88). Expanse and shortness "spring in turn… from the specific way in which the temporality of Dasein… temporalizes itself."

## §8 — The summary "definition" (§34, p.153)

"Boredom is the entrancement of the temporal horizon, an entrancement which lets the moment of vision belonging to temporality vanish. In thus letting it vanish, boredom impels entranced Dasein … into the moment of vision as the properly authentic possibility of its existence" (p.153 / GA p.230 / pdf 88). Heidegger immediately disciplines this: the definition "does not tell us much if it is taken as an assertion in which something is supposed to be established, … instead of as a more incisive directive for interpretation." The two structural moments, "always explicated one-sidedly," now prove "not… false, but presumably… over-emphasised"; boredom must be grasped "in terms of the unity of its essence" (p.154 / GA p.230 / pdf 88).

The definition is read off the third form, which is admittedly not "universal enough to fit all forms" — but "the third form of boredom is not an arbitrary form… but with respect to the first and second… is the more profound, i.e., at the same time the more essential." Philosophy as "knowledge of the essence" must grasp "the essentiality of something," never "the most universal possible essence that fits all forms, i.e., the emptiest" (p.155 / GA p.232 / pdf 89). A long excursus on philosophizing follows ("the poet should create, not talk"; "Philosophy is only there to be overcome"; we must understand a philosopher "better than he understood himself," p.156 / GA p.232–233 / pdf 89). The point is not to claim boredom "absolutely… illuminated," but to win "the readiness to ask after a particular boredom of our Dasein" (p.156 / GA p.233 / pdf 90).

He then dismantles a prejudice "constantly reinforced by our own discussions": that the forms develop factically, "as though the first were the cause of the second and… the second passed into the third." On the contrary: "the first form precisely holds the others down and keeps them at bay, in particular the third" (p.156 / GA p.235 / pdf 90). Reversing the order: "the third form is the condition of the possibility of the first and thereby also of the second." "Only because this constant possibility — the 'it is boring for one' — lurks in the ground of Dasein can man be bored… by the things and people around him" (p.157 / GA p.235 / pdf 90). The forms are "fluid," with "manifold intermediate forms," "according to the depth which man grants his own Dasein."

## §9 — Temporality as what properly bores; the suppression (§§35–36, p.157)

What "properly bores us" must remain concealed from everyday consciousness. In the second form what bores us was "time in its standing"; but "now in the third form of boredom what leaves us empty in the manner of entrancing us is the time of Dasein as such," and what holds us in limbo "is this time in its possibility as moment of vision… horizon and moment of vision" (p.157 / GA p.236 / pdf 90). Hence: "What is boring is neither beings nor things as such… neither objects nor subjects, but temporality as such" (p.158 / GA p.236 / pdf 90). Temporality "constitutes the ground of the possibility of the subjectivity of subjects," so the "legitimate illusion can arise that things are boring, and that it is people themselves who are bored" (p.158 / GA p.236 / pdf 90).

§36 closes on the "ordinary assessment." Everyday morality holds boredom "disturbing, unpleasant, and unbearable," "unworthy and to be condemned," "a sign of shallowness," from which one is "secure" if one "sets a proper task" (p.158 / GA p.236 / pdf 90). Heidegger's verdict: it is "hard to tell which is the greater in this morality — its hypocritical self-assuredness or its banality" (p.159 / GA p.238 / pdf 91). The "decisive reason for misunderstanding boredom is the failure to appreciate the essence of attunement in general," tied to "an apparently self-evident and absolute conception of Dasein." Two things are missed: "that attunement in itself makes manifest… Dasein itself," and "that it can do this only if it arises from the ground of the essence of Dasein, … in a way that is for the most part withdrawn from Dasein's freedom" (p.159 / GA p.238 / pdf 91). The sting is that the dominance of the ordinary understanding "suppresses profound boredom," keeping it "within the field of the busy activity of Dasein in its superficiality" — so "a certain conception of feelings… is not as harmless as we think" (p.159 / GA p.238 / pdf 91).

## §10 — Boredom-bridge relevance

This profound, low-arousal form maps directly onto the **third pole** of the King–Salvo bridge. The team's own empirical distinction between a high-arousal, searching-gaze boredom and a low-arousal "zombie stare" finds, in the third form, its phenomenological deep-structure: profound boredom is "the more silent, the less public, the quieter, the more inconspicuous and wide-ranging" (p.134 / GA p.203 / pdf 79) — precisely the affective signature of a flat, depleted, *low-arousal* state in which the subject is neither searching nor evading. Three structural features make the mapping load-bearing.

First, **impersonality and de-individuation**. The dissolution of name, standing, vocation, and fate into "an undifferentiated no one" (p.135 / GA p.203 / pdf 79) is the phenomenal correlate of a physiological/attentional baseline in which goal-directed engagement has collapsed; there is no "user" pursuing a task. This is the opposite pole from the first form's high-arousal restlessness (fcm-04) and warns the bridge against reading low arousal as mere "absence" of engagement: it is a positive, overpowering attunement that "no longer permits" passing-the-time (p.136 / GA p.205 / pdf 80).

Second, **entrancement (*Gebanntheit*) as the temporal signature of "dragging time without searching."** The third form explains how subjects can report *time dragging* while showing neither the active disengagement-behaviors of the first form nor the surface-occupation of the second: time itself "entrances" (*bannt*) Dasein in the single threefold horizon (p.147 / GA p.222 / pdf 84), and the lengthening of the while "oppresses" rather than liberates (p.153 / GA p.229 / pdf 88). For the bridge's anomalous "clinical immersion" stimulus — boredom-like on EEG yet with reported dragging time — the third form supplies the missing category: *Langeweile* as horizon-expansion, decoupled from clock-time and from gaze-level search.

Third, **the *Augenblick* as the absent rupture**. Profound boredom is defined by the Augenblick's *vanishing-yet-pressing* (p.153 / GA p.229 / pdf 88): the possibility of resolute, situated action is held before Dasein precisely as refused. For an arousal-based bridge model, this names the third pole's diagnostic marker — not absence of stimulation but a refused call to action — distinguishing profound boredom (low arousal, refused possibility) from flow/engagement (the Augenblick *enacted*) and from situational boredom (high arousal, evasive passing-the-time). Finally, §36's thesis that the ordinary assessment *suppresses* profound boredom (p.159 / GA p.238 / pdf 91) is a methodological caution the bridge should carry: instruments and design conventions that treat boredom as a mere unpleasant state "to be eliminated" will systematically under-detect exactly this deepest, stillest, low-arousal form.
