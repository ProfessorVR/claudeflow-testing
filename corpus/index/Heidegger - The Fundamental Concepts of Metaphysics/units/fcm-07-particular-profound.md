# fcm-07 — The Question Concerning a Particular Profound Boredom as the Fundamental Attunement of Our Contemporary Dasein

**Work:** Martin Heidegger, *The Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics: World, Finitude, Solitude* (*Die Grundbegriffe der Metaphysik*), GA 29/30, Freiburg lecture course WS 1929–30; trans. McNeill & Walker, Indiana UP 1995.
**Unit:** Part One, Chapter Five, §§37–38 (incl. §38a, §38b). Book pp.160–168 / GA pp.[240–49] / pdf 92–96.
**Reading order; GA pagination read from the running heads (non-linear, never computed). Triple locus throughout.**

## §1. Where the chapter stands: from formal structure to a determinate attunement

Chapters Two through Four built up the *formal* interpretation of the three forms of boredom, culminating in the third — profound boredom, *es ist einem langweilig* ("it is boring for one"). Chapter Five is the **pivot**: it turns that formal analysis onto a single, determinate Dasein, our own. Heidegger opens §37 by reflecting on the peculiar kind of knowing the interpretation has produced — not accumulated information, the way "a lecture course on zoology or modern history" might leave us with "a further cluster of notes" (p.160 / GA pp.240 / pdf 92), but the opposite: each hour we "make less progress" and approach "a standstill," having "worn through the ground" until we "begin to float," entering "an attunement." What has been won is therefore not a result but a "more transparent receptivity for this attunement that we call boredom" (p.160 / GA pp.240 / pdf 92).

This receptivity reframes the whole undertaking. It is "easy to master boredom by busily passing the time," and "difficult not to be opposed to a profound boredom," difficult "to let oneself be attuned through and through by it" so as "to hear something essential from it" (p.160 / GA pp.240 / pdf 92). The methodological hinge of Part One — that an attunement must be *awakened*, not ascertained — is here sharpened into a demand: profound boredom "only becomes awake if we do not counteract it" (p.160 / GA pp.240 / pdf 92).

## §2. §37 — the demand "not to counteract" and the defense of the question

The demand "not to counteract" immediately "meets with mistrust precisely from us today" (p.161 / GA pp.240–42 / pdf 92). When "everything is pressing for decision," is not the refusal to take counteraction "a call for a paralysing lack of courage, for resignation, for a feeling of doom and despair?" (p.161 / GA pp.240–42 / pdf 92). Heidegger rejects the dichotomy. Not-counteracting means "neither this passivity nor that activity," but "something this side of the two: Dasein's keeping to itself, which is a waiting" (p.161 / GA pp.240–42 / pdf 92). This waiting is "not indeterminate" but "directed out toward an essential questioning of Dasein itself" (p.161 / GA pp.240–42 / pdf 92).

The question so directed is not "where man stands" in world history — that is the province of the "servants of culture who ape their own inventions" — but "how things stand concerning man, concerning the Da-sein in man" (p.161 / GA pp.240–42 / pdf 92). Stated definitively: "Has man in the end become boring to himself?" (p.161 / GA pp.240–42 / pdf 92). Crucially, this question is meant to "provide a hold." Common understanding "never understands … that a question is able to provide a hold," since for it a hold is achieved "only by the answer," which is "a fixed proposition, a dogma, a conviction" (p.161 / GA pp.240–42 / pdf 92). The whole chapter operates in this key: it *questions* rather than asserts, because "essential knowledge is possible only from out of and in an originary questioning" (p.161 / GA pp.240–42 / pdf 92).

## §3. §37 — repeating the question "more transparently"

Having clarified boredom's essence, Heidegger is "in a position to repeat the question more transparently" (p.161 / GA pp.240–42 / pdf 92), because we now see (1) "the structure and structural moments of boredom, being left empty, and being held in limbo and their original unity"; (2) that this unity "springs from that temporality in which the essence of boredom oscillates"; (3) the "various fundamental forms of boredom" (p.161 / GA pp.240–42 / pdf 92). The reconsideration immediately corrects a possible misreading. We cannot ask whether "contemporary people become bored by particular things" or "become more bored than in other epochs" (p.161 / GA pp.240–42 / pdf 92), because that would treat boredom as something occasioned and warded off by "a passing the time." The question "can only mean: Is it ultimately boring for the Dasein in contemporary man as such?" (p.162 / GA pp.242–43 / pdf 93). We are asking about "one profound boredom … i.e., one in particular … one of our Dasein," not "profound boredom in general or universally" (p.162 / GA pp.242–43 / pdf 93). The earlier formal interpretation, which seemed to offer "an objective recipe for boredom," was "an illusion"; we understand profound boredom "only … from out of a particular, i.e., essential boredom" (p.162 / GA pp.242–43 / pdf 93).

## §4. §38a — being-left-empty as the *telling refusal of any essential oppressiveness*

§38 questions "in the direction of the structural moments of being left empty and being held in limbo and their unity" (p.162 / GA pp.242–43 / pdf 93). Subsection (a) takes up **being-left-empty** (*Leergelassenheit*). Profound boredom's being-left-empty "means being delivered over to beings' telling refusal of themselves as a whole" — "an emptiness as a whole" (p.162 / GA pp.242–43 / pdf 93). This emptiness "does not mean a pure Nothing" but "emptiness in the sense of telling refusal, self-withdrawal, thus emptiness as lack, deprivation, need [*Not*]" (p.162 / GA pp.242–43 / pdf 93).

The decisive move is the test of the candidate. Are we affected by such a need? "More than one," we retort: "everywhere there are disruptions, confusion, crises, catastrophes" — "the contemporary social misery, political … the powerlessness of science, the erosion of art, the groundlessness of philosophy, the impotence of religion" (p.162 / GA pp.242–43 / pdf 93). And against these, "renewed attempts and efforts" are "just as intense and clamorous"; "groups, associations, circles, classes, parties" are organized, each "with its program" (p.163 / GA pp.243–44 / pdf 93). But this very reaction is self-undermining as evidence of emptiness: the "bustling self-defense against these needs precisely does not allow any need to emerge as a whole" (p.163 / GA pp.243–44 / pdf 93). Particular needs "are therefore no proof of the emptiness as a whole," because "'as a whole' does not mean merely adding together individual needs and miseries" (p.163 / GA pp.243–44 / pdf 93). "We look in vain for such a need as a whole" (p.163 / GA pp.243–44 / pdf 93); the question seems to "remain without grounds," to be "arbitrary," not sticking "to what is there before us" (p.163 / GA pp.243–44 / pdf 93).

The turn comes by re-pointing the question. Perhaps it "has not yet been pointed in the right direction" (p.163 / GA pp.243–44 / pdf 93). The need in question "cannot possibly have the character of those needs we have enumerated." It is "not the fact that this or that need oppresses [*bedrängt*]" — "rather what oppresses us most profoundly and in a concealed manner … the very absence of any essential oppressiveness [*Bedrängnis*] in our Dasein as a whole" (p.164 / GA pp.244–46 / pdf 94). This is the chapter's keystone diagnosis. The absence of oppressiveness "is the emptiness as a whole, so that no one stands with anyone else … in the rooted unity of essential action"; we are "servants of slogans, adherents to a program," but "none is the custodian of the inner greatness of Dasein" (p.164 / GA pp.244–46 / pdf 94). "The mystery [*Geheimnis*] is lacking in our Dasein," and so the "inner terror that every mystery carries with it" — which "gives Dasein its greatness" — "remains absent" (p.164 / GA pp.244–46 / pdf 94). Thus "the absence of oppressiveness is what fundamentally oppresses … the fundamental emptiness that bores us" (p.164 / GA pp.244–46 / pdf 94). It is not hidden but "attested by the very activities with which we busy ourselves," in which "there is ultimately a universal smug contentment in not being endangered" (p.164 / GA pp.244–46 / pdf 94) — a contentment that makes us "believe that we no longer need to be strong in the ground of our essence," concerning ourselves "only with learned competencies that can be instilled," which can never replace "strength and power" (p.164 / GA pp.244–46 / pdf 94).

## §5. §38b — being-held-in-limbo as the most extreme demand and the moment of vision

Subsection (b) turns to **being-held-in-limbo** (*Hingehaltenheit*), which must be questioned "in order to first attain a complete questioning" (p.164 / GA pp.244–46 / pdf 94). "The deepest, essential need in Dasein" is "not that a particular actual need oppresses us," but "that an essential oppressiveness refuses itself" — and that "we scarcely apprehend … this telling refusal" because "what announces and tells of itself in such telling refusal remains inaudible" (p.164 / GA pp.244–46 / pdf 94). All telling refusal "has its incisiveness only in the fact that what is refused in it is also announced as such in all its harshness" (p.165 / GA pp.246–47 / pdf 94); "whoever makes no demand upon themselves can never know of a refusal," but "sways in a contentment that has what it wants and wants only what it can have" (p.165 / GA pp.246–47 / pdf 94).

So we "are now allowed only to ask: What is simultaneously announced in this absence of oppressiveness?" (p.165 / GA pp.246–47 / pdf 94). The specific being-held-in-limbo of profound boredom was earlier disclosed as "being impelled toward the extremity of that which makes Dasein possible … toward the moment of vision" (p.165 / GA pp.246–47 / pdf 94). Hence the question: which **moment of vision** (*Augenblick*) "must be simultaneously announced as that which properly makes possible in this telling refusal" (p.165 / GA pp.246–47 / pdf 94)? Commensurate with "that emptiness as a whole," "the most extreme demand [*Zumutung*] must be announced to man" — "not some arbitrary demand … but the demand pure and simple," namely "that Dasein as such is demanded of man, that it is given to him — to be there" (p.165 / GA pp.246–47 / pdf 94).

We "know this" yes and no (p.165 / GA pp.246–47 / pdf 94). We have "forgotten that man, if he is to become what he is, in each case has to throw Dasein upon his shoulders"; that Dasein "is not something that one takes for a drive in the car," but "something that man must specifically take upon himself" (p.165 / GA pp.246–47 / pdf 94). Because we suppose we "no longer need … to throw ourselves open to danger," we "have also already slipped out of the danger-zone of Dasein" (p.165 / GA pp.246–47 / pdf 94); the absence of oppressiveness shows "most pointedly" in that today "no one overreaches themselves in their Dasein," but we "at most manage to complain about the misery of life" (p.165 / GA pp.246–47 / pdf 94). Therefore "man must first resolutely open himself again to this demand"; this "disclosive resolution" is "contained in the telling refusal and simultaneous telling announcement of the moment of vision" (p.165 / GA pp.246–47 / pdf 94).

What the moment of vision demands is "that the moment of vision itself be understood … as the innermost necessity [*Notwendigkeit*] of the freedom of Dasein" (p.166 / GA pp.247–48 / pdf 95) — that Dasein "must first of all bring itself into the realm of what is free again," comprehending "itself as Da-sein" (p.166 / GA pp.247–48 / pdf 95). Before itself "not as a fixed ideal or rigidly erected archetype," but as that which "must first precisely wrest its own possibility from itself again" (p.166 / GA pp.247–48 / pdf 95).

## §6. The temporal oscillation and the impossibility of "ascertaining" the attunement

Heidegger then reasserts the deep methodological constraint. If this absence "really oppressed us," there "would have to go together a hunger for the most extreme and primary possibility of this moment of vision" (p.166 / GA pp.247–48 / pdf 95). But "we cannot ever objectively assert or ascertain in itself that this emptiness leaves us empty" and impels us toward the moment of vision; we "cannot ascertain the oscillation between the expanse of this emptiness and the extremity of this moment of vision," cannot "register this profound boredom … as though it were a matter of fact" (p.166 / GA pp.247–48 / pdf 95). We "can only ask" whether it "attunes our Dasein through and through" — whether contemporary man, "in everything … in all its doing and acting," "acts counter to the possibility of that profound boredom arising" (p.166 / GA pp.247–48 / pdf 95); whether he "narrows down that expanse of his concealed and most profound need to those needs for which he immediately finds some self-defense" (p.166 / GA pp.247–48 / pdf 95); whether he "has always already broken off and twisted and blunted that extremity of the most incisive moment of vision," keeping it blunt "through his haste to react, through the abruptness of his programs" — a haste he "confuses with the resolute disclosedness of the moment of vision" (p.166 / GA pp.247–48 / pdf 95). He may even "conceal his Dasein … from himself … in spite of all his psychology and psychoanalysis, indeed precisely through psychology, which today even presents itself as depth psychology" (p.166 / GA pp.247–48 / pdf 95).

To question this fundamental attunement does not "justify and continue the contemporary human traits," but "liberate the humanity in man … to let the Dasein in him become essential" (p.167 / GA pp.248–49 / pdf 95). This liberation is "not placing him in some arbitrary position, but loading Dasein upon man as his ownmost burden": "only those who can truly give themselves a burden are free" (p.167 / GA pp.248–49 / pdf 95). Such questioning is where it can "be decided whether we can summon the courage of mood [*Mut*]" for what the attunement gives us to know — to "help bring to word" what Dasein "wishes to speak about," "the word … which addresses us and summons us to action and to being" (p.167 / GA pp.248–49 / pdf 95).

## §7. The hinge into Part Two — naming *world*

The unit closes by handing the analysis forward. "What we ought to find oppressive … is the absence of any oppressiveness as a whole, the very expanse of this emptiness as a whole" (p.168 / GA pp.249, 252–53 / pdf 96). What does "'as a whole'" mean, and how can Dasein "find itself placed in this way among beings as a whole"? Heidegger answers by christening the term that governs Part Two: "we shall designate the expanse of this 'as a whole', which manifests itself in profound boredom, as **world**" (p.168 / GA pp.249, 252–53 / pdf 96). The moment of vision "is the look of resolute disclosedness of Dasein for its Da-sein," "always singular and unique" (p.168 / GA pp.249, 252–53 / pdf 96), opening the paired questions of world and **individuation** — which §39 will root in "the essence of time." Profound boredom is thus not left behind; it is the soil from which the metaphysical questions grow. This concretely cashes out §18c's first naming of profound boredom as the concealed fundamental attunement of the contemporary situation (cf. p.74 / GA pp.110–11 / pdf 49).

## Boredom-bridge relevance

This unit gives the King–Salvo bridge its **diagnostic register** and its strongest single conceptual asset for the empirical tension. Three contributions stand out, all imported onto the empirical data as `anticipatory-application` (the FCM categories are Heidegger's; the lab findings remain `faithful`):

1. **The emptiness that refuses, masked by surface busyness ↔ the second-form keystone.** Heidegger's central figure here — being-left-empty as "the telling refusal of any essential oppressiveness," concealed by "bustling self-defense" and "smug contentment in not being endangered" (p.164 / GA pp.244–46 / pdf 94) — is the philosophical template for the bridge's keystone contradiction: on the clinical-immersion stimulus, the 2023 EEG reads ≈boring (hollow depth) while the 2024 gaze-variance reads ≈engaging (occupied surface, p = 0.0004). The second form (being-bored-*with*) predicts exactly this surface-occupied / depth-hollow split, and §38a supplies its ontological description: the surface activity is not the opposite of the emptiness but its very disguise.

2. **"Haste to react" / restless self-defense ↔ high-arousal boredom (first-form *Zeitvertreib*).** The contemporary comportment that keeps the moment of vision "blunt" "through his haste to react, through the abruptness of his programs" (p.166 / GA pp.247–48 / pdf 95) maps onto the lab's **high-arousal boredom**, where the bored subject "looks around the screen" producing high gaze-variance — restless passing-the-time. The "zombie stare" / low-arousal pole (low gaze-variance, Subjects 1 & 2), by contrast, answers to profound boredom's *stillness*, in which counteraction has fallen away.

3. **"We can only ask" — boredom cannot be ascertained as a matter of fact ↔ the measurement caution.** Heidegger's insistence that one "cannot register this profound boredom … as though it were a matter of fact" and "can only ask" whether it attunes Dasein through and through (p.166 / GA pp.247–48 / pdf 95) is the philosophical warrant for the bridge's methodological recommendation: a bipolar engaged↔bored scale cannot hold the phenomenon; the next study should instrument the *divergence* of a surface channel (gaze) from a depth channel (DMN/alpha proxy) and add a time-perception (*Hingehaltenheit*) probe, rather than reading off a single state-value. The **moment of vision** (*Augenblick*), announced only in the refusal and never objectively present, names precisely the latent "depth" dimension the gaze instrument cannot see and the future experiment must triangulate.
