# FCM Unit 13 — *Man Is World-forming*: Manifestness, the "As", the Apophantic *Logos*, and Projection

**Work.** Martin Heidegger, *The Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics: World, Finitude, Solitude* (FCM), GA 29/30, trans. William McNeill & Nicholas Walker (Bloomington: Indiana University Press, 1995). Freiburg winter semester 1929–30.

**Coverage.** Part Two, Chapter Six (*The Thematic Exposition of the Problem of World through an Examination of the Thesis That 'Man Is World-forming'*), §§64–76. Book pp. 274–366 / GA pp. [398–532] / pdf 149–195. Loci are triple: `(p.NN / GA pp.NN / pdf NN)`, `pdf = floor((book_page+24)/2)`.

This is the largest of the "map" units and the culmination of Part Two. It moves from the first characterization of world (the manifestness of beings and the "as"), through the explicit *return* of the world-question to the direction disclosed by profound boredom, into a long Aristotle-anchored interpretation of the apophantic *logos*, and finally to projection (*Entwurf*) as the primordial structure of world-formation. Throughout, **world = manifestness = disclosure** — the formulation that ties world-formation back to attunement-as-disclosure.

---

## §1. The primary characteristics of world: manifestness and the "as" (§64)

Heidegger sets down a formula: "the manifestness of beings as such, of beings as beings, belongs to world" `(p.274 / GA pp.397 / pdf 149)`. Bound up with world is the enigmatic "as", "**beings as such, something as something**", a possibility "quite fundamentally closed to the animal." Only where beings are manifest as beings is there the possibility of experiencing this or that being as determined thus, and only there does the relation to beings have the character of attending to whatever is encountered "in the sense of letting it be or not letting it be." This letting-be (and so the possibility of *not*-letting-be) defines *comportment* (*Verhalten*), distinguished from the captivated *behaviour* of the animal; comportment presupposes restraint (*Verhaltenheit*) and a *stance* (*Haltung*), and so selfhood. Three characteristics of world are enumerated: the manifestness of beings as beings; the "as"; the relation to beings as letting-be (comportment, stance, selfhood) — "Nothing of this kind is to be found in animality."

## §2. Undifferentiated manifestness and the slumbering of Dasein's relationships (§65)

In everydayness, beings of fundamentally diverse kinds are nonetheless manifest "in a remarkable undifferentiatedness" `(p.275 / GA pp.398 / pdf 150)`. We "board the tram, talk to other people, call the dog, look up at the stars, all in the same way." This levelled-out presence-at-hand gives everydayness its security and inevitability; Heidegger insists it is "an essential character of beings." Yet philosophy has neglected it, "because this all too self-evident phenomenon is what is most powerful in our Dasein" — and "that which is most powerful is therefore the deadly enemy of philosophy." Within this undifferentiated comportment the *fundamental relationships* (*Grundverhältnisse*) of man toward beings transpire without being awakened; they are not grounded in everydayness but can be awakened out of it. So there are fundamental *kinds* of manifestness, and accordingly kinds of beings as such — a point already sharpened by the interpretation of animality.

## §3. The manifestness of living nature; transposedness (§66)

Living nature is manifest to us neither as a rung on a ladder nor as the "wall" it becomes under scientific-theoretical observation `(p.276 / GA pp.401 / pdf 151)`. Man's ontological relationship toward nature is "a peculiar transposedness into the encompassing contextual ring of living beings." The intersecting encircling rings of captivation (sea-urchin, bee, woodworm, woodpecker, squirrel) "hold us captive in a quite specific way", so that "man exists in a peculiar way in the midst of beings" — living nature holds us captive "from out of our essence." This recalls the Part-Two animality analysis and prepares the contrast with the world-openness of man.

## §4. The occurrence of manifestness — return to profound boredom (§67–§68)

§67 makes the methodological hinge explicit. World is "the accessibility of beings as such rather than beings in themselves" `(p.280 / GA pp.405 / pdf 152)`, and accessibility "is grounded in possible manifestness." Manifestness is never found *in* beings as a property (as hardness belongs to the stone); it "is something that occurs and occurs with beings themselves." This opens the decisive questions: does world first arise in each case, "is man world-forming?"

The return is then declared: the problem of world is to be developed "in that direction which was disclosed to us through **the interpretation of profound boredom as a fundamental attunement** of human Dasein" `(p.281 / GA pp.408 / pdf 152)`. Heidegger restates the doctrine of attunement: attunements are "fundamental ways of Dasein itself, in which one is attuned in such and such a way" — "ways of Dasein in which Dasein becomes manifest to itself." Crucially, "attunement precisely makes beings as a whole manifest and makes us manifest to ourselves as disposed in the midst of these beings." He recalls the essential moments of profound boredom — "being left empty and being held in limbo … being entranced and being drawn into the moment of vision" — as showing "how the utter abyss of Dasein in the midst of Dasein discloses itself in this attunement." §68 then fixes the provisional concept: world is *not* the totality of beings, *not* their accessibility, *not* the manifestness underlying that accessibility — "world is rather the manifestness of beings as such as a whole" `(p.282 / GA pp.410 / pdf 153)`. World-formation is taken deliberately in a threefold sense: the Dasein in man "brings it forth", "gives an image or view of the world … sets it forth", and "constitutes the world, contains and embraces it" `(p.284 / GA pp.411 / pdf 154)`.

## §5. The "as" as a structural moment; the detour through logic (§69–§70)

The "as a whole" stands in connection with the enigmatic "as" `(p.287 / GA pp.415 / pdf 155)`. Formally the "as" is a *relation* (a as b, "a insofar as it is b") and so "belongs to the simple propositional statement." But the statement is "true or false", and to be true is to make manifest — "the statement is true because it contains a manifestness of the matter itself." The "as" therefore finds "its common home" in the statement. This is why the problem of world "finds itself led back to logic" — and why Western metaphysics has treated logic as "the proper basis of metaphysics" `(p.290 / GA pp.420 / pdf 157)`. Heidegger flags this as an ossified self-evidence whose "limited legitimacy" must be exposed.

§70 inserts a methodological warning against two misinterpretations: taking metaphysical concepts (1) as something *present-at-hand* and (2) as *isolated* from one another. Against both, all philosophical concepts are **formally indicative**: "the meaning-content of these concepts does not directly intend or express what they refer to, but only gives an indication … that anyone who seeks to understand is called upon … to undertake a transformation of themselves into their Dasein" `(p.297 / GA pp.430 / pdf 160)`. The worked example is being-toward-death from *Being and Time* §46: the absurd inference that one must "constantly be thinking about death" mistakes resolute disclosedness (*Entschlossenheit*) — which "is not some present-at-hand condition that I possess, but … something which possesses me" and "is what it is … only and always as the moment of vision [*Augenblick*]" `(p.295 / GA pp.427 / pdf 159)` — for a state. World, "here perhaps more strongly than anywhere else", tempts us to seek "something that is present at hand."

## §6. The apophantic *logos* in Aristotle (§71–§72)

§71 takes the simple propositional statement ("a is b") as the point of departure precisely because it "has decisively determined the doctrine of discourse in general, of the *logos* (logic)" — even though it is "not an originary one." The aim is "to penetrate to the former through this structure", letting the statement "dissolve into a much more far-reaching dimension" `(p.303 / GA pp.439 / pdf 163)`.

§72 is the unit's Aristotelian core, read chiefly from *De Interpretatione*. *Logos* "means discourse, everything that is spoken and sayable"; the Greeks define man as *zōon logon echon*, the animal lacking discourse as *zōon alogon* `(p.305 / GA pp.442 / pdf 164)`. Every *logos* is *semantikos* — "**giving something to be understood**", forming "a sphere of understandability." But this giving is "not *physei*" (not a natural event like digestion) — it is *kata syntheken*, by agreement, which Aristotle glosses as "whenever a *symbolon* occurs." Heidegger refuses the modern "symbol": *symbolon* means "joint, seam, or hinge" — the two halves of a guest-friends' ring that "fit one another" `(p.309 / GA pp.447 / pdf 166)`. What Aristotle "sees quite obscurely under the title *symbolon* … is nothing other than what we today call transcendence. There is language [*Es gibt Sprache*] only in the case of a being that by its essence transcends."

Not every *logos* is apophantic: requesting (*euchē*) gives something to be understood without pointing out; "the examination of these *logoi* belongs to rhetoric and poetics" `(p.310 / GA pp.448 / pdf 167)`. The propositional *logos* is the *logos apophantikos*, distinguished because *aletheuein* or *pseudesthai* (revealing or being-deceptive) "lies in it as underlying it." Heidegger stresses the *either/or*: the inner essence is the *possibility* of one or the other. And "even concealing is a pointing out", grounded in "a tendency to point out."

The decisive ground comes from *De Anima* III.6: the possibility of true/false rests on a *synthesis noematon hosper hen onton*, "an assembling … of whatever is apprehended … in such a way that whatever is apprehended forms a unity" `(p.314 / GA pp.454 / pdf 169)`. At the basis of the *logos* lies *noēsis*, *nous* — "**a taking together that takes apart**": "*synthesis noematon* is inherently, in itself, also already *diairesis*" `(p.316 / GA pp.457 / pdf 170)`. This is exactly "what we call the 'as'-structure." And the reversal: the "as" is "not some property of the *logos*, stuck on or grafted onto it, but the reverse" — "the 'as'-structure for its part is in general the condition of the possibility of this *logos*" `(p.317 / GA pp.458 / pdf 170)`. The *syn* ("together", "whole") shows an essential connection to the "as a whole."

Apophansis (the two forms *kataphasis* — pointing toward — and *apophasis* — pointing away) is "**letting beings be seen as beings, as what and how they are**", and not limited to the now but covering past and future `(p.320 / GA pp.463 / pdf 172)`. Aristotle's analysis of *onoma* (name) and *rhēma* (verb, *Zeitwort*) turns on time: the verb "co-signifies time" (*prossemainein chronon*). And the "is" — the copula — "co-signifies a *synthesis*" and "is not a real predicate" (so too Kant, A592ff./B620ff.) `(p.328 / GA pp.475 / pdf 176)`. Its undifferentiated manifold of *what-being*, *that-being*, and *being-true* is "the originary and primary essence of the copula"; particular meanings arise "via limitation." Hence: "metaphysics is decided by our position with respect to the problem of the copula" `(p.331 / GA pp.479 / pdf 177)` — and faces "the massive task of unhinging this tradition."

## §7. The ground of assertion: pre-predicative manifestness and the tripartite occurrence (§73–§74)

§73 inquires back into the ground of the whole structure of assertion. The result is a demotion of the proposition: "the assertion is neither originary nor independent." A true assertion such as "The board is black" does not first make the board manifest; "the blackboard must already have become manifest to us as such a being in order for us to assert something about it." The "*logos apophantikos* merely takes apart [*legt … auseinander*], in the assertion, what is already manifest" `(p.341 / GA pp.494 / pdf 183)`. Therefore "propositional truth is the fundamental form of truth" is denied: the *logos* points to "a **pre-predicative manifestness**", a "**pre-logical truth**", that grounds its being-true-or-false "in preceding it."

The *logos* is an *ability* (*Vermögen*) — "having a relation to beings as such at one's disposal" — possible "only where there is freedom" `(p.340 / GA pp.493 / pdf 182)`. Being free for beings as such grounds the binding character of things, and so the *either/or* of truth and falsity: the *logos* "does not first produce a relation toward beings as such, but … is grounded in such a relation." Man's being-open is "a being held toward …", over against the animal's "being taken by …" `(p.343 / GA pp.498 / pdf 183)`.

From this Heidegger extracts the three moments of "the originary dimension of the assertion", a "fundamental occurrence in the Dasein of man": **[1] holding the binding character of things toward us; [2] completion; [3] unveiling the being of beings** `(p.350 / GA pp.508 / pdf 187)`. "Completion" (*Vollzug*) is "**the prior forming of the 'as a whole'**" — not adding a missing piece but, as in craftsmanship, "seeing the whole and forming it in advance." This is illustrated by "The board is badly positioned": in this apparently isolated judgement "we always already speak out of beings that are manifest as a whole" — the lecture theatre — so that "ordinary understanding cannot see the world for beings" `(p.347 / GA pp.504 / pdf 186)`. §74 then names world-formation "the fundamental occurrence in Dasein" and the essence of world "the **prevailing of world**" (*Walten der Welt*), "more originary than all those beings that press themselves upon us" — a prevailing that "can only ever be prepared, never effected" `(p.351 / GA pp.510 / pdf 188)`.

## §8. The "as a whole" as world; the ontological difference (§75)

The "as a whole" is now identified with world: it "admits precisely the manifestness of manifold beings in the various contexts of their being" and "we ourselves are comprehensively included in this 'as a whole'", in ways such as "being left empty, being held in limbo, being fulfilled or being sustained" — the very moments of the boredom analysis `(p.354 / GA pp.512 / pdf 188)`. From this the enigmatic distinction between *being* and *beings* emerges. Ordinary understanding "fails to hear the being of beings and is able to acquaint itself only with beings." Heidegger sets out the distinction in nine points: above all, "**it is not we who make it, rather it happens to us as the fundamental occurrence of our Dasein**", it "must already have occurred if we wish to experience beings in their being such and such", and so "stands at the commencement of Dasein itself" `(p.357 / GA pp.520 / pdf 190)`. Named thematically, this is "the **ontological difference**" — yet Heidegger warns that even ontology (and transcendental philosophy) "must fall", since the radicalization of that idea was only "a necessary stage."

## §9. Projection (*Entwurf*) as the primordial structure of world-formation (§76)

The culmination. The tripartite occurrence is grasped in its primordial structure as **projection** (*Entwurf*). Taken first from everyday usage (planning, "anticipatory regulating of human comportment"), the word is given terminological status for "that occurrence which fundamentally makes possible all familiar projection." Projection "does not refer to some sequence of actions … rather … the unity of an action, but of an originary and properly unique kind of action" `(p.363 / GA pp.527 / pdf 194)`. The prefix *Ent-* names how projecting "carries whoever is projecting out and away from themselves" — yet this removal is "a peculiar turning toward themselves", because it has "the character of raising away into the possible … into the possible in its possibly being made possible." Projection "binds us not to what is possible, nor to what is actual, but to making-possible."

So projection unifies the three moments: it "lets the binding character of things spring forth", it is "intrinsically completing in the sense of a casting ahead that is the forming of an 'as a whole'", and it is "the irruption into this 'between' of the distinction" between being and beings, in which "the 'as' springs forth" `(p.364 / GA pp.528 / pdf 194)`. The decisive thesis: "**Projection is world-projection. World prevails in and for a letting-prevail that has the character of projecting**" `(p.363 / GA pp.527 / pdf 194)`. And §76 confirms the Aristotelian reading: projection "intrinsically unites contradictory things: binding together and separating" — it "takes apart as it were (*diairesis*) … yet … precisely in such a way that … there occurs an intrinsic turning toward … such that that which has been projected is that which binds and binds together (*synthesis*)" `(p.365 / GA pp.530 / pdf 195)`. In projection "world is formed": man "irrupts as an actual being in the midst of what can now be manifest as beings", as *Da-sein*, as "ex-sists." Man "is that inability to remain and is yet unable to leave his place"; "thrown in this throw, man is a transition … Man is history, or better, history is man" `(p.365 / GA pp.531 / pdf 195)`. Only because man is thus "essentially absent" and "mistaken concerning what is actual" can he "become seized by terror" — and only there is "the bliss of astonishment", *enthousiasmos*, "the breath of all philosophizing", witnessed by Nietzsche in Zarathustra's "intoxicated song" ("The world is deep, / Deeper than day can comprehend"), "in which we also experience what the world is" `(p.366 / GA pp.531 / pdf 195)`.

## Bridge relevance — world as manifestness/disclosure

This unit supplies the deepest tie-back from the King–Salvo bridge to attunement-as-disclosure. §67 makes the move explicit: the world-question is unfolded "in that direction which was disclosed … through the interpretation of profound boredom", and §68 grounds it — "attunement precisely makes beings as a whole manifest and makes us manifest to ourselves." The entire apparatus that follows (the "as", the apophantic *logos*, the ontological difference, and finally projection) is therefore a single elaboration of **world = manifestness = disclosure**, continuous with Part One's disclosive attunement. Two consequences carry the bridge. First, §73's demotion of the proposition — "the assertion … merely takes apart what is already manifest", so "propositional truth is [not] the fundamental form of truth" — licenses treating articulated, measurable report as *founded* on a prior, pre-predicative manifestness; this is precisely the surface/depth split the pipeline operationalizes when it triangulates an attunement (EEG/gaze/phenomenology) rather than trusting a self-report. Second, §76's projection, as the "letting-prevail" in which "world prevails", names the disclosive opening that a boring stimulus either widens or forecloses; boredom matters to the lab not as a feeling-state but as a modulation of the manifestness "as a whole" within which any particular engaged-or-disengaged behavior first shows up as such.
