## A. Primary figures & interlocutors cited within FCM

### Ancient & classical

**Aristotle.** The pervasive ancient interlocutor. Cited as the objection to the poet-witness (*Metaphysica* A 2, 983a3f., "πολλὰ ψεύδονται ἀοιδοί," fcm-00); as the authority on sleep as *akinēsia* / *desmos* (*De Somno*, fcm-03); as the source of the *physis/logos/aletheia* complex and the dual orientation of *prōtē philosophia* (*Metaphysics*, fcm-02); as the warrant for the transference/*metaphora* schema (*Poetics*, fcm-04); as melancholy-of-exceptional-men (*Problemata* XXX.1, 953a10ff., Bekker, fcm-09); and as the keystone of the apophantic-*logos* analysis (*De Interpretatione*; *De Anima* III.6 430a–b; *Metaphysics* E.4 1027b, fcm-13). — (p.5 / GA pp.6–8 / pdf 14) — fcm-00, fcm-01, fcm-02, fcm-03, fcm-04, fcm-09, fcm-13

**Heraclitus.** Pre-Socratic authority for the *physis/logos/aletheia* complex: Frgm. 93 (the Oracle gives a sign), 123 (*physis* loves to hide), 112 (*sophia* = saying/doing the unconcealed *kata physin*), 54, 30; also DK 108 (*to sophon* "separated off"). — (p.27 / GA pp.41 / pdf 26) — fcm-01, fcm-02

**Plato.** Cited for the awake/asleep (*hypar/onar*) contrast (*Republic*, fcm-01); as the site where school-formation begins (the Academy) and *logos tinos* (assertion-about); reckoned among the "provisional and extrinsic" theories Aristotle's radicalization surpassed (fcm-02, fcm-13). The SS-1929 sibling course treated the cave allegory (*Republic* Bk VII, fcm-14). — (p.16 / GA pp.23–24 / pdf 20) — fcm-01, fcm-02, fcm-13

**Xenocrates.** Director of the Academy credited (via Sextus Empiricus) with first founding the threefold division of philosophy into logic / physics / ethics — the scholastic codification on which "decline" depends. — (p.35 / GA pp.52–54 / pdf 29) — fcm-02

**Sextus Empiricus.** Documentary source (*Adversus Mathematicos* VII.16, ed. Bekker 1842) for the claim that the logic/physics/ethics division was prefigured in Plato and introduced by Xenocrates, the Peripatetics, and the Stoics. — (p.36 / GA pp.54–55 / pdf 30) — fcm-02

### Medieval & early-modern scholastic

**Thomas Aquinas.** §13 keystone: equates *prima philosophia* = *metaphysica* = *theologia/scientia divina* via the threefold *maxime intelligibile* (Prooemium to his commentary on Aristotle's *Metaphysics*); *metaphysica = de ente* (ontology), *theologia = de Deo*; binds metaphysics to God by faith. — (p.46 / GA pp.69–74 / pdf 35) — fcm-02

**Franz Suárez.** §14: the Spanish Jesuit (1548–1617; *Disputationes metaphysicae*, 1597) who first develops the metaphysical problem independently and systematically, decisively shaping modern metaphysics and Descartes; placed by Heidegger "even above Aquinas" in acumen. — (p.51 / GA pp.77–78 / pdf 37) — fcm-02

**René Descartes.** Marker of the beginning of modernity (fcm-00); inherits the Suárezian metaphysics and inaugurates the demand for absolute certainty (*Meditationes de prima philosophia*, fcm-02); the conception of man as consciousness "in the Cartesian sense" is contested as the chief obstacle to original time (fcm-06); cited in the §49 subject/consciousness excursus (fcm-11). — (p.2 / GA pp.2–3 / pdf 13) — fcm-00, fcm-01, fcm-02, fcm-06, fcm-11

**Leibniz.** Named among the modern metaphysicians (fcm-01); the Leibniz sentence ascribed on German p.24 could not be located by the editor (fcm-14). — (p.16 / GA pp.23–24 / pdf 20) — fcm-01

### Modern (German Idealism & 19th-c.)

**Immanuel Kant.** First (after the Christian/modern takeover) to "get a grip" and make metaphysics itself a problem (cf. Heidegger's *Kant and the Problem of Metaphysics*, fcm-02); invoked for the "dialectical illusion" and for the "is" as not a real predicate / a positing (1763 essay; *Critique of Pure Reason* A592ff./B620ff., fcm-13); cited in the subject/consciousness excursus (fcm-11). — (p.45 / GA pp.68–69 / pdf 34) — fcm-02, fcm-11, fcm-13

**G. W. F. Hegel.** Named among the modern metaphysicians (fcm-01); "the last great metaphysician in Western metaphysics," for whom metaphysics coincides with logic (*Science of Logic*) — the foil for §74 (fcm-13); cited in the absolute-idealism excursus (fcm-11). — (p.23 / GA pp.34–35 / pdf 23) — fcm-01, fcm-11, fcm-13

**J. G. Fichte.** Modern exemplar: the *Wissenschaftslehre* names the science that has absolute knowledge as its object and thereby grounds metaphysics; epitomizes the certainty-priority of modern metaphysics. — (p.55 / GA pp.83–84 / pdf 39) — fcm-02

**F. W. J. Schelling.** Borrowed-from in §76: projection as "the look into the light of a possible making-possible" (after the *Philosophische Untersuchungen über das Wesen der menschlichen Freiheit*, 1809). — (p.366 / GA pp.531 / pdf 195) — fcm-13

**Thomas Hobbes.** Cited (§72; *De corpore*, *Logica*) for a theory of the copula concentrating the meaning of subject and predicate on one and the same thing (*quid/quidditas*). — (p.329 / GA pp.477 / pdf 176) — fcm-13

**Lotze, Windelband, Rickert, Lask.** Named (§72) as developing the theory of judgement toward validity/being-valid (in Rickert, toward a value-philosophy); judged one-sided for missing the undifferentiated manifold of the "is." — (p.330 / GA pp.480 / pdf 177) — fcm-13

### The lead-witness & the philosophy of culture

**Novalis (Friedrich von Hardenberg).** Primary lead-witness: the fragment "Philosophy is really homesickness, an urge to be at home everywhere" (*Schriften*, ed. Minor, 1923, vol. 2, p.179, Frgm. 21) supplies the word from which Heidegger derives *Heimweh* and the world/finitude/individuation triad. Conceded "merely a poet," then vindicated; recalled at fcm-04 (boredom as homesickness) and fcm-09. — (p.5 / GA pp.6–8 / pdf 14) — fcm-00, fcm-01, fcm-04, fcm-09

**Friedrich Nietzsche.** The common source of the four cultural diagnoses (the Dionysian/Apollonian opposition, from *The Will to Power*; "Dionysos versus the Crucified," fcm-03); invoked at the close of the world-formation analysis via *Zarathustra*'s "intoxicated song" ("The world is deep…") and the *enthousiasmos* of astonishment (fcm-13); cited in the Appendix ("One repays a teacher badly…"; *Will to Power* No. 466, fcm-14). — (p.72 / GA pp.107–110 / pdf 48) — fcm-03, fcm-13, fcm-14

**Oswald Spengler.** First cultural diagnosis: the decline of life in and through spirit (*ratio*); *Der Untergang des Abendlandes*. — (p.69 / GA pp.104–106 / pdf 46) — fcm-03

**Ludwig Klages.** Second diagnosis: spirit as the adversary of the soul, a sickness to be exorcised so life may be liberated; *Der Geist als Widersacher der Seele*. — (p.70 / GA pp.104–106 / pdf 47) — fcm-03

**Max Scheler.** Third cultural diagnosis (a balance of life and spirit, *Der Mensch im Weltalter des Ausgleichs*, fcm-03); also the anthropological foil whose hierarchical sequence material-being / life / spirit Heidegger judges a fundamental error barring access to metaphysics, while granting his question essential (fcm-10). — (p.71 / GA pp.106–107 / pdf 47) — fcm-03, fcm-10

**Leopold Ziegler.** Fourth (least original) diagnosis: a mediating epoch, a "new Middle Ages" sublating the opposition of life and spirit; *Der europäische Geist*. — (p.71 / GA pp.106–107 / pdf 47) — fcm-03

**Søren Kierkegaard.** Credited with the first philosophical comprehension of the moment of vision (*Augenblick*), opening the possibility of a new epoch of philosophy. — (p.150 / GA p.225 / pdf 87) — fcm-06

### Biologists & philosophers of life (Part Two)

**Jakob Johann von Uexküll.** Source of the *Umwelt* ("environmental world") talk Heidegger's thesis appears to contradict and critically appropriates (*Umwelt und Innenwelt der Tiere*, 2nd ed. 1921; *Theoretische Biologie*, 1928); the "second decisive step" in biology — *Umwelt* = the disinhibiting ring; "one of the most fruitful things philosophy can learn from contemporary biology." — (p.192 / GA pp.284 / pdf 108) — fcm-10, fcm-11, fcm-12

**Hans Driesch.** The "first decisive step" in biology: sea-urchin embryology (*Die Lokalisation morphogenetischer Vorgänge*, 1899) establishes wholeness (*Ganzheit*); but his neovitalism (entelechy) is repudiated — "vitalism is just as dangerous as mechanism." — (p.261 / GA pp.380 / pdf 143) — fcm-12

**Wilhelm Roux.** Contested biologist: defined the organism as "a complex of instruments" / "a complicated instrument" (*Entwickelungsmechanik*); the definition Heidegger says must collapse. — (p.212 / GA pp.310 / pdf 118) — fcm-11

**Hans Spemann.** Named exemplar of a researcher "of stature" (the leadership/fate-of-science argument, fcm-10); his "equally outstanding" investigations reset Driesch's developmental result on a new basis (fcm-12). — (p.190 / GA pp.281 / pdf 107) — fcm-10, fcm-12

**Johannes Müller.** Physiologist (*Handbuch der Physiologie des Menschen*, 1844); identified irritability (*Reizbarkeit*) as the mark of "living substance"; used by Heidegger to argue stimulation is grounded in prior disinhibition. — (p.255 / GA pp.372 / pdf 140) — fcm-12

**Emanuel Rádl.** *Untersuchungen über den Phototropismus der Tiere* (1905); explained Bethe's bee-experiments; cited for freshwater-crab phototropism (light-seeking ≠ grasping the light as such). — (p.245 / GA pp.356–58 / pdf 134) — fcm-12

**F. J. J. Buytendijk.** Dutch biologist; contested for the claim that the animal's bond to environment is "almost as intimate as the unity of the body itself" — for Heidegger the body's unity is grounded in the unity of captivation. — (p.258 / GA pp.376 / pdf 141) — fcm-12

**Karl Ernst von Baer.** Embryologist "of the grand style" who saw something essential in the early 19th c., buried by Darwinism; the figure *Being and Time* §12 names as the biological source of "*Umwelt*." — (p.260 / GA pp.378 / pdf 142) — fcm-12

**Theodor Boveri.** *Die Organismen als historische Wesen* (1906); cited as having attempted to talk about organisms as historical beings — raising the question what "history" the species/individual animal possesses. — (p.266 / GA pp.386 / pdf 145) — fcm-12

**Charles Darwin / Darwinism.** Darwin's moth-and-candle question is invoked re phototropism; Darwinism's externalized concept of "adaptation" and self-preservation (an "economic perspective upon man") is repeatedly criticized as misleading. — (p.251 / GA pp.366 / pdf 138) — fcm-12

**"Vitalism / teleology / anti-mechanism" (contemporary biology, generic).** The slogans under which biology resists physico-chemical reduction; Heidegger endorses the negative tendency but warns it is as burdened with misunderstanding as mechanism. — (p.188 / GA pp.278 / pdf 106) — fcm-10, fcm-12

**Edmund Husserl.** Cited (§56) as the one through whom the kind of questioning that "adopts a position before all argumentation … was brought back to life — and that always means radicalized"; named as the decisive characteristic of phenomenology. — (p.232 / GA pp.338 / pdf 127) — fcm-11

**"Idealist philosophy" (generic).** Holder of the "old truth" that properties are representations transferred by subjects onto objects — the position whose self-evidence Heidegger suspends in the transference-schema critique. — (p.85 / GA pp.127–29 / pdf 53) — fcm-04

### Scriptural / literary (cited at the margins)

**St. Paul / Apocrypha (4 Ezra).** Romans 8:19 (*apokaradokia tēs ktiseōs*, the "yearning expectation of creatures"); 4 Ezra IV.7,12; cited at the close of the animal chapter to gesture toward suffering/yearning in creation, while insisting biology knows nothing of it. — (p.273 / GA pp.396 / pdf 148) — fcm-12

**Paul Valéry / Paul Celan.** Valéry's "The Young Parca," translated by Celan, is Heidegger's birthday gift to Fink — marking the poetising-and-thinking motif. — (p.369 / GA pp.~535 / pdf 196) — fcm-14

### Editorial apparatus & dedicatee (end-matter, fcm-14)

**Eugen Fink.** Dedicatee of the Appendix and of the whole volume; Husserl student who "entered another, and yet the same school of phenomenology"; d. 25 June 1975, prompting the posthumous dedication. — (p.367 / GA pp.~533 / pdf 195) — fcm-14

**Friedrich-Wilhelm von Herrmann.** Volume editor; author of the Editor's Epilogue (Dec 1982) and the Postscript to the Second Edition (March 1992); records that Heidegger "emphasized the detailed analysis of boredom" and tied it to "What Is Metaphysics?" (24 July 1929). — (p.373 / GA pp.~539 / pdf 198) — fcm-14

**Walter Brocker & Heinrich Wiegand Petzet.** Course attendees whose reports establish the "Individuation" vs "Solitude" subtitle discrepancy. — (p.370 / GA pp.~536 / pdf 197) — fcm-14

**Simon Moser.** Former student whose bound shorthand transcript became Heidegger's annotated "personal record" used by the editor. — (p.371 / GA pp.~537 / pdf 197) — fcm-14

**Ute Guzzoni.** Prepared the early-1960s typescript of the manuscript (with a DFG grant Heidegger applied for). — (p.371 / GA pp.~537 / pdf 197) — fcm-14

**Herbert Marcuse & Alois Siggemann.** Authors of the two SS-1929 transcripts surfacing after publication, forcing the Postscript's retraction. — (p.374 / GA pp.~540 / pdf 199) — fcm-14

**Hermann Heidegger.** Administrator of Heidegger's estate; consulted on the un-held Vol. 29 course. — (p.371 / GA pp.~537 / pdf 197) — fcm-14

### Bridge sources — the King–Salvo VR-clinical-immersion boredom lineage

*(Titles, authors, venues, paper IDs, and DOIs transcribed verbatim from the three bridge-source digests under* `…/bridge-sources/`*.)*

**[Physiological pilot — ASEE 2023, #37129]** King, C. E., K. R. Feeney, Q. Tang, M. Das, and D. Salvo. "Work in Progress: Physiological Assessment of Learning in a Virtual Reality Clinical Immersion Environment." *2023 ASEE Annual Conference & Exposition*, Baltimore, MD, June 25, 2023. Paper ID #37129. — *Bridge role:* the depth-channel (EEG/DMN) evidence and the time-drag self-reports. Within-subjects pilot, N = 3; three 17-min videos (boring / exciting / clinical 360° surgery); boredom operationalized via Fahlman; DMN (alpha 8–12 Hz / theta 4–8 Hz) as boredom marker. Key data: all three felt the clinical video longer than 17 min (*Hingehaltenheit*); clinical EEG resembled the *boring* control (N=1-clean, suggestive only).

**[Eye-tracking — ASEE 2024, #44685]** King, C. E., M. Lo, M. Das, and D. Salvo. "Assessment of Student Engagement in Virtual Reality Clinical Immersion Environments through Eye Tracking." *2024 ASEE Annual Conference & Exposition.* Paper ID #44685. UCI IRB Exempt No. 2023-2678. 11 pp. — *Bridge role:* the surface-channel (gaze) evidence and the high/low-arousal boredom distinction. N = 12 (+4 webcam); gaze-variance clinical-vs-boring **p = 0.0004** (clinical resembles the *engaging* video on this feature). The "windfall": high-arousal boredom (looking around the screen → high gaze-variance) vs low-arousal boredom ("zombie stare," low gaze-variance, Subjects 1 & 2). Carries the keystone cross-instrument contradiction (2023 EEG → clinical ≈ boring; 2024 gaze → clinical ≈ engaging).

**[Phenomenological evaluation — *Biomedical Engineering Education* 2024]** King, Christine E., and Dalton Salvo. "Phenomenological Evaluation of an Undergraduate Clinical Needs Finding Skills Through a Virtual Reality Clinical Immersion Platform." *Biomedical Engineering Education* (2024) 4:381–397. **DOI 10.1007/s43683-024-00139-5.** Received 30 Aug 2023 / accepted 22 Jan 2024 / published online 5 Mar 2024. Innovation Article, Open Access (CC BY 4.0). 17 pp. — *Bridge role:* the lived-experience / qualitative input (surveys + interviews under an explicitly phenomenological method; N = 22 cohort, N = 20 survey respondents, BME 179, UCI, spring 2022). Does *not* operationalize boredom or probe time-perception; supplies presence/immersion framing and second-form (surface-vs-depth) texture — notably "more present, less beneficial" and the "expert disenchantment" / novelty-decay result.
