# Bridge Source Digest — Phenomenological Evaluation (King & Salvo, *Biomedical Engineering Education* 2024)

**Citation:** Christine E. King, Dalton Salvo. "Phenomenological Evaluation of an Undergraduate Clinical Needs Finding Skills Through a Virtual Reality Clinical Immersion Platform." *Biomedical Engineering Education* (2024) 4:381–397. DOI 10.1007/s43683-024-00139-5. Received 30 Aug 2023 / accepted 22 Jan 2024 / published online 5 Mar 2024. Innovation Article, Open Access (CC BY 4.0). 17 pp. (Salvo, Dept. of English, UCI, is co-author; per the Author Contributions he "conceived of and conducted the surveys and interviews, collected and analyzed the final data.")

**Role in bridge:** the **lived-experience / qualitative** input — the one paper of the three built on first-person self-report (surveys + interviews) under an explicitly named phenomenological method, rather than physiological signals (EEG/HR) or gaze. *Caveat to keep honest:* unlike the 2023 physiological pilot and 2024 eye-tracking study, this paper does **not** operationalize boredom, does not use a deliberately-boring stimulus, and contains **no time-perception probe**. Its negative-affect channel is **frustration / discomfort / "hassle"-driven disengagement** with the VR artifact, plus a counterintuitive "expert disenchantment" result. So its boredom relevance is **indirect and inferential**, and the bridge should lean on it for the *phenomenological framing of presence/immersion* and for **second-form (surface-vs-depth) and first-form (restless avoidance)** texture — not for time-drag, which the sibling papers carry.

## Design
- **Pilot elective course**, BME 179 (UCI, spring 2022), upper-division (mostly juniors/seniors). Course materials reached ~150 students; the study cohort was **N = 22** piloting it as an elective; the post-course survey/figures report **N = 20 respondents**.
- **Stimuli/conditions = four formats of the same operating-room footage** (180° stereoscopic OR capture + physician POV head-cam, five operations: DBS, TAVR, spinal deformation, cataract, corneal transplant): (1) **traditional 2D video** (YouTube), (2) **2D PC** interactive build (Unreal Engine), (3) **VR: Google Cardboard** (phone + YouTube, video-only — no game-like environment), (4) **VR: Meta Quest 2** (full interactive "rooms" environment). Quest 2 headsets were loanable from the library.
- What students did: reverse-engineered a medical device, took virtual "tours" of surgical suites, watched physician/engineer/nurse interviews, kept an "innovation notebook," did gap analyses, and interviewed stakeholders — then rated formats. IRB Exempt Nos. 904 (course assessment), 15531 / 1184 (filming).

## Method
Explicitly **phenomenological** — the authors call it "a phenomenological approach, a frequent choice among VR researchers." Mixed instruments: **pre-course survey** (demographics + prior VR/video-game familiarity + expectations), **post-course questionnaire** (most/least beneficial format; did VR elicit greater presence/immersion/embodiment), and **personal interviews** with survey volunteers seeking their "real-time experiences in the virtual realm," "feelings of presence, embodiment, and their emotional responses." The method's own framing in short anchor-phrases: "intensely subjective experiential phenomena"; phenomenology asks how "body … environments, and the objects … play a role in shaping conscious thought"; the aim is to "focus on the engagements between users and the … virtual environment." Note their **stronger phenomenological move**: VR is read as "first works through sensory deprivation" then "supplanting" objective sense-data with the virtual — a deliberate "narrowing of the students' focus." Analysis is descriptive/thematic from survey percentages + interview quotes; **no formal coding scheme or thematic-analysis citation** is given (so "phenomenological" here is methodological orientation + survey/interview, not a Giorgi/IPA protocol).

## Findings (authors' own — faithful)
- **[faithful] Presence up:** **70%** said the VR version elicited a greater felt sense of presence (10% equal, 20% no increase). Abstract: VR "did greatly increase immersion … presence and embodiment."
- **[faithful] But "most beneficial" went to 2D:** **40%** chose traditional 2D as most educationally beneficial; only 40% combined across the two VR formats (20% Cardboard, 20% Quest); PC 10%. **Worst** = Google Cardboard (40%).
- **[faithful, central tension] Presence ≠ preference.** Students felt more present in VR yet judged 2D more useful. The two reasons given for preferring 2D: **convenience/ease-of-use (44.5%)** and **physical discomfort/disorientation (38.9%)**; "having to go to the library … is 'a hassle.'" Of the Quest 2 headsets in the library, **"not a single student went there to check one out."**
- **[faithful, counterintuitive] Expert disenchantment:** students with **more** prior VR/video-game experience were **more** likely to pick 2D as most beneficial — attributed to the rudimentary build failing "production standards of contemporary VR." Authors flag the **novelty effect** ("novelty factor will degrade over time") as a partial but not primary cause; tagged by them as "mostly our own speculation."
- **[faithful] Among the VR-preferring minority:** 66.6% cited the embodied first-person presence via the HMD; 26.7% cited **focus from removal of distractions** (HMD cuts "computer and phone notifications, background sounds … other people").
- **[faithful] Honest limit:** they "cannot conclusively claim" VR is more/less pedagogically efficacious than 2D; open-access deployment let students default to the most convenient format, so conditions were not evaluated "on equal grounds." Real-time collaboration absent.
- **No lived-time / boredom / "drag" data.** The words boredom and time-perception do not figure; engagement is discussed only as a positive ("increased levels of engagement," from a cited 3D-vs-2D study). The negative pole is frustration/discomfort, not boredom.

## Bridge hooks (→ `fcm-king-salvo-bridge.md`)
All mappings below are `provenance: anticipatory-application` (Heidegger's categories imported onto the authors' data); the authors' findings as stated above are `faithful`.

1. **Presence-high-yet-judged-empty ("more present, less beneficial") ↔ second form (being-bored-*with*).** `provenance: anticipatory-application`. The strongest hook: the student is demonstrably *taken in* by the surface (felt presence ↑) yet the situation is reported as hollow/not-worthwhile — the second form's "occupied surface, hollow depth," now corroborated *qualitatively* where the sibling papers show it *physiologically* (2023 EEG≈boring) and *behaviorally* (2024 gaze≈engaging). This is the qualitative third leg of the three-instrument second-form case.
2. **"A hassle" → defaulting to the convenient format / never checking out the headset ↔ first form (being-bored-*by*) + *Zeitvertreib* (passing-the-time).** `provenance: anticipatory-application`. The restless slide to the path of least resistance, the refusal to *dwell* with the demanding artifact, reads as the first form's avoidance and its time-killing comportment — choosing the channel that lets one *not be held*.
3. **Physical discomfort / disorientation as a being-left-empty trigger ↔ structural moment "being-left-empty" (*Leergelassenheit*).** `provenance: anticipatory-application`. The VR refuses to "offer" itself (weight, nausea, no note-taking, no playback control); the environment withholds, leaving the user empty — Heidegger's first structural moment, here surfacing as embodied friction rather than as tedium.
4. **Distraction-removal / "forced narrowing of focus" via sensory deprivation ↔ the limbo of being-held (*Hingehaltenheit*), read against the grain.** `provenance: anticipatory-application` (speculative). The authors frame HMD sensory deprivation positively (better focus); Heidegger would note the same narrowing can tip into being *held in limbo* — captivated-yet-suspended — which is precisely the ambiguity the second form names. Flag as the weakest hook: no time data here to confirm the "long while."
5. **Expert disenchantment / novelty-decay ↔ second-form onset over exposure.** `provenance: anticipatory-application`. As fascination wears off (the experienced gamer is no longer captivated by the medium), the surface-engagement that masks emptiness erodes and the hollowness shows — a temporal pathway *into* second-form boredom. Useful as a mechanism the bridge can pair with the 2024 paper's arousal split, but **note** the authors offer novelty-decay as speculation and never measure it longitudinally.
6. **"What this adds vs. the physio papers":** this is the **only voice-of-the-subject source** — quote-level evidence of *how the emptiness is described*. The bridge should mine its interview quotes for the *phenomenal description* that the EEG/gaze data can only point at, while **not** importing time-drag or a boredom-operationalization it does not contain.
