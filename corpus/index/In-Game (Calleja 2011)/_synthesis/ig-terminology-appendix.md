## Discussion: the terminology profile

### Calleja's coinages — sparingly deployed, structurally decisive

*In-Game* is a remarkably *terminologically restrained* book given its theoretical ambition. The number of words Calleja actually *coins* is small, but each is structurally indispensable.

The unambiguous neologisms are:

- **Alterbiography** (Ch.7) — the emergent narrative arising in/through play; the player's accumulated experiential-life-history of a game-existence, distinguishable from the designer-encoded scripted-narrative. The term has a tripartite sub-typology: *alterbiography of self* (the player's own avatar's life), *of entity* (the life of a controlled NPC-entity), *of miniatures* (the collective life of multiple small units in strategy games).
- **Player Involvement Model (PIM)** (Ch.3) — Calleja's six-dimensions-by-two-temporal-phases framework. While "involvement" is a borrowed noun, "Player Involvement Model" as a designated technical construct is Calleja's.
- **Incorporation** (Ch.10) — the term is not new; what is new is its deployment as the *synthetic technical alternative* to immersion and presence. Calleja's *incorporation* is doubly-directional: the player incorporates the game-world (cognitively/affectively assimilates), and the game-world incorporates the player (extends the player's effective field). This dual-directional reading is the conceptual payoff of the whole book.

Calleja also performs significant **sense-extensions** of existing terms:

- **Worldness** — Klastrup-coined; Calleja redeploys in Ch.5 as a property of virtual-environments' inhabitable-coherence.
- **Subgames** — a designer's term given technical-precision as nested-goal-structures within the goal-hierarchy of Ch.9.
- **Macro phase / Micro phase** — common temporal-distinction terminology repurposed for PIM's two-phase off-line/in-session structure.

### Pre-existing terms redeployed

The vast majority of *In-Game*'s vocabulary is **pre-existing terminology redeployed under new structural constraints**. The most notable cases:

- **Involvement** (the umbrella) — Calleja takes a vague engagement-synonym from media psychology and forces it to do work that "immersion" and "presence" can no longer do.
- **Magic circle** (Ch.9) — Huizinga's term, taken up via Salen+Zimmerman and Juul, and qualified against by Pargman+Jakobsson, Malaby, Taylor. Calleja does not destroy the magic circle; he relaxes its borders.
- **Family resemblances** (Ch.1) — Wittgenstein's term imported wholesale as a methodological constraint on game-definition.
- **Cybertext** / **ergodicity** (Aarseth 1997) — foundational to Ch.2 and Ch.7.
- **Ludus** / **paidia** (Caillois 1962) — Ch.9's foundational play-typology.
- **Lusory attitude** (Suits 1978) — Ch.9 anchor.
- **Third spaces** (Oldenburg 1999) — appropriated via Steinkuehler+Williams (2006) for Ch.6 MMO sociality.
- **Immersive fallacy** (Salen+Zimmerman 2003) — Ch.2 working concept.
- **Cognitive maps** (Tolman 1948; Lynch 1960) — Ch.5 spatial-internalization core.
- **Imageability** (Lynch 1960) — Ch.5 spatial-environment property.
- **Topophilia** (Tuan 1974) — Ch.5 affective-bond-with-place.
- **Logic of transparency** (Bolter+Grusin 1999) — Ch.2 critical concept.
- **Excitatory homeostasis** (Bryant+Davies; Grodal) — Ch.8 affective-arousal concept.
- **Focalization** (Genette 1980) — Ch.7 narratological term extended to game-perspective.

### Terminological promiscuity acknowledgments

A revealing feature of Calleja's discourse is his explicit **acknowledgment of terminological promiscuity** — both his own and the field's. The book's opening pages diagnose the conflation of *immersion* with *presence* with *engagement* with *flow* with *absorption* with *transportation*. Calleja proposes *involvement* not because it is technically tighter (it is not — it is also a vague word in everyday English) but because it is *less loaded* and thus available for technical re-stipulation. The compound-term cost is high — "kinesthetic involvement," "spatial involvement," "shared involvement," "narrative involvement," "affective involvement," "ludic involvement" — six adjective-noun phrases that must be kept distinct and which the reader has to internalize. The terminological economy is paid by the reader; Calleja in exchange offers structural-decompositional precision that "immersion" alone never could.

### Terms imported via *mediated-exegetical chains*

A characteristic move of *In-Game* is the **mediated-exegetical citation**: Calleja invokes a term that *appears* to draw on a classical thinker but in fact reaches the term through an intermediary. The clearest cases:

- **Imagination, synthesis, suspension of disbelief** in Ch.7 → reach Sartre (*Psychology of Imagination*) and Iser (*Act of Reading*) via **Marie-Laure Ryan's** *Narrative as Virtual Reality* (2001). Calleja's phenomenological-imagination vocabulary is Ryan-mediated; he does not read Sartre directly for analytic apparatus.
- **Experiential gestalts** in Ch.10 → reach the experientialist-cognitive-science framework via **Lakoff+Johnson** (*Metaphors We Live By*, 2003), supplemented by Damasio's *Feeling of What Happens* (2000). There is no direct phenomenological reading.
- **Third spaces** in Ch.6 → reach Oldenburg's third-spaces concept via **Steinkuehler+Williams** (2006). Calleja cites Oldenburg directly but the appropriation is mediated.
- **Frame analysis / footing** style social-play vocabulary in Ch.9 → reach Goffman-like sensibilities via **Pargman+Jakobsson** without Calleja citing Goffman.

These mediated chains are diagnostic: Calleja's intellectual horizon is the contemporary 2005–2010 game-studies literature, and his ingress points to canonical theory always run through that literature's commentary apparatus.

### The terminological signature of Calleja's intellectual horizon

What is conspicuously **missing** from the terminology profile (mirroring the absences confirmed in the bibliography and interlocutor map):

- **Zero Aristotelian terms**: no *phantasia*, no *pathos*, no *phronesis*, no *kinēsis* in the Aristotelian-philosophical sense (the word "kinesthetic" appears as a contemporary movement-perception term, not via Aristotle).
- **Zero Burkean terms**: no pentad-vocabulary (act, scene, agent, agency, purpose, attitude). The fact that *agency* appears prominently in Ch.4 is misleading — Calleja's agency is Murray-derived + Malaby-contingency-conditioned, not Burke-pentadic.
- **Zero Gibsonian terms by attribution**: "affordance" appears 11 times in the body text per the Phase 0 cite-check, but Calleja never cites Gibson and never uses "ecological." His affordances are pre-theoretic / generic-designer-discourse.
- **Zero Uexküllian terms**: no Umwelt, no Funktionskreis, no Merkwelt, no Wirkwelt, no perception-tone / effect-tone, no biosemiotic vocabulary at all.
- **Zero Merleau-Pontian terms**: no chair, no chiasm, no flesh, no body-schema as Merleau-Pontian technical concepts. *Body* appears as everyday-body and embodiment-cognitive-science body (Damasio-Lakoff register).
- **No flow** (Csikszentmihalyi): missing from Ch.8 despite the affective-arousal topic.

This profile reinforces the global finding: the **incorporation** apparatus is built on second-order cognitive science (Lakoff+Johnson + Damasio) plus game-studies + qualitative-virtual-worlds methodology, NOT on phenomenology or classical philosophy. Every Layer A/B/C augmentation in the dissertation is anticipatory relative to this terminological horizon.
