**Asymmetric pattern**: the two *transportation-ancestors* (Murray, Ryan) are critique-and-rescued — their underlying intuition (transportation; spatiality) is preserved under incorporation. The three *presence-theorists* (Slater, Witmer+Singer, Lombard+Ditton) are wholesale displaced — their vocabulary and analytic apparatus are not reconstituted. The five *game-studies immersion-as-absorption theorists* are critique-and-superseded — their partial insights (multidimensionality, attentional continuum, anti-mimetic-realism) are absorbed but their overall frameworks are replaced by the six-dimensional PIM.

## Entries

### 1. Murray 1998 — *Hamlet on the Holodeck* — **critique-and-rescue**

- **Prior claim** (≤40 words): Deep immersion is the sensation of being transported into an elaborately simulated place, akin to literary immersion but intensified by interactivity; the canonical articulation of the *transportation* metaphor for digital media.
- **Calleja's response** (≤40 words): The transportation-vocabulary ("deep immersion") is replaced, but the underlying intuition of being-transported-into-a-place is preserved and reconstituted in Ch.10 as incorporation's *habitation* metaphor (book p.172 confirms).
- **What incorporation absorbs**: the transportation pole of the absorption/transportation partition.
- **What it refuses**: the unidirectional-plunge entailment of "deep immersion".
- **What it recasts**: transportation becomes one axis of the bidirectional double-axis structure (player absorbs environment AND is absorbed into it).

### 2. Ryan 2001 — *Narrative as Virtual Reality* — **critique-and-rescue**

- **Prior claim**: Spatial / textual-world immersion: representation of inhabitable space is the defining feature of immersion-suitable media; three modes — spatial, temporal, emotional immersion.
- **Calleja's response**: Quoted approvingly at pp.31–32 to defend spatiality against Salen+Zimmerman's Tetris move. Ryan operates as co-belligerent on the transportation side; spatiality-as-defining-feature is preserved and elevated to the *spatial+kinesthetic cornerstone* of incorporation.
- **Absorbs**: representation-of-inhabitable-space-as-defining-feature.
- **Refuses**: the "plunge into a textual world" unidirectionality.
- **Recasts**: spatial immersion becomes the SPATIAL dimension of the PIM, gated by the cornerstone-pair requirement of Ch.10.

### 3. Slater + Wilbur 1997 / Slater 2003 — **displace-and-replace**

- **Prior claim**: Immersion is a quantifiable technological-system property (display fidelity, tracking, latency); presence is the psychological correlate; fidelity-determinism follows. Form/content partition: presence = form; engagement = content.
- **Calleja's response**: Sustained critique at pp.20–22. Form/content cannot be cleanly separated because interpretive apparatus and prior lived experience are constitutive of presence. The "rejection of technological determinism" at IG-10 p.173 is explicit: a tabletop board game can be more incorporating than low-fidelity VR.
- **Absorbs**: nothing (paradigm of the displace-and-replace pattern).
- **Refuses**: immersion-as-hardware-property; fidelity determinism; form/content partition.
- **Recasts**: incorporation requires *subjective disposition* (not technological envelopment) as the constitutive condition.

### 4. Witmer + Singer 1998 — *Measuring Presence* — **displace-and-replace**

- **Prior claim**: Immersion as psychological state of envelopment; presence = involvement + immersion; presence-questionnaire as measurement instrument.
- **Calleja's response**: Treated at pp.21–22 as symptomatic of presence-theory's terminological confusion (Witmer+Singer's psychological-envelopment inverts Slater+Wilbur's hardware-immersion). The envelopment-as-submersion picture is the canonical "unidirectional plunge" / "exclusionary logic" critique target (IG-10 pp.167–169). Calleja's qualitative methodology refuses the questionnaire-quantification approach.
- **Absorbs**: nothing.
- **Refuses**: psychological-envelopment; presence-questionnaire psychometrics; presence-as-quantifiable-state.
- **Recasts**: replaces single-direction envelopment with bidirectional double-axis incorporation.

### 5. Lombard + Ditton 1997 — *At the Heart of It All* — **displace-and-replace**

- **Prior claim**: Presence as "perceptual illusion of non-mediation"; six characterizations of presence (transportation, realism, immersion, social-actor-within-medium, medium-as-social-actor, etc.).
- **Calleja's response**: Praised at pp.23–25 for the synthetic move (six characterizations distilled to a common thread) but critiqued via McMahan's failed Myst III application — when operationalized, the six-categories framework reduces to feature-list pigeonholing.
- **Absorbs**: nothing (the perceptual-illusion-of-non-mediation locus is exactly what the intrusion-integration thesis of IG-10 pp.171–172 refuses).
- **Refuses**: perceptual-illusion-of-non-mediation; interface-erasure framing.
- **Recasts**: the six characterizations are replaced by the six PIM dimensions (a different six, grounded in involvement rather than presence-feature).

### 6. McMahan 2003 — *Immersion, Engagement and Presence* — **critique-and-supersede**

- **Prior claim**: Perceptual / psychological / social-realism three-fold immersion, operationalized on Myst III: Exile (page-turning, elevators, zeppelins as social-realism cues).
- **Calleja's response**: Sustained critique at pp.24–25. The social-realism claims apply trivially to most digital games in similar genres, doing no game-specific analytic work; McMahan drifts terminologically (treating immersion as a form of involvement, away from its presence-theory transportation-connotations).
- **Absorbs**: the impulse toward multidimensional resolution (partial).
- **Refuses**: feature-list pigeonholing; category-checklist analytic style.
- **Recasts**: the three-fold immersion classification is wholly superseded by the six-dimensional PIM, which provides the per-game analytic resolution McMahan's scheme failed to deliver.

### 7. Salen + Zimmerman 2003 — *Rules of Play* ("immersive fallacy" + Tetris-immersibility) — **critique-and-supersede**

- **Prior claim**: The "immersive fallacy" is the design-belief that perfect mimetic-realism is the desideratum; absorption-style immersion is observable in non-spatial games (e.g., Tetris).
- **Calleja's response**: Partial endorsement of the attack on mimetic-realism-imperative (Ch.2 challenge 3, resistance to fidelity-determinism); partial critique of the Tetris move at pp.25–28 (via Gorfinkel and Laramée), which sidelines spatiality as defining and conflates absorption with transportation.
- **Absorbs**: the anti-mimetic-realism position; resistance to fidelity-determinism.
- **Refuses**: the Tetris-as-immersion move (Tetris lacks habitable space; incorporation requires the spatial+kinesthetic cornerstone, so Tetris is acknowledged as absorption-style *involvement* but not *incorporation*).
- **Recasts**: the "immersive fallacy" framing itself is superseded by the six-dimensional PIM.

### 8. Douglas + Hargadon 2001 — *The Pleasures of Immersion and Engagement* — **critique-and-supersede**

- **Prior claim**: Immersion (within-frame schema; directed interaction) vs engagement (extra-textual critical stance; disruption of schema); attentional continuum from unconscious to conscious involvement; collapses ergodic/non-ergodic distinction (Balzac/Dickens/Krantz = Space Invaders).
- **Calleja's response**: Praised at pp.29–30 for recognizing the attentional continuum — carried forward into Ch.3's macro/micro temporal-phase apparatus and into Ch.10's "sustainment-but-fleeting" property. Critiqued for collapsing ergodic vs non-ergodic, which Calleja explicitly restores ("My definition and description of incorporation precludes its application to any non-ergodic media", IG-10 p.173).
- **Absorbs**: the attentional-continuum insight.
- **Refuses**: the ergodic/non-ergodic collapse.
- **Recasts**: within-frame/extra-textual schema distinction is superseded by the multidimensional PIM; books/films return to a different theoretical category.

### 9. Brown + Cairns 2004 — *A Grounded Investigation of Immersion in Games* — **critique-and-supersede**

- **Prior claim**: Engagement / engrossment / total-immersion three-degree ladder; levels 1–2 = absorption; level 3 = transportation = presence. Grounded interview methodology.
- **Calleja's response**: Productively flagged at pp.30–31 for the continuum-of-attentional-intensity insight (involvement-as-prerequisite-to-transportation), which Calleja rescues. Critiqued for mixing metaphors within a single ladder (levels 1–2 absorption-sense; level 3 transportation-sense) — exacerbating the very definitional confusion the authors set out to resolve.
- **Absorbs**: the continuum-of-involvement insight (preserved as macro/micro phases + multidimensional PIM).
- **Refuses**: metaphor-mixing within a single typology.
- **Recasts**: the three-degree ladder is replaced by the 6-dim × 2-phase grid culminating in incorporation as the synthesis-attractor (single-coherent metaphor, not a ladder).

### 10. Ermi + Mäyrä 2005 — *Fundamental Components of the Gameplay Experience* (SCI model) — **critique-and-supersede**

- **Prior claim**: Multidimensional gameplay-experience model with three modes — sensory, challenge-based, imaginative (SCI) immersion (empirically derived from child interviews at DiGRA 2005).
- **Calleja's response**: Major acknowledged contribution to multidimensionality + medium-specificity at pp.31–32. Critiqued for deriving the meaning of "immersion" from child interviews, which collapses the academic-technical sense into the colloquial sense.
- **Absorbs**: multidimensionality + medium-specificity (sensory ≈ kinesthetic+spatial perceptual layer; challenge-based ≈ kinesthetic+ludic; imaginative ≈ narrative+spatial+affective).
- **Refuses**: the three-mode SCI taxonomy as such; the term "immersion" for the phenomenon (which it relabels "involvement").
- **Recasts**: the three-mode taxonomy is expanded to six dimensions with the addition of SHARED as its own dimension and the explicit separation of affective from imaginative.
