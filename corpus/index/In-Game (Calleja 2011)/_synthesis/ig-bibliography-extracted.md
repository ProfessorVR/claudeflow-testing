## Discussion: the bibliographic profile of *In-Game*

Calleja's References section is a strikingly coherent artifact of the early-2010s game-studies moment. Of the 137 non-game publications, **57 fall in the B (game-studies + new-media) bucket** — well over forty percent — confirming that Calleja's primary disciplinary home is game studies proper and that his secondary horizons are negotiated through that prism. Within Category B the citation pattern is recognizable to anyone who knows the DiGRA / *Game Studies* journal scene of 2005–2010: Aarseth (three entries), Juul (two), Salen + Zimmerman (one but very heavily worked), Murray, Pearce, Eskelinen, Jenkins, Klevjer, Schott, Yee (two), Steinkuehler + Williams. The DiGRA conference proceedings produce a substantial gray-literature substrate (Ermi + Mäyrä 2005; Mateas + Stern 2005; Poels 2009; Van den Hoogen et al. 2009), as do CHI (Brown + Cairns 2004) and a cluster of smaller venues (Medi@terra, IFIPS, GDC).

The **presence / VR bucket (Category C, 21 entries)** is the second-densest and is largely concentrated in Chapter 2's literature review. The familiar canon is here intact: Lombard + Ditton (1997), Witmer + Singer (1998), Slater (1997, 2003), Sheridan (1992), Sutherland (1965, 1968), Steuer (1992), Held + Durlach (1992), Minsky (1980), Ijsselsteijn (2004), Ijsselsteijn + Riva (2003), McMahan (2003), and the Waterworths (2003). What is conspicuously **absent** in this bucket is just as informative as what is present: Calleja cites **no Frank Biocca, no Tronstad, no Csikszentmihalyi** (verified by Phase 0 cite-check; cf. `manifest.json:cite_check.other_key_theorists`). Csikszentmihalyi's absence is the most surprising — flow theory is the standard reference for affective gameplay states, and yet Chapter 8 makes no appeal to it.

The **narrative + literary bucket (Category D, 20 entries)** is heaviest in Chapter 7. Marie-Laure Ryan appears twice (the 2001 *Narrative as Virtual Reality* and 2006 *Avatars of Story*), Genette twice (the 1980 *Narrative Discourse* and the 1988 *Narrative Discourse Revisited* — the latter doubly significant because Calleja invokes Genette's model-revision precedent in his own Conclusion as a self-positioning move), and the classical-narratological foundation is rounded out by Chatman, Prince, Bal, Culler, Walsh, and Iser. The literary anchors are eclectic: Borges, Proust, Tolkien (the *Monsters and the Critics* essays), Sartre's *What Is Literature?* and *The Psychology of Imagination* (the latter loadbearing in Ch.7 on imagination-driven alterbiography), and Walton's *Mimesis as Make-Believe*.

The **phenomenology + cognitive bucket (Category E, 15 entries)** is smaller than one might expect for a book whose synthesis chapter (Ch.10) takes the title "From Immersion to *Incorporation*" — a thesis that *appears* to demand robust phenomenological grounding. In fact, the phenomenological substrate is delivered almost entirely through Lakoff + Johnson's *Metaphors We Live By* (2003) and Damasio's *The Feeling of What Happens* (2000), supplemented by Dennett (1991) and a single, isolated, single-citation Heidegger entry (the 1977/1993 *Question Concerning Technology*, anchoring a single sentence at p.21 in Chapter 2 on interpretive comportment). **There is no Merleau-Ponty, no Husserl, no Sein und Zeit-era Heidegger, no Sartre on phenomenology proper (the Sartre cites are literary), no Schutz, no Ihde.** This profile is the single most important fact about Calleja's intellectual horizon: the "incorporation" thesis is built on second-order experientialist cognitive science (Lakoff + Johnson + Damasio), not on first-order phenomenology. The Phase 0 cite-check confirmed this rigorously.

The **qualitative-method bucket (Category F, 8 entries)** is tiny but methodologically load-bearing: Taylor's *Play between Worlds* (2006), Steinkuehler's 2005 dissertation, Klastrup's 2004 dissertation, Fine's *Shared Fantasy* (1983), Jarvinen's 2008 dissertation, Liboriussen's 2009 dissertation, Van Rooyen-McLeay's 1985 thesis, and Copier's 2007 dissertation. These are the *named* precedents for the 25-interview-plus-focus-group qualitative virtual-worlds method that Chapter 3 announces. Notably **absent**: Glaser + Strauss / Charmaz (grounded theory), Spradley (ethnography), and any reference text on the philosophy of qualitative method. Calleja's methodological apparatus is exemplar-based, not textbook-based.

The **other bucket (Category G, 27 entries)** is heterogeneous: Caillois's *Man, Play and Games* (1962), Huizinga's *Homo Ludens* (1955), Suits's *The Grasshopper* (1978), Wittgenstein's *Philosophical Investigations* (which supplies the family-resemblance methodology of Chapter 1), Lefebvre's *The Production of Space*, Soja's *Thirdspace*, Giddens, Deleuze + Guattari, Lewis-Williams, Oldenburg's *The Great Good Place* (foundational to Ch.6's third-spaces appropriation via Steinkuehler + Williams), Ehrmann's "Homo Ludens Revisited," Naess, Schank + Abelson, Locke + Latham, Rosenbloom + Newell, and three film references (Bay, Spielberg, the Wachowskis, plus Peter Jackson's *Lord of the Rings*). The *Oxford Dictionary of English* appears as a citation — a tell of careful philological precision.
