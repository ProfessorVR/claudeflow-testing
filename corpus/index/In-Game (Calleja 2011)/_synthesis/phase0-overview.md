## §1. Bibliographic spine and author profile

Gordon Calleja's *In-Game: From Immersion to Incorporation* was published by MIT Press in 2011 as part of the press's growing game-studies catalogue. The volume runs 186 numbered body pages (Introduction through Conclusion), plus a 14-page Appendix ("A Tale of Two Worlds"), 4 pages of Notes, a 14-page References section, and a 6-page back-of-book Index, totalling 224 PDF pages. The PDF in our corpus has its front matter (half-title, title, copyright, dedication, contents, acknowledgements) already trimmed; the file opens directly with the body Introduction on PDF page 1, which is also book page 1. PDF page-offset therefore equals zero on every body page and across the entire editorial apparatus — a clean mapping verified on ten landmarks (the start of every numbered chapter plus the Appendix opening and four interior samples at PDF pages 100 and 150, which fall correctly within the chapters their book-page assignments predict). The electronic ISBN 9780262295451 is detectable from the per-chapter PDF download stamps left by the MIT Press direct-platform delivery system.

Calleja's institutional affiliation at the time of publication is the Center for Computer Games Research, IT University of Copenhagen. The book is a substantially expanded version of his King's College London PhD dissertation, in which he conducted three years of qualitative interview-based and participant-observation research on two then-paradigmatic massively multiplayer on-line games — *World of Warcraft* (Blizzard Entertainment 2004) and *Planetside* (Sony Online Entertainment 2003). Twenty-five experienced players were interviewed in semi-structured two-session formats inside the games' virtual environments, supplemented by extended personal participation, focus groups, on-line forum monitoring, blog tracking, and machinima review. The grounded-theoretic distillation of those interview corpora is the volume's empirical engine; the Player Involvement Model (PIM) and the concept of *incorporation* are its theoretical yield.

Calleja's stance is methodologically qualitative-experiential and theoretically eclectic. He draws explicitly on Wittgensteinian family-resemblance reasoning for definitional questions (Chapter 1), on a constellation of game-studies and presence-theory authors for the literature-review apparatus (Chapter 2), and on Lakoff and Johnson's experientialist ontology together with Damasio's cognitive-neuroscience phenomenology for the metaphor-theoretic core of the incorporation argument (Chapter 10). The volume does not draw on classical phenomenology, classical rhetoric, ecological psychology, or biosemiotics — an absence that has structural consequences for the dissertation's planned three-layer augmentation, documented in detail in §6 below.

## §2. Argument structure

The volume executes a clean five-move argument that is unusually well-signposted for a research monograph.

**Move 1 (Chapter 1 — Games Beyond Games).** Calleja delineates which members of the broad family of games his analysis will apply to, refusing essentialist definition in favour of family-resemblance reasoning. He surveys the three traditional axes of game studies — formal, experiential, and sociocultural — and positions the book on the experiential axis while drawing freely on the other two when they illuminate experience.

**Move 2 (Chapter 2 — Immersion).** Calleja conducts a literature review of immersion and presence, identifying four conceptual challenges that the discourse has failed to address: terminological promiscuity (immersion is used interchangeably to mean absorption and transportation); inattention to the specific affordances of non-ergodic media; technological determinism (especially in presence-theory's fidelity-based accounts); and a missing continuum of attentional intensity that would distinguish degrees of experiential involvement. The chapter is the densest citation chapter in the book and supplies the foils against which the rest of the volume defines itself.

**Move 3 (Chapter 3 — The Player Involvement Model).** The book's load-bearing theoretical chapter. Calleja introduces a six-dimensional model of player involvement, cross-cut by a two-phase temporal distinction. The six dimensions are **kinesthetic** (control and movement), **spatial** (exploration and habitation), **shared** (co-presence with other agents), **narrative** (scripted-story plus emergent "alterbiography"), **affective** (emotion-in-gameplay), and **ludic** (rules, goals, choices, agency). The two phases are **macro** (off-line involvement — anticipation, planning, community participation, between-session thinking) and **micro** (moment-to-moment in-session engagement). Every subsequent dimension chapter is structurally indexed to this 6 × 2 grid.

**Move 4 (Chapters 4 through 9 — the six dimension expositions).** Each dimension chapter follows the same structural template: a "Macro Phase: <subtitle>" opening section sets the off-line condition for the dimension, followed by micro-phase sections that develop moment-to-moment behaviour. Each chapter is illustrated with paradigmatic games and grounded in qualitative-interview excerpts. Chapter 8 (Affective) is conspicuously short at twelve pages, suggesting an under-developed dimension; the other five run eighteen to twenty-two pages each.

**Move 5 (Chapter 10 — From Immersion to Incorporation).** The synthesis chapter. Calleja argues that the experiential phenomenon to which "presence" and "immersion" both ineptly gesture is better termed *incorporation* — a process in which the game environment becomes assimilated into player consciousness in a manner coextensive with everyday being-in-the-world, rather than displacing the physical world via a unidirectional plunge. Lakoff and Johnson's experientialist ontology supplies the metaphor-theoretic apparatus, and Damasio supplies the consciousness-side cognitive-neuroscience grounding. The chapter ends by mapping incorporation onto the six involvement dimensions and showing how it emerges from their integrated operation.

**Coda (Chapter 11 — Conclusion).** A reflective epilogue that positions the volume as a first structural step in modelling player experience, comparing the inevitable inadequacies of structural modelling to the formalism that preceded poststructural literary theory (the Genette/*Narrative Discourse*/*Narrative Discourse Revisited* comparison is explicit) and gesturing toward eventual poststructural successor models.

**Appendix.** *A Tale of Two Worlds* gives readers unfamiliar with MMOGs the *World of Warcraft* and *Planetside* genre-and-topography profiles needed to follow the case-grounded analyses scattered through Chapters 4–9. The Appendix's interior figure numbering (Figures 12.1 and 12.2) reveals that the production-side editorial treatment of the Appendix is as a substantive Chapter 12, not as adjunct material.

## §6. Cite-check results — the critical Phase 0 finding

This is the load-bearing diagnostic for the entire pipeline downstream. The plan's three-layer augmentation strategy assumed varying levels of presence for each augmenting tradition in Calleja's text; the cite-check largely *falsifies* those assumptions and requires the Manual's Section E framing to be revised.

### 6.1 Burke (Layer B)

**Verdict: ABSENT.** Zero hits across the entire PDF for "Burke", "Kenneth Burke", "Grammar of Motives", "pentad", "consubstantiation", or "identification". Burke is not in references; not in index. The dissertation's pentad augmentation is wholly **creative**, not exegetical. Every `maps-to-pentad-term` edge produced downstream must carry `provenance = "anticipatory-projection (dissertation)"`.

### 6.2 Heidegger (Layer A — phenomenological substrate)

**Verdict: PRESENT BUT MINIMAL.** Three in-text hits, all clustered around a single citation at book page 21 (Chapter 2 Immersion), to Heidegger's *The Question Concerning Technology and Other Essays* (1993/1977, Harper & Row edition). The citation is invoked in the limited context of interpretive comportment — "what is critically removed and we must actively think about what we are doing." Calleja makes no engagement with *Sein und Zeit*, *Dasein*, *In-der-Welt-sein*, *Zuhandenheit*, *Vorhandenheit*, or any element of the Heideggerian fundamental ontology that grounds the BCAP / Bowin AMT corpus indices. The dissertation's BCAP-grounded Heidegger augmentation therefore remains **anticipatory**, not exegetical. (Calleja's Heidegger is the late-Heidegger of technology critique; the dissertation's is the SZ-era Heidegger of being-in-the-world. Different Heideggers.)

### 6.3 Aristotle (Layer A — classical-rhetorical substrate)

**Verdict: ABSENT.** Zero hits for "Aristotle", "phantasia", "pathos", or "phronesis". The dissertation's Aristotelian augmentation is wholly creative. No Phantasia-Cluster bridge can be claimed as exegetical; all such bridges are anticipatory projections.

### 6.4 Merleau-Ponty (phenomenological control case)

**Verdict: ABSENT.** Zero hits for "Merleau-Ponty" or "Phenomenology of Perception". A striking absence given that Calleja's kinesthetic and spatial chapters obviously invite phenomenological-bodily treatment. Where Calleja gestures phenomenologically (especially in Chapter 10) he routes through Lakoff and Johnson's experientialist ontology and Damasio's neuroscience-of-consciousness rather than through classical phenomenology.

### 6.5 J. J. Gibson and affordance theory (Layer C — the pivotal finding)

**Verdict: NAME ABSENT; "AFFORDANCE" PRESENT BUT GENERIC.** Zero hits for "J. J. Gibson", "Gibson, J", "Gibson 1979", or "ecological psychology". Eleven hits for "affordance(s)" — used in the generic engineering-and-design sense (e.g., "the surrounding space into affordances for fluid and rapid movement", "affordances for engagement"), never attributed to Gibson and never coupled with the term "ecological". Gibson does not appear in the References section. Gibson does not appear in the Index. The hypothesis advanced in plan v3 §5 Phase 0 that Calleja's Gibson citation would doubly-anchor the Layer C Uexküll bridge **is falsified by this finding.**

**Consequence**: Layer C must be repositioned. The Manual's Section E "Gibson-as-the-implicit-Uexküll-bridge" framing should be rewritten to acknowledge that Calleja's affordance-talk is theoretically thin — he uses the term as common designer-vocabulary, not as a citation of a particular cognitive-ecological tradition. The forward-reference to a future Gibson pipeline as the proper anchor for Layer C remains valid (and indeed becomes *more* important, since the Gibson grounding now has to come exclusively from that pipeline rather than from Calleja).

### 6.6 Uexküll (Layer C — biosemiotic substrate)

**Verdict: ABSENT.** Zero hits for "Uexk", "Funktionskreis", "Umwelt", "Merkwelt", "Wirkwelt", "perception-tone", "effect-tone", or "biosemiot". The dissertation's Uexküllian augmentation is wholly creative, and — given §6.5 — is **singly anchored** in the Uexküll Foray corpus index, with no Calleja-side foothold via Gibson.

## §7. Tension list (≥6)

1. **Immersion vs. incorporation** — the volume's central announced tension; immersion is replaced as primary frame, although the volume retains immersion as a phenomenon-description.
2. **Calleja's qualitative grounded-theoretic methodology vs. presence theory's experimental-quantitative methodology** — Chapter 2's critique of Witmer+Singer's presence-questionnaire approach turns on this incompatibility; Chapter 11's methodological epilogue defends qualitative structural modelling against both quantitative measurement and poststructural anti-modelling.
3. **Structural modelling vs. anti-essentialism** — Calleja explicitly invokes Wittgensteinian family-resemblance to refuse essentialist definition of games (Chapter 1) while simultaneously building a six-dimensional model that *taxonomically* organizes player experience (Chapter 3). The Conclusion concedes that any such model is a "first considered step" inadequate to phenomenal subjectivity.
4. **Unidirectional plunge vs. coextensive habitation** — Chapter 10's signature critique of immersion-as-submersion: incorporation rejects the "container" metaphor of immersion in favour of a metaphor of *habitation* in which everyday-world and game-world coexist rather than displace one another.
5. **Sensory fidelity vs. experiential incorporation** — against Slater's high-fidelity model of presence (Ch.2): a tabletop board game can be more incorporating than a low-fidelity VR setup.
6. **Play decoupled from games** — Chapter 11 + the Caillois/paidia framework in Chapter 9 argue that not every engagement with what we call games produces "play"; the magic-circle concept is engaged but problematized (via Juul, Salen+Zimmerman, Malaby).
7. **Single-author analytic model vs. interview-data subjectivity** — methodological tension between the model's general-applicability claim and the WoW + Planetside-bounded empirical base; Calleja explicitly notes the model's status as a "first considered step".
8. **Macro vs. micro phase asymmetry** — micro-phase analyses are far better developed in each dimension chapter than macro-phase analyses, which often consist of a single opening section; the macro-phase concept is the volume's most under-developed analytic axis.

Continuous prose flows cleanly across page boundaries; figure captions are detectable and parse correctly; the References section parses with standard period-spaced bibliographic punctuation; the back-of-book Index extracts as line-per-entry text. No rotated pages were observed. The book uses single-column body layout throughout.

Known artefacts requiring Phase-2 normalization:

- **Ligatures**: words containing `ﬁ` and `ﬂ` are preserved as Unicode ligatures (e.g., "ﬁ rst", "ﬂ ats") rather than expanded. A normalization step is required during Phase 2 chunking.

- **Verso-page running heads**: page-number then "Chapter N" sequence on verso pages (e.g., `100\nChapter 6`). Preserve as page-anchor signal during chunking, do not include in body content.
- **Figure captions**: captions extract as floating text adjacent to surrounding prose (e.g., `Figure 4.1\n<caption text>`). Tag as figure-caption type during Phase 2.
- **Right-single-quotes**: rendered as curly typographic quotes (’) — consistent.

**No OCR remediation is required.** Standard ligature-normalization and stamp-stripping suffice.

## §12. Deeper findings from the close reading

### 12.1 The Introduction (book pp. 1–6) in full

Calleja opens autobiographically with a vignette from his home village of Gzira, Malta — the arrival of the Commodore Amiga emptying the streets of football-playing children and migrating the football tournaments indoors. The vignette serves multiple rhetorical purposes: it positions Calleja as a member of the first generation acclimatized to "cybernetic circuits" as a matter of everyday life rather than a research subject of estrangement; it foreshadows the Chapter 10 argument that virtual environments are coextensive with rather than separate from the physical everyday; and it grounds the Chapter 5 spatial-involvement claim about exploration's appeal for those in spatially constrained environments (Maltese kids without forests and mountains gain those terrains through games). The opening question — "What exactly makes digital games so involving?" — is the volume's organizing prompt.

The Introduction's central theoretical move is announced immediately: the terms *presence* and *immersion* are problematic, "the latter is the more commonly used term in popular and academic discussions of game involvement, but its general use has diminished in analytical value." Calleja then promises a "more precise conceptualization of the phenomenon that is specific to game environments." The promised concept is *incorporation*. Two structural rhetorical signposts are set up here: (a) presence and immersion are not denied but reframed as descriptions of a *composite* phenomenon better described by the involvement model, and (b) incorporation is "ultimately a metaphor like presence or immersion" but avoids problematic connotations — Calleja is candid that the move is *metaphorological reframing*, not the discovery of a new natural-kind referent. This metaphorological self-consciousness is a stylistic constant of the volume.

The "Brief Overview" section then walks through the eleven chapters in order, summarizing each in two to four sentences. Notably absent from this overview is any forward-reference to the Appendix or to qualitative-methodology, suggesting the Introduction was probably finalized before the Appendix's promotion to substantive status.

### 12.2 The Conclusion (book pp. 181–186) in full

The Conclusion executes three rhetorical moves. First, a phenomenological-narrative recap — the experiential trajectory of acclimatizing to a new game world over the first few hours — restates the incorporation thesis in second-person prose ("You take the first uncertain steps in the unexplored spaces…you become accustomed to your digital manifestation of self"). The willing-suspension-of-disbelief topos is engaged and dismissed: belief in game worlds, Calleja argues, is not suspended but rather *created* through action, movement, navigation, communication, and interaction. The cybernetic-looping of simulation and imagination is reaffirmed as the constitutive mechanism.

Second, an "Implications" subsection reframes the volume's contribution as a challenge to three problematic binaries — game/nongame, work/play, real/virtual — through a tripartite model of media-object + player + sociocultural context. Calleja explicitly insists that "the physical and the virtual are both aspects of what we perceive as real" and that the physical real should not be taken as the stable reference point against which virtual phenomena are measured. This is the volume's strongest ontological commitment.

Third, an "Imagining Worlds into Being" coda offers a longue-durée framing of human world-making — from spoken-word fiction-sharing, to writing and print, to mechanical moving images, to networked digital simulation — and positions incorporation as the contemporary mode of experiencing humanly-created worlds. The final paragraph is unabashedly evangelical: "incorporation is an experience that is compelling for designers, consumers, and technologists alike."

A reflective subsection embedded in the Conclusion (pp. 183–184) acknowledges the volume's structural-modelling limits via the comparison to Genette's *Narrative Discourse* and the post-publication *Narrative Discourse Revisited*. Calleja explicitly anticipates that his own model will require revision and positions the volume as enabling later, poststructural sensitivity rather than precluding it. This methodological humility is consequential for the dissertation's planned augmentations: Calleja himself invites the model's extension.

### 12.3 Chapter 3 (Player Involvement Model) opening (book pp. 35–36)

Chapter 3 opens with a paragraph clarifying that *involvement* is the broader, prior category and that *presence* and *immersion* are downstream "higher-order cognitive processes" predicated on involvement, which is in turn predicated on *attention*. This three-tier stratification (attention → involvement → presence/immersion) is sometimes lost in subsequent chapters but is the volume's most explicit theoretical hierarchy.

The "Research Process" subsection that follows (p. 36) declares the methodology — three years of qualitative work, twenty-five in-depth interviews, two MMOG case sites, focus groups, in-environment interviewing, supplementary blog and forum monitoring — and names the methodological precedents: T. L. Taylor (2006), Constance Steinkuehler (2005), Lisbeth Klastrup (2004). This is the Appendix's seed and the dissertation's empirical-adaptation template.

The "Structure of the Model" subsection then announces the dual-grid: dimensions × phases. Calleja's framing of *why* the macro/micro split is necessary is explicitly grounded in interview data — players reported involvement-states differently when they were playing versus when they were not. The model is bottom-up from interview transcripts rather than top-down from theory.

### 12.4 Chapter 10 (Incorporation) opening (book pp. 167–168)

Chapter 10 opens with a clean recapitulation of the four conceptual challenges from Chapter 2: terminological promiscuity, non-ergodic-affordance neglect, technological determinism, and missing attentional continuum. Calleja then introduces a fifth implicit challenge — the "unidirectional plunge" metaphor — and uses it as the bridge to incorporation. The argument is that immersion-as-submersion presupposes that the everyday world can be *excluded* by submergence in the virtual, which is empirically untrue and theoretically misleading.

The Lakoff-Johnson invocation (p. 167) is direct and quoted at length. The Lakoff-Johnson "experiential gestalt" — a category formed through recurrent embodied experience and serving as the structure of metaphorical coherence — becomes Calleja's bridge between everyday phenomenology and virtual habitation. The metaphor of habitation, he argues, draws on our existing experiential gestalts of inhabiting the everyday world. This is the volume's most theoretically dense paragraph and the natural anchor for any phenomenological extension.

Calleja's care here to avoid contrasting virtual and real worlds (citing Lévy 1998) anticipates the Conclusion's ontological commitment. The dissertation's planned Aristotelian/Heideggerian Layer A would deepen this paragraph considerably — but Calleja himself does not pursue that depth.

### 12.5 Appendix (book pp. 187–188) opening

The Appendix opens with a methodological clarification — "for the sake of readers who have not experienced the games firsthand" — and proceeds through "Genres" and "Topographies" subsections. The Genres subsection differentiates MMORPGs from MMOFPSs in role-performance terms (Calleja notes that the "role-playing" tag in MMORPGs is misleading relative to its pen-and-paper origins in *Dungeons and Dragons*, citing Fine 1983 and Mackay 2001) and in mechanical terms (MMOFPSs require "twitch-based" skills). The Topographies subsection then sketches Azeroth as a 96-region fantasy world with two factions and contested zones, noting the "unnaturally abrupt" region transitions that nevertheless do not break the players' sense of exploratory novelty.

The two figures in the Appendix (numbered 12.1 and 12.2) compare regional aesthetics in WoW and Planetside respectively. The Appendix's tone is descriptive rather than analytic — its function is to provide enough world-knowledge for Chapters 4–9's case-grounded analyses to be intelligible to non-MMOG readers — but the *what-counts-as-a-region-transition* and *how-do-players-actually-experience-discontinuous-aesthetic-coding* material is rich seed for the dissertation's empirical-adaptation work.
