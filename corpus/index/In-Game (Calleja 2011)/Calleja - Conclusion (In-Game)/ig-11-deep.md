## 1. The Implications: from suspension-of-disbelief to cybernetic looping

The Conclusion opens with a second-person experiential vignette of the first hours with a new digital game (pp.181-182, lines 6291-6308): the apprenticeship to mechanics, the uncertain first steps in unexplored spaces, cognitive-map formation, scripted-narrative reception, multiplayer chat-and-voice onboarding. The vignette mirrors the autobiographical-experiential opening of the Introduction. The rhetorical bookending is itself a meta-textual signal: the book wraps its theoretical apparatus inside an experiential frame that performs the very phenomenon (incorporation as gradual habituation) it argues for.

Out of this vignette Calleja extracts a diagnostic move (lines 6309-6318). The Coleridgean gloss "willing suspension of disbelief" — Coleridge unnamed, treated as common currency — is dismissed as ill-fitting for games. In literary or filmic reception we receive what the writer or scriptwriter gives us; in games belief is *created through action, movement, navigation, communication, and other forms of interaction* (lines 6314-6315). Belief is enacted, not withheld. This is the Conclusion's most compressed action-as-constitutive claim — and the natural cross-pipeline candidate for an aisthesis-kinesis bridge to Bowin's AMT index, and a clear point at which Aristotelian *kinēsis* can claim traction Calleja himself does not develop.

The dismissal sets up a compressed restatement of incorporation (p.182, lines 6323-6346). Belief in game worlds, Calleja argues, is sustained through the cybernetic looping of simulation and imagination. The landscapes, characters, objects, and events become real in players' minds not only because they appeal to the imagination but also because that imagination has an existence outside the player's mind in the simulated and digitally materialized world generated by the computer (lines 6328-6331). This is the volume's most explicit imagination-externalization claim and the strongest direct candidate for a Phantasia-Cluster bridge: *phantasma* as externalized imagistic cognition is precisely the analytic Hawhee (rhetorical vision) and Papachristou (deliberative *phantasia*) have prepared. Calleja never uses *phantasia*; the bridge is anticipatory, but the substrate is laid here in plain prose.

When imagined habitation combines with validation-of-location, Calleja continues, we are not merely suspending disbelief but incorporating the computer-generated world into our consciousness. The game environment shifts from a space of external-digital-puppet manipulation to a place continuous with the haunts of our everyday lives. For most players the merging of virtual and actual spaces is a normal part of gaming — neither alarming addiction nor irresponsible escapism — but an experiential state characteristic of engagement with certain digital games, one whose phenomenology has specificities that bear further scrutiny.

That last clause is the dissertation's most leverageable line in the volume (captured at full strength in §4). Two local observations: "place continuous with the haunts of our everyday lives" evokes Heideggerian everyday-environmentality — Zuhandenheit, *Mitsein* — without naming it (Heidegger is cited once in the volume, p.21 inside IG-02; not here). And "experiential state... with a phenomenology... that bear further scrutiny" surfaces a phenomenological register without phenomenological apparatus. The residual-phenomenology flag propagated from IG-03 + IG-10 is confirmed: Calleja deploys phenomenological-register vocabulary while citing no phenomenologist, leaving the analytic space open for the dissertation's Layer-A augmentation.

The "Implications" section then restates the volume's tripartite frame (pp.182-184, lines 6347-6392). The media-object/player/sociocultural-context reframing displaces game/nongame, work/play, real/virtual binaries through its emphasis on mediated interactivity. Players are bound to virtual environments via social and cultural codes designed into the game and acquired through remediated everyday-life practice. The chapter's distilled here/there critique follows: immersion and presence imply a physical reality replaced by a virtual world in a dichotomy that misrepresents our mode of being; incorporation supplants the dichotomy by being coextensive with physical being. A quiet reality-stratification caveat (lines 6371-6376) refuses the physical real as the stable comparator to which virtual phenomena are measured up. The virtual is a crucial aspect of contemporary reality. The metaphysical commitment is understated but real.

The section then issues its first future-research charge (lines 6377-6392): accurate analysis of player involvement will remain limited unless we decouple play from the media-objects we currently call games. The diagnosis has media-archaeological reach. For the dissertation it is a license to bring Aristotelian play-as-practice analytics to bear across present, future, and pre-digital media-objects. It also generates a tension: Calleja calls for decoupling play from games even though the apparatus that licenses the call was developed through analysis of present-day MMORPGs and FPSs.

## 2. The Genette analogy: structural-modelling-as-formalism and the poststructural-successor framing

The reflexive turn occurs in the second half of "Implications" (pp.183-184, lines 6393-6430) and is the load-bearing theoretical move of the unit. Any model for analyzing player experience will have inadequacies, Calleja proposes; to illustrate, he turns to Gérard Genette's *Narrative Discourse* (1980) — the chapter's sole named interlocutor — as exemplar of an incisive structural model that nonetheless required revision. Genette's analysis of narrative time, based on Proust's *À la recherche du temps perdu*, was answered later in *Narrative Discourse Revisited* (1988). Calleja disclaims that the present volume immediately requires revision but uses the Genette case to license the structural-modelling-then-revision arc as canonical trajectory.

Models, he concedes (lines 6406-6411), are analytical constructs that may lack the suppleness for complex phenomena bearing on subjectivity and consciousness; there will always be aspects of the phenomenon challenging to classify. The concession invokes the tribulations of structuralism, challenged by poststructuralism for the latter's greater attentiveness to elements that undo the work of taxonomy (lines 6411-6415). The structuralism-to-poststructuralism arc is offered as canonical successor trajectory: structuralism's tools must come first to make possible the later analytics that supersede them.

Calleja then issues the chapter's most consequential reflexive self-positioning (lines 6416-6430). It is still worthwhile to begin mapping a complex phenomenon through structural models; these can then be elaborated, refined, and eventually revised until research outgrows them. Poststructuralism's analytical sophistication, he notes, was made possible by structuralism and formalism before it, and by the evolution of the literary texts that necessitated such analysis. Games, Calleja argues, have yet to reach that textual sophistication. This book is, therefore, a first considered step — flawed but essential — toward more evolved and sensitive modeling; a refinement of concepts that have thus far described player involvement and immersion.

"First considered step" is the volume's reflexive frame. Held alongside the residual-phenomenology line of p.182 and the decoupling-play diagnosis of p.184, it forms a triadic cluster constituting Calleja's invitation to a successor work. The Genette analogy is consequential precisely because it generalizes: structuralism succeeded by poststructuralism becomes the legitimating template for PIM's eventual outgrowth.

A longitudinal observation: Genette also appears in IG-07 (focalization, narrative time). The IG-07-to-IG-11 Genette-thread is the closest the volume comes to a "theoretical lineage" self-identification: Calleja's reflexive register is narratology-borrowed. But the Genette anchor is *within-discipline* literary-theory; it supplies no exegetical anchor to any of the dissertation's three augmentation layers. All three remain wholly anticipatory relative to the Conclusion. What the Conclusion supplies is not framework but license.

## 3. The future-research agenda: phenomenological deepening, decoupling-play, poststructural successor, cultural-historical arc

Drawing together passages already noted, the Conclusion sets out a four-part future-research agenda:

(i) **Further phenomenological scrutiny of incorporation** (p.182, lines 6343-6346). The experiential state characteristic of game engagement has a phenomenology with specificities that bear further scrutiny. Stated without naming any phenomenologist.

(ii) **Decoupling play from media-objects-currently-called-games** (p.184, lines 6390-6392). Accurate analysis of player involvement requires that the construct "play" not be tethered to the media-objects we presently call games. A media-archaeological charge.

(iii) **Structural-to-poststructural successor modeling** (p.184, lines 6411-6430). PIM is held as a first considered step; refinement, revision, and eventual outgrowth (by analogy to poststructuralism's overcoming of structuralism) are explicitly invited.

(iv) **Continuing the cultural-historical arc of imagined-world transmission** (p.185, lines 6445-6453). The genealogical sequence — spoken-word imagined worlds in proximal communities → writing and print → mechanical moving image → networked digital simulation — is the future-trajectory anchor at the broadest scale.

Each of these is given a distinct future-research edge in the CSV (E07-E11). What is striking is how lean the Conclusion is in *citing* successor frameworks. None of the four future-research themes carries an exegetical anchor to any named theorist beyond Genette. They are all open-architecture invitations.

## 4. The dissertation-positioning leverage: Calleja's invitation, the dissertation's occupation

The chapter's reflexive frame is the volume's single strongest dissertation-positioning leverage point. The argument runs as follows.

**The constituent lines**:

- Line 6426-6427: "first considered step" — the volume's self-positioning.
- Line 6428: horizon of "more evolved and sensitive modeling."
- Line 6345-6346: incorporation has a phenomenology that bears further scrutiny.
- Line 6390-6392: decouple play from media-objects currently called games.
- Line 6418-6420: structural models elaborated, refined, eventually revised.
- Line 6411-6415: structuralism-challenged-by-poststructuralism as canonical successor trajectory.

**The integrated claim**: together these constitute Calleja's invitation to a successor work. Calleja supplies no framework — he names no Aristotle, no Heidegger, no Burke, no Uexküll — but he supplies the *authorization-structure* for one to be developed. The dissertation's strongest Calleja-attested positioning argument is therefore: the dissertation IS the kind of successor work Calleja's reflexive frame anticipates. The Aristotelian/Heideggerian augmentation (Layer A) occupies precisely the space the "phenomenology bears further scrutiny" line opens. The Burkean augmentation (Layer B) is a refinement-and-elaboration in the canonical structural-to-poststructural arc Calleja licenses. The Uexküllian augmentation (Layer C) — though Gibson-mediation is falsified for this unit per Phase 0 — supplies the kind of perception-action-loop granularity Calleja's "environment's affordances and geography" gestures at without theoretical commitment.

**The residual-phenomenology flag confirmed**: per the propagated IG-03 + IG-10 finding, in his closing reflections Calleja uses phenomenological-register vocabulary ("phenomenology... that bear further scrutiny," "experiential state characteristic of engagement") without naming any phenomenologist. The register is residually phenomenological; the apparatus is absent. This is *maximally* dissertation-leverageable: Calleja has cleared a phenomenological space and named it without committing to any tradition's claim on it. The dissertation's Layer-A apparatus moves into a space marked open.

**The decoupling-play move as media-archaeological license**: the dissertation's media-archaeological reach — analyzing pre-digital play, non-game play, and contemporary games on a single Aristotelian footing — is licensed by Calleja's own diagnostic that the category "game" must not over-determine the construct "play."

**A leverage-strength rating**: the Conclusion's positioning leverage is MAXIMUM relative to other units. IG-03 (PIM) and IG-10 (incorporation) carry maximum *dissertation-instrument* leverage — they supply the analytic to be wielded. The Conclusion carries maximum *dissertation-positioning* leverage — it supplies the argument for why the wielding-and-augmenting is licensed.

## 6. Imagining worlds into being: the closing peroration

The "Imagining Worlds into Being" section (pp.184-185, lines 6431-6465) shifts from methodological reflection to cultural-historical peroration. Digital games, Calleja observes, foster a close relationship between the collective resources of the design team and the play experience; in multiplayer games and virtual worlds the relationship is complicated by participation of other players (and, to a more limited degree, by AI behavior) who modulate what designers intended. Players have an important role in describing setting, ambiance, characters, physics, rules, tempo. This collective-co-production claim restates the Shared dimension (IG-06) at perorative scale.

The cultural-historical arc situates digital games as the latest stage of a long media-historical sequence (lines 6445-6453): from sharing imagined worlds through images and the spoken word in proximal communities, to writing and print, to mechanical moving-image, to networked digital simulation enabling global instant sharing. Today we inhabit each other's creations. This is the volume's most expansive media-archaeological gesture; for the dissertation it doubles as the framing premise for the decoupling-play move (the category "play" must be analyzable across this entire sequence).

The chapter closes with an incorporation-as-sought-after peroration (lines 6457-6465) spanning from calm pleasures (watching a sunset with friends) to high-arousal play (competitive FPS, piloting a speeding jet through a gorge). Incorporation is compelling for designers, consumers, and technologists alike and is becoming an increasingly sought-after experience most readily provided by virtual game environments. The increasing variety of these media-objects is, Calleja closes, striking evidence of complex human needs that such simulations engage. The arousal-spectrum lightly restates Affective Involvement (IG-08), but affect remains thin: the Conclusion's primary registers are methodological reflection, future-research charge, and cultural-historical situating. ## 7. Tensions

Three tensions are captured in the JSON:

- **T-IG11-01**: Provisional structural-modelling commitment vs. critique of taxonomy-resistant phenomena. Not a contradiction but a productive provisional commitment: structural modelling holds until poststructural attentiveness becomes possible.

- **T-IG11-02**: Phenomenological register without phenomenological apparatus. The chapter's residual-phenomenology language invokes a phenomenological register with no phenomenologist named. Productive tension for the dissertation's Layer-A augmentation, which occupies precisely this opened-but-unfilled analytic space.

- **T-IG11-03**: Decoupling-play from games vs. games-as-the-volume's-object. Calleja calls for decoupling play from current media-objects even though the apparatus that licenses the call was developed through analysis of present-day games. The tension is generative: it is part of what makes the apparatus claimable for media-archaeological extension.
