## 1. Dimension operationalization

Kinesthetic involvement, as Calleja constructs it across these eighteen pages, names the player's experience of *exerting agency through control and movement* of avatars and miniatures within a virtual environment. The dimension is indexed across the macro / micro phase split inherited from chapter 3: macro-phase kinesthetic involvement is general capability over game outcomes, taken in a Giddens-inflected sense of acting-otherwise under designed contingency; micro-phase kinesthetic involvement is the moment-to-moment in-game control whose modes range across a symbolic / mimetic / symbiotic continuum and whose pleasure peaks in the internalization-of-controls that lets movement be felt as unmediated. Ergodic engagement is the precondition — kinesthetic involvement is non-existent for the consumer of "pre-established media" — and movement-fluency is the *substrate* (Calleja's closing claim, book pp. 71–72) on which the other five dimensions depend. The operational definition is therefore inseparable from a dependency claim: there is no spatial habitation, ludic goal-pursuit, shared co-presence, narrative arc, or affective response that does not rest on at least a basic kinesthetic competence in the environment.

## 2. Macro-phase agency

The chapter opens by enrolling, qualifying, and rejecting three theoretical accounts of agency before settling on a fourth. Janet Murray (1998) supplies the foundational vocabulary — agency as "the satisfying power to take meaningful action and see the results of our decisions and choices" — together with Gareth Schott (2006), who emphasizes the *subjective experience* of agency as the principal contributor to engagement. Calleja preserves both citations' rhetorical weight but immediately complicates Murray's semantics with a worked Counter-Strike: Source example: covering a corridor, the player lobs a grenade into a room moments before two teammates rush in from a back door he could not see; the grenade kills them, the round is lost, the outcome is neither intended nor satisfying — yet the exertion of agency is unambiguous. Murray's intention-and-satisfaction conjunction is too narrow.

The Mateas + Stern (2005) refinement, which formulates agency as the triad of intention-formation, action, and interpretation of perceptible effects, supplies the useful local-vs-global distinction: local agency is seeing the immediate reactions to one's interaction; global agency is the knowledge of longer-term causal consequences. But this account, too, remains anchored in perception of effects — and Calleja wants room for *Planetside* mines that may decide a future battle without the player ever witnessing it. The decisive turn is Anthony Giddens (1984): agency is *capability* of doing things in the first place, not intention; the agent is "one who exerts power or produces an effect," and what matters is that the individual could, at any phase in a sequence, have acted differently. With Giddens, Calleja can keep unintended and persistent consequences inside the kinesthetic dimension without forcing a feedback-loop of perception around them.

Thomas Malaby's (2007) "contrivance of contingency" then enrolls the macro-phase agency analysis into a definition-of-games: games differ from everyday social life precisely in that their rules are designed to *calibrate* contingencies — to produce a mix of predictable and unpredictable outcomes admitting culturally shared, open-ended interpretations — rather than to *reduce* unpredictability. Tic-tac-toe ceases to be a game once one has memorized every permutation; in MMOGs, the appeal is intensified by persistence — actions ramify across other players' experience in ways that often exceed intention. The Evita participant-quote on Planetside's "freedom to act" instantiates the thesis. Failure-as-intrinsic follows: athletic contests and FPS games make the risk-of-failure constitutive of the kinesthetic register; "all of our actions in games, as in life, are performative in this sense" (Malaby 2007, paraphrased at p.58).

A final macro-phase calibration distinguishes games by how player ability is modulated through avatar attributes. Counter-Strike: Source is the limit case of zero attribute-modulation (avatar stats are irrelevant to player success); World of Warcraft is the opposite extreme (a twenty-level gap cannot be closed by skill); Oblivion is intermediate (numeric sword-wielding skill modifies damage-per-blow); Planetside sits closer to the skill end, allowing a level-one dexterous player to eliminate a level-twenty-five opponent. The variation establishes that the kinesthetic dimension is not single-valued across genres — its design knobs include the very ratio of skill-to-attribute.

## 3. Micro-phase in-game control: avatar, miniature, oscillation

Calleja then pivots from generalized agency to its specific moment-to-moment exertion. The first analytic move is the avatar / miniatures distinction. The avatar is the single entity in which player presence is fixed (first- or third-person); miniatures are entities the player partially or fully controls but which do not represent the player — cavalry in Medieval II: Total War, workers in Age of Empires, falling blocks in Tetris. The term "miniatures" registers the *external / omniscient* perspectival posture that miniature-control encourages: the world is presented to the player as a miniature one, and the player occupies a vantage that does not coincide with any in-world locus.

Modes of control in game environments fan out from this distinction. Individual miniature control (Tetris) shows that a single play episode interleaves three PIM dimensions in microsecond succession: rotating the falling block (kinesthetic), monitoring the upcoming block (ludic), and clearing layers to control the game space (spatial). Simultaneous miniature control (Empire: Total War, Figure 4.1) scales the kinesthetic dimension to commander-level distributed attention — up to twenty multi-miniature units commanded as whole, sub-groups, or individuals — without ceasing to be kinesthetic. Single-avatar control consolidates attention on one entity; even Pong's vertical pixel-bar counts as an avatar because it is the locus of agency-exertion within the environment. The Kestra participant quote on first-person preference ("you can control almost every aspect of the movement") anchors the intimacy claim. Then the avatar / miniature oscillation case (FIFA 2009, Figure 4.2): control vests in one active player at a time but is simultaneously distributed across formation, player-run, and tackle-assist functions, so that attention oscillates between the active locus and the field of contributing miniatures.

## 4. The symbolic / mimetic / symbiotic trichotomy (Figure 4.3) — chapter's primary analytic innovation

The chapter's central typology — and the most consequential single analytic contribution of these eighteen pages — appears under "Modes of Game Control." Forms of input are arrayed along a continuum: at one pole, **symbolic control** (keys, controller buttons, thumb sticks) carries no mimetic relationship between the player's actual motion and the avatar's response — WASD keys do not resemble walking; at the opposite pole, **symbiotic control** maps the player's actions directly onto the avatar with some modification, exaggeration, and designed restriction, the paradigm being Kinect (first known as Project Natal), which uses camera-based whole-body capture together with facial-, voice-, and proximity-recognition with the explicit design ambition of *eliminating the controller altogether*. Between these poles, **mimetic control** is a partial mapping — Wii Sports tennis (Wii-mote swing → on-screen racket swing), Grand Slam Tennis, light-gun rail shooters such as The House of the Dead, steering wheels for racing — in which the controller either reproduces a tool / vehicle or solicits a body-motion mimetic of the on-screen action. Figure 4.3 visualizes this continuum.

Calleja's three substantive arguments around the trichotomy:

1. **Market-driven trajectory.** Industry development is shifting from symbolic to symbiotic, because symbiotic control recruits casual-gamer, older-demographic, and cross-gender markets that symbolic control did not. Nintendo's Wii success and Microsoft's Kinect / Project Natal cross-demographic marketing are the cited evidence; Kinect's "platform for the whole family" framing is read as a take-over-the-living-room media-and-social-hub ambition.

2. **Hybrid persistence.** Many games combine modes: Wii Sports boxing is wholly mimetic, but Wii Sports baseball alternates mimetic (throwing motion to pitch) with symbolic (button-press to alter pitch type); No More Heroes uses nunchuk-thumb-stick for navigation and Wii-mote swings for actions. The hybrid persistence is explained by a substantive functional asymmetry: spatial navigation is "difficult to recreate mimetically." Some game-functions, in other words, do not yield to symbiotic translation — the continuum has zones of irreducible asymmetry that the typology itself does not fully theorize (one of the chapter's open tensions).

3. **Symbolic control is unmarked in fluent FPS play.** Even in symbolic-control FPS games, attention is not normally directed to the keys themselves — the dexterity of WASD-timing is real but invisible, surfacing only when the player is uncertain about a key-binding or learning a new control scheme. Symbolic control's strangeness is asymmetric: it is most visible *before* fluency.

Paradigm games per category, in Calleja's own analytic deployment:

- **Symbolic**: Counter-Strike: Source (agency-and-failure paradigm; WASD-and-mouse FPS dexterity); Tetris (single-miniature focus); Empire: Total War (simultaneous-miniature command); FIFA 2009 (avatar / miniature oscillation); Max Payne (bullet-time / fantasy-realization); Mirror's Edge (continuity-of-movement); Red Orchestra (disposition-to-act / kairotic anticipation); Planetside / World of Warcraft / Oblivion (the MMOG / RPG ecology).
- **Mimetic**: Wii Sports (paradigm — tennis swing; boxing wholly mimetic; baseball hybrid); Grand Slam Tennis; The House of the Dead (light-gun); steering wheels for racing.
- **Symbiotic**: Kinect / Project Natal (paradigm — full body-capture + voice + face + distance).

The trichotomy is, in dissertation-deployable terms, *a measurable design variable*: any game's kinesthetic-design position is locatable along it, and the position has predictive implications for demographic reach, attentional structure (key-vs-body), and the form internalization-of-controls can take.

## 5. Kinesthetic pleasure

The chapter's longest subsection moves from a brief genealogy of movement-affordances — Space Invaders' single axis, Pacman's plane, Max Payne's full walk/crouch/sprint/jump with bullet-time supplement — to the phenomenology of kinesthetic pleasure proper. Three threads run through it: bullet-time as designed fantasy-realization; the first-person / third-person perspectival difference; and the *internalization of controls* that is, for Calleja, the pleasure peak.

Bullet-time and the fantasy of moving "with Neo-like speed" externalize cinema-imported internal imagery — Max Payne (Figure 4.4), Enter the Matrix, F.E.A.R. — and offer the player satisfaction both of performance and of cinematic visualization; the Baal Planetside quote on "mega hyperjumps" in Matrix Online anchors the affective register. First-person presence works by perceptual collapse: thumbing the space key and pressing W is interpreted by the player not as a character's jump but as a jump *she has made*. Third-person play, by contrast, produces a *double awareness* — of the surrounding environment as portrayed by the camera, and of the space as it relates to the avatar — which forces re-anchoring of line-of-sight when the player switches modes (the BunkerBoy86 Planetside quote on third-person-look-then-first-person-aim is the paradigm).

The pleasure peak is the disappearance of mediation. Three vocabularies — one academic, two participant-vernacular — converge:

- **Bladerunner's "Quan"** (an idiom borrowed from the film *Jerry Maguire*) names the state in which on-screen actions feel willed-by-mind rather than mediated through keys and mouse. Calleja preserves the participant's vocabulary as a vivid illustration of internalization without elevating it to canonical concept.
- **Sue Morris (2002)** supplies the chapter's only academic anchor: the skilled FPS player's hand movements become *unconscious to a degree*; complex keystroke-and-mouse sequences are analyzed, designed, memorized, practiced, and then internalized to the point where individual moves are absorbed into a player's style of playing — players must "think with their fingers" until "the player feels like an extension of the game or the game feels like an extension of the player."
- **Sunniva** describes "continuous focus" — a wave-like, surfing-like steady motion that holds her attention on the game. Her word for it is *flow*, used vernacularly. Calleja does not promote the term into a Csikszentmihalyi citation, and the volume-wide cite-check confirms zero Csikszentmihalyi engagement.

The closing of "Kinesthetic Pleasure" registers the phenomenological mineness of avatar-action — Evita's "I am now flying an aircraft … it's me." This is the chapter's strongest residual-phenomenology site (see §7 below).

Mirror's Edge (Figure 4.5) then receives sustained treatment as the paradigm of *designed continuity of movement*: a parkour-inspired game whose reward structure for perfect-timing momentum recodes obstacles as "affordances" for fluid motion. The chapter's lone use of "affordance" is generic — without Gibson attribution, without "ecological" vocabulary, and (per the volume's cite-check) without any anchoring in affordance theory at all. The Gibson-mediation hypothesis for the dissertation's Layer C Uexküll bridge is *falsified* by this passage: Calleja's affordance-talk is theoretically thin and cannot carry a Gibson bridge.

The Swalwell (2008) critique is the subsection's most substantive disciplinary intervention. Swalwell's LAN-party research (which cites Van Rooyen-McLeay's 1985 master's-thesis observation of arcade-player and onlooker physical excitation — "his legs uncontrollably danced beneath him" — and the wail or twitch of bystanders) treats player and onlooker kinesthetic experience as continuous. Calleja contests this: only the player is inside the *circuit* between game and player; only the player has a sense of agency in the environment; only the player bears the failures and accomplishments of control. Onlookers are excited by both the on-screen events and the players' visible excitation, and players in turn modulate their own excitement in response to having an audience, but the onlookers remain extrinsic to the agency-circuit. Treating the two registers as equivalent — Swalwell's slip, in Calleja's reading — confuses the purely perceptual experience of a medium's representational layer with the kinesthetic-plus-ergodic combination that defines player involvement. This is the line of what counts as kinesthesia in Calleja's vocabulary: ergodic-internal to the circuit, not sympathetic-external to it.

Finally, the *disposition to act* — the cross-reference to chapter 3's Red Orchestra sniper passage — recasts kinesthetic involvement as a *temporally extended* condition. The sniper waiting motionless over a vacant street is kinesthetically involved if she is tense with anticipation to move-the-crosshairs, lead-the-target, hold-her-breath, and press-the-trigger at the kairotic moment. "The expectation of performance can be just as engaging as the performance itself" (paraphrased from p.70). Onlookers cannot share this anticipation; the kinesthetic register includes readiness, not only realized input.

## 6. Anticipatory Burke / Uexküll / Aristotelian-Heideggerian mappings

Per the v3 plan §5 and the volume-wide cite-check (`_synthesis/manifest.json` cite_check field), all three dissertation augmentation layers are *creative / anticipatory* relative to Calleja's text — none are exegetical. Calleja's text contains zero Burke, zero Aristotle, zero Merleau-Ponty, zero Uexküll, zero Csikszentmihalyi, zero Gibson-by-name; the single Heidegger reference (at book p.21, outside this chapter) is to *The Question Concerning Technology* and is not BCAP-era. Each of the mappings below therefore carries the provenance flag *anticipatory-projection (dissertation)*.

**Burke pentad → Agency.** The chapter's whole expository thrust — exertion of agency through control and movement — is structurally aligned with Burke's Agency pentad-term (the means / instruments of action). The symbolic / mimetic / symbiotic continuum is, in pentadal vocabulary, a typology of Agency. The secondary mapping is *consubstantiation / identification* (Burke 1950) as theoretical complement to Calleja's player-as-extension-of-game framing (Morris cited): the rhetorical figure by which actor and instrument become one substance is structurally what Calleja describes as the disappearance of mediation. This is the cleanest pentad mapping in the volume — every other PIM dimension has a more contested pentad fit. Cite-check exegetical anchor: NONE.

**Uexküll Funktionskreis.** The player-avatar feedback loop — key-press → avatar response → perceptual feedback → next key-press — thematized most explicitly in the Morris "think with their fingers" passage and the Bladerunner "Quan" passage, is structurally identical to Uexküll's perception-action functional cycle: Merkmal → Merkorgan → Innenwelt → Wirkorgan → Wirkmal. The dissertation's stronger move is that the symbolic / mimetic / symbiotic continuum reads as *variable tightness of Merkorgan-Wirkorgan coupling*: symbolic = loose coupling via encoded mediation; mimetic = partial body-mapped coupling; symbiotic = maximally tight body-mapped coupling. The trichotomy thereby becomes a quantitative dimension within an Uexküllian biosemiotic frame. Cite-check exegetical anchor: NONE. The earlier hypothesis (v3 plan) that Calleja's "affordance" use establishes a Gibson-mediated bridge is *falsified* — the lone affordance-use at p.69 is generic; Gibson does not appear in the volume.

**Aristotelian aisthesis-kinesis + dunamis / hexis via Bowin AMT; kairotic readiness via Heidegger BCAP — STRONGEST CHAPTER FOR THIS ANCHOR.** Three convergent textual sites underwrite this:

1. **Ergodicity-as-disposition propagated from IG-03.** Calleja's IG-03 framing of the player as disposed-to-act pre-figures Aristotelian *dunamis* (potency) and *hexis* (settled disposition acquired through habituated practice) without the vocabulary. IG-04 supplies the strongest realization: the "disposition to act" phrase appears verbatim at p.70 in the Red Orchestra sniper passage, where the player's kinesthetic involvement is constituted by *readiness-to-act*, not by realized motion. Kinesthetic involvement, on the Aristotelian translation, is *dunamis-actualized-as-energeia through skill-acquired hexis*.

2. **Continuity-of-motion analysis.** Sunniva's "continuous motion" / "continuous focus" and Mirror's Edge as "a game about creating a continuity of movement through an obstacle-filled space" are the paradigm Bowin AMT (Aristotelian Motion and Time) bridge targets. Bowin's aisthesis-kinesis treatment supplies the ontological vocabulary the chapter lacks for what "continuous motion" *is*.

3. **Kairotic anticipation.** The waiting-sniper passage's "tense with anticipation … at the right moment" maps onto Heideggerian BCAP analyses of kairos and anticipatory comportment, and onto Aristotelian hexis-as-readiness. The internalization-of-controls passages (Morris, Bladerunner, Evita) bridge to Heideggerian zuhanden / equipment-totality: the controls have *withdrawn* into ready-to-hand availability when the player feels-as-extension-of-the-game.

Cite-check exegetical anchor: NONE for the SZ-era / BCAP / Bowin AMT register — the single Heidegger cite (p.21) is to *Question Concerning Technology* and is outside both this chapter and the temporal-phenomenological vocabulary the bridge requires.

## 7. Residual phenomenology Layer-A anchor sites

Per the propagated IG-10 finding, Calleja deploys phenomenological-register language in several sites without citing Merleau-Ponty, Husserl, Heidegger SZ-era, or any phenomenologist. Six sites in this chapter:

1. **Morris citation on internalization (p.68)** — "the player feels like an extension of the game or the game feels like an extension of the player." Direct paraphrase of Morris (2002); Morris herself does not invoke phenomenology. Layer-A targets: Heideggerian zuhanden / equipment-totality (BCAP); Bowin AMT hexis; potential Merleau-Pontian body-schema (not in current corpus).

2. **Bladerunner "Quan" passage (p.67)** — "your mind is willing those actions." Participant-vernacular but Calleja preserves it because it names the unmediated-willed-action signature. Layer-A targets: Aristotelian *boulesis* / *orexis-cum-energeia* via Bowin AMT; potential Husserlian I-can / kinesthetic-sensation (not in current corpus).

3. **Sunniva "continuous focus" / "wave" passage (p.67)** — temporal-continuity vocabulary. NOT Csikszentmihalyi-cited. Layer-A targets: Bowin AMT continuity-of-motion; Heideggerian ekstatic temporality (BCAP).

4. **Evita "it's me" / first-person-jump passage (pp.66, 68)** — the chapter's strongest residual-phenomenology site. The "mineness" of avatar-action is described phenomenologically without any phenomenologist cited: thumbing the space key in an FPS is interpreted by the player as a forward jump *she has made*, not as a character's jump she controls; "I am now flying an aircraft … it's me." Layer-A targets: Merleau-Pontian body-schema extension; Heideggerian In-der-Welt-sein via mediated equipment (BCAP analogue); body-as-mine / Leib-Körper distinction.

5. **Disposition-to-act / Red Orchestra sniper passage (p.70)** — "tense with anticipation … the active intention to perform the action at the right time … the expectation of performance can be just as engaging as the performance itself." The strongest IG-03→IG-04 propagated dunamis / hexis bridge. Layer-A targets: Heideggerian kairotic readiness / anticipatory comportment (BCAP); Aristotelian hexis-as-readiness via Bowin AMT.

6. **Closing coda on movement-as-foundational (pp.71-72)** — "most other aspects of involvement in games are dependent on developing at least a basic fluency of movement in the environment." Structurally phenomenological without phenomenologist anchor. Layer-A targets: Merleau-Pontian motor-intentionality as substrate of perception; Heideggerian zuhanden-as-precondition-for-environmental-understanding (BCAP).

These six sites together constitute the chapter's *philosophy-shaped hole* — a sustained register of phenomenological description in a text whose author has read no phenomenology in the body-schema / kairos / hexis register. The dissertation's Layer-A intervention is, in IG-04 more than in any other PIM chapter, an *anchoring* of an existing analytic vocabulary rather than an imposition of a foreign one.

## 9. Summary

IG-04 is a structurally simple chapter doing structurally consequential work. Its theoretical centerpiece is the symbolic / mimetic / symbiotic control continuum (Figure 4.3) — the chapter's primary analytic innovation, and a measurable design variable the dissertation can operationalize. Its agency analysis is Giddens-inflected (capability, not intention or satisfaction) and contingency-saturated (Malaby's calibration thesis). Its phenomenology — internalization of controls, the disappearance of mediation, the mineness of avatar-action, the disposition-to-act of the waiting sniper — is sustained, vivid, and bibliographically unanchored: Morris is the only academic foothold; the rest is participant-vernacular and Calleja's own observation. This makes IG-04 the strongest dimension-chapter site in the volume for the dissertation's three augmentation layers: Burke (Agency pentad-term, cleanly), Uexküll (Funktionskreis as player-avatar circuit, with the symbolic / mimetic / symbiotic continuum reread as Merkorgan-Wirkorgan coupling tightness), and Aristotle / Heidegger (kinesthetic involvement as *dunamis-actualized-through-hexis*; readiness as kairotic comportment; the mineness of avatar-action as Merleau-Pontian body-schema target). The chapter's six residual-phenomenology anchor sites supply the textual hooks. Calleja closes by claiming kinesthetic involvement as the *substrate* of the other PIM dimensions — a claim that the dissertation's Aristotelian-substrate ontology is positioned to ground.
