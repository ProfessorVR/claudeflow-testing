## 1. Argumentative spine

Calleja's Introduction is programmatic rather than exegetical. Across five working pages it executes four moves in sequence: a pervasiveness preamble that licenses theoretical attention to digital games (book p.1); an autobiographical Gzira/Malta vignette that positions the author as first-generation cybernetic native (book pp.1–2); a diagnostic statement of the presence/immersion quandary together with the announcement of *incorporation* as the volume's answer (book pp.2–3); and a chapter-by-chapter preview titled "A Brief Overview" that names every dimension and forward-points to every load-bearing argument (book pp.3–5).

The spine that ties these moves together is lexical-strategic. Calleja avoids using "immersion" and "presence" as primary analytic verbs and instead treats them as flawed inheritances. The genus he installs is *involvement* — the umbrella term under which the six dimensions will be ranged and which spans both the macro phase (off-line) and the micro phase (in-session). The species he installs is *incorporation* — the most intensified and internalized blend of involvement dimensions. The lexical hierarchy (involvement covers; incorporation culminates) is the Introduction's architecture, and it is what the entire remainder of the book proceeds to fill in.

Two metalinguistic moves deserve emphasis. First, Calleja diagnoses the inherited terms differently: "immersion" has lost analytical value through popular and academic overuse, while "presence" is fractured by divergent and even conflicting usage among its main theorists (book p.3). The terms are not interchangeably bad; they are bad for different reasons, which means their replacement need not unify them. Second, Calleja concedes that incorporation is "ultimately a metaphor" like its predecessors. The robustness he claims for it is not literal-ontological accuracy but rather (i) avoidance of certain problematic connotations and (ii) suitability as scaffolding for further research. This is the volume's first warning sign for any reader who wants to ground incorporation ontologically: Calleja explicitly disclaims that warrant.

## 2. The four-conceptual-challenges preview

The Introduction announces that Chapter 2 will "outline four challenges for understanding the experiential phenomenon they refer to in the context of game research" (book p.3). The four challenges are not named or enumerated in the Introduction — only their existence and chapter-location are forward-pointed. Calleja later returns to them in Chapter 10, where incorporation is argued to dissolve all four (per IG-10 deep findings: monolithic-conception, single-dimensional agency, exclusionary logic, and undifferentiated metaphor-mixing). At the Introduction-level the appropriate edge-type is therefore `announces` (forward-pointer), not `defines` or `contests`.

This treatment is deliberately spare. By withholding the four challenges, the Introduction forces the reader to take the diagnostic claim on credit and proceed to Chapter 2. The volume's load-bearing argument that incorporation answers four discrete deficits is thus structurally deferred — which means the Introduction alone cannot ground a reader's confidence that incorporation is more than a relabeling exercise. The earned argument lives in IG-02 plus IG-10.

## 3. The six dimensions preview

The Introduction is the only place in the volume where all six dimensions are previewed in tight succession with definitional one-clause glosses (book p.4, fulltext lines 129–136). Calleja runs through them in this order and with these characterizations:

| Dimension | One-clause gloss (paraphrased) |
|---|---|
| Kinesthetic | control and movement |
| Spatial | exploration and learning of the game's spatial domain |
| Shared | co-presence, collaboration, and competition with other agents (human or AI) |
| Narrative | formation of an ongoing story plus interaction with the scripted narrative |
| Affective | the affect generated during gameplay |
| Ludic | decision-making in the pursuit of game- and self-assigned goals |

The dimensional preview is wrapped inside the macro/micro frame: each dimension will be analyzed on both phases — macro-involvement covering all off-line forms of involvement (initial attraction, reasons for returning, community participation, off-line planning) and micro-involvement covering moment-to-moment in-session engagement. Two structural facts follow. First, the six dimensions × two phases yields the twelve operative analytic slots into which Chapters 4–9 unfold. Second, the *order* of dimensions in the preview (kinesthetic, spatial, shared, narrative, affective, ludic) is the same order in which Chapters 4–9 are presented and in which IG-03's central PIM apparatus elaborates them — a small but consistent organizational signal.

The Introduction does not specify boundary criteria distinguishing the dimensions from one another, paradigm examples per dimension, or decision rules for ambiguous cases (e.g., spatial vs. kinesthetic in *Half-Life 2*). All of that arrives in IG-03 and Chapters 4–9. The Introduction's contribution at the dimension-level is the simple act of naming all six in one place.

## 4. The incorporation announcement

The volume's first occurrence of *incorporation* in its theoretical sense lands at book pp.2–3 (fulltext lines 82–87). Three claims are bundled together:

1. **The phenomenon is a blend**, not a stand-alone state. Presence/immersion are reframed as blendings of multiple experiential phenomena afforded by involving gameplay. This is the conceptual move that makes the multi-dimensional PIM both necessary and sufficient.
2. **Incorporation is gradient-cumulative**, not categorical. The blend culminates in incorporation only when sufficiently *intensified* and *internalized* — two criteria that the Introduction names but does not operationalize.
3. **Incorporation is metaphor + scaffold**, not ontological discovery. The concession that incorporation is "ultimately a metaphor like presence or immersion" disarms any reading that takes incorporation as a literal account of what happens in the head of a player.

This packaging is consequential for downstream analysis. Per the IG-10 deep finding propagated into this unit, Murray's "deep immersion" (1998 *Hamlet on the Holodeck*) is **absorbed, not refused**: incorporation is Calleja's term for immersion-as-transportation, expanded by the double-axis structure that IG-10 installs (book p.172, fulltext lines 6058–6062 per the IG-10 deep edges). The Introduction does not yet articulate the bidirectional axis, the spatial+kinesthetic cornerstone requirement, or the simultaneity condition — all of these are IG-10 deliverables — but it leaves room for all three. Crucially, the Introduction's "avoids problematic connotations" framing (not "replaces the phenomenon") is the first quiet move toward IG-10's absorptive posture toward Murray and Ryan in particular.

The unresolved problem at the Introduction-level is what gates the transition from involvement to incorporation. The Introduction installs intensity + internalization as criteria without specifying thresholds; anyone reading only the Introduction could misapply incorporation as a synonym for strong involvement (see tension IG-00.T3). IG-10 closes this gap with the spatial+kinesthetic cornerstone and the simultaneity-of-both-axes requirement.

## 5. Cross-pipeline bridges

Five anticipatory bridges surface from the Introduction's vocabulary and announcements:

**Dalton dissertation.** This is the highest-priority bridge per plan v3 §1.2. The Introduction itself is the doorway for dissertation adoption: by announcing the PIM as the volume's apparatus and explicitly offering it as "a common, holistic framework that will facilitate further research" (book p.5, fulltext lines 158–161), Calleja licenses the instrumental use that the dissertation will make of it. Two specific Intro lemmas migrate cleanly to dissertation use: "subjective distance between player and game environment" (a candidate dissertation primitive for what virtual-worlds analysis measures) and "blending of experiential phenomena" (a candidate dissertation justification for multi-dimensional analysis).

**Phantasia Cluster.** The Introduction's "transported our imaginations to the places represented on screen" (book p.1, fulltext lines 20–21) and "having the chance to inhabit those landscapes, albeit in an abstractly represented simulation" (book pp.1–2, fulltext lines 26–27) are imagistic-cognitive moves that anticipate phantasma operations without naming them. Forward-points to IG-05 Spatial Involvement as the primary Phantasia bridge anchor. Anticipatory provenance — Calleja never invokes *phantasia* vocabulary.

**Heidegger BCAP / Bowin AMT.** The macro/micro temporal-phase distinction previewed at book p.4 is the strongest pre-augmentation bridge to phenomenological time-analysis. Calleja's preview uses no phenomenological vocabulary — macro and micro are pitched operationally (off-line vs. moment-to-moment) — but the distinction maps directly onto BCAP / Bowin AMT terrain. Anticipatory.

**Uncomfortable Situations (Gross 2017).** The affective-involvement dimension is named in the Introduction's preview (book p.4) but does no analytic work there. The primary Gross bridge is IG-08; the Introduction contributes only the dimension-name placeholder. Note from Phase 0 cite-check: Calleja never cites Gibson, so the Gross↔Calleja bridge cannot be anchored via a shared Gibson affordance theory.

**Uexküll *Foray*.** The cybernetic-circuit + symbiotic-machine-relationship framing (book pp.1–2, fulltext lines 40–46) is structurally analogous to the *Funktionskreis* perception-action loop. The habitation metaphor (book p.5, fulltext lines 153–156) is structurally analogous to *Umwelt* as bounded subjective environment. Both mappings are singly-anchored anticipatory projections per Phase 0 — the Gibson-as-implicit-Uexküll-bridge hypothesis is falsified (Calleja uses "affordance" 11 times across the book without Gibson attribution; Gibson appears in neither references nor index). The Uexküll bridge therefore cannot be claimed as a mediated bridge already latent in Calleja; it remains a singly-anchored projection awaiting the future Gibson pipeline.

## 6. Residual-phenomenology flags

Four passages exhibit residually phenomenological vocabulary without crediting any phenomenologist — strong candidates for Manual Section E Layer A anchoring.

**RP1 (moderate).** "Subjective distance between player and game environment" together with "the sensation of inhabiting the space represented on-screen" (book p.2, fulltext lines 63–66). The lexicon — distance-as-felt, inhabiting-as-mode-of-being, sensation-of — does phenomenological work. The closest unstated anchors are Heidegger's *Entfernung* (de-distancing, *Being and Time* §23) and Merleau-Ponty on inhabited space. Calleja cites neither.

**RP2 (moderate).** "Cybernetic circuit" + "symbiotic relationship with machines" (book pp.1–2, fulltext lines 40–46). Quasi-phenomenological framings of human–machine coupling. Calleja's later citation of Dovey + Kennedy (in IG-03) does some grounding work, but the Introduction-level vocabulary outpaces it. Closest unstated phenomenological anchors: Merleau-Ponty's chapter on the blind man's cane (technical incorporation as bodily schema extension) and Uexküll's *Funktionskreis*.

**RP3 (mild).** "Blending of experiential phenomena" + "intensified and internalized blend" (book p.3, fulltext lines 79–82). The term "internalized" has a developed phenomenological lineage (Husserl on *Vergegenwärtigung*; later embodied-cognition reuses) but may be doing more colloquial than technical work in Calleja's prose. Lower-yield anchor candidate.

**RP4 (high — highest-yield).** Habitation as the surviving spatial-phenomenological metaphor (book p.5, fulltext lines 153–156): "the sense of virtual environment habitation." This is the trope Calleja's entire constructive project rides on, and it is the trope he never philosophically grounds. Heidegger's *Bauen Wohnen Denken* (1951) is the most direct anchor; Bachelard's *Poetics of Space* an alternative. The dissertation's Heideggerian wing should expect to do significant work here — habitation is exactly the term that demands Layer A grounding because Calleja installs it without explanation.

All four flags share a common structure: Calleja deploys vocabulary with phenomenological provenance to do constructive theoretical work without naming the tradition. This is consistent with the volume-wide cite-check finding (zero Aristotle, zero Merleau-Ponty, only one Heidegger citation at p.21 to *Question Concerning Technology*). The dissertation's Layer A augmentation is therefore not exegetical recovery of latent Calleja-phenomenology but rather creative grounding of Calleja's quasi-phenomenological vocabulary in the philosophical tradition that vocabulary belongs to.

## 7. Tensions internal to the Introduction

Three tensions are operative within the Introduction itself (independent of cross-pipeline projections).

**T1 — metaphor-disclaimer vs. analytic-superiority claim.** Calleja simultaneously concedes that incorporation is "ultimately a metaphor like presence or immersion" and claims it "provides a more robust concept for researchers to build on." Robustness cannot live in literal accuracy — Calleja just denied that — so it must live in something else: in the metaphor's structural tracking of the multi-dimensional phenomenon, or in its pedagogical scaffolding properties. This tension is consequential for any project that wants to ontologically ground incorporation (e.g., the dissertation's Layer A). The grounding must be argued against the grain of Calleja's metalinguistic stance, not as exegetical recovery of something latent.

**T2 — other-media frameworks: rethinking vs. expansion.** Calleja says digital-game involvement requires "an expansion and, at times, a rethinking" of theories developed for other media. The two operations differ in degree: expansion preserves the framework's core, rethinking revises it. Calleja does not specify which inherited frameworks get which treatment. The chapter-by-chapter posture turns out variable (e.g., per IG-03 deep, Aarseth's ergodicity is extended; per IG-10 deep, presence-theory's exclusionary logic is repudiated). The Introduction leaves the gradient unresolved.

**T3 — involvement universal vs. incorporation gated.** Involvement is offered as the umbrella; incorporation is the intensified + internalized culmination. The Introduction names intensity and internalization as criteria but does not specify thresholds — neither what counts as sufficiently intensified nor what counts as sufficiently internalized. IG-10 closes this gap with the spatial+kinesthetic cornerstone and the simultaneity-of-both-axes requirement, but a reader of the Introduction alone has no way to decide whether a given experience counts as incorporated. This is a structural feature of the Introduction's programmatic role.

## 8. Consistency with IG-03 and IG-10 propagated findings

**IG-10 finding (Murray absorbed, not refused).** Per propagated finding from IG-10 (book p.172, fulltext lines 6058–6062): "Incorporation is our term for immersion as transportation" — Murray's 1998 deep-immersion concept is preserved-and-expanded inside incorporation, not displaced. The Introduction is consistent with and quietly anticipates this stance. Its phrasing — incorporation "avoids a number of problematic connotations that are present in [presence and immersion]" — speaks of connotations being avoided, not of phenomena being refused. The phenomenon-cluster captured by the inherited terms is preserved; only the terms and their connotative baggage are replaced. This means a reader could in principle reconstruct the IG-10 absorptive-not-refusive posture from the Introduction's own language, although the Introduction does not explicitly make the move.

**IG-03 finding (PIM as structural articulation of involvement, with macro/micro phases).** The Introduction's preview at book p.4 is fully consistent with IG-03's apparatus. The Introduction installs the dimension-list and the macro/micro distinction; IG-03 supplies the boundary criteria, operational definitions, and decision rules. No contradiction.

## 9. Game-loci summary

Three games are named in the Introduction, all passing-illustrative rather than analytic.

- ***Kick-Off*** (Dino Dini, 1989) — book p.2, Gzira vignette. Exemplar of digital game offering structured competitive play with rule-enforcement by the machine. Anticipates the machine-upheld-rules motif developed in IG-01 (Coded Rules) and IG-09 (ludic involvement under rule-constraint).
- ***Grand Theft Auto IV*** (Rockstar North, 2008) — book p.3, family-resemblance pairing. One pole of the heterogeneity argument, paired with *Tetris*. Recurs in IG-01 as a virtual environment with embedded games.
- ***Tetris*** (Pajitnov, 1985) — book p.3, family-resemblance pairing. Abstract-puzzle pole. Recurs in IG-02 as Salen+Zimmerman's immersive-fallacy exemplar and in IG-10 as excluded from incorporation by the spatial+kinesthetic cornerstone requirement.

Platforms named (Atari VCS, Spectrum ZX, Amstrad CPC, Commodore 64, Commodore Amiga) at book p.1 are not game-loci; no specific titles are attached to them in the Introduction.

## 10. Interlocutors

This is a deliberate rhetorical strategy: the Introduction names concepts, not people.
