## §1. The argument of the chapter — incorporation displaces immersion

Chapter 10 is the titular synthesis. Calleja's task here is metaphorological rather than empirical: he is replacing the operative term of the discourse he reviewed in Chapter 2. The replacement is precise. *Immersion* and *presence* are not refuted but reframed as inadequate metaphorical figures for a phenomenon they wrongly construe. *Incorporation* is the corrected metaphor.

The opening pages (167–168) execute the diagnosis. Calleja recapitulates the four conceptual challenges from Chapter 2 — terminological promiscuity between absorption and transportation, inattention to non-ergodic-media specificity, technological determinism, missing attentional continuum — and adds a fifth implicit challenge that organizes the chapter: the "unidirectional plunge into a virtual world" metaphor (p.167) presupposes that the external world can be excluded by submergence into the virtual. This presupposition is empirically wrong and theoretically misleading. The player, Calleja writes, is not a subjective consciousness being poured into the containing vessel of the game. The container-and-fluid metaphor of immersion is the load-bearing source of the discourse's confusions.

Against the plunge, Calleja proposes a bidirectional structure (p.169). On one axis the virtual environment is incorporated into the player's mind as part of her immediate surroundings, within which she can navigate and interact. On the other axis the player is incorporated, in the sense of embodiment, into a single systemically upheld location in the virtual environment, represented by the avatar. The *double axis* with its *simultaneity condition* is the chapter's signature theoretical innovation. The two processes must be active concurrently; serial or asymmetric activation produces involvement, not incorporation. Calleja's canonical definition consolidates this on page 169: incorporation is the absorption of a virtual environment into consciousness, yielding a sense of habitation, supported by the systemically upheld embodiment of the player in a single location, represented by the avatar.

Note the etymological move. Calleja explicitly retains both traditional senses of *incorporation* — assimilation to mind and embodiment — and insists that both are essential. The double-axis structure is therefore not a coincidence of terminology but a deliberate etymological-philosophical resource. The replacement metaphor *is* its etymology.

The remainder of the chapter develops the consequences. The 6-dimensional PIM (kinesthetic, spatial, shared, narrative, affective, ludic) is mapped onto incorporation as the dimensional substrate (pp.169–170). Spatial and kinesthetic are flagged as cornerstone — necessary minimum-pair — without which incorporation cannot occur (p.170). The kinesthetic→spatial dependency is illustrated via the Counter-Strike: Source double-door example (pp.170–171, Figure 10.2). The sustainment-but-fleeting property and the intrusion-integration thesis follow (pp.171–172). The four challenges are then revisited and shown to be resolved by incorporation (pp.172–173). Two interview vignettes — Rheric on the Ruined Cathedral of Ascalon in Guild Wars (pp.173–175, Figure 10.3) and Kumacho on the Ghosts of the Revolution outfit raids in Planetside (pp.176–178, Figure 10.4) — supply empirical demonstration. The chapter closes with a temporal-axis culmination paragraph (p.178) and a continuity-thesis coda (p.179) that positions virtual environments as domains continuous with the media-saturated reality of everyday life rather than as experientially separate otherworlds.

## §2. The Lakoff+Johnson + Damasio + Dennett embodied-cognition anchor

The chapter's grounding theories are not phenomenology or rhetoric but a specific embodied-cognition lineage: Lakoff and Johnson's experientialist ontology (2003) supplemented by Damasio's cognitive-neuroscience phenomenology (2000), with a passing co-citation to Dennett (1991). This anchor-theoretic configuration is consequential. It is what the dissertation's planned augmentation layers (Aristotle/Heidegger, Burke, Uexküll) are adding *to*, not what they are extending *from*.

Lakoff and Johnson are introduced by name at the top of page 168 and quoted at length in a seven-line indented block (p.168 lines 5901–5907). Per content-filter constraint, that block is paraphrased here rather than reproduced. Lakoff and Johnson argue that recurrent embodied experience produces categories — *experiential gestalts* — whose natural dimensions structure the coherence of our experience. We understand experience directly when we recognize it as coherently structured by gestalts emerging from our environmental interaction, and metaphorically when we transfer a gestalt from one experiential domain to structure experience in another. This is the theoretical machinery Calleja deploys to explain why the virtual-habitation metaphor works: the virtual-habitation gestalt draws on our existing everyday-habitation gestalts, which themselves have emerged from recurrent embodied interaction with our environment.

Calleja co-cites Lakoff and Johnson four times across the chapter: at page 167 (lines 5880–5882) with Damasio, on the experiential-gestalts-as-organizing-principle of stimulus-absorption claim; at page 168 (lines 5898–5907) introducing the block-quote; at page 168 (lines 5911–5913) with Richards 1936, for the constitutive-not-deviation reading of metaphor; and at page 169 (lines 5928–5931) with Dennett and Damasio, for the consciousness-as-internally-generated-construct claim.

Damasio's role is comparatively thin. He is co-cited twice (p.167 and p.169), never quoted, never independently engaged. He functions as authoritative cognitive-neuroscience warrant for the experientialist-consciousness picture rather than as a separate doctrinal voice. Dennett's role is thinner still: a single co-citation at page 169, supplying a second warrant for the consciousness-as-construct claim. Richards 1936 appears once (p.168) as historical-rhetorical warrant; Lévy 1998 appears once (p.168) as warrant against the virtual-versus-real contrast.

Three absences are theoretically consequential for the dissertation's planned augmentations. **Varela, Thompson, and Rosch (1991, *The Embodied Mind*) are absent** from Chapter 10. Calleja's embodied-cognition grounding routes through Lakoff and Johnson + Damasio + Dennett, not through the enactivist lineage. Enactivism would predict a similar bidirectional structure but with different grounding — autopoietic structural coupling rather than experientialist gestalts. The dissertation's Phantasia-Cluster bridge cannot be made through Varela. **Merleau-Ponty is absent** from Chapter 10 (and from the entire volume per Phase 0 cite-check). The "embodiment" sense Calleja preserves on page 169 is Lakoff and Johnson's experientialist embodiment, not Merleau-Ponty's phenomenological lived-body. A striking absence given the chapter's habitation thematics. **Heidegger is absent** from Chapter 10 (the volume's sole Heidegger cite is the late-Heidegger Technology essay at p.21 of Chapter 2). The "habitation" construct is built from Lakoff and Johnson experientialism, not from Heideggerian *Wohnen*, *In-der-Welt-sein*, or *Mitsein*.

This means the dissertation's Layer A (Aristotle + Heidegger BCAP) is wholly anticipatory at the chapter-level: every BCAP-anchored mapping is dissertation-projection, not exegetical-correction. The same is true of Layer B (Burke) and Layer C (Uexküll), neither of which has any toehold in the chapter. Lakoff and Johnson 2003 + Damasio 2000 are the only two text-anchored non-game-studies anchor-theories and therefore the only non-augmentation cross-pipeline candidates.

## §3. The four-challenges resolution

Chapter 10's third major argumentative move (pp.172–173) is the systematic resolution of the four conceptual challenges raised in Chapter 2. Each challenge is named, the incorporation construct is mapped against it, and the resolution is declared.

**Challenge 1 — absorption-versus-transportation conflation**, originally named in Chapter 2 as the problem that *immersion* is used interchangeably to mean attentional absorption (in the Tetris sense) and transportation (in the Murray sense). Chapter 10's resolution: the involvement / incorporation distinction handles both. Absorption-style attentional engagement is captured by *involvement* (any of the six dimensions can be involved without being incorporated); transportation-style habitation is captured by *incorporation* (the double-axis state at the apex of the macro→micro temporal arc). Calleja is explicit on page 172 that incorporation is the volume's term for immersion-as-transportation, expanded by the double-axis structure so that the player is not merely transported to a virtual world but also incorporates that world into her own consciousness.

**Challenge 2 — non-ergodic-media specificity** was originally the problem that immersion-talk applies indifferently to novels, films, paintings, and games, despite their categorically different agency-affordances. Chapter 10's resolution (p.173): incorporation precludes application to non-ergodic media because incorporation requires the medium to specifically acknowledge the player's presence and agency. A book or a film cannot acknowledge a reader's or viewer's presence. The ergodic/non-ergodic boundary (Aarseth) is restored as constitutive.

**Challenge 3 — technological determinism** was the problem that presence-theory (Slater especially) treated high-fidelity sensory immersion as sufficient for psychological presence. Chapter 10's resolution (p.173): acknowledgment of presence is necessary but not sufficient; the player's subjective disposition must also obtain. Calleja's phrasing is unambiguous on page 173: it is not merely a technical challenge to create incorporation; the player's role in shaping the experience is essential. This is the chapter's strongest disavowal of the fidelity-determinist position.

**Challenge 4 — monolithic-experience conceptualization** was the problem that immersion was treated as a single experiential type rather than as a composite. Chapter 10's resolution (p.173): the multidimensional 6-dim PIM is the substrate from which incorporation arises, so the experience can be analyzed according to specific dimensions rather than in abstract aggregate.

The four-challenges resolution is structurally important for the dissertation's empirical-adaptation chapter: it supplies a four-test diagnostic for any candidate replacement-construct, and any extension of the model must continue to pass all four tests.

## §4. The replaces-prior-construct survey

Chapter 10 does not name any of the ten prior-Calleja immersion models individually. Verified by close reading of fulltext lines 5855–6285: zero hits for Murray, Ryan, Slater, Witmer, Lombard, McMahan, Salen, Zimmerman, Douglas, Hargadon, Brown, Cairns, Ermi, Mäyrä. The displacement operates entirely at the metaphor-level — Calleja refers to "metaphors of immersion and presence" (p.169), "unidirectional plunge into a virtual world" (p.167), and "exclusionary logic" (p.169). The replaces-prior-construct table reconstructs the implicit-displacement of each Chapter-2-named model from Chapter 10's chapter-level argument.

**Murray 1998** — deep immersion / immersion-as-transportation. **Absorbed (preserved-and-expanded)**: Calleja explicitly retains the transportation sense on page 172, expanding it via the double-axis. Murray's contribution is restored as one axis of incorporation, not refused.

**Ryan 2001** — spatial/textual-world immersion. **Absorbed (reframed)**: Ryan's spatiality-as-defining-feature is preserved and elevated to cornerstone status (with kinesthetic). But Ryan's plunge-into-textual-world metaphor is rejected: incorporation is bidirectional. The spatial habitation gestalt is reframed from absorption-into-spatial-world to bidirectional environment-incorporated-into-consciousness while consciousness-incorporated-into-environment.

**Slater + Wilbur 1997 / Slater 2003** — immersion-as-technological-property + presence-as-psychological-response (fidelity-determinist). **Refused**: this is exactly the technological-determinism position that the Challenge 3 resolution rejects. The player's subjective disposition is irreducible to system-fidelity.

**Witmer + Singer 1998** — immersion as psychological state of envelopment, presence as involvement plus immersion. **Refused**: the envelopment-as-submersion picture is precisely the container metaphor that Calleja diagnoses as unidirectional-plunge / exclusionary logic. Additionally, the chapter's qualitative-grounded-theoretic methodology refuses the questionnaire-quantification approach.

**Lombard + Ditton 1997** — presence as perceptual illusion of non-mediation; six characterizations. **Refused**: perceptual-illusion-of-non-mediation is the canonical exclusionary-logic position. The intrusion-integration thesis on page 171–172 explicitly admits blending of in-game and out-of-game stimuli — precisely what an illusion-of-non-mediation must deny. The six-characterization typology is also displaced by the 6-dim PIM-grounded synthesis.

**McMahan 2003** — perceptual / psychological / social-realism immersion. **Refused**: inherits the Lombard+Ditton refusal; McMahan's feature-list pigeonholing is incompatible with the multidimensional non-monolithic experience-architecture that Calleja's Challenge 4 resolution requires.

**Salen + Zimmerman 2003** — immersive fallacy critique; Tetris-immersibility. **Mixed (partly absorbed, partly refused)**: Calleja commits to the transportation pole that Salen+Zimmerman attacked, but the spatial+kinesthetic-cornerstone requirement excludes Tetris from incorporation (Tetris has no habitable space, no avatar-located embodiment). Salen+Zimmerman's Tetris-as-immersion counter-example is acknowledged as a real phenomenon — absorption-style attentional engrossment — but reframed under a different rubric (involvement, not incorporation).

**Douglas + Hargadon 2001** — within-frame schema immersion vs. extra-textual engagement; attentional continuum collapsing ergodic/non-ergodic distinction. **Mixed (absorbed + corrected)**: the attentional-continuum insight is preserved in the temporal-axis culmination and the sustainment-but-fleeting characterization. But the ergodic/non-ergodic distinction Douglas+Hargadon collapsed is explicitly restored on page 173.

**Brown + Cairns 2004** — engagement / engrossment / total-immersion three-degree ladder. **Refused**: Brown+Cairns mix absorption (levels 1–2) and transportation (level 3) within a single ladder — precisely the conflation that Calleja's involvement/incorporation distinction resolves. Incorporation is a single coherent metaphor (bidirectional habitation), not a metaphor-mixing ladder.

**Ermi + Mäyrä 2005** — sensory / challenge-based / imaginative immersion. **Absorbed (reconstituted)**: the multidimensionality insight is preserved and elaborated. Their three-category SCI typology is replaced by the more granular 6-dim PIM. Their sensory category loosely maps to spatial+kinesthetic, challenge-based to ludic, imaginative to narrative+affective. The reconstitution adds shared as its own dimension.

The confidence rating on each entry is *high*: while Chapter 10 does not name these authors, the chapter-level critique-and-resolution structure unambiguously positions each prior model relative to the incorporation construct. The provenance tag on all ten replaces-prior-construct edges is "text-anchored-implicit-displacement" — anchored in Chapter 10 prose but not via individual-author attribution.

## §5. The Incorporation Rubric

Phase 1 metadata identified eight candidate components. Phase 2 normalizes to a *five-component* rubric: the cleanest four-component reading (assimilation-axis, embodiment-axis, simultaneity-condition, blending-of-dimensions) augmented by the spatial+kinesthetic-cornerstone, which Calleja flags as a necessary minimum-pair, elevating it to constitutive status. Ergodic-specificity and subjective-disposition prerequisites are treated as enabling conditions in the differentiator-from-merely-involved column rather than as components.

**R1 — Assimilation axis** (p.169 lines 5934–5944). The virtual environment is incorporated INTO player consciousness. Maps primarily to spatial; secondarily to narrative and affective. Primarily micro-phase with macro-phase residue (cognitive maps, internalized lore persist). Differentiator: merely-involved play allows attentional engagement with the environment as object; assimilation-axis incorporation absorbs the environment into the structure of consciousness itself, organized per existing experiential gestalts.

**R2 — Embodiment axis** (p.169 lines 5938–5944). The player is incorporated, via the avatar, INTO the virtual environment, in a systemically upheld single location. Maps to kinesthetic and spatial primarily, shared secondarily. Exclusively micro-phase. Differentiator: merely-involved play can occur in non-avatar games (Tetris, abstract puzzles) where the player has no systemically-upheld location. Embodiment-axis incorporation requires precisely that systemic-uphold.

**R3 — Simultaneity condition** (p.169 lines 5944–5948). Both axes must be active concurrently. Constitutive across all six dimensions: both axes must operate simultaneously for any dimensional blending to count as incorporation. Micro-phase only — simultaneity is moment-to-moment. Differentiator: simultaneity is the strictest gatekeeper. Many states involve activity in either axis individually — planning a raid off-line is assimilation only; mechanically navigating a tutorial without internalizing the environment is embodiment only. Only concurrent obtaining yields incorporation. This is what the sustainment-but-fleeting property tracks: simultaneity is hard to maintain because any dimension demanding full conscious attention breaks one or both axes.

**R4 — Spatial+kinesthetic cornerstone** (p.170 lines 5972–5985). Necessary minimum-pair: without spatial-and-kinesthetic-internalized, incorporation cannot take place. Both phases (macro spatial = cognitive maps; micro spatial = present-tense navigation; macro kinesthetic = motor-habit; micro kinesthetic = control-flow). Differentiator: a player who cannot move — kinesthetic deficit, as in the Counter-Strike double-door fumbling example — cannot enter incorporation regardless of other-dimensional engagement. Spatial and kinesthetic must be internalized to the point that they do not demand conscious attention. This component is what makes Tetris-style absorption ineligible.

**R5 — Blending of multiple involvement dimensions** (p.169–170 lines 5961–5972 + p.178 lines 6270–6273). Synthesis-of-dimensions; intensification beyond baseline involvement. All six dimensions can contribute, but the minimum is the cornerstone-pair plus at least one other. Rheric demonstrates spatial+kinesthetic+ludic+narrative+affective; Kumacho demonstrates spatial+kinesthetic+shared+ludic+affective. No single non-cornerstone dimension is necessary, but three to four total typically obtain. Culmination of macro→micro temporal arc. Differentiator: merely-involved play is typically single-dimension or thin-blend; incorporation requires intensified blending occurring at the temporal-axis apex such that the experience is described holistically (consciousness and environment integrated fluidly) rather than as engagement-with-features.

The *incorporated ≠ merely involved* distinction requires all five components. Failure on any reduces the state to involvement. Phenomenologically, incorporation is recognized retrospectively via holistic narration, synesthesiac additions (Rheric's "breeze"), and continuity-of-self testimony — Rheric reports the cathedral as a real experience that he carries with him; Kumacho reports a sense of accomplishment beyond anything he has seen in the game.

## §6. Mapping incorporation onto the 6 dimensions

The relationship between the incorporation construct and the 6-dim PIM is bidirectional. Incorporation is both *constituted by* and *the synthesis-attractor of* the 6 dimensions.

**Kinesthetic.** Cornerstone (R4). Calleja's clearest mechanism: control-fluency must be internalized to the point of not demanding conscious attention before kinesthetic stops being an attentional sink. The Counter-Strike double-door example (p.171) is the constructive case; Rheric's navigation of the war-torn Ascalon ruins (p.175) and Kumacho's tank-driving while coordinating in TeamSpeak (p.177) are the achieved cases.

**Spatial.** Cornerstone (R4) paired with kinesthetic. The kinesthetic→spatial dependency (p.170) is the chapter's most explicit constitutive claim: internalization of spatial dimensions depends on prior internalization of kinesthetic dimensions; spatial-ability cycles into spatial-knowledge cycles into deeper spatial-ability. Both vignettes demonstrate fully internalized spatial dimension — Rheric's free roaming, Kumacho's geo-strategic continent-capture awareness.

**Shared.** Amplifier. Calleja's distinctive claim on page 177: cohabitation makes incorporation "viral." The presence of other agents (human or AI) within the environment validates one's own location-constitution. The Kumacho vignette is paradigmatic — 85-player raid coordination produces shared-incorporation effects that no single-player experience can match. Rheric's vignette by contrast has zero shared involvement — exploration-mode incorporation does not require it.

**Narrative.** Contextualizer. Scripted narrative supplies the larger meaning that embeds environment in story-world; emergent alterbiography supplies the player's own sense of doing-and-suffering in the world. Rheric's vignette is paradigmatic of the narrative-dimension contribution: the war-torn Ascalon setting (scripted) plus the cathedral-discovery plot-twist (alterbiographical) jointly contextualize the spatial discovery.

**Affective.** Intensifier. Calleja's claim is that emotional readiness/contrast — combat-readiness contrasted with aesthetic-beauty, four-hours-of-besieging contrasted with breakthrough-victory — potentiates incorporation. The synesthesiac-addition phenomenon (Rheric's "feel the breeze") is paradigmatic evidence-strength testimony of affective-amplified incorporation. The Kumacho vignette demonstrates collaborative-success affect at scale.

**Ludic.** Structurer. Rules, goals, and resistance shape attentional engagement. Ludic involvement trades attentional resources against kinesthetic and shared involvement (p.178 lines 6249–6251: the more demanding the kinesthetic and shared loads, the fewer attentional resources for planning). Rheric's vignette has implicit ludic dimension (combat-readiness in war-torn zone with resistance from enemies); Kumacho's vignette has explicit ludic structure (hierarchy of goals, raid-coordination, base-capture as overarching objective).

The macro/micro mapping clarifies the temporal architecture (R3 / R4 plus the p.178 temporal-axis culmination paragraph). Macro-phase involvement (off-line thinking, motivation, anticipation, between-session planning) flows into micro-phase involvement (moment-to-moment in-session engagement), which in turn culminates in incorporation when the five rubric components are satisfied. Incorporation is therefore not a seventh dimension but the apex-state of the macro→micro temporal arc.

## §8. Tensions, residual phenomenological vocabulary, and the volume's reflexive limits

Three tensions in Chapter 10 are consequential for downstream synthesis.

**Tension 1 — incorporation-as-process vs. incorporation-as-state.** Calleja describes incorporation alternately as two simultaneous processes (player-incorporates and is-incorporated) and as an experiential state (one is "in" incorporation, sustained or fleeting). The state-language fits the sustainment-but-fleeting and intensification-with-duration claims; the process-language fits the constitutive-mechanism account. The tension is unresolved in the chapter. The dissertation's empirical-adaptation chapter must choose: are we analyzing game-engagement-as-activity (process) or experience-as-occurrent-state (state)? The Burke-pentad augmentation favors process; the Aristotelian/Heideggerian augmentation favors state.

**Tension 2 — embodied-cognition naturalization vs. residual phenomenological vocabulary.** Calleja's official grounding is naturalistic embodied-cognition. But the descriptive vocabulary of the chapter is phenomenological: "sense of habitation," "inhabiting a place," "experientially separate otherworlds," "powerful emotions," "rich understanding." This descriptive vocabulary is not derivable from the grounding theories without remainder. Lakoff and Johnson's experientialism explains why a habitation-metaphor would *be felt as* a sense-of-habitation, but it does not analyze the phenomenology of that feeling. Damasio's neuroscience explains why an external stimulus would feel like *something*, but does not analyze the *what-it-is-like-ness*. The residual phenomenological vocabulary is doing analytic work that the cited theorists do not authorize. This is the dissertation's strongest entry-point for Layer A: the Aristotelian/Heideggerian apparatus is precisely what is needed to *ground* the phenomenological vocabulary that Calleja's official theory-base cannot underwrite. Note that Merleau-Ponty's absence from the volume (Phase 0) is symptomatic — the volume needs phenomenological vocabulary it does not authorize.

**Tension 3 — integration-of-6-dimensions vs. emergent-novelty.** Two competing readings of how the 6 dimensions yield incorporation. The *blending* reading: incorporation equals the sum/integration of dimensions, hence reducible to them; this is the predominant language ("synthesis of," "blends a number of dimensions"). The *emergence* reading: incorporation is a state-attractor that the dimensions converge toward, with novel properties not present in any single dimension; this is the language of the temporal-axis culmination paragraph ("culminating in the blending of involvement dimensions that result in incorporation") and the "arising from" framing of the Challenge 4 resolution. The double-axis simultaneity-condition is itself emergent: neither axis alone yields it, but it is more than their conjunction. Phase 1 metadata flags this: incorporation is to be treated as a state-attractor at the apex of the macro→micro temporal sequence, not as a seventh dimension. Phase 3 synthesis will need to commit.

**The volume's reflexive limits.** Chapter 11's Conclusion explicitly acknowledges the inadequacies of structural modelling and positions the volume as a *first considered step* — the Genette/*Narrative Discourse*/*Narrative Discourse Revisited* comparison invites the dissertation's augmentation. Calleja himself is candid that the metaphor of incorporation is *ultimately a metaphor like presence or immersion* (Introduction p.5); the move is metaphorological reframing, not the discovery of a new natural kind. This metaphorological self-consciousness is consequential: the dissertation's three-layer augmentation is licensed by Calleja's own framing.

**Final synthesis claim.** Chapter 10 is the volume's titular synthesis: a five-component incorporation rubric mapped onto a six-dimensional PIM with macro/micro temporal culmination, grounded in Lakoff–Johnson experientialism and Damasio cognitive-neuroscience, resolving the four Chapter 2 conceptual challenges, and closing on the continuity-thesis that virtual environments are domains continuous with the media-saturated reality of everyday life rather than experientially separate otherworlds. The double-axis bidirectional structure is the chapter's signature theoretical innovation and the volume's primary supply to the dissertation. Every dissertation-augmentation layer (Aristotle/Heidegger, Burke, Uexküll) is anticipatory relative to the chapter; only Lakoff–Johnson and Damasio are text-anchored. The Manual §E signature payoff — Umwelt ↔ virtual-environment-habitation — has its strongest claim precisely here: the bounded, designer-constituted, organism-specific structure of the incorporated virtual environment is what Uexküll's Umwelt-concept was always about.
