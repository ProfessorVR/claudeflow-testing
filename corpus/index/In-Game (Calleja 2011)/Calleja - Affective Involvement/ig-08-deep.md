# IG-08 Affective Involvement — Phase 2 Deep Analysis

## Dimension Operationalization

Calleja's affective dimension is the emotional-tonal modulation of player engagement: the mood-and-emotion tonality running through the other five dimensions of the Player Involvement Model. Affect is generated, on his account, by the cybernetic feedback loop between mind and machine, where cognitive, emotional, and kinesthetic channels recursively couple (p.135, lines 4732-4737). What distinguishes affective involvement in digital games from affective response to non-ergodic media is not the underlying emotional repertoire but the conjunction of textual interpretation with the performed practice of playing — a conjunction Calleja terms the rhetorical strategies of affect (p.136, lines 4762-4765). The dimension operates on two phases. The macro phase concerns off-line and on-ramp affective engagement: the choice of a game for arousal-regulation purposes, the anticipatory affective register that draws a player toward a title in the first place, and the cultural-imaginative escapism that game-worlds afford. The micro phase concerns moment-to-moment designed and precipitated emotional response during gameplay itself. A third subsection, Aesthetics and Affect, operates as a swing-section bridging the two phases by examining how aesthetic environments and adaptive systems jointly produce the affective register a player inhabits during play. The closing coda terminologizes the dimension as that which "accounts for the rhetorical strategies of affect that are either purposefully designed into a game or precipitated by the individual player's interpretation" (p.146, lines 5156-5159) — the designed-versus-precipitated distinction is the dimension's signature analytic move.

The full chapter occupies twelve pages (135-146), making it the volume's shortest dimension chapter. It is structurally sparser than its peers along several axes — a sparsity that is itself a substantive Phase 2 finding, developed in a later section of this analysis.

## Macro Phase: Escapism and Affect, with Tuan as Principal Voice

The Macro Phase opens with the cybernetic-feedback-loop premise: digital games are powerful affect-media because the cognitive-emotional-kinesthetic loop produces a recursive input-output process that non-ergodic media cannot match. The single emotion-theoretic citation that supports this opening is a parenthetical co-citation of Bryant and Davies 2006 and Grodal 2000 at line 4738 — these are the chapter's only two emotion-theory citations, and Grodal receives no further engagement. From Bryant and Davies, Calleja imports the construct of excitatory homeostasis (p.136, lines 4748-4761): the media-psychology proposition that individuals select entertainment to achieve an optimal arousal level, with understimulated persons gravitating toward arousing content and overstimulated persons toward calming content. Games, on this view, offer a wide range of arousal-regulation activities and (in single-player titles) the ability to tweak difficulty settings to fine-tune the affective register; MMOGs compensate for limited setting-adjustment by providing varied activities suiting different emotional needs.

The first occurrence of the chapter's signature phrase — "rhetorical strategies of affect" — appears at lines 4762-4764 in service of the claim that game design, like other forms of textual production, is imbued with affective rhetoric. The recursive input-output process emphasizes this rhetorical power beyond what non-ergodic media achieve. No rhetorical theorist is cited to anchor this language; the term arrives unsupported.

The bulk of the Macro Phase (pp.136-139, roughly four pages of the chapter's twelve) is then occupied by an escapism-defense apparatus organized around Yi-Fu Tuan 1998, J. R. R. Tolkien 1983, and a light citation of Andrew Evans 2001. Calleja first draws the escape-versus-escapism distinction (lines 4778-4795): escape names a permanent one-way move (fleeing danger, migrating from war-torn states), whereas escapism implies movement-with-return-expectation — the escapist remains grounded in a location or context that must be returned to. Both share the striving toward a better state of being, but only escapism is the proper category for game-engagement. The defense then mounts the imagination-as-escapism-faculty argument: mental imagery permits the retention of past experiences, potential future actions, and present-moment awareness simultaneously, and this faculty is what allows the temporary detachment that defines escapism. The Tuan block quote (lines 4824-4831, drawn from Escapism pp.5-6) supplies the keystone claim: a human being is "an animal who is congenitally indisposed to accept reality as it is," and culture rests on the faculty of "seeing what is not there." A second Tuan citation (lines 4842-4845, p.23) defines the real as "local pattern or order" — legibility, decreased contingency — and emphasizes that both natural and designed environments belong to the real, with interpretability as the determinant.

Tuan is Calleja's principal Macro-phase interlocutor, and the choice is consequential. Tuan is a geographer of place and imagination, not an emotion theorist proper. The chapter's anchor for affect-theory thus arrives via cultural geography rather than via any of the obvious emotion-science or affect-theory traditions. The relativization-of-escapism argument that follows (lines 4846-4858) extends Tuan's framing: if reality is relative to context and interpretation, then the avoidance of reality is also relative — no activity is essentially escapist (the baker-imagining-rock-stardom and musician-dreaming-of-bread-making symmetry illustrates the point). Oriel's testimony about Menethil Harbor in World of Warcraft (lines 4867-4873) — sunset waiting for the boat, Gadgetzan night-flying, snow-crunching in Winterspring, red-and-gold leaves in Azshara, "I want to live there" — anchors the aesthetic-habitation-as-affect argument that the relativization sets up.

Tolkien 1983 ("On Fairy Stories") is then introduced in two extended block quotes. The first (lines 4883-4889, p.132) supplies the "secondary worlds" doctrine: the author makes a world the reader's mind can enter, in which what the author relates accords with the laws of that world; "willing suspension of disbelief" is rejected as a misdescription of what happens during reading-into-the-world. The second (lines 4894-4905, p.148) supplies Tolkien's defense of escapism against academic scorn — the Escape of the Prisoner versus the Flight of the Deserter distinction, which Calleja deploys to inoculate game-engagement against the same scorn. The Macro Phase closes (lines 4906-4911) by positing digital games as continuators of secondary-world creation, with MMOGs extending the tradition through persistence and synchronous-shared experience.

## Micro Phase: Emotional Affect during Gameplay (Bryant + Davies and Grodal Without Further Theory)

The Micro Phase opens with the designed-versus-precipitated affect distinction (lines 4913-4927): the rhetorical strategies designed into game environments aim at specific emotional responses, but designers cannot dictate the effects absolutely. Player disinterest, interruptions from external demands, divergent personal interpretation, and ineffective design all attenuate the designer-to-player affective transmission. Yet design choices still tend to encourage particular reactions, which Calleja takes as licensing analysis of designed affect notwithstanding the gap.

The mode-of-representation argument follows (lines 4928-4933): although certain (unnamed) theorists and designers have argued that graphical power is not what makes games compelling, the evocative power of graphics and sound should not be discounted. Inauro's testimony then anchors the atmospherics-as-affect-design thesis (lines 4934-4939): Duskwood Forest, Silverpine Forest, and the Undead starting zones around Brill in World of Warcraft are "atmospheric because of the lighting, sound, good monster types and placement... thematically consistent," and explicitly "so Mirkwood (from The Hobbit)." This testimony is the chapter's clearest single atmospherics exhibit.

The verisimilitude-versus-media-pastiche paradigm case follows (lines 4956-4991), pairing Medal of Honor: Allied Assault (2015 Inc., 2002) with Red Orchestra: Ostfront 41-45 (Tripwire Interactive, 2006). Medal of Honor draws its visual style strongly from contemporary Hollywood World War II films — Saving Private Ryan (Spielberg, 1998) and Pearl Harbor (Bay, 2001) — with lighting, palette, and dramatic action reminiscent of these films, and several scenes reproduced wholesale either as cut-scenes or as playable level sections. Red Orchestra takes the opposite approach: stronger historical accuracy in graphical style and audio effects; limited interface information (only fatigue, injuries, ammo clips); no crosshairs, requiring iron-sights aiming; bolt-action single-shot rifles; prone or kneeling stances for recoil compensation; meticulous uniform, weapon, and object fidelity. Graphical style, audio effects, and physics together create "very different affective experiences in these two ostensibly similar games" (lines 4990-4991). The paradigm case demonstrates that affect is sourced from two distinct registers — atmospherics on one hand, mechanic-mediated constraint on the other.

The Micro Phase closes (lines 4992-5019) with the suppressing-fire-as-apprehension paradigm — the chapter's clearest single exhibit of designed affect at the mechanic level. Suppressing fire, Calleja explains, aims not necessarily at hitting opponents but at making it harder for them to shoot back and at creating "a sense of apprehension." Red Orchestra accomplishes this through graphical distortion (screen blur and shake when leaning out to return fire), loud audio (cover-strike noises that impede registration of surroundings), and environmental physics (destructible cover generating dust and fragments). The combination of visual impediments, iron-sights aiming, and historically-accurate weapon-inaccuracy produces "a heightened sense of urgency, if not panic, in the opposition players." This is mechanic-as-affect-design at its clearest in the volume.

## Aesthetics and Affect: The Swing-Section

The third named subsection bridges Macro and Micro. It opens with Aprile's testimony about Night Elf Land in World of Warcraft (lines 5022-5027): "I love Night Elf Land. It's serene and pretty and the music is nice here... Whenever I don't feel like playing, I come here. I sit and watch the noob night elves come in, listen to their oooohs and aaaawwws. It's cute. I remember when I first came here. It's calming." The testimony anchors the aesthetic-mood-regulation argument: WoW's designers, aware that persistent worlds require positive-emotion-inspiring places to sustain extended participation, created attractive regions with varying palettes of "tastefully blended colors" and a design policy aimed at broad appeal (lines 5028-5036).

The horror counterweight then occupies the second half of the swing-section. F.E.A.R. (Monolith, 2005) is introduced as the action-FPS plus horror paradigm (lines 5037-5085): linear plot, environment-explorable level architecture, sequences alternating between combat, paranormal horror, and plot-furthering terror. Calleja's analysis of F.E.A.R.'s adaptive AI is particularly substantive: AI agents duck, take cover, call for covering fire, and flank from behind reactively rather than via predetermined triggering. The flanking attacks "leave players in an alerted state, watching their backs more carefully. This, in turn, leads to a more intense engagement in the spatial and ludic involvement dimensions of the game" (lines 5071-5073) — Calleja's clearest observation of cross-dimensional affective amplification. The horror sequences (lines 5074-5085) then describe the way prior combat-intensity amplifies subsequent horror: caution generated by potential ambush leaves the player primed to spin and discharge in panic at noise; objects flying off shelves, swaying and flickering light bulbs, and the sudden appearance of a little girl scampering away on all fours generate "an intensified sense of shock and uncanniness."

Left 4 Dead 2 (Valve Corporation, 2009) then exemplifies the adaptive-AI "director" paradigm (lines 5086-5123). The director generates enemies, weapons, ammunition, and other objects depending on the current game-state, calibrating dramatic tension and emotional investment; audio segments, visual effects, and character voiceovers either help or hinder players while contributing to the "tense and dark atmosphere"; and the director sends additional zombie hordes when players "appear too relaxed." The Hunter special-zombie mechanic, which knocks a player to the ground and rapidly hacks at her until a teammate rescues her, generates "considerable panic" exacerbated by the fact that other players are simultaneously under attack. Calleja's strongest single formulation of the chapter's dual-sourcing thesis arrives here: Left 4 Dead 2 is so evocative "because its rhetorical strategies of affect tightly combine the aesthetic and systemic dimensions" (lines 5118-5120). This is the second occurrence of the chapter's signature phrase, and the most theoretically loaded.

The section closes (lines 5124-5151) with a typology of available affective registers — high-adrenaline horror, peaceful aesthetics, media-pastiche visual style, startling-effect exhilaration — and with the packaged-experience defense against Salen and Zimmerman 2003, who are among "a number of game designers who have expressed doubt about the trend toward improving representation at the cost of innovations in design." Calleja's response is that digital games are "not only game systems but, more importantly, are digitally mediated experiences that aim to satisfy the desires generated by movies, literature, or free-ranging fantasy" (lines 5149-5151). The coda (lines 5152-5159) then defines the dimension as encompassing "various forms of emotional engagement, ranging from the calming sensation of happening upon an aesthetically soothing scene, the adrenaline rush of an on-line competitive first-person shooter, or the uncanny effect of an eerie episode in an action-horror game" and as "accounting for" rhetorical strategies of affect, whether designed or precipitated. This is the third and final occurrence of the signature phrase.

## What Is Missing: The Under-Developed-Dimension Status as Substantive Finding

Per Phase 2 special guidance and the propagated findings from IG-03 and IG-10, this Phase 2 deep-analysis treats the chapter's under-developed status as a substantive analytic observation rather than a description-deficit. The Phase 0 flag of "shortest dimension chapter" is corroborated at four distinct levels.

First, page count: at twelve pages (135-146), the chapter is roughly one-third shorter than the typical dimension treatment in the volume. Chapter 4 (Kinesthetic) runs to about eighteen pages, Chapter 5 (Spatial) to about twenty, Chapter 6 (Shared) to about sixteen, Chapter 7 (Narrative) to about eighteen, and Chapter 9 (Ludic) to about twenty.

Second, subsection count: the chapter contains three named subsections (Macro Phase: Escapism and Affect; Micro Phase: Emotional Affect during Gameplay; Aesthetics and Affect) plus a brief unnamed coda. Other dimension chapters typically carry four to five named subsections. The architectural sparsity is independent of and additional to the page-count sparsity.

Third, emotion-theory citation density: the chapter contains exactly two emotion-theory citations (Bryant and Davies 2006; Grodal 2000), and these supply only one imported construct (excitatory homeostasis). The other named interlocutors — Tuan, Tolkien, Evans, Salen and Zimmerman — are cultural-imagination or game-design theorists, not emotion theorists. Chapter 4 by contrast mobilizes seven to eight distinct interlocutors on kinesthetics. The Affective chapter's theoretical density is roughly four times lower than Chapter 4's on the topic the dimension most centrally concerns.

Fourth, theoretical-framework absence: no affect-theoretic, cognitive-emotion-scientific, phenomenological, or rhetorical-theoretic framework supplies the chapter's scaffolding. The specific absences are striking. Antonio Damasio does not appear in this chapter, despite being the most obvious candidate for grounding the cognitive-emotional-kinesthetic feedback-loop premise that opens the chapter — and despite Damasio being cited later in the volume (Chapter 10 Incorporation, pp.167 and 169, in the Lakoff-Johnson-Damasio experientialist-ontology bloc). The Chapter 10 mobilization of Damasio for incorporation-grounding makes the Chapter 8 non-mobilization for affect-grounding a notable non-use rather than a mere oversight. Paul Ekman (basic-emotion theory), Nico Frijda (emotion as action-readiness — directly germane to the cybernetic-feedback-loop framing), Robert Plutchik (emotion-wheel), and Silvan Tomkins (affect theory) are all absent. The affect-theory tradition is absent: no Brian Massumi (the affect-versus-emotion split would have addressed the chapter's mood-emotion conflation directly), no Eve Sedgwick, no Lauren Berlant (cruel-optimism would have grounded negative-affect-as-meaningful), no Sara Ahmed, no Sianne Ngai (ugly-feelings would have grounded frustration-as-engagement-driver). Phenomenology of mood is absent: no Heidegger Stimmung or Befindlichkeit (which would have grounded the WoW aesthetic-mood-regulation as environmental-attunement rather than emotion-as-arousal-tweak), no Hermann Schmitz or Gernot Böhme atmosphere-philosophy (which would have natively grounded the atmospherics-as-affect-source thesis). Csikszentmihalyi flow theory is absent despite topical adjacency. Bernard Perron, the obvious game-affect interlocutor, is absent. And the rhetorical-theoretic tradition is absent: no Burke, no Aristotle, no Hawhee, no Cicero or Quintilian on movere, despite the triple invocation of "rhetorical strategies of affect."

Three further substantive absences are worth specific note. (1) The mood-versus-emotion distinction is theoretically load-bearing in affect theory but is flattened in the chapter through the paired-noun construction "moods and emotional states" deployed interchangeably at lines 4737-4738, 4753-4757, and 4861. (2) Cross-dimensional affective amplification is repeatedly observed (lines 4807-4813 on negative-affect-engagement-driving; lines 5071-5073 on F.E.A.R. flanking-AI heightening spatial and ludic dimensions; lines 5118-5123 on Left 4 Dead 2 cooperative-panic combining aesthetic and systemic dimensions) but no mechanism-theory is supplied. (3) Negative affect is treated only as "better than experiencing boredom" (line 4812) — the affect-theory tradition since Sedgwick and Ngai treats negative affect as substantively generative rather than as boredom-displacement, but this is not engaged.

The chapter is consequently the volume's premier site for substantive theoretical augmentation. It is anticipatory-bridge dense rather than exegetically dense.

## The Rhetoric-Without-Burke Anchor

The chapter's triple invocation of "rhetorical strategies of affect" — at lines 4762-4764, 5118-5120, and 5156-5158 — is the strongest single anticipatory anchor in the volume, per the propagated findings from IG-03 and IG-10. Each occurrence is theoretically load-bearing. The first frames game design as a form of textual production "imbued with the rhetorical strategies of affect," extended in games by the recursive input-output process beyond what non-ergodic media achieve. The second formulation — that Left 4 Dead 2 succeeds because "its rhetorical strategies of affect tightly combine the aesthetic and systemic dimensions" — is the chapter's clearest single statement that affect is dually sourced from environmental (perception-side) and mechanic (action-side) rhetoric. The third occurrence, in the coda, terminologizes the dimension itself as that which accounts for designed and precipitated rhetorical strategies of affect.

The phrase is doing real theoretical work — it is not ornament. Yet at no point does Calleja invoke any rhetorical-theoretic tradition that would warrant the language. The rhetoric is residual: rhetoric-talk without rhetorical theory. This is the volume's loudest anticipatory hook for Burkean augmentation in particular and for Aristotelian augmentation more broadly.

## Anticipatory Augmentation Mappings: Burke, Uexküll, and Aristotle/Gross

### Layer B (Burke pentadic Attitude — the strongest single anticipatory anchor)

Kenneth Burke extended the pentad to a hexad by adding Attitude precisely to capture the affective-modal dimension of motivation — the "how" of Agency, the manner or mood inflecting action. Calleja's affective dimension maps onto Attitude with exceptional cleanliness: affect is the mood-tonality of involvement that modulates and is modulated by kinesthetic Agency, spatial Scene, shared Agents, narrative Act, and ludic Purpose. The triple "rhetorical strategies of affect" without Burke is the volume's strongest anticipatory hook. Burke's *Grammar of Motives* together with Debra Hawhee's *Moving Bodies* (which extends Burke into bodily-rhetoric territory the cybernetic-feedback-loop framing demands) jointly supply the rhetorical-theoretic scaffolding the chapter's phrasing implicitly requires. The designed-versus-precipitated distinction maps directly onto Burke's rhetorical-motive-versus-received-attitude pairing. The cross-dimensional affective amplification phenomenon maps onto Burke's account of Attitude as the modulator across the other pentad terms.

### Layer C (Uexküllian Merktöne and Wirktöne — prominent per plan §1.4)

Jakob von Uexküll's tone-doctrine holds that perceptual and effector signs in the Umwelt are not affect-neutral data but carry intrinsic emotional-tonal coloration. The tick perceives butyric acid not as molecule-data but as food-tone or attack-tone. Calleja's atmospherics passages are paradigmatically tone-colorations of perception-action loops: Duskwood's "dark gloomy fantasy forest" tone (Inauro), Night Elf Land's "serene and pretty" tone (Aprile), Menethil Harbor's sunset-tone (Oriel), F.E.A.R.'s tense-and-dark atmosphere, Left 4 Dead 2's director-modulated tense-and-dark atmosphere, suppressing-fire-induced apprehension-tone. The Inauro Duskwood-as-Mirkwood passage at lines 4934-4939 is the cleanest single Uexküllian-tone exhibit in the volume.

The Uexküllian framework resolves the chapter's atmospherics-versus-mechanics tension (T1) at the level of biosemiotic theory: Merktöne (perception-tones) and Wirktöne (effect-tones) are native dual structures of the Umwelt, integrating perception-side and action-side affective tonality without remainder. Calleja's Left 4 Dead 2 statement that "rhetorical strategies of affect tightly combine the aesthetic and systemic dimensions" is, in Uexküllian register, the observation that perception-tones and effect-tones jointly constitute the player's affective Umwelt. The suppressing-fire mechanic is paradigmatically Wirktöne modulation through destructible-cover physics and screen-blur on lean-out; the F.E.A.R. light-bulb-swaying and scampering-child sequence is paradigmatically Wirkmal-modulation, where changing how objects behave reshapes the Umwelt's affective tone.

### Layer A (Aristotelian pathē + Gross 2017 Uncomfortable Situations — strongest dissertation-bridge)

Aristotle's *Rhetoric* II catalogues the pathē — the patterned emotional responses produced by rhetorical situations: anger and calm, friendship and enmity, fear and confidence, shame and shamelessness, pity, indignation, envy, emulation. Aristotle's account is paradigmatically a situated-affect doctrine avant la lettre: pathē are designed-rhetorical-effects produced by the conjunction of speaker, hearer, and situation. The triple "rhetorical strategies of affect" maps directly onto Aristotle's analysis of pathē as the second of the three pisteis (along with ethos and logos).

Gross 2017 (Uncomfortable Situations) is a confirmed corpus-index pipeline, which makes the Aristotle-Gross pairing the strongest dissertation-bridge in the volume. Gross's situated-affect frame, drawing on Berlant's cruel-optimism, Barrett's constructed-emotion, and Barsalou's situated-conceptualization, supplies what Calleja's chapter manifestly lacks: a theory of how situated game-environments precipitate specific patterned affective responses (Barsalou); a theory of negative affect as substantively meaningful rather than arousal-tweak (Berlant and Ngai); a theory of mood-versus-emotion distinction (Barrett); a theory of cross-dimensional affective amplification (Gross's situated-affect across cognitive-emotional-bodily channels). The excitatory-homeostasis passage receives a Gross critique of arousal-regulation reductionism; the negative-affect-as-legitimate passage receives a Berlant cruel-optimism and Ngai ugly-feelings grounding; the atmospherics-as-affect-design passages receive a Barsalou situated-conceptualization grounding; the adaptive-AI-director passage receives a Gross dynamic-situated-affect grounding.

## Cross-Pipeline Bridges

Beyond the three augmentation layers, four cross-pipeline bridges are anticipated. The Uncomfortable Situations (Gross 2017) bridge is the prominent dissertation-bridge per plan §1.4 and is the substance of Layer A above. The Phantasia Cluster (Hawhee + Papachristou) bridges via Calleja's extended Tuan-on-imagination passage, in which imagination as the faculty of "seeing what is not there," retaining past and potential future and present-moment awareness simultaneously, is a paradigmatic phantasia operation in classical-rhetorical register. Tolkien's "secondary worlds" frame is structurally a phantasia-of-habitable-world. The Heidegger BCAP bridge anchors on the Stimmung doctrine that supplies the mood-versus-emotion distinction Calleja conflates, and on the ekstatic-temporal-attunement themes touched by Tuan-via-Calleja's "mentally detaching ourselves temporarily from the present moment" formulation. The Aristotle pathē + Bowin AMT bridge anchors on the rhetorical-strategies-of-affect triple-invocation and on the cognitive-emotional-kinesthetic feedback-loop framing — Bowin's aisthesis analyses speak directly to the three-channel formulation Calleja states once and does not develop.

## The Dissertation-Augmentation Site

Per plan §1.4 and the propagated findings from IG-03 and IG-10, the Affective Involvement chapter is the volume's premier dissertation-augmentation site. Three features converge to make it so. First, the topic itself — affect, mood, emotion — is the dimension that benefits most from contemporary theoretical resources, since affect theory, cognitive-emotion science, phenomenology of mood, and rhetorical theory of pathē have all developed substantially in ways the chapter does not engage. Second, the chapter's confirmed under-developed status along four axes means that augmentation can substantively extend rather than merely re-describe Calleja's treatment. Third, the chapter's anticipatory anchors are exceptionally clean: the triple "rhetorical strategies of affect" for Burke and Aristotle, the Inauro Duskwood passage and the Aprile Night Elf Land passage for Uexküllian Merktöne, the suppressing-fire and adaptive-AI-director passages for Uexküllian Wirktöne and for Gross situated-affect, the cross-dimensional amplification passages for Gross's multi-channel situated-affect frame, and the cybernetic-feedback-loop premise for Bowin AMT aisthesis.

The dissertation's empirical-adaptation chapter, which selects Calleja's PIM as the operationalizable substrate for virtual-worlds research, accordingly has its strongest substantive intervention available in this chapter. Where Chapters 4-7 and 9 admit augmentation primarily as supplementation to already-substantive treatments, Chapter 8 admits augmentation as foundational scaffolding. The Aristotle pathē + Gross 2017 frame can supply the theoretical engine that the chapter manifestly lacks — and can do so in ways that respect rather than displace Calleja's analytic moves (the designed-versus-precipitated distinction, the macro-micro phase split, the cybernetic-feedback-loop premise, and the cross-dimensional amplification observations all survive and are amplified under the Gross frame). The Burke pentadic Attitude reading then supplies the rhetorical-theoretic vocabulary Calleja's triple "rhetorical strategies of affect" requires but does not name. The Uexküllian Merktöne and Wirktöne reading supplies the perception-action affective-tonality doctrine that resolves the atmospherics-versus-mechanics tension Calleja acknowledges but does not theorize.

The chapter is, in short, the volume's strongest invitation to dissertation-grade theoretical intervention — an invitation issued by what is not said as much as by what is said.
