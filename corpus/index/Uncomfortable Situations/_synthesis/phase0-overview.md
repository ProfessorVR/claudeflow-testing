## 1. Bibliographic spine

Daniel M. Gross's 2017 single-author monograph *Uncomfortable Situations: Emotion between Science and the Humanities* (University of Chicago Press, 174 body pp.) is structured as **Introduction + 4 chapters + co-authored Epilogue**. The volume is positioned as a methodological sequel to Gross's *The Secret History of Emotion: From Aristotle's Rhetoric to Modern Brain Science* (Chicago 2006), continuing the same author's project of treating emotion as constitutively rhetorical and situated, against reductive cognitivist or neuroscientific framings.

Each chapter pairs the volume's recurring theoretical apparatus — situated conceptualization (Barsalou lab), affect theory (Berlant, Massumi via critical adjacency), affordance theory (J. J. Gibson), and the Aristotelian rhetoric-of-emotion inheritance — with a single canonical primary text:

- Ch. 1: Charles Darwin, *The Expression of the Emotions in Man and Animals* (1872)
- Ch. 2: Olaudah Equiano, *The Interesting Narrative of the Life of Olaudah Equiano, or Gustavus Vassa, the African* (1789)
- Ch. 3: Ann Radcliffe, *The Romance of the Forest* (1791)
- Ch. 4: Jane Austen, *Sense and Sensibility* (1811)

The Epilogue, co-authored with behavioral neuroscientist Stephanie D. Preston (Univ. of Michigan; *The Altruistic Brain*, 2014), executes the volume's titular cross-disciplinary brief: it stages an exchange between humanities-side rhetorical analysis and neuroscience-side fMRI/empathy research, anchored on C. P. Snow's "The Two Cultures."

## 2. Argument structure (the "uncomfortable situation" thesis)

Gross's organizing claim — implicit in the Introduction and operative in every chapter — is that **emotion is neither (a) a biologically natural kind invariant across history, nor (b) a culturally constructed performance fully decoupled from biology, but rather a rhetorical-situated phenomenon** that emerges from the negotiated transaction between an organism, its environment-affordances, and the discursive frames available to that organism. The book's title-term, *uncomfortable situation*, names the load-bearing category: a situation where one's habituated emotional repertoire fails to fit the predicament, exposing the rhetorical-constructive layer of emotion to view.

Methodologically, this puts Gross in alliance with:

1. **Lawrence Barsalou and Lisa Feldman Barrett's "situated conceptualization"** of emotion (Wilson-Mendenhall et al. 2011 *Neuropsychologia*, opening footnote of Intro). Barrett's *How Emotions Are Made: The Secret Life of the Brain* (2017) appears throughout.
2. **Lauren Berlant's affect-theory** account of *Cruel Optimism* (Duke 2011) — particularly the diagnosis of attachments that injure us. Berlant edited Gross's Darwin chapter as it first appeared in *Critical Inquiry* 37.1 (2010).
3. **J. J. Gibson's ecological theory of affordances** (*The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception* 1979) — extended by Gross into a theory of *negative*, *hostile*, or *rhetorical* affordances.
4. **The Aristotelian rhetoric-of-emotion inheritance** — Gross's *Secret History of Emotion* established this framework; *Uncomfortable Situations* presupposes rather than re-derives it.

Against this, Gross critiques:
- **Paul Ekman's universal-basic-emotions** program — Darwin appears not (as Ekman has it) as a forerunner of decontextualized facial-expression coding, but as a rhetorician who used emotion-expression illustrations as arguments.
- **Cognitive-utility models** of emotion (Kahneman-Tversky implicitly) — Austen's *Sense and Sensibility* is read as already containing a critique of "expected utility" calculations.
- **Pragmatic phenomenology** as practiced by Hubert Dreyfus — Equiano-chapter argues that Merleau-Ponty's late "Freedom" chapter is more politically capable than the Dreyfus reading admits.
- **"Two Cultures" essentialism** — the Epilogue with Preston contests Snow's dichotomy by demonstrating that neuroscience and humanities methods *can* converge, but only when the experimental object (e.g. the Phelps amygdala/race study) is itself reframed as a rhetorical-situational object.

## 6. Tension list (target ≥8)

1. **Darwin-as-rhetorician vs. Darwin-as-proto-Ekman.** Gross reads Darwin's 1872 photographic illustrations as rhetorical arguments; the Ekman tradition reads them as evidence of biologically universal basic emotions.
2. **Equiano's "bearing up" vs. pragmatic-phenomenological optimism.** Gross argues Equiano's vested-interest disposition exceeds what Dreyfus-style Merleau-Ponty readings can articulate; Afro-Pessimism shadows but is not endorsed wholesale.
3. **Galen's humour-mixing vs. Austen's negotiated mixed feelings.** Ch.4 critiques the "disequilibrium model" (Galen, with a long afterlife) — feelings are not internal proportions but socially-negotiated outcomes.
4. **Gibson affordances *as* rhetorical vs. *as* purely ecological.** Gross extends Gibson against the orthodox ecological-psychology reading by introducing historical-cultural affordances ("cop car roof says *harangue on me*"), risking Gibson-orthodoxy backlash.
5. **Sciences-humanities convergence (Epilogue) vs. irreconcilable difference (titular question).** Question-mark in the Epilogue title is operative — the chapter argues neither pure convergence nor pure dichotomy.
6. **Berlant's pessimistic affect theory vs. Gross's reformist-rhetorical project.** *Cruel Optimism*'s diagnosis is structural; Gross's "uncomfortable situation" theory leaves space for rhetorical-pedagogical intervention.
7. **Barrett-Barsalou situated conceptualization (scientific) vs. affect-theory situatedness (humanities).** The book wagers that they can be made to talk; the methodological scaffolding for that wager is the volume's main contribution.
8. **Universal basic-emotions claim (Ekman) vs. Modern Racism Scale + amygdala correlation (Phelps).** Epilogue argues that even the most "biological" race-perception findings (Phelps lab) only make sense as situational-rhetorical phenomena.
9. **Aristotelian rhetoric-of-emotion (classical) vs. neuroscience-of-emotion (contemporary).** The book bridges, but the bridge is itself the contested object.
10. **Religious / prosthetic emotion (Intro) vs. secular / rhetorical emotion.** Imitation of Christ as performative emotional installation complicates any pure secularization narrative.
