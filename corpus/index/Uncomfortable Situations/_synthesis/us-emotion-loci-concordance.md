# US Emotion-Theory Loci Concordance

**Volume**: Daniel M. Gross, *Uncomfortable Situations: Emotion between Science and the Humanities* (University of Chicago Press, 2017)
**Compiled**: 2026-05-10
**Page-offset rule**: book p. *n* = PDF p. *n* + 10.
**Tier rule**: Tier-A = named in ≥3 units; Tier-B = named in ≤2 units.

---

## 1. Scope and method

This concordance catalogs the emotion-theory and affect-theory loci that *Uncomfortable Situations* mobilizes across all six units (Introduction, four chapters, and the co-authored Epilogue). It draws on every Phase-2 unit JSON (`us-00-intro.json` through `us-05-epilogue.json`), the endnotes captured at `/tmp/uncomfortable-notes-raw.txt`, and the back-of-book index captured at `/tmp/uncomfortable-index-raw.txt`. Where Gross cites a specific page within a secondary work — Berlant's p.5 in *Cruel Optimism*, Williams's p.321 in *The Long Revolution* — that page is recorded as the locus anchor; where no internal page is cited, the locus is anchored by Gross's note number and PDF page.

Sixty loci are recorded, exceeding the ≥25 minimum and ≥25 floor stipulated for the emotion concordance. Nine loci attain Tier-A by appearing across ≥3 of the six units — among them Berlant (*Cruel Optimism*), Wilson-Mendenhall et al. 2011, Barsalou (the lab), Ekman's Oxford-edition Commentaries, Darwin's *Expression*, Ahmed's *Queer Phenomenology*, Chandler's *Archaeology of Sympathy*, Reddy's *Making of Romantic Love*, Kagan's *What Is Emotion?*, and Griffiths–Scarantino 2009 "Emotions in the Wild." Tier-B loci, more numerous, are largely single-chapter anchors (Knight Dunlap 1932 in Austen; Patterson *Slavery and Social Death* in Equiano; Phelps et al. 2000 in the Epilogue).

## 2. Categorial map

Loci sort into four categories that the volume itself treats as the major emotion-theory lineages it negotiates.

**(a) Situated-emotion (Barsalou–Barrett heritage).** The volume opens with Wilson-Mendenhall, Barrett, Simmons, and Barsalou's 2011 *Neuropsychologia* paper "Grounding Emotion in Situated Conceptualization" (EM-003) as its first footnote — the dinner-party scenario at p.1110 is the inaugural exhibit. The lab returns in Chapter 4 (Austen) for catch-trial methodology at pp.1106, 1108, 1112, 1124–1125, and in the Epilogue, where Wilson-Mendenhall is the primary humanities-side counterweight to Phelps's amygdala/race fMRI study. Barrett's Tier-B *TICS* paper on "Language as Context for the Perception of Emotion" (Lindquist–Gendron co-authored, 2007) supplies Fig. 4.4. Knight Dunlap's 1932 *American Journal of Psychology* article (EM-040) is recovered as a pre-Barsalou canonical formulation of situated-emotion ambiguity ("fear-or-anger? Both!" — p.574). Griffiths and Scarantino's 2009 "Emotions in the Wild" (EM-041) operationalizes "emotional capital" and "emotional affordances" at pp.440, 444–445, and their 2011 "Don't Give Up on Basic Emotions" qualifies the basic-emotions program rather than rejecting it.

**(b) Affect-theory.** Berlant's *Cruel Optimism* (EM-001) is foundational — the situation-definition at p.5 (Intro n.3) is what Gross supplements rhetorically — and her edited *Compassion* volume (EM-002, 2004 Intro at p.3) supplies the compassionate-conservatism political-stake argument that Chapter 1 deploys against Cognitive Approaches to Literature. Massumi's "Autonomy of Affect" (1995) and *Parables for the Virtual* (2002) appear via Ruth Leys's critique ("The Turn to Affect," *Critical Inquiry* 2011, p.437). Williams's *The Long Revolution* p.321 ("creditable emotion") is the historicist counter-reference against pre-ideological affect. Ahmed's *Queer Phenomenology* — pp.28–30 in the Intro on comfort, and pp.4–5, 14, 17, 25, 27, 41, 56, 85, 87, 134, 138–139, 154, 165 in the Equiano chapter — is the volume's most extensively-cited affect-theory monograph; the Equiano chapter foregrounds Ahmed's queer-phenomenological account of orientation under slavery. Sedgwick appears only as adjacency. Afro-Pessimism in Chapter 2 surfaces through Moten ("Knowledge of Freedom," *CR* 2004, p.299), Sexton ("The Social Life of Social Death," *InTensions* 2011, p.28), and Hartman's *Scenes of Subjection* (especially p.10, "barbarous archive"). Butler's *Psychic Life of Power* (Intro pp.27–28, "passionate attachments") and Patterson's *Slavery and Social Death* anchor the philosophical apparatus around Equiano's attachment to Pascal.

**(c) Basic-emotions (Ekman / Damasio / Darwin).** Darwin's *The Expression of the Emotions in Man and Animals* (1872; cited from Oxford 1998 third edition, ed. Ekman) is the primary text of Chapter 1 and the volume's most heavily-anchored emotion-theory locus: E pp.21, 22, 82, 139–140, 217–218, 234, 260, 383, 392, 436n16, plus Fig. 1.9. Gross treats Darwin as a rhetorician *avant la lettre*, not an Ekman precursor. Ekman's editorial Commentaries (E pp.21, 83) and 1999 *Handbook* essay "Basic Emotions" (pp.46, 55) are critiqued at Chapter 1's spine; FACS and the *Pictures of Facial Affect* set appear as Fig. 1.5 (PDF 43) and Fig. 1.8 left panel. Damasio's *Feeling of What Happens* pp.66–67 reports the Adolphs–Tranel–Damasio *Nature* 1998 amygdala-trustworthiness study, which is cross-listed here and in the neuroscience concordance.

**(d) Sentimental-literature theorists.** Chandler's *Archaeology of Sympathy* (EM-018) at pp.13, 277, 302 (Intro n.5) and the additional Schiller-mixture reference at Intro n.40 make Chandler the chief Tier-A sentimental-literature theorist. Goldsmith's *Blake's Agitation* pp.12–13 (Intro n.6) cites Chandler. Barker-Benfield's *Culture of Sensibility* (1996), Markman Ellis's *Politics of Sensibility* (2004), and Lynn Festa's *Sentimental Figures of Empire* (2006) form the Intro-n.4 sentimental-literature literature-review cluster. Paul Goring's *Rhetoric of Sensibility* p.153 (Ch.4 n.31) supplies the witnessed-eloquence + mechanics-of-scenes apparatus for reading Austen alongside Richardson, Fielding, McKenzie, and Sterne. Wollstonecraft enters twice: as a *Vindication* p.65 reference for the Dashwood-women parallel (Ch.4 n.36) and as the *Analytical Review* reviewer of Equiano (Ch.2 n.44).

## 3. Preston-side empathy loci

The Epilogue's emotion-theory loci concentrate Preston's contribution: *The Altruistic Brain* (Oxford 2014) as background, the foundational Preston–de Waal 2002 *BBS* perception-action-model paper, the Preston–de Waal 2011 "Altruism" *Handbook of Social Neuroscience* chapter (Epilogue n.16), the Preston–Hofelich 2012 *Emotion Review* empathy taxonomy (Epilogue n.41), and Hofelich's 2012 University of Michigan dissertation (Epilogue n.38; indexed at pp.142, 143). Baron-Cohen's *Zero Degrees of Empathy* (Epilogue n.19), Frith's "Social Brain" *Phil. Trans. B* 362.1480 (Epilogue n.18), and Iacoboni's *Mirroring People* (Epilogue n.3) round out the social-cognitive-neuroscience field that Preston surveys. Carolyn Dean appears twice: her 2003 *Differences* article "Empathy, Pornography, and Suffering" (Epilogue n.4) and her 2014 contribution to the Biess–Gross volume "Erasures: Writing History about Holocaust Trauma" (Epilogue nn.39–40, PDF 153–154) — the latter staging the empathy-failure thread that the Epilogue closes on.

## 4. Cross-pipeline notes

Berlant, Ekman, Ahmed, and Wilson-Mendenhall et al. are the four Tier-A emotion-theory loci with cross-pipeline relevance: each provides an anchor for the Heidegger-and-Rhetoric (2005) bridge insofar as the rhetorical-ontology framework Gross sets out in HR-01-GROSS presupposes exactly the kind of situated, audience-investing emotion-theory that these loci jointly underwrite. Per user directive the bridge is light. Sedgwick, Massumi, and Berlant collectively orient the affect-theory periphery; Ahmed alone is cited extensively enough to anchor a queer-phenomenological reading-thread across the volume.

## 5. Confidence and caveats

OCR confidence is high across the notes apparatus. Some Greek/German diacritics in citation-formatted bibliographies are imperfect (e.g. "Mécanisme" rendered as "Mecaiusme" in places), but author–work–year tuples are recoverable. The Phelps et al. 2000 *Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience* paper — the Epilogue's neuroscience centerpiece — is treated in this concordance only insofar as it figures in the emotion-theory frame; the full citation and internal-page anchors are recorded in the neuroscience concordance.
