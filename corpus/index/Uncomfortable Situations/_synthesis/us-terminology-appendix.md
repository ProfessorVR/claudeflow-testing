# Terminology Appendix — *Uncomfortable Situations*

Extracted from the back-of-book Index (pp. 175–181) under **Option C hybrid filtering**: every concept entry and every italicized work-title entry is included regardless of page-count; every author/interlocutor entry with ≥2 page references is included; author entries with only a single page or single-note reference are excluded as bibliographic crumbs. Final count: **152 lemmas**. Each entry in `us-terminology-appendix.json` carries `page_anchors`, `sub_entries`, `appears_in_units`, and a brief ≤30-word working definition based on volume usage.

## Top ~50 load-bearing lemmas — alphabetical glossary

Each gloss below is the volume's operative working sense (1–2 sentences); see JSON for full page anchors.

- **affect** (TERM-001). Pre-personal, pre-conceptual bodily intensity that Massumi insists is autonomous from emotion. Gross's running position is that the affect/emotion split is a methodological habit, not an empirical fact.
- **affordances** (TERM-003) / **affordance theory** (TERM-004). Gibson's term for action-possibilities offered by an environment to an organism. Ch. 3 reframes literary environments — especially gothic settings — as rhetorical affordance structures.
- **Afro-Pessimism** (TERM-005). The Moten / Wilderson / Sexton position that Black life is not lived within the modern-system codes of state and civil society. Operative in Ch. 2's reading of Equiano.
- **Ahmed, Sara** (TERM-006). Queer-phenomenologist whose work on comfort, orientation, and whiteness is the cross-chapter affect-theory bridge.
- **Aristotle** (TERM-009). Rhetoric Book II's pathē remains Gross's running model for emotion-as-judgment-in-situation across all six units.
- **astonishment** (TERM-011). Le Brun-derived passion linked classically to admiration and fear; the primary category in Ch. 4 for Austen's mixed-feelings scenes.
- **attachment** (TERM-012). Merleau-Pontian intentional bond; in Ch. 2, the analytic of Equiano's relationship to master and to freedom.
- **Austen, Jane** (TERM-013) — primary author of Ch. 4.
- **Barrett, Lisa Feldman** (TERM-014). Affective neuroscientist whose constructed-emotion / situated-conceptualization program is the volume's empirical scaffold.
- **Barsalou, Lawrence** (TERM-015). Cognitive scientist of grounded conceptualization; co-author of the 2011 epigraph paper.
- **basic emotions program** (TERM-016). Ekman's universal-discrete-emotions program. The volume's recurrent critique-object.
- **Bentham, Jeremy** (TERM-019). Utilitarian whose hedonic calculus is the genealogical target of Ch. 4 and the antecedent of Kahneman.
- **Berlant, Lauren** (TERM-020). Affect theorist; *Cruel Optimism* (2011) is the operative situation-analytic Gross supplements rhetorically.
- **Bitzer, Lloyd** (TERM-021). Rhetorician whose 1968 essay on rhetorical situation defines exigence; Gross translates exigence as "basic discomfort."
- **Boyd, Brian** (TERM-026). Literary Darwinist whose *On the Origin of Stories* anchors Ch. 3's critique of evocriticism.
- **brain science** (TERM-028). Damasio/LeDoux/Ekman emotion neuroscience — the running scientific antagonist.
- **Burke, Kenneth** (TERM-029). Rhetorician of comfort/discomfort and recalcitrance.
- **CAL — Cognitive Approaches to Literature** (TERM-039). The umbrella school whose Theory-of-Mind reductionism Gross consistently complicates.
- **comfort** (TERM-043). The volume's central organizing concept: bourgeois comfort as ideological scaffold whose internal discomfort generates rhetorical situation.
- **consilience** (TERM-045). E. O. Wilson's program of unified humanities-and-science explanation; running antagonist in Ch. 3 and Epilogue.
- **Damasio, Antonio** (TERM-053). Neuroscientist of somatic-marker hypothesis; persistent foil.
- **Darwin, Charles** (TERM-054). Author of *The Expression of the Emotions* (1872); recovered in Ch. 1 as rhetorician of emotional situations.
- **discomfort** (TERM-058). The master concept — basic discomfort = rhetorical exigence; the negative condition that produces rhetorical situation.
- **Dreyfus, Hubert** (TERM-062). Phenomenologist of embodied cognition; primary ally.
- **Ekman, Paul** (TERM-070). The volume's principal scientific antagonist (FACS + basic-emotions program).
- **emotion** (TERM-071). Recast from internal-mental-state to externalized, situated rhetorical phenomenon.
- **emotion science** (TERM-072). The post-Darwinian laboratory science (Ekman/LeDoux/Damasio); the volume's critical target.
- **empathy** (TERM-074). Lipps-derived term central to mirror-neuron and social-neuroscience programs; the Epilogue's primary critical object.
- **environments** (TERM-076). The Gibsonian/Lewinian organism-context — Ch. 3's analytic framework.
- **exigences** (TERM-082). Bitzer's term for the imperfection-marked-by-urgency that calls a rhetorical situation into being; translated as discomfort.
- **Expression of Emotions in Man and Animals, The** (TERM-083). Darwin's 1872 primary text of Ch. 1.
- **Extended Mind (EM) theory** (TERM-084). Clark-Chalmers thesis that cognition extends into tools and environment.
- **Facial Action Coding System (FACS)** (TERM-085). Ekman's facial-musculature coding scheme.
- **fear** (TERM-087). Ekman 'basic emotion' whose neuroscientific operationalization the Epilogue critiques via the Phelps amygdala-race studies.
- **Freud, Sigmund** (TERM-090). Source of *Unbehagen in der Kultur* — the German word lurking behind Gross's title.
- **Gibson, James J.** (TERM-093). Ecological psychologist; affordance theorist; anchor of Ch. 3.
- **Gothic fiction** (TERM-096). Walpole/Radcliffe/Lewis genre — the literary archive of hostile environments in Ch. 3.
- **hedonic psychology** (TERM-100). Kahneman-Diener empirical happiness research.
- **Hegel** (TERM-101). Phenomenology of Spirit master-slave dialectic anchors Ch. 2.
- **Heidegger, Martin** (TERM-102). GA 18 (1924 lectures on Aristotle's Rhetoric) is the hidden philosophical hinge.
- **Interesting Narrative…, The** (TERM-109). Equiano's 1789 primary text of Ch. 2.
- **Kagan, Jerome** (TERM-112). The volume's preferred internal-to-science critic of Ekman.
- **Kahneman, Daniel** (TERM-113). Behavioral economist whose hedonic-psychology program structures Chs. 2 and 4.
- **Lewin, Kurt** (TERM-119). Gestalt social psychologist; "hopeless situation" and valence-of-environments anchor Ch. 3.
- **literary Darwinism** (TERM-121). Carroll/Boyd/Gottschall school applying Darwinian theory to literature.
- **Merleau-Ponty** (TERM-128). Phenomenologist whose *Phenomenology of Perception* (1945) is Ch. 2's second epigraph.
- **mixed feelings** (TERM-131). Schiller-derived concept central to Ch. 4 — Austen's analytic of emotional ambivalence as situational rather than psychological.
- **negation** (TERM-137). Horn's natural-history-of-negation framework; key to litotes-affordance argument in Intro and Ch. 4.
- **neuroscience** (TERM-140). The Epilogue's primary critical object; review of money-incentive, reverse-inference, racialized-fear research.
- **Noë, Alva** (TERM-142). Philosopher of perception; *Out of Our Heads* (2009) supplies the contemporary situated-cognition baseline.
- **pathos** (TERM-150). Classical-Aristotelian rhetorical appeal; recast as situated emotional attribution rather than internal feeling.
