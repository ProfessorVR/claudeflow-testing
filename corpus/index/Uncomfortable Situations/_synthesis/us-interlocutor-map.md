# Interlocutor Map — *Uncomfortable Situations*

Collated from the six unit JSONs (US-00 through US-05). Aggregate before deduplication: ~225 mentions; after canonical normalization (diacritic folding, citation-variant collapse): **170 unique interlocutors**. Of these, **30 are Tier-A** (named in three or more units, or promoted on the strength of substantive two-unit anchor roles) and **140 are Tier-B** (one-or-two-unit named appearances). The structural pattern is straightforward: a small group of theorists carries the volume's argumentative throughline across all chapters, while each chapter recruits its own dense set of local interlocutors specific to its primary literary object.

## Tier-A figures by discipline

### Situated cognition / affective neuroscience (the volume's empirical scaffold)

The three figures who do the most argumentative work in the empirical register are **Lisa Feldman Barrett**, **Lawrence Barsalou**, and **C. D. Wilson-Mendenhall**, lead authors of the 2011 *Neuropsychologia* paper "Grounding Emotion in Situated Conceptualization" that supplies the volume's epigraph. Barrett and Barsalou recur across US-00, US-04, and US-05 as the affirmative basis for Gross's "situated emotion" — the explicit alternative to the basic-emotions program of **Paul Ekman** (Tier A, four units: US-00, US-01, US-04, US-05) and the somatic-marker neuroscience of **Antonio Damasio** (Tier A, three units: US-01, US-03, US-05). Both Ekman and Damasio function as the volume's persistent antagonists — what Gross calls the "impoverished Darwinism" branch of the brain-science of emotion. **Jerome Kagan** (Tier A by promotion, US-01 + US-04) provides the volume's preferred internal-to-psychology critique of Ekman: Kagan's *What Is Emotion?* (Yale 2007) is the most-quoted methodological objection to the basic-emotions program.

### Hedonic psychology / behavioral economics

**Daniel Kahneman** is one of the volume's two or three most frequently invoked theorists (Tier A, four units: US-00, US-02, US-04, US-05). His prospect-theory work with **Amos Tversky** (Tier B, single-unit but heavily cited), the *Well-Being* anthology with **Ed Diener** and **Norbert Schwarz**, and "Norm Theory" with **Dale Miller** structure Ch. 2 (Equiano's "hedonic treadmill" reading) and Ch. 4 (Austen on prospects and reference points). **Jeremy Bentham** (Tier A by promotion, US-02 + US-04) is the historical-utilitarian foil whose calculus Kahneman and Gross both rewrite.

### Rhetoric and classical philosophy

**Aristotle** (Tier A, four units: US-01, US-02, US-03, US-04) is the volume's permanent background. **Lloyd Bitzer** (Tier A by promotion, US-00 + US-03) supplies the operative concept of *exigence* that Gross translates as "basic discomfort." **Kenneth Burke** (Tier A by promotion, US-00 + US-03) supplies the complementary apparatus of "recalcitrance" and "comfort/discomfort." **Thomas Rickert** (Tier B, two units) is the modern rhetorical-theory ally on *Ambient Rhetoric*. Gross himself — through *The Secret History of Emotion* (Chicago 2006) and the Biess/Gross co-edited *Science and Emotion after 1945* — is the rhetorician's anchor in the literature.

### Phenomenology and philosophy of mind

**Hubert Dreyfus** (Tier A, three units: US-00, US-01, US-02), **Maurice Merleau-Ponty** (Tier A, four units: US-00, US-02, US-03, US-05), and **Martin Heidegger** (Tier B by single-unit count but Tier A in effect via GA 18 in Intro n.37) form the phenomenological spine of the book. Dreyfus's "Why Heideggerian AI Failed" (2007) is the volume's preferred reading of the embodied-mind program. **Alva Noë** (Tier A, three units) is the contemporary philosopher-of-perception ally whose *Out of Our Heads* (2009) and *Varieties of Presence* (2012) Gross runs throughout. **Andy Clark** (Tier A by promotion, US-00 + US-01) and **Andrea Scarantino** (Tier A by promotion, US-03 + US-05) are the extended-mind / affordance-philosophy interlocutors.

### Affect theory and history of emotions

**Lauren Berlant** (Tier A by promotion, US-00 + US-01) is the volume's most cited affect-theory ally — *Cruel Optimism* (2011) and the *Compassion* anthology are recurrent reference points, and Berlant edited the *Critical Inquiry* essay that became Ch. 1. **Brian Massumi** (Tier B, single-unit but cited multiple times) is the foil for the affect/emotion distinction. **William M. Reddy** (Tier A, three units: US-00, US-04, US-05) supplies the historical-ethnography-of-emotions program, and the "emotive" concept structures the Paxtun-ambivalence reading in Ch. 4. **Ruth Leys** (Tier A by promotion, US-00 + US-05) is the volume's preferred meta-critic of the affect turn. **Sara Ahmed** (Tier A by promotion, US-00 + US-02) supplies queer-phenomenology framing for both the Introduction's "comfort" thread and the Equiano chapter's orientation analysis. **Sianne Ngai** and **Philip Fisher** (both Tier B single-unit) are the literary-affect interlocutors.

### Literary criticism and cognitive cultural studies

**Lisa Zunshine** (Tier A, four units: US-00, US-01, US-03, US-04) is the strawman of the Cognitive Approaches to Literature (CAL) school whose Theory-of-Mind reductionism Gross consistently complicates. Allied / antagonist figures in this register include **Brian Boyd**, **Joseph Carroll**, **Blakey Vermeule**, **Patrick Colm Hogan**, **Ellen Spolsky**, **Mary Thomas Crane**, **Jane F. Thrailkill**, **Suzanne Keen**, and **David Herman** (all Tier B single-unit). **Steven Pinker** (Tier B but cited in four chapters' notes) is the consilience strawman.

### Primary authors

**Charles Darwin** (Tier A by promotion, US-00 + US-01), **Olaudah Equiano** (Tier A, three units), **Ann Radcliffe** (Tier A by promotion, US-00 + US-03), **Jane Austen** (Tier A, four units), **Laurence Sterne** (Tier A by promotion, US-00 + US-01), **Mary Wollstonecraft** (Tier A, three units), **Horace Walpole** and **Matthew Lewis** (both Tier A by promotion as gothic-canon ancestors).

### Critical race and Black studies

**Saidiya Hartman** (Tier A by 2-unit appearance, US-00 + US-02) anchors the slavery-as-emotional-attribution thread; **Fred Moten**, **Frank Wilderson**, **Jared Sexton**, **Frantz Fanon**, **W. E. B. Du Bois**, **Henry Louis Gates Jr.**, and **Paul Gilroy** all appear at Tier B (single-unit in Ch. 2). **Saidiya Hartman**, Moten, and Sexton together form what the volume calls its "Afro-pessimism" register.

### Feminist / queer theory

**Sara Ahmed** (Tier A, two units); **Judith Butler**, **Iris Marion Young**, **Gayle Salamon** (Tier B single-unit US-02). Ahmed's *Queer Phenomenology* is the cross-chapter bridge.

### Empathy neuroscience (Epilogue concentration)

**Stephanie D. Preston** (Tier A by promotion, US-00 + US-05 — co-author of the Epilogue) and **Elizabeth Phelps**, **Frans de Waal**, **Alicia Hofelich**, **Marco Iacoboni**, **Simon Baron-Cohen**, **Chris Frith**, **Russell Poldrack**, **Hannah Faye Chua**, **Tania Singer** — all Tier B but concentrated in US-05 — form the empathy-circuit literature that the Epilogue treats critically.
