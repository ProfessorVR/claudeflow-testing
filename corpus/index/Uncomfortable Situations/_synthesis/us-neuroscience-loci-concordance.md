# US Neuroscience Loci Concordance

**Volume**: Daniel M. Gross, *Uncomfortable Situations* (Chicago 2017)
**Compiled**: 2026-05-10

## 1. Scope and method

This concordance catalogs the neuroscience and cognitive-science loci that *Uncomfortable Situations* mobilizes across all six units. Thirty-five loci are recorded, exceeding the ≥18 floor. Five attain Tier-A status (Wilson-Mendenhall et al. 2011; Barrett 2017 *How Emotions Are Made* and the constructed-emotion program more generally; Kagan 2007 *What Is Emotion?*; Kahneman–Miller 1986 "Norm Theory"; Griffiths–Scarantino 2009 "Emotions in the Wild"; Noë *Out of Our Heads*; and Pinker's "Toward a Consilient Study of Literature"). The categorial map separates loci across nine sub-fields: fMRI / social-neuroscience (Phelps lab, Wilson-Mendenhall lab, Adolphs–Tranel–Damasio), constructed-emotion theory (Barrett's program), fMRI methodology critique (Poldrack reverse-inference; Menon), behavioral-economics (the Kahneman + Tversky cluster), empathy / altruism (the Preston lab and adjacencies), situated cognition / philosophy of mind (Noë, Dreyfus), social-cognitive / mirror-neuron (Frith, Iacoboni), early situated-emotion (Dunlap 1932, Kagan 2007), and literary-Darwinism (Pinker as critique-target).

## 2. The Epilogue centerpiece: Phelps et al. 2000

**Phelps, E. A., O'Connor, K. J., Cunningham, W. A., Funayama, E. S., Gatenby, J. C., Gore, J. C., Banaji, M. R., "Performance on Indirect Measures of Race Evaluation Predicts Amygdala Activation," *Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience* 12.5 (2000): 729–738** is the load-bearing experimental object of the Epilogue. Five internal pages of the original paper are mobilized at book pp.137–140 of *Uncomfortable Situations*: p.729 (the study design), p.730 (the Modern Racism Scale), p.733 (the fMRI scan protocol), p.734 (the results), and p.736 (the interpretation). The Phelps team's correlation between Modern Racism Scale scores and amygdala activation to Black faces is the apparently-most-biological-possible exhibit that the Epilogue then reframes — without dismissing the underlying neuroscience — as a situational-rhetorical object whose proper interpretation requires the situated-emotion framework that Wilson-Mendenhall et al. 2011 supplies on the humanities-friendly side of the same field. The index records "Phelps, Elizabeth A., 137–40, 141, 145." Gross's earlier 2012 essay "Toward a Rhetoric of Cognition" (in Jordynn Jack's *Neurorhetorics*) is self-cited at Epilogue n.34 as the origin of this experiment-critique.

## 3. The Wilson-Mendenhall / Barsalou / Barrett axis

The volume opens with **Wilson-Mendenhall, Barrett, Simmons, and Barsalou, "Grounding Emotion in Situated Conceptualization," *Neuropsychologia* 49.5 (2011): 1105–1127** as its first footnote (Intro n.1). Internal pages 1105 (the abstract framing), 1106 (concepts as Wittgensteinian-without-core), 1107, 1108 (the fear-runner vs. fear-presenter design), 1110 (the dinner-party scenario; the training-phase using two situation types), 1112 (the figure), 1120, 1124 (social-fear activations in left dlPFC + IFG executive-control regions vs. physical-fear motor-region activations), and 1125 (the "specific instances dynamically constructed" claim) are mobilized in Ch.4 nn.17, 22 and the Epilogue's WILSON-MENDENHALL-2011 internal-pagination block.

Barrett's broader constructed-emotion program — *How Emotions Are Made: The Secret Life of the Brain* (2017, Houghton Mifflin Harcourt) — appears as a Tier-A presupposition across the Intro, Austen, and Epilogue. Barrett, Lindquist, and Gendron's *TICS* paper "Language as Context for the Perception of Emotion" (11.8 [2007]: 327–332) supplies Fig. 4.4 (the Jim Webb angry/elated photograph) at book p.117 (Ch.4 n.18). Barrett's earlier 2006 "Are Emotions Natural Kinds?" *Perspectives on Psychological Science* 1.1: 28–58 (the emotion-paradox foundational paper) is background-only.

## 4. Damasio amygdala-trustworthiness (Ch.1)

**Adolphs, Tranel, and Damasio, "The Human Amygdala in Social Judgment," *Nature* 393 (1998): 470–474** is the foundational amygdala-trustworthiness paper, summarized at Damasio's *The Feeling of What Happens* (1999) pp.66–67 (Ch.1 nn.11–12). Damasio's monograph also appears in the Epilogue at p.400 (Epilogue n.32). Index "Damasio, Antonio, 31–32, 41, 84, 153n15, 153n19" confirms the cross-unit distribution.

## 5. Poldrack reverse-inference critique (Epilogue)

**Russell Poldrack, "Can Cognitive Processes Be Inferred from Neuroimaging Data?" *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 10.2 (2006): 59–63** anchors the Epilogue's reverse-inference critique of fMRI (Epilogue n.29). Vinod Menon's "Dissociating Prefrontal and Parietal Cortex Activation during Arithmetic Processing," *NeuroImage* 12.4 (2000): 357–365 (Epilogue n.24) provides a methodological reference for the Epilogue's fMRI-design critique. These two loci together destabilize Phelps-2000-style amygdala-activation-to-emotion inferences without rejecting the underlying experimental work.

## 6. Behavioral-economics cluster (Ch.4 + Equiano)

Six loci form the Kahneman + Tversky cluster mobilized in Ch.4 and (via the *Well-Being* compendium) Ch.2:

- **Kahneman + Tversky, "Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk," *Econometrica* 47 (1979): 263–291** — loss aversion + relative-vs-absolute value (Ch.4 n.24).
- **Kahneman, "Reference Points, Anchors, Norms, and Mixed Feelings," *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes* 51.2 (1992): 296–312** — the title-namesake article for Ch.4's reference-point thesis; the $3000-vs-$5000-raise example; alternative reference points (vehicle, freedom-from-debt). Ch.4 n.24.
- **Kahneman + Miller, "Norm Theory: Comparing Reality to Its Alternatives," *Psychological Review* 93.2 (1986): 136–153 at 145** — emotional amplification; counterfactual availability; phenomenology of surprise; perceived abnormality; actions-of-focal-individual mutability ("blame the victim"). Tier-A: Intro n.41, Ch.2 n.15, Ch.4 n.33.
- **Schkade + Kahneman, "Does Living in California Make People Happy?" *Psychological Science* 9.5 (1998): 340–346** — the focusing-illusion source (Ch.4 n.27).
- **Kahneman, *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (FSG 2011)** — the post-Nobel popularization, cited at Ch.4 n.21 for the Cicero-vivacity comparison.
- **Kahneman, Diener, Schwarz (eds.), *Well-Being: The Foundations of Hedonic Psychology* (Russell Sage 1999)** — pp.xi, 3, 9, 13, 16, 28, 63–80, 311–312, 362, 370, 803–855; instant utility, hedonic treadmill, becoming/being-paraplegic, anchoring effects, ethnic-demographic variance. Mobilized deeply in the Equiano chapter (Ch.2 nn.15–20, 36, 40) and supplementally in Ch.4 (n.29). Index "*Well-Being* (Kahneman), 59, 159n16."

**Brickman, Coates, and Janoff-Bulman, "Lottery Winners and Accident Victims," *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 36.8 (1978): 917–927** anchors the foundational hedonic-treadmill paper at Ch.2 n.16. **Knight Dunlap, "Are Emotions Teleological Constructs?" *American Journal of Psychology* 44.3 (1932): 572–576 at 574** is recovered as a pre-Barsalou canonical formulation of situated-emotion ambiguity ("fear-or-anger? Both!") at Ch.4 n.19 and book p.118. Index "Dunlap, Knight, on situated emotion, 118" confirms.

## 8. Situated cognition / philosophy of mind

**Alva Noë's *Out of Our Heads* (Hill and Wang 2009)** is the volume's most heavily-cited philosophy-of-mind monograph: pp.19–24 (the New Phrenology section, the chapter-1 methodological core), p.24 (brain-scans-are-not-pictures), p.31 (mother-as-psychological-structure), pp.51, 58, 59, 74, 75, 83, 87, 101 ("In the Beginning Was the Situation"), pp.141 and 157 (the bird-flight-by-feathers analogy). Ch.1 nn.13, 14, 39, 50; Ch.2 nn.25, 41. Index "Noe, Alva, 15, 18, 19, 29, 76, 154n25." Noë's second monograph *Varieties of Presence* (Harvard 2012) pp.125–130 anchors the "rhetorical structure of perception" reference in Ch.2 n.24.

**Hubert L. Dreyfus, "Why Heideggerian AI Failed," *Artificial Intelligence* 171 (2007): 1137–1160 at 1160** appears in Ch.1 n.39 (the embodied-AI argument). Dreyfus's "Intelligence without Representation," *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences* 1.4 (2002) is referenced in Ch.2 n.28.

**Griffiths and Scarantino, "Emotions in the Wild," in *Cambridge Handbook of Situated Cognition* (2009)** is the cross-pipeline situated-perspective-on-emotion programmatic paper at pp.438, 440, 441, 443, 444–445 (cultural-scaffolding; emotional capital; emotional affordances). Intro n.7; Epilogue GRIFFITHS-SCARANTINO-2009. Companion **Scarantino + Griffiths, "Don't Give Up on Basic Emotions," *Emotion Review* 3.4 (2011): 444–454** at Epilogue n.5.

## 9. Social-cognitive / mirror-neuron periphery

## 10. Pinker as cross-volume critique-target

**Pinker, "Toward a Consilient Study of Literature," *Philosophy and Literature* 31.1 (2007): 162–178** appears in Ch.1 (cheesecake-for-the-mind, n.41), Ch.3 (evocritical apparatus), and Epilogue n.6 — making it a Tier-A target across the volume. Pinker's *How the Mind Works* (1997) pp.264, 376, 526 also anchors the Ch.3 evocritical critique on savanna preference and aesthetic theory; it is catalogued in the literary concordance (LIT-042) since the citation runs through landscape-and-longing aesthetics.
