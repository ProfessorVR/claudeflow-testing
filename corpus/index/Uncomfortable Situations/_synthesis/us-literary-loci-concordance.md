# US Literary Loci Concordance

**Volume**: Daniel M. Gross, *Uncomfortable Situations* (Chicago 2017)
**Compiled**: 2026-05-10
**Page-offset rule**: book p. *n* = PDF p. *n* + 10.

---

## 1. Scope and method

This concordance catalogs the literary primary-text loci that *Uncomfortable Situations* anchors across the volume. By "literary primary" I mean Gross's load-bearing literary citations: the four core primary texts (one per chapter), the Intro's Sterne and Austen *Persuasion* loci, Darwin's literary citations of Shakespeare and Spenser (via the *Expression*), all ancillary Gothic texts (Lewis's *The Monk*, Walpole's *Castle of Otranto* and *Mysterious Mother*), the eighteenth-century sentimental corpus (Sterne, Fielding, Richardson, McKenzie), the political-philosophy primaries that anchor the Austen reading (Wollstonecraft, Bentham, Mill, Adam Smith), the biblical and devotional primaries (KJV passages, Toplady's hymn), and the art-historical / aesthetic primaries (Lessing's *Laokoön*, Winckelmann, Bell's *Anatomy*, Constable's *Netley Abbey*). Fifty loci are catalogued, exceeding the ≥20 minimum for the literary concordance.

Anchors are recorded at the level of internal page (Austen p.198), chapter (Equiano ch. X), volume-and-page (Radcliffe — Chard ed.), or paragraph (Hegel ¶191) as Gross provides. Editions are tracked where they affect anchor reliability (Carretta for Equiano, Chard for Radcliffe, Kinsley/Lynch for *Persuasion*, Kinsley/Pinch for *Emma*, Ian Jack for Sterne).

## 2. Core primaries (one per chapter)

The volume's four chapter-spine primaries each receive its own dense anchor list. **Darwin, *The Expression of the Emotions in Man and Animals*** (LIT-011 in the emotion concordance) anchors Ch. 1; its internal-page anchors (E pp.21, 22, 82, 139–140, 217–218, 234, 260, 383, 392, 436n16, Fig. 1.9) are catalogued in detail in the emotion concordance under the basic-emotions category. **Equiano's *Interesting Narrative*** (LIT-007) is the most extensively-cited single literary text in the volume, with Gross drawing on the Carretta Penguin Classics edition: the epigraph at p.77; chapter I (pp.40, 43, 45); chapter II (the Middle Passage at pp.52, 55–60, 64); chapters IV–V (Pascal-sells-Equiano at pp.93–94, 97); chapter VII (the Robert King certificate at pp.163–164, and the West-Indies inadmissibility of black testimony at pp.161–162); chapter VIII (the prevenient-grace fall from ballast at pp.87–88); chapter X (the Mount Calvary conversion at pp.189–190, the Toplady-hymn shipwreck at pp.198–199, the early-years predestinarian remark at p.119, the Isaiah 65:24 citation at p.182); and chapters X–XII (the natural-rights critique at pp.109–112). **Radcliffe's *Romance of the Forest*** (LIT-008) is anchored across all three volumes of the Chard edition, with twenty-five separate internal-page citations from p.7 ("Be comforted" / paradiastole) through p.349 (Verneuil's perlocutionary "I know not whether I ought to congratulate or console you"). **Austen's *Sense and Sensibility*** (LIT-001) is anchored at thirteen internal-page loci — the opening "happily settled" at p.3, the closing "happily resettled" at p.289, and the critical-mass cluster at pp.198–221 where Elinor's expected-utility framing collides with Marianne's relative-value retort and Lucy Steele's £200-per-annum secret calculation.

## 3. Austen across the volume

Austen surfaces in three different units. Beyond the Ch. 4 primary text, she anchors the volume's epigraph through *Persuasion* p.198 (Oxford, Kinsley/Lynch ed.) — Lady Russell "must learn to feel that she had been mistaken" — and she serves as the Darwin-to-Austen bridge through *Emma* p.321 (Oxford, Kinsley/Pinch ed.) where Harriet's "no unhappy reverie" is the chapter-1 litotes case. *Pride and Prejudice* appears via Fig. 4.2 (the 1833 Greatbatch illustration of Mr. Bennet's astonishment after Darcy saves Lydia). Austen is one of the volume's three Tier-A literary loci by the cross-unit count.

## 4. Sterne across the volume

Sterne is the second Tier-A literary locus, with *A Sentimental Journey* (Ian Jack Oxford ed.) supplying the Intro's extended set-piece (pp.18–19, hand-holding scene, Intro n.14) and the Ch. 1 closing case (pp.9, 119, 120, 121 — "fear not, envied not" peasants apostrophe). The Intro additionally invokes Walter Shandy's "love is not so much a SENTIMENT as a SITUATION" from *Tristram Shandy* p.475 (Oxford, Ian Campbell Ross ed.) as the coining vocabulary for the entire volume. The Sterne index entry — "Sterne, Laurence, 2, 6–9, 11, 24, 45–49, 124" — confirms the wide distribution.

## 5. Gothic ancillaries

Three Gothic ancillary primaries circulate around Radcliffe in Ch. 3 and the Intro forecast. **Lewis's *The Monk*** (1796) opens Ch. 3 with Antonia's "Is not my ruin completed? Am I not undone, undone for ever?" — short fragment cited at PDF 87, no extended verbatim. **Walpole's *Castle of Otranto*** (1764) appears in the genre-genealogy at PDF 87 and via the 2nd-edition Preface (1765) in the Intro at PDF 32. **Walpole's *Mysterious Mother*** (1768) supplies Radcliffe's epigraph to ch. 3 of *Romance of the Forest* — "How these antique towers and vacant courts / Chill the suspended soul... What a kind of being is circumstance!" — which Gross at PDF 113 calls his nutshell-argument-in-an-epigraph. **Radcliffe's *Mysteries of Udolpho*** also appears in the Intro at p.10 as the projective-emotion example.

## 6. Sentimental-novel corpus

The Intro and Ch. 4 jointly mobilize a wider sentimental-novel corpus: **Sarah Fielding's *David Simple*** (Sabor ed. pp.236–238, Intro n.9); **Richardson's *Pamela*** (via Reddy n.13 and Goring n.31); **McKenzie's *The Man of Feeling*** (via Goring p.153 n.31); Sterne (above). Pope appears twice: pseudonymously in Horn (p.304, Ch. 1 n.45) and via the OED 'bear' entry citation of *Essay on Man* (1733) — the latter anchoring the lexical case for Equiano's "bearing up." Shakespeare anchors twice independently of the Darwin-via-Expression route: the OED 'bearing' entry citation of *Much Ado About Nothing* (1600) in the Equiano chapter, and *As You Like It* II.1.3–7 ("Here feel we but the penalty of Adam") as background to Radcliffe's epigraphic poetics. Toplady's hymn No. 312 "The Spiritual Victory" (1776, Carretta n.43) is sung by Equiano during the Cadiz shipwreck.

## 7. Political-philosophy primaries (Austen chapter cluster)

**Wollstonecraft's *Vindication of the Rights of Woman*** p.65 (Norton 1988, Ch. 4 n.36) supplies the "equivocal humiliating situation" block-quote as the Dashwood-women parallel; her *Analytical Review* vol. 4 (1789) pp.27–29 (Ch. 2 n.44) is also catalogued in the emotion concordance as Equiano's contemporary reviewer. **Bentham** (1776 / 1780, Ch. VI "Of Circumstances Influencing Sensibility") and **Mill** (*London and Westminster Review* 33 [1840]: 257–302) supply the utilitarian backdrop that Austen's S&S is read as critiquing. **Adam Smith's *Theory of Moral Sentiments*** p.59 (Prometheus 2000) is cited at Ch. 1 n.30 as the "patron-saint-of-universal-emotion" ironical anchor — the same Smith that Chandler runs through (Chandler p.302).

## 8. Biblical and devotional primaries

Tier-A among the non-novel primaries, biblical citations cluster at two locations: Intro p.14 (Eph 5:2, John 4:19, 1 Pet 3:14 — the four-mode reading of *Imitation of Christ*) and Equiano X (the conversion set-piece at pp.189–190 mobilizing Acts 4:12, Romans 7:9, John 3:5, Romans 8:14, Acts 8:26; with Isaiah 65:24 cited at p.182 and an Isaiah 42:16 paraphrase at p.63). Aquinas's *Summa Theologiae* (passions tracts) is invoked at Intro p.14 without direct page citation.

## 9. Visual / art-historical primaries

The Darwin chapter's Laocoön reading mobilizes four visual-art primaries: **Virgil's *Aeneid* 2.223** (Ahl trans. p.35) on the mugitus; **Winckelmann's *Reflections*** p.30 (Fusseli trans. 1765) on edle; **Lessing's *Laokoön*** (cited via Darwin E p.436n16); **Charles Bell's *Anatomy and Philosophy of Expression*** pp.174–175 (London 2006 [1844]) as Darwin's polemical foil and Fig. 1.7 source. The Radcliffe chapter contributes **Constable's *Netley Abbey*** (c.1833, Fig. 3.2) and **Gilpin's *Observations*** (1788) p.13 on ruined abbeys naturalized into landscape. The Equiano-period geological-aesthetic background runs through Burnet's *Telluris Theoria Sacra* (Amsterdam 1694, via Chard's editorial note 390n265 in Radcliffe ch.).

## 10. Philosophical primaries that read as literary scaffolding

Three additional philosophical primaries are catalogued here rather than in the rhetoric concordance because they anchor literary readings rather than rhetorical-theoretical positions: **Hegel's *Phenomenology of Spirit*** (Findlay trans., paragraphs 186, 187, 188, 189, 191, 193, 198, 206, 217 — master-slave dialectic and unhappy consciousness) provides the philosophical scaffolding for the Equiano chapter; **Merleau-Ponty's *Phenomenology of Perception*** (Colin Smith trans., pp.84, 100, 136, 172n1, 228, 245, 263, 289, 425) anchors both the Equiano reading and an ancillary Koffka reference in Radcliffe; **Heidegger's GA 18** (1924 Aristotle lectures, Michalski ed. p.261, *Aus-der-Fassung*) supplies Intro n.37 as a BCAP-pipeline bridge.

## 11. Special handling: content-filter

The Equiano slave-narrative violence and the Radcliffe / Lewis Gothic-threat scenes are paraphrased throughout this concordance with page anchors only. No primary-text passage exceeds the 25-word limit. Lewis's Antonia "undone" fragment (≈17 words) is the longest verbatim citation, taken directly from Gross's own short quotation at PDF 87.
