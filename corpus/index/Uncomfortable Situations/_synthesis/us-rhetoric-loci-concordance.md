# US Rhetoric Loci Concordance

**Volume**: Daniel M. Gross, *Uncomfortable Situations* (Chicago 2017)
**Compiled**: 2026-05-10
**Page-offset rule**: book p. *n* = PDF p. *n* + 10.
**User directive note (load-bearing)**: Gross 2005 (HR-01-GROSS) ↔ Gross 2017 bridge is LIGHT only — same-author concept-continuity, no full author-evolution deliverable.

## 1. Scope and method

This concordance catalogs the rhetorical-classical loci and Gross-self-citation loci that *Uncomfortable Situations* mobilizes across its six units. Thirty-one loci are catalogued, exceeding the ≥15 floor. Four loci attain Tier-A status (Aristotle's *Rhetoric* generally, Bitzer 1968 "The Rhetorical Situation," Gross's own *Secret History of Emotion* 2006, and the Biess–Gross *Science and Emotion after 1945* 2014). Gross 2005's "rhetorical ontology" essay (HR-01-GROSS) is recorded as Tier-B per user directive — the bridge stays light.

The concordance separates loci into three categorial groups: classical rhetoric (Aristotle, Cicero, Plato, Galen, Le Brun, Peacham, Skinner — twelfth-century-BCE through early-modern), modern rhetoric (Bitzer, Burke, Austin, Horn, Rickert, Catchings, Miller/Haynes, Mailloux, Reid, Innocenti, Wimsatt–Beardsley), and Gross's own self-bridges (2005 HR-01-GROSS, 2006 *Secret History*, 2012 "Toward a Rhetoric of Cognition," 2014 Biess–Gross co-edited volume).

## 2. Aristotle across the volume

Aristotle is the Tier-A backbone of the classical-rhetorical apparatus, indexed at "21, 69, 166n5; on emotion, 40, 41; on hyperbole, 47." Direct citations are concentrated in four loci. **Aristotle, *Rhetoric* Book II (pathē)** is the methodological background across all four chapters but appears most explicitly in Ch.1 (the hatred passage at Darwin E p.234 read as Aristotelian cast-of-characters specification) and Ch.3 (twice — at PDF 102 and PDF 107). **Aristotle, *Rhetoric* 1384b32–35 (Cydias on Samos)** anchors Ch.3's tribunal-of-conscience figure: storytelling as constituting a negative affordance. **Aristotle, *Rhetoric* — the "available means of persuasion" definition** is invoked at PDF 107 as the definition Gibson would blanch at. **Aristotle, *Poetics*** anchors the cathartic mode of the Intro's 7-mode typology (Intro p.10 — Oedipus's suffering producing pleasant relief). The "Aristotle on hyperbole, 47" index entry corresponds to Horn's discussion of Pope-pseudonymous litotes in Ch.1. *Politics* is implicit background only — *zoon politikon* is not directly cited but is presupposed for the rhetoric-of-citizenship axis.

## 3. Frede, Plato, Galen — Ch.4 mixed-feelings cluster

Dorothea Frede's "Mixed Feelings in Aristotle's *Rhetoric*" (Rorty ed. 1996, pp.258–285) is the secondary-source anchor in Ch.4 n.5 for the Phileban-vs.-Aristotelian debate. Gross contests Frede's subsumption of Aristotelian innovation to a Phileban remedial framework: Aristotle, Gross argues, nowhere uses Plato's mixture vocabulary (a point Frede herself admits at p.268). **Galen's humours tradition** is the negative target — Ch.4's titular "mixed feelings" *displace* Galenic disequilibrium with socially-negotiated convergence on character. Gail Kern Paster's *Humoring the Body* (2004) is the modern-historical anchor at Ch.4 n.1. **Plato's sophistry** background runs into the paradiastole discussion at book p.122. The classical-French Le Brun lineage anchors Fig. 4.3 at book p.116 — astonishment-with-fear / admiration-with-astonishment illustrations from *A Method to Learn to Design the Passions* (UCLA Clark Memorial 1980).

## 4. Cicero across Ch.3 and Ch.4

Cicero appears at two related loci. In Ch.3 at PDF 103 the "vir bonus dicendi peritus" formula is invoked as the anthropocentric anchor that Gross contrasts against the Gibsonian environment-that-includes-the-extrahuman. In Ch.4 (n.21) Cicero's *Verrines* return via Beth Innocenti's 1994 *Rhetorica* article, where vivid description (vivacity) supplies the rhetorical analogy for Barsalou-Barrett's scan-protocol mini-narratives. The index confirms "Cicero, 93, 119."

## 5. Renaissance rhetoric — paradiastole

Two Renaissance-rhetorical loci cluster in Ch.4. **Peacham** (presumably *The Garden of Eloquence* 1577/1593) supplies the paradiastole figure: "when the truth is opposed by false terms," recast as Kahneman's negotiator-reframing parallel. **Quentin Skinner's** *Renaissance Figures of Speech* (Cambridge 2007, p.163) is the secondary source for paradiastole / *in utramque partem*, but Gross contests Skinner's generic account by foregrounding Kahneman's recalcitrant-world subject — paradiastole is not merely renaming but renaming-under-resistance. Index "Skinner, Quentin, 122" confirms this is book p.122.

## 6. Bitzer 1968 — the modern-rhetorical pivot

Lloyd Bitzer's 1968 "The Rhetorical Situation" (*Philosophy and Rhetoric* 1.1: 1–14, at p.6) is a Tier-A locus across the Intro, Equiano, and Radcliffe chapters. The Intro (n.36, PDF 30) recasts Bitzer's "exigence" as "basic discomfort"; Ch.3 (PDF 103, n.18) treats Bitzer's audience-and-constraints triad as a proto-affordance theory, with the Mario Savio + Jack Weinberg case study. Index "Bitzer, Lloyd, 150n36; on constraints, 93, 163n19" confirms cross-chapter weight. **Kenneth Burke** is paired with Bitzer twice: *The Philosophy of Literary Form* pp.60–61 (Intro n.38; Ch.1 n.48) on literature-as-equipment-for-living; *Permanence and Change* p.255 (Ch.3 n.19) on "recalcitrance" as 1930s anticipation of Bitzerian constraints. **J. L. Austin's** perlocutionary/illocutionary distinction is invoked at PDF 119 to translate Gibson's direct/indirect divide rhetorically.

## 7. Modern-rhetorical periphery

**Laurence Horn's** *A Natural History of Negation* (Stanford 2001 [1989], pp.xiii, 303–304) supplies the neo-Gricean apparatus for the negation-as-rhetorical-operator argument in Ch.1 (litotes as Q-based-implicature signaling stereotype-violation). **Thomas Rickert's** *Ambient Rhetoric* (Pittsburgh 2013) is cited in Intro n.29 alongside object-oriented philosophy (Serres, Latour, Harman, Bennett) — and Rickert is the manifest-flagged cross-pipeline bridge candidate per the Phase-0 overview (Rickert thanked in Acknowledgments; concept-overlap on situatedness/affordance/environment-as-rhetorical-agent). **Steven Mailloux** is thanked in Acknowledgments but receives no direct citation. **Susan Miller's** *Trust in Texts* p.14 and **Cynthia Haynes's** "pathos@play.prosthetic.emotion" *Works and Days* 13.1–2 (1995) are the etymological sources for the "prosthetic emotion" concept (mode 4 of the Intro 7-mode typology, n.17); **Libby Catchings's** dissertation work is credited as an additional source. **Wimsatt and Beardsley's** "The Intentional Fallacy" (Ch.2 n.37) registers a New-Critical caution within the rhetorical/pathic apparatus. **Thomas Reid's** *Inquiry into the Human Mind* (Intro n.15) anchors the direct-perception background for Gibson.

## 8. Gross's self-bridges (3 + 1)

Per user directive the **HR-01-GROSS "rhetorical ontology" essay (2005)** is treated as a *light* bridge: same-author concept-continuity is recorded — the 2005 essay's rhetorical-ontology thesis is the methodological precursor for *Uncomfortable Situations*' situated-emotion framework — but no full author-evolution deliverable is produced. The locus is tagged Tier-B for that reason. The heavier self-bridge weight goes to **Gross 2006, *The Secret History of Emotion: From Aristotle's Rhetoric to Modern Brain Science*** (Chicago) — Tier-A, cited across Intro p.3 (positioning), Ch.1 n.11 (Damasio critique at pp.21–50), Ch.4 nn.2 and 32 ("The Politics of Pride in David Hume and David Simple"), and presupposed throughout the Epilogue. **Gross 2012, "Toward a Rhetoric of Cognition"** in *Neurorhetorics* ed. Jordynn Jack (Routledge) is self-cited at Epilogue n.34 as the origin of the Phelps-experiment critique. **Biess and Gross (eds.) *Science and Emotion after 1945*** (Chicago 2014) is the seed-volume of the Gross-Preston Epilogue; cross-referenced at Intro nn.24 and 42, Ch.2 n.14 (Daniel Dror's chapter), and Epilogue nn.8 and 33.

## 9. Cross-pipeline edge candidates

Three light edges to HR-01-GROSS (per manifest, primary tier):

1. **Rhetorical-ontology → rhetoric-of-emotion** (concept-continuity): the 2005 essay's claim that rhetoric is the medium of being-with-others-in-language is the methodological precondition for the 2017 volume's situated-emotion framework. No full author-evolution deliverable produced.

2. **Aristotle-Rhetoric-II-pathē (HR pipeline) → US Tier-A Aristotle locus** (RHET-001): a single secondary-tier edge runs through the Phase-0 manifest from US into the BCAP-heritage anchor in HR.

3. **Gross-Kemmann co-editorship** (background-historical context): HR-01-GROSS was an edited-collection introduction, not a load-bearing monograph statement. Lightweight bridge only.

A separate edge to **Rickert's *Ambient Rhetoric*** is anticipated by the manifest but should be deferred: while Rickert is named in *Uncomfortable Situations* Intro n.29 (PDF 26), the bridge requires a corpus-index entry for Rickert that does not yet exist in this corpus (verify in Phase 3E).
