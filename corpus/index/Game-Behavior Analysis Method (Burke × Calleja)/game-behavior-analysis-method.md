## Part A — Theoretical Integration: Why Two Axes

### A.1 The gap each framework leaves

**Calleja's PIM** answers *what is the player doing, and how are they engaged?* It decomposes involvement into six dimensions — **kinesthetic, spatial, shared, narrative, affective, ludic** — each operating across a **macro** phase (off-line: planning, forums, anticipation) and a **micro** phase (in-session, moment-to-moment), culminating in **incorporation** (the player absorbed into, and absorbing, the gameworld). It is a phenomenology of engagement. What it does not supply is *motive*: it can establish that a player is intensely ludically and kinesthetically involved without explaining why the behavior takes the shape it does or what the player is doing it *for*.

**Burke's pentad** answers *why — under what grammar of motive?* Any stretch of human action can be addressed through five terms — **Act** (what was done), **Scene** (where/in what situation), **Agent** (who), **Agency** (by what means), **Purpose** (to what end) — plus the later sixth term **Attitude** (in what disposition), and, decisively, through the **ratios** between them (how Scene constrains Act, how Act remakes Agent, and so on). It is a grammar of motives. What it does not supply is the *structural texture* of engagement through which a motive is actually lived in a game — the phase-distinction, the dimensional intensities, the internalization trajectories that Calleja makes visible.

### A.2 The fused unit of analysis

DIA treats these as **two axes of a single apparatus**:

- **Axis M (Motive — Burke):** the six terms + the ratios (the *dynamics*) + identification/consubstantiation (the *bond*).
- **Axis I (Involvement — Calleja):** the six dimensions × macro/micro phase.

The **atomic unit of a DIA analysis is the grid cell** `(motive term × involvement dimension × phase)` — e.g. *(Agency × Kinesthetic × micro)* = "the player's moment-to-moment use of the control scheme as the *means* of action." The **ratios** describe motion *between* cells. The **keystone** — consubstantiation/incorporation — is read *across* the whole grid: it is simultaneously the motive-grammar endpoint (Burke's *ultimate identification*) and the involvement endpoint (Calleja's *incorporation*).

This is not two reports stapled together. A single behavioral phenomenon is read *once*, through a grid whose rows and columns are each other's explanations: the motive term tells you why the dimensional involvement has the character it does, and the dimensional involvement tells you how the motive is concretely enacted.

### A.3 What each axis adds to the other

| Axis M (Burke) adds to Calleja | Axis I (Calleja) adds to Burke |
|---|---|
| **Motive** behind dimensional intensity (why this involvement, toward what) | **Phase distinction** (macro/micro) the pentad lacks |
| **Cross-dimensional ratios** (PIM treats dimensions as independent; ratios are inherently multi-dimensional) | **Dimensional granularity + intensity + internalization trajectory** |
| **Identification/consubstantiation** as the motive-mechanism beneath incorporation | **A grounded, interview-validated phenomenology** of the lived state the pentad only schematizes |
| **Attitude** as the disposition coloring any act | **Empirical method** (grounded-theoretic, in-environment interview) for evidencing claims |

### A.4 The conflict the method must hold (not collapse)

Burke is **grammatical-motivational** — action explained by motives located in pentad ratios; Calleja is **phenomenological-experiential** — action explained by player-reported lived states. The integration must treat Burke's motive-attribution as a *theoretical supplement* to, not a *displacement* of, Calleja's experiential primacy. Where the registers pull apart (e.g. Calleja's metaphor-disclaimer about incorporation vs. Burke's substance-realism), DIA **moves between registers as the analytic question requires** and marks which register is doing the work. The keystone bridge is a **homology, not an equation** (five divergences; see Part B.4). The method's discipline is to keep the two voices distinguishable on the page.

## Part B — The Unified Grid

### B.1 The validated crosswalk (the grid's spine)

From `validated-crosswalk.json` (Phase 2; bindings confirmed bidirectionally against both texts):

| Motive term (Burke) | Primary involvement dim(s) (Calleja) | Secondary | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Act** | Ludic (the *choosing*), Narrative | Kinesthetic (execution pole) | validated |
| **Scene** | Spatial | Kinesthetic (body-orientation) | validated — strongest |
| **Agent** | Shared | — | validated (deepened by consubstantiation) |
| **Agency** | Kinesthetic, Ludic (the *rule-system*) | — | validated |
| **Purpose** | Ludic, Narrative | Affective (mastery/regulation) | validated |
| **Attitude** | Affective | *(transverse — modifies all five above)* | validated + extended |

**The four-binding bijective core** — `Scene↔Spatial`, `Agent↔Shared`, `Agency↔Kinesthetic`, `Attitude↔Affective` — was reached *independently* from both directions and is the grid's load-bearing structure.

**The Ludic split** (the one productive divergence): the **rule-system** (mechanics, cooldowns, economy) is **Agency** (means); the **act of choosing-under-repercussion** is **Act + Purpose**. One Calleja dimension is dramatistically two moments; the method reads them as separate cells.

### B.2 The ratios — the dynamics layer

Cells are static placements; **ratios are the engine.** The ratios most diagnostic of game behavior (Burke's gloss → the game-behavior question DIA asks):

| Ratio | Game-behavior question | Bound dimension-pair |
|---|---|---|
| **Scene–Act** | How does the gameworld constrain/enable what the player does? | Spatial × Ludic/Narrative |
| **Act–Agent** | How does what the player does remake who they are (alterbiography)? | Ludic/Narrative × Shared |
| **Agency–Purpose** | How do available means shape — or *become* — the player's ends? | Kinesthetic/rule-system × Ludic-goal |
| **Scene–Agent** | How does the gameworld/social-rank qualify the inhabiting player? | Spatial/Shared |
| **Agent–Agent** (co/counter) | How do co-agents and counter-agents structure motive? | Shared (cohabitation/cooperation/competition) |

The **Agency–Purpose** ratio is the single most diagnostic for compulsion: when means drift into becoming their own ends (the grind, the speedrun, the daily-login), Burke's "means become end" *is* the behavioral signature Calleja's ludic-intensity-without-stated-goal records.

### B.3 Attitude — the transverse modifier

Attitude is not a sixth column; it is the *disposition-with-which* any cell is enacted. The five player-attitude registers (from `rm-attitude-as-sixth-term` §E), each foregrounding **Affective** involvement:

**Competitive** (agonistic striving up the rank-hierarchy) · **Completionist** (engagement sustained by the impossibility of final acquisition — the suspended act) · **Explorer** (dwelling-toward and ascending-through the scene) · **Devotional** (reverent self-subordination to the gameworld's order — toward incorporation at its fullest) · **Ironic-meta** (detached, playing *with* the game's rhetoric — speedrun, meta-gaming).

A player oscillates among registers; any act can be performed in more than one. The register is the affective-dispositional coding of the whole grid for that episode.

### B.4 The keystone — Consubstantiation ↔ Incorporation (read across the grid)

The vertical binding that the whole method turns on (`rm-identification-consubstantiation-manual` §D):

- Burke's **consubstantiation** — to identify A with B is to make them "substantially one … yet a distinct locus of motives — both joined and separate" (RM-01 p.21), grounded in *acting-together*.
- Calleja's **incorporation** — assimilation axis (world → consciousness) + embodiment axis (player → world via avatar) + simultaneity condition (IG-10 p.169).
- **Homology:** "substantially one" → assimilation; "distinct locus / the avatar" → embodiment; "both-joined-and-separate" → simultaneity; *acting-together* → the spatial+kinesthetic cornerstone. **Ultimate identification** (mystic dissolution into boundless being, RM-07 p.333) → incorporation at its fullest.
- **Five divergences keep it a homology, not an equation:** medium (symbolic vs phenomenological), ontological status (real-or-persuaded vs experiential), grounding theory (substance-metaphysics vs experientialist cognition), directionality (asymmetric-deployed vs bidirectional), scope (society-wide+self-address vs ergodic-bounded).

When a DIA analysis finds the player *substantially one with* avatar/guild/world while remaining a distinct locus of motives, it is reading consubstantiation and incorporation as the same event from two sides.

## Part C — The Method (step-by-step)

**DIA is a runnable protocol.** Inputs: a documented player episode — a play recording or detailed trace; observation notes; (ideally) a think-aloud or post-session semi-structured interview (Calleja's grounded-theoretic standard); and, for the macro phase, the player's off-line traces (forum/Discord posts, theorycrafting, guild activity). Output: a structured **DIA report** + a **filled grid** + a **one-paragraph behavioral diagnosis**.

### Step 0 — Scope & circumference
Fix the episode boundary and the *representative anecdote* (Burke) / *unit of analysis* (Calleja): is the object a single session, a specific incident (a raid wipe, a betrayal, a first clear), a whole character-career, or a player's relationship to the game over months? Set the **circumference** deliberately — too wide and motive blurs; too narrow and the macro phase vanishes. State it explicitly.

### Step 1 — Involvement profile (Axis I)
Map all six PIM dimensions × macro/micro. For each present dimension, note **intensity** (low/med/high), **internalization trajectory** (conceptual→inhabited; conscious→fluent; scripted→alterbiographic), and **evidence** (which observation/interview datum supports it). Use Calleja's boundary criteria (e.g. inhabitation-criterion for Spatial; the symbolic/mimetic/symbiotic continuum for Kinesthetic). *This is the "what is the player doing, structurally" pass.*

### Step 2 — Motive grammar (Axis M)
Fill the six pentad terms for the episode — Act / Scene / Agent / Agency / Purpose / Attitude — recording what concretely occupies each (note that any term may have several fillers, and that Agent may *shift* between player-as-self, avatar, and character). Identify the **featured term(s)**: which term is the locus of motive this episode foregrounds (e.g. an exploration session features Scene; a ranked match features Agency+Purpose). *This is the "why — under what grammar" pass.*

### Step 3 — Ratio dynamics
Identify the operative ratios (Part B.2) and name the **dominant** one. Ask what the player *attributes their behavior to* — the situation (scene-act), their own development (act-agent), the tools (agency-purpose), the other players (agent-agent). The dominant ratio is the episode's motive-engine.

### Step 4 — Crosswalk binding (the fusion)
Bind Axis M to Axis I using `validated-crosswalk.json`. For each featured motive term, read it through its bound dimension(s); for each high-intensity dimension, read it through its bound motive term. The fusion produces statements no single framework yields, e.g.: *"the high ludic-micro intensity (I) is an Agency-Purpose collapse (M): the rule-system's means have become the player's ends."* Apply the **Ludic split** explicitly: is the ludic involvement about the *rule-system* (Agency) or the *choosing* (Act+Purpose)?

### Step 5 — Identification & consubstantiation (the keystone)
Analyze the player's nested identifications — with **avatar/character** (embodiment), **guild/faction/playerbase** (acting-together, "'Belonging' is rhetorical"). Locate where **consubstantiation** occurs (the player substantially one with X while remaining a distinct locus) and assess **incorporation** against the rubric (R1 assimilation, R2 embodiment, R3 simultaneity, R4 spatial+kinesthetic cornerstone, R5 blending). Note any **mystery / hierarchy / courtship** dynamics structuring the social field (Shared), and any **victimage / the kill** (scapegoating, toxicity) discharging hierarchic guilt.

### Step 6 — Attitude (the disposition)
Identify the player's dominant attitude register(s) — competitive / completionist / explorer / devotional / ironic-meta (Part B.3) — and the **elation↔accidie** arc (sustained engagement vs. post-flow-break disengagement). Attitude is the affective-dispositional coloring of the whole grid; it is also an *incipient act of identification* (the hinge to Step 5).

### Step 7 — Diagnosis & ends
Synthesize Steps 1–6 into a **one-paragraph behavioral diagnosis** that names: the featured motive term(s), the dominant ratio, the keystone state (mere-involvement vs incorporation; consubstantial vs strategic belonging), the attitude register, and the resulting behavior. Then apply it to the **end** the analysis serves (Part E).

> **Output template — the filled grid.** A 6×6 table (motive terms × dimensions) with the active cells marked and annotated, plus: featured-term(s), dominant-ratio, keystone-verdict, attitude-register, and the one-paragraph diagnosis. See the worked example (`worked-example-wow.md`) for a completed instance.

## Part D — Analytic-Question Battery

Reorganized by the unified grid (merges and de-duplicates Burke `gm` §D and Calleja `ig` §D, plus integration questions). Ask only those relevant to the episode's featured terms.

**Scene / Spatial**
1. What features of the gameworld foreground themselves as the *containing* condition of the player's acts? (Scene-Act)
2. Is space encountered as navigable inhabited place or as conceptual map? Can the player meaningfully get lost?
3. What does the player's *physical room* contribute as outer Scene (couch vs. arcade vs. tournament stage)?

**Act / Ludic(choosing) + Narrative**
4. What is the session-arc act, and what sub-acts compose it (microtactic → tactic → strategy)?
5. Where does *act* collapse into mere *motion* (sheer grinding, autopilot)? (Burke's act-motion watershed)
6. How does scripted narrative interweave with the player's alterbiography? Which dominates?

**Agent / Shared**
7. Who is acting — the player-as-self, the avatar, the character? When does the agent-position *shift*?
8. Who are the co-agents (guild, party) and counter-agents (PvP opponents, AI, the designer-as-counter-agent)?
9. Where do super-agents emerge (the guild, the server, the community, the studio)?

**Agency / Kinesthetic + Ludic(rule-system)**
10. What means is the player wielding — controller (Kinesthetic), rule-system (Ludic), other players' moves, third-party tools?
11. Where does the control scheme become transparent (fluent) vs. announce itself (friction)?
12. *Ludic split:* is a given ludic behavior about operating the rule-*system* (Agency) or about *choosing-under-repercussion* (Act+Purpose)?

**Purpose / Ludic-goal + Narrative**
13. Game-internal goal? Flow-seeking? Affective regulation? Social status? Mastery? Exploration? Narrative resolution? External (streaming/esports)?
14. **(Agency–Purpose, the compulsion diagnostic)** Where do means become ends — the grind, daily-login, speedrun, min-max — as a purpose-drift?
15. What is the ultimate term / "god-term" (RM-05) that ranks all the player's other goals (the win-condition, the prestige currency, the meta apex)?

**Attitude / Affective (transverse)**
16. In what disposition is the player acting — competitive / completionist / explorer / devotional / ironic-meta?
17. Where is the episode on the elation↔accidie arc (sustained flow vs. post-break disengagement)?
18. What is the player's attitude *toward the game's rhetoric* — playing within it, or with it (meta/irony)?

**Keystone / Identification, consubstantiation, incorporation**
19. With whom/what is the player made substantially one (avatar, guild, faction, world) — and where do they remain a distinct locus of motives?
20. Does the episode meet the incorporation rubric (R1–R5), or is it mere-involvement? Which component fails if not?
21. Is belonging *consubstantial* (genuine acting-together) or *strategic/persuaded* (cunning identification, "by default")? (RM-01 tension T1)
22. **(Social hierarchy)** Where do mystery, hierarchy, and courtship structure the Shared field (elite allure, guild initiation/hazing, rank deference)?
23. **(Victimage)** Does hierarchic guilt discharge in a scapegoat (toxicity, blaming, out-group hostility)?

**Cross-cutting**
24. Which single grid region (term × dimension) carries the episode's behavioral weight — and what would change if it were absent?
25. Which register (Burke-motivational vs Calleja-experiential) better explains a contested datum, and why?

## Part E — Ends: What the Method Is For

DIA earns its complexity by yielding diagnoses neither framework produces alone. Four primary ends:

### E.1 Game-design critique
Diagnose **compulsion loops and dark patterns** structurally rather than moralistically: a daily-login or gacha loop is an **Agency–Purpose collapse** (means engineered to become ends) operating on **Ludic** involvement, often sustained by **hierarchy/mystery** (FOMO, opaque rates) on the **Shared/Affective** dimensions. The method names *which* motive-mechanism a design exploits and *which* involvement dimension carries it — actionable for ethical design review.

### E.2 Player-experience research
The filled grid is a **coding instrument** for qualitative play research, parallel to and extending Calleja's grounded-theoretic scheme: it codes not only *what* dimensions are involved but *what motive* and *what disposition* animate them, with both-sides evidentiary anchoring.

### E.3 Social & identity behavior
Explain **guild attachment, toxicity, and betrayal** via the keystone + social-motive apparatus: attachment is **consubstantiation** through acting-together; toxicity is **victimage** discharging **hierarchic** guilt; a guild-betrayal (e.g. the EVE *Guiding Hand* heist) is a **consubstantiation-rupture** — the betrayer exploited a Shared-dimension identification that was real enough to be weaponized. DIA gives these a vocabulary beyond "engagement."

### E.4 Comparative game analysis
Different games **foreground different grid regions**: a walking simulator lives in Scene×Spatial + Attitude(explorer); a fighting game in Agency×Kinesthetic + Act; an MMO raid in Agent×Shared + the keystone. Mapping a game's *characteristic grid region* is a compact comparative signature.
