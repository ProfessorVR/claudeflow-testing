# Phase 2 Analysis: BT-D2-U2 (§§54–60)

## Section 1: Unit Metadata

- **unit_id**: BT-D2-U2
- **part**: Part One
- **division**: Division Two
- **chapter**: II — Dasein's Attestation of an Authentic Potentiality-for-Being, and Resoluteness
- **§-range**: §§54–60
- **H. page range**: H.267–301
- **Eng. page range**: 312–348
- **Unit thesis**: This unit establishes that Dasein's authentic potentiality-for-Being is attested by conscience (*Gewissen*), whose call summons Dasein from its lostness in the "they" back to its ownmost Being-guilty. The existential meaning of "guilt" is reinterpreted as Being-the-basis-of-a-nullity — a structural feature of care itself — and the authentic response to the call, wanting-to-have-a-conscience, is shown to constitute resoluteness (*Entschlossenheit*): reticent, anxiety-ready self-projection upon one's ownmost Being-guilty.

## Section 2: Hierarchical Outline

### 2.1 The problem of attestation and the phenomenon of conscience (§§54–55)
- Dasein is lost in the "they"; an authentic potentiality-for-Being must be attested (*bezeugt*) by Dasein itself, not imposed from outside (§54).
- The attestation is found in the "voice of conscience" (*Stimme des Gewissens*), a primordial phenomenon that must be investigated purely existentially, prior to any theological, psychological, or biological explanation (§54).
- Conscience belongs to Dasein's disclosedness: it is constituted by state-of-mind, understanding, falling, and discourse, and is revealed as a call (*Ruf*), a mode of discourse (§55).
- Dasein, absorbed in the "they," fails to hear (*uberhort*) itself; conscience breaks through this listening-away by calling silently and without hubbub (§55).

### 2.2 The character of the call and its origin in care (§§56–57)
- The call of conscience is indefinite in content — it asserts nothing, gives no information, passes over the they-self — yet it summons Dasein to its ownmost potentiality-for-Being-its-Self (§56).
- Conscience discourses solely and constantly in the mode of keeping silent (*Schweigen*); it dispenses with any kind of utterance (§56).
- The caller is Dasein itself in its uncanniness (*Unheimlichkeit*): primordial, thrown Being-in-the-world as the "not-at-home," unfamiliar to the everyday they-self (§57).
- Conscience manifests itself as the call of care (*Ruf der Sorge*): the caller (Dasein in its thrownness) is anxious about its potentiality-for-Being; the one called (the same Dasein) is summoned from its fallen lostness in the "they" (§57).
- The "world-conscience" or "universal conscience" interpretation is rejected as a dubious fabrication; conscience is in each case *mine* (§57).

### 2.3 Understanding the appeal: Being-guilty as existential nullity (§58)
- The call summons Dasein to its Being-guilty (*Schuldigsein*), which must be understood existentially, not morally (§58).
- Ordinary significations of "guilt" — having debts, being responsible for, making oneself responsible — all share the formal character of "Being-the-basis for a lack" in the Being of another (§58).
- The existential-formal definition: Being-guilty = "Being-the-basis-of-a-nullity" (*Grundsein einer Nichtigkeit*), which excludes any reference to moral law, ought, or presence-at-hand (§58).
- Dasein's Being is care, which comprises facticity (thrownness), existence (projection), and falling. In each moment, nullity pervades: Dasein has not laid its own basis (thrown), can never get its basis into its power (null projection), and in choosing one possibility forecloses others (§58).
- Care itself, in its very essence, is permeated with nullity through and through; thus Dasein as such is guilty (§58).
- Being-guilty does not first result from indebtedness; on the contrary, indebtedness becomes possible only on the basis of a primordial Being-guilty (§58).

### 2.4 Critique of ordinary conscience, and the existential structure of resoluteness (§§59–60)
- Four objections from the ordinary interpretation of conscience are addressed: (1) conscience is essentially critical; (2) conscience relates to definite deeds; (3) the "voice" is not radically related to Dasein's Being; (4) the analysis omits "good" and "bad" conscience (§59).
- The "good" conscience is shown to be neither a self-subsistent form nor a founded form of conscience but rather a tranquillizing suppression of Being-guilty — it is not a conscience-phenomenon at all (§59).
- The "bad" conscience and "warning" conscience are shown to be derivative; the existential Interpretation reveals the ordinary interpretation's orientation toward presence-at-hand and concern as its source of distortion (§59).
- The authentic understanding of the call — wanting-to-have-a-conscience (*Gewissen-haben-wollen*) — constitutes resoluteness: reticent self-projection upon one's ownmost Being-guilty, in the readiness for anxiety (§60).
- Resoluteness is authentic disclosedness: constituted by anxiety as state-of-mind, understanding as self-projection upon Being-guilty, and discourse as reticence (*Verschwiegenheit*) (§60).
- The Situation (*Situation*) is the "there" disclosed in resoluteness; it is not a present-at-hand framework but is essentially closed off for the "they" (§60).
- Resoluteness does not detach Dasein from its world but is authentically nothing else than Being-in-the-world; it brings the Self into concernful Being-alongside the ready-to-hand and into solicitous Being with Others (§60).
- Resoluteness, however, is only that authenticity which in care is the object of care — the authenticity of care itself. The chapter closes by noting that an authentic potentiality-for-Being-a-whole still requires the connection to being-towards-death (anticipatory resoluteness), deferred to Chapter III (§60).

## Section 6: Tensions & Open Questions

1. **Nullity and the ontological meaning of the "not" (§58)**: Heidegger acknowledges that the "ontological meaning of the notness [*Nichtheit*] of this existential nullity is still obscure" (H.285–286). He raises the question of whether every "not" signifies something negative in the sense of a lack, whether dialectic can establish the "not" as a problem, and whether the ontological source of notness has ever been sought. He explicitly states that these conditions can be found "only by taking the meaning of Being in general as a theme and clarifying it" — pointing toward the unwritten Division Three. This is a structural gap in the published text. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D2-U2-P2.

2. **The indeterminate content of the call and the "existentiell indefiniteness" of resoluteness (§§56, 60)**: The call of conscience gives no information, recommends no programme of action, and says "nothing" that could be talked about. Resoluteness likewise has no specific existentiell content — it is "the disclosive projection and determination of what is factically possible at the time" (H.298). Whether this formal emptiness is a philosophical strength (preserving the ontological level) or a weakness (rendering the analysis incapable of distinguishing authentic from inauthentic existentiell choices) is a persistent interpretive question. [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D2-U2-P3, BT-D2-U2-P5.

3. **Conscience as "mine" vs. the sociality of resoluteness (§§57, 60)**: Heidegger insists that conscience is "in each case mine" and that the call comes from Dasein in its uncanniness. Yet §60 argues that resoluteness brings the Self into solicitous Being with Others and can become the "conscience" of Others. The relationship between the radical individuation of the call and the social dimension of resolute existence is not fully worked out. [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D2-U2-P1, BT-D2-U2-P3.

4. **Resoluteness without being-towards-death (§60)**: The chapter concludes by acknowledging that resoluteness as exhibited here "still remains a purely existential project for which Dasein's attestation is missing" — it has not yet been connected to being-towards-death, and thus the authentic potentiality-for-Being-a-whole has not yet been secured. This is an explicit structural incompleteness within the unit, resolved only in §62 (anticipatory resoluteness). [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D2-U2-P3.

5. **The status of the "voice" metaphor (§§55–56)**: Heidegger rejects the idea that conscience is a literal voice or utterance — it "discourses solely and constantly in the mode of keeping silent" (H.273) — yet the entire analysis operates through the vocabulary of calling, hearing, appeal, and summoning. Whether this vocabulary is merely a concession to ordinary language or carries its own phenomenological weight (given that discourse is an existentiale) is interpretively significant. [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D2-U2-P1.
