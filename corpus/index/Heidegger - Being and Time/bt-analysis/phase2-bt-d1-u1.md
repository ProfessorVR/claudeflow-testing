# Phase 2 Analysis: BT-D1-U1 (§§9–13)

- **part**: Part One
- **division**: Division One

- **§-range**: §§9–13
- **H. page range**: H.41–62
- **Eng. page range**: 67–90
- **Unit thesis**: This unit establishes the theme of the existential analytic by specifying Dasein's formal-existential characteristics (existence, mineness, authenticity/inauthenticity, everydayness), demarcates the analytic from anthropology, psychology, and biology, introduces being-in-the-world as the unitary basic state (*Grundverfassung*) of Dasein, distinguishes existential Being-in from categorial spatial containment, and demonstrates that knowing the world is a founded mode of Being-in rather than the primary relation between Dasein and world.

## Section 2: Hierarchical Outline

### 2.1 The theme of the analytic of Dasein (§9)
- Dasein's "essence" lies in its existence (*Existenz*): it is an entity whose Being is in each case "to be," not a fixed substance with properties.
- Dasein is characterized by *Jemeinigkeit* (mineness): Being is in each case *mine*; one must always use the personal pronoun ("I am," "you are").
- Mineness is the condition for authenticity (*Eigentlichkeit*) and inauthenticity (*Uneigentlichkeit*) as modes of Being; these are grounded in the fact that Dasein is characterized by mineness, not in some value-laden distinction.
- The starting point of the analytic is Dasein in its average everydayness (*Durchschnittlichkeit*): the undifferentiated, proximal character that is ontically closest yet ontologically farthest.
- Existential characteristics of Dasein are called *existentialia*, sharply distinguished from *categories* (characteristics of non-Dasein entities).

### 2.2 Demarcation from anthropology, psychology, and biology (§10)
- The existential analytic comes *before* any psychology, anthropology, or biology; these disciplines have missed the real philosophical problem of the Being of Dasein.
- Descartes left the Being of the *sum* ontologically undetermined despite investigating the *cogitare* of the *cogito sum*; every post-Cartesian inquiry into subjectivity inherits this omission.
- Dilthey's *geisteswissenschaftliche Psychologie* and Husserl's personalism (via Scheler) both gesture toward the question of personal Being but fail to pose the ontological question of the Being of Dasein explicitly.
- Traditional anthropology operates with two clues — the Greek definition (*zoon logon echon*, animal rationale) and the theological definition (*imago Dei*) — both of which leave the Being of the entity "man" ontologically undetermined.
- Biology, psychology, and anthropology all fail to provide an ontologically adequate answer to the question of the kind of Being belonging to the entity we ourselves are.

### 2.3 Primitive Dasein and the "natural conception of the world" (§11)
- The analytic of everydayness is not identical with the ethnographic study of "primitive" Dasein; everydayness is a mode of Being even in highly developed cultures.
- Primitive Dasein can have positive methodological significance because its phenomena are "less concealed."
- The difficulty of achieving a "natural conception of the world" (*natuerlicher Weltbegriff*) shows that even the ordering principles for such a concept presuppose an ontological understanding of "world."

### 2.4 Being-in-the-world as a unitary phenomenon; Being-in as existentiale (§§12–13)
- Being-in-the-world (*In-der-Welt-sein*) is a unitary structural whole that cannot be broken into independent parts but can be analyzed into three constitutive items: (1) "in-the-world" (worldhood), (2) the "who" (Dasein in its everydayness), (3) Being-in (*In-Sein*) as such.
- Being-in (*In-Sein*) is an existentiale: it is not the spatial containment of one present-at-hand thing inside another (categorial "in-one-another-ness") but derives from *innan-* (to dwell, to reside, to be familiar with).
- "Being alongside" (*Sein bei*) the world, as an existentiale, is ontologically distinct from the categorial "Being-present-at-hand-together" of two worldless entities; Dasein is never merely "next to" things.
- Being-in-the-world, ontologically understood, is essentially concern (*Besorgen*): all ways of Being-in have concern as their kind of Being.
- Knowing the world (*Erkennen*) is a *founded* mode of Being-in: it arises only when Dasein holds back from its engaged concern and takes up a new stance (*Seinsstand*) toward what is already disclosed; it presupposes Being-in-the-world rather than first establishing a relation to the world.
- The subject-object schema — in which a worldless subject must somehow "transcend" to reach an external object — misrepresents Dasein's primordial relation to world because Dasein is always already "outside" alongside entities in a world.

## Section 6: Tensions & Open Questions

1. **Authenticity and inauthenticity as "value-neutral" vs. the language of "losing" and "winning" (§9)**: Heidegger insists that inauthenticity "does not signify any 'less' Being or any 'lower' degree of Being" (H.43) and that both modes are grounded in mineness rather than in an evaluative hierarchy. Yet he describes inauthenticity as a mode in which Dasein "can also lose itself and never win itself; or only 'seem' to do so" (H.42–43). Whether this language is purely formal-structural or carries an implicit normative charge is a persistent interpretive question. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U1-P2.

2. **Existentialia vs. categories: sharpness of the distinction (§9)**: Heidegger draws a strict line between existentialia (structural characters of Dasein) and categories (characteristics of non-Dasein entities), grounded in the "who"/"what" distinction (H.45: any entity is either a "who" or a "what"). Yet the later analysis will sometimes describe the same phenomenon — e.g., spatiality, temporality — in both existential and categorial registers. Whether the distinction holds absolutely or admits of borderline cases is left programmatic. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U1-P1, BT-D1-U1-P5.

3. **The status of the body (§12)**: Heidegger argues that Being-in is an existentiale, not spatial containment, and that Dasein's "spatiality" is existential, not corporeal. He explicitly denies that Being-in can be explained by saying "man's 'spatiality' is a result of his bodily nature" (H.56). Yet he does not thematize the body (*Leib*) itself, and the analysis of concern, dwelling, and touching proceeds without ever accounting for corporeality. This lacuna — noted by many commentators — remains unaddressed in the published text. [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D1-U1-P5.

4. **Knowing as "founded" vs. knowing as co-original (§13)**: Heidegger argues that knowing is a founded mode of Being-in that arises from a "deficiency" (*Defizienz*) of concern — a holding-back from practical engagement (H.61). This raises the question of whether theoretical comportment is genuinely derivative or whether Dasein always already comports itself in ways that involve something like cognition. The relationship between founding and derivativeness is asserted but not fully argued. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U1-P6.

5. **Everydayness as starting point: methodological neutrality? (§9)**: Heidegger designates average everydayness as the starting point because it is ontically proximal — but precisely because it is ontologically "farthest and not known at all" (H.43), it risks importing unexamined presuppositions into the analysis. The question of whether the analytic can genuinely begin from everydayness without already being guided by the ontological distinctions it claims to discover (the existential-analytic circle) is acknowledged but deferred. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U1-P1, Phase 0 Tension #1 (hermeneutic circle).

6. **Being-in-the-world and the problem of the "whole" (§12)**: Heidegger insists that Being-in-the-world is a *unitary* phenomenon that "cannot be broken up into contentswhich may be pieced together" (H.53), yet immediately proceeds to articulate three structural moments. Whether this articulation already violates the unitariness of the phenomenon — or whether the three "moments" are genuinely equiprimordial — is a methodological tension intrinsic to the analysis. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U1-P4.
