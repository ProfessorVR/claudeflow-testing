# Phase 2 Analysis: BT-D1-U9 (§§43–44)

## Section 1: Unit Metadata

- **unit_id**: BT-D1-U9
- **part**: Part One
- **division**: Division One
- **chapter**: VI (pt 2) — Reality, worldhood, and truth
- **§-range**: §§43–44
- **H. page range**: H.200–230
- **Eng. page range**: 244–273
- **Unit thesis**: This unit demonstrates that Reality (*Realität*) is ontologically grounded in care and that truth (*Wahrheit*) is primordially disclosed as the uncoveredness (*Entdecktheit*) belonging to Dasein's disclosedness (*Erschlossenheit*), thereby showing that the traditional conceptions of Reality as presence-at-hand and truth as propositional agreement (*adaequatio*) are derivative rather than primordial phenomena. Together, §§43–44 close Division One by establishing that the existential analytic of Dasein provides the ontological foundations for the problems of Reality and truth that the tradition has misconceived.

## Section 2: Hierarchical Outline

### 2.1 Dasein, worldhood, and Reality (§43)

- The understanding of Being has been diverted toward presence-at-hand (*Vorhandenheit*); "Being" acquires the meaning of "Reality," and substantiality becomes the basic characteristic of Being (H.201). This diversion gives the concept of Reality a peculiar ontological priority that must be dismantled.
- **§43a — Reality as a problem of Being, and whether the "external world" can be proved**: The question of the external world presupposes a subject-object split foreign to Dasein's basic state. Kant's "Refutation of Idealism" is critiqued: Kant proves only that changing and permanent things which are present-at-hand are necessarily present-at-hand together, not that Dasein as Being-in-the-world encounters a world (H.202–206). The "scandal of philosophy" is not that the proof is lacking but that such proofs are expected and attempted again and again (H.205).
- **§43b — Reality as an ontological problem**: Reality (*Realität*) as the Being of entities present-at-hand within-the-world is ontologically conceivable only if the phenomena of within-the-world-ness and worldhood have been clarified. Dilthey's analysis of Reality as resistance (*Widerstand*) is a positive contribution but remains ontologically indefinite because it lacks an analytic of Dasein (H.209–210). Scheler takes up Dilthey but likewise fails to provide ontological foundations. The experience of resistance is possible only by reason of the disclosedness of the world (H.210–211). The *cogito sum* must be reinterpreted: the "sum" is asserted first, as "I am in a world" (H.211).
- **§43c — Reality and care**: All modes of Being of entities within-the-world are founded ontologically upon the worldhood of the world and upon care. Reality has no priority among modes of Being. Reality is dependent upon the understanding of Being; Being (not entities) is dependent upon Dasein's existence. Only as long as Dasein *is* can Being be understood, and only then can entities be encountered as entities (H.212).

### 2.2 Dasein, disclosedness, and truth (§44)

- Philosophy since Parmenides has associated truth with Being. Aristotle notes that the earliest thinkers were "compelled by the truth itself" (*anankazomenoi hyp' autēs tēs alētheias*, H.213). The question is what "truth" signifies if it has a primordial connection with Being rather than being merely a property of judgments.
- **§44a — The traditional conception of truth and its ontological foundations**: Three traditional theses are identified: (1) the "locus" of truth is the assertion/judgment; (2) truth consists in the "agreement" (*adaequatio*) of knowledge with its object; (3) Aristotle assigned truth to the judgment as its primordial locus (H.214). Heidegger traces the *adaequatio intellectus et rei* through Thomas Aquinas and Avicenna, analyzes what "agreement" means ontologically, and shows that truth as confirmation (*Bewährung*) is Being-uncovering: the assertion is toward the Thing itself, and what gets confirmed is the entity's showing itself in its selfsameness (H.218).
- **§44b — The primordial phenomenon of truth and the derivative character of the traditional conception**: Being-true means Being-uncovering (*Entdeckend-sein*). This definition preserves the oldest sense of *alētheia* — unconcealment, taking entities out of their hiddenness (H.219–220). The most primordial phenomenon of truth is Dasein's disclosedness, constituted by state-of-mind, understanding, and discourse. "Dasein is 'in the truth'" (H.221). But equally, because Dasein is essentially falling, "Dasein is in the 'untruth'" — its state of Being is such that it is in untruth (H.222). Truth (uncoveredness) must be wrested from entities; it is a kind of "robbery" (*Raub*, H.222). Truth as disclosedness is shown to found assertion-truth: when uncoveredness is expressed and preserved in assertion, it acquires the character of presence-at-hand, becoming a relationship between things present-at-hand — and thus the *adaequatio* (H.224–225).
- **§44c — The kind of Being which truth possesses, and the presupposition of truth**: Truth "is" only as long as Dasein is; before Dasein, there was no truth and after Dasein there will be none (H.226–227). Newton's laws were neither true nor false before their discovery; this does not mean they become false once Dasein ceases. "We presuppose truth because 'we', being in the kind of Being which Dasein possesses, are 'in the truth'" (H.228). The presupposition of truth belongs to Dasein's facticity as thrown projection. Scepticism cannot be refuted because any sceptic, in so far as he *is*, has already presupposed truth (H.229). The a priori character of truth is grounded not in an idealized subject but in Dasein's factical constitution (H.229–230).

### 2.3 Retrospect and transition to Division Two (close of Division One)

- The unit closes by noting that the question of the meaning of Being remains unanswered. Care has been established as Dasein's primordial state of Being, and modes of Being other than Dasein's (readiness-to-hand, presence-at-hand, Reality) have been distinguished. But the question remains: has Dasein been brought into view as a *whole*? (H.230). This question motivates the transition to Division Two and the analysis of being-towards-death.

## Section 6: Tensions & Open Questions

1. **Being-dependence vs. entity-independence (§43c, H.212)**: Heidegger insists that Being (not entities) is dependent on Dasein's understanding, while entities can "be" independently. But if Being is always the Being *of* entities, then what sense does it make for entities to "be" without Being being understood? The distinction between ontological dependence (Being requires Dasein) and ontic independence (entities persist without Dasein) is asserted but not fully elaborated. The §44c claim that "before Dasein there was no truth" intensifies this tension. [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D1-U9-P2, BT-D1-U9-P8.

2. **Realism, idealism, and Heidegger's "third way" (§43a–c)**: Heidegger claims his position differs from both realism (which holds the world must be proved) and idealism (which reduces Being to consciousness), yet shares something with idealism insofar as "Being can never be explained by entities" (H.207–208). Whether Heidegger's position genuinely overcomes the realism/idealism opposition or tacitly presupposes a form of idealism (Being depends on Dasein) is one of the most contested interpretive questions in the secondary literature. [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D1-U9-P1, BT-D1-U9-P2.

3. **Truth's temporality and the status of "eternal truths" (§44c)**: Heidegger's claim that truth "is" only as long as Dasein is directly challenges the notion of eternal truths. The Newton example is meant to clarify — the laws were not "true" before discovery, though once uncovered entities show themselves as what they already were. But this raises the question of what ontological status the "already were" has if truth is bound to Dasein's existence. Heidegger acknowledges the question remains open (H.227–228). [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D1-U9-P8.

4. **The derivation of *adaequatio* from disclosedness (§44b)**: Heidegger provides a genetic account of how primordial truth (uncovering) degrades into the traditional correspondence conception via the preservation of uncoveredness in assertion. The mechanism (assertion → ready-to-hand expression → presence-at-hand relation → agreement between present-at-hand things) is sketched but compressed. Whether this derivation is a necessary structural degradation or a contingent historical development is not fully clarified. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U9-P7.

5. **Truth and untruth as equiprimordial (§44b, H.222)**: If Dasein is equiprimordially in the truth and in untruth, truth must always be "wrested" from concealment. This introduces a dynamic, agonistic dimension to truth. But the relationship between this structural feature and Dasein's fallenness raises the question of whether authenticity is required for truth or whether everyday uncovering already counts as genuine truth. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U9-P5, BT-D1-U9-P6.

6. **Aristotle's position on truth (§44b, H.226)**: Heidegger claims Aristotle never defends the thesis that truth's primordial locus is in judgment and that the *logos* for Aristotle can both uncover and cover up. This reading of Aristotle is historically significant but contested — many scholars read *Metaphysics* Γ and *De Interpretatione* as placing truth primarily in the *logos apophantikos*. Heidegger's Aristotle serves his own argument, and the accuracy of this retrieval is debatable. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U9-P4, BT-D1-U9-P7.
