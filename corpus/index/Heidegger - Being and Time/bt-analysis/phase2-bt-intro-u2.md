## Section 2: Hierarchical Outline

### 2.1 The Dasein-analytic as horizon for the question of Being (§5)
- Dasein is ontico-ontologically prior: it has a pre-ontological understanding of Being.
- The analytic of Dasein is oriented toward Dasein's *average everydayness* (*durchschnittliche Alltäglichkeit*) — the indifferent mode that is neither authentic nor inauthentic.
- The analytic is *provisional* — it merely lays bare the horizon for interpreting Being.
- Temporality (*Zeitlichkeit*) is announced as the meaning of the Being of Dasein (care), and *Temporalität* as the horizon for the question of Being in general.
- The distinction between *Zeitlichkeit* (Dasein's temporality) and *Temporalität* (Being's temporality) is structurally central but only the former is developed in the published text.

### 2.2 The Destruktion of the history of ontology (§6)
- Dasein's being is essentially historical (*geschichtlich*); its understanding of Being is shaped by tradition (*Überlieferung*).
- Tradition both transmits and conceals: it delivers interpretations but blocks access to the primordial experiences from which they arose.
- Destruktion is *not* negation — it is a positive retrieval, tracing hardened concepts back to their experiential sources.
- The three stations of Destruktion (Part Two, never published): (1) Kant's doctrine of schematism (time), (2) Descartes' *cogito sum* and *res cogitans* (subject), (3) Aristotle's essay on time (*Physics* Δ) — the ancient determination of Being as *ousia/parousia* (presence).
- The tradition interprets Being as *Anwesenheit* (presence) — a specific mode of time (the "present") that was never thematized as such.

### 2.3 The phenomenological method (§7)
- **A. Phenomenon** (*Phänomen*): from Greek *phainomenon* — "that which shows itself in itself, the manifest." Distinguished from: (a) semblance (*Schein*) — showing-itself as something it is not; (b) appearance (*Erscheinung*) — announcing-itself through something else. The *formal* concept: "that which shows itself." The *phenomenological* concept: Being, which is hidden precisely because it is closest and most familiar.
- **B. Logos**: from *legein* — "to let something be seen" (*apophainesthai*). Discourse as *apophansis*: letting-be-seen from the thing itself. Truth (*aletheia*) as *unconcealment* — not agreement of judgment with object but the uncovering of beings.
- **C. Synthesis**: "Phenomenology" = *legein ta phainomena* — letting that which shows itself be seen from itself. Not a "standpoint" or "direction" but the maxim: "To the things themselves!" Phenomenology is the method of ontology; *only as phenomenology is ontology possible*. The phenomenology of Dasein is *hermeneutic* — interpretation in the primordial sense. It is also *transcendental* — every disclosure of Being discloses the *transcendens*.

### 2.4 Design of the treatise (§8)
- Part One: Interpretation of Dasein in terms of temporality. Three divisions: (1) preparatory fundamental analysis of Dasein, (2) Dasein and temporality, (3) time and Being.
- Part Two: Phenomenological destruction of ontology. Three divisions: (1) Kant/schematism, (2) Descartes/cogito, (3) Aristotle/time.
- Only Part One, Divisions One and Two are published. Division Three and all of Part Two remain unwritten.

## Section 6: Tensions & Open Questions

1. **Zeitlichkeit vs. Temporalität (§5)**: §5 distinguishes Dasein's temporality (*Zeitlichkeit*) from the temporality of Being (*Temporalität*) and promises that the latter will be shown to be the horizon for the question of Being in general. Only Zeitlichkeit is developed in the published text. The relationship between the two — whether Temporalität is simply the "generalization" of Zeitlichkeit or something structurally different — is one of the deepest unresolved questions. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-Intro-U2-P2.

2. **The Destruktion as promise vs. execution (§6)**: The three-station Destruktion (Kant, Descartes, Aristotle) is announced with considerable specificity but never carried out within B&T. §6 provides the rationale but not the analysis. The published lectures (GA 18, GA 21, GA 24, GA 25) partially fulfill this promise, but within B&T itself the Destruktion remains a promissory note. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-Intro-U2-P3.

3. **Presence (*Anwesenheit*) as the determination of Being (§6)**: Heidegger claims that Greek ontology understands Being as presence — *ousia* as *parousia* — and that this is a specific (but unthematized) temporal determination. The full demonstration requires the Destruktion of Aristotle's essay on time (Part Two, Division Three), which is never given. Within B&T, the analysis of within-time-ness (§§78–83) partially addresses this but does not complete the argument. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-Intro-U2-P4.

4. **Truth as unconcealment vs. truth as propositional (§7B)**: §7B establishes *aletheia* as the primordial sense of truth (unconcealment/uncovering). §44 will return to this extensively. The tension: if truth is primordially a feature of Being/Dasein's disclosedness rather than of propositions, what is the ontological status of falsity, error, and the covering-up of beings? Heidegger addresses this in §44 but the tension persists. [INTERP-low]

5. **Phenomenology as "presuppositionless" vs. hermeneutically situated (§7C)**: §7C claims phenomenology has no "standpoint" — it is purely methodological ("to the things themselves"). But §7C also calls it a *hermeneutic* of Dasein, which operates within a hermeneutic circle (§§32–33). Whether these two claims are compatible is a deep methodological tension. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-Intro-U2-P5.
