# Phase 2 Analysis: BT-D2-U6 (§§78--83)

## Section 1: Unit Metadata

- **unit_id**: BT-D2-U6
- **part**: Part One
- **division**: Division Two
- **chapter**: VI -- Temporality and Within-time-ness as the Source of the Ordinary Conception of Time
- **§-range**: §§78--83
- **H. page range**: H.404--437
- **Eng. page range**: 456--488
- **Unit thesis**: This unit demonstrates that the ordinary (vulgar) conception of time as an infinite, levelled-off sequence of "nows" derives from within-time-ness, which is itself a mode of ecstatico-horizonal temporality. Heidegger traces the genesis of clock-time from Dasein's concernful time-reckoning, recovers the existential-ontological content of Aristotle's definition of time (*Physics* IV), compares and contrasts this derivation with Hegel's formal-dialectical connection of time and spirit, and closes with the still-open question of whether time itself manifests as the horizon of Being -- the question that the unwritten Division Three was to address.

## Section 2: Hierarchical Outline

### 2.1 The incompleteness of the foregoing analysis and the transition to within-time-ness (§78)
- The existential analytic has shown temporality as the meaning of care and historicality as grounded in temporality, but has not yet addressed the time "in which" entities are encountered -- the factical, public time of everyday concern.
- Reckoning with time is an elemental behaviour prior to any clock-use; it must be clarified before the question of what it means for entities to be "in time" can be answered.
- The chapter is divided into six tasks: Dasein's concern with time (§79), within-time-ness and clock-time (§80), the genesis of the ordinary conception (§81), comparison with Hegel on time and spirit (§82), and the concluding question of fundamental ontology (§83).
- Heidegger announces a provisional comparison with Hegel, whose results concerning time and spirit agree with the existential-ontological interpretation but whose orientation is "precisely the opposite."

### 2.2 Dasein's temporality and the structures of concerned time: datability, significance, spannedness, publicness (§79)
- Concern expresses itself in the "now," "then," and "on that former occasion," which are not bare now-points but are always datable ("now that..."), significant ("the time for..."), spanned ("during..."), and public ("the time which 'they' reckon").
- Datability reflects the ecstatical constitution of temporality: the "now" interprets the Present, the "then" interprets awaiting (future), the "on that former occasion" interprets retaining (having-been).
- Spannedness (*Gespanntheit*) belongs to time because temporality is ecstatically stretched along (*erstreckt*); the "during" articulates the "in-between" of the until-then and the from-then.
- Public time arises because Dasein exists as Being-with-Others; the interpreted time has always already been given a public character. Dasein does not know this time "as its own" but utilizies the time with which "they" reckon.
- World-time is designated as such not because it is present-at-hand within-the-world but because it belongs to the world in the existential-ontological sense: it is datable, significant, spanned, and public.

### 2.3 Within-time-ness, clock-time, and the levelling-off into now-time (§§80--81)
- Time-reckoning through astronomical and calendrical dating has existential-ontological necessity grounded in Dasein's thrownness: "Dasein's thrownness is the reason why 'there is' time publicly."
- The "natural clock" (the sun's course) is discovered along with Dasein's factical thrownness; artificial clocks refine and make independent this natural measure.
- Looking at the clock is essentially "saying 'now'" -- a discursive articulation of a making-present that temporalizes in ecstatical unity with awaiting and retaining.
- Aristotle's definition of time is recovered as an existential-ontological interpretation: "time is that which is counted and which shows itself when one follows the travelling pointer, counting and making present in such a way that this making-present temporalizes itself in an ecstatical unity with the retaining and awaiting which are horizontally open according to the 'earlier' and 'later'."
- The ordinary conception arises through a progressive "levelling off" (*Einebnung*): datability and significance are stripped from the "nows," which become a pure, present-at-hand succession -- the "now-time" (*Jetzt-Zeit*).
- The principal thesis of the ordinary interpretation -- that time is infinite -- arises from covering over the finite futurity of Dasein's Being-towards-death. The inauthentic "they" flees finitude and projects time as an endless sequence.

### 2.4 Hegel on time and spirit, compared with the existential-ontological derivation (§§81--82)
- Hegel defines time as "the negation of the negation" (of punctuality/space), arriving at the "now" as the Being of time. His analysis moves within the traditional horizon of presence-at-hand and levels off the "now" to its most extreme formal abstraction.
- Spirit "falls into time" because time and spirit share the formal structure of the negation of a negation. Spirit as "the concept" is self-differentiating negativity; time as "intuited becoming" is abstract negativity. Their kinship is formal-ontological.
- Heidegger's critique: Hegel operates with a completely levelled-off world-time whose origin in temporality remains concealed. Spirit does not "fall into time" but rather "exists as the primordial temporalizing of temporality." Factical existence "falls" not *into* time but *from* primordial, authentic temporality.
- The comparison serves to make plain Heidegger's own position: temporality temporalizes world-time, within the horizon of which history can "appear" as historizing within-time. Hegel, lacking the distinction between temporality and within-time-ness, reverses this order.

### 2.5 The concluding question: from the temporality of Dasein to the meaning of Being in general (§83)
- The existential-temporal analytic has interpreted Dasein's primordial wholeness on the basis of temporality, but the aim of fundamental ontology is the meaning of Being in general, not only the Being of Dasein.
- The question of Being requires that the "idea of Being in general" be clarified in the light of time, and the treatise has only provided the preparatory grounds for this.
- The concluding questions -- "Is there a way which leads from primordial time to the meaning of Being? Does time itself manifest itself as the horizon of Being?" -- remain unanswered, pointing forward to the unwritten Division Three ("Time and Being").

## Section 6: Tensions & Open Questions

1. **The structural incompleteness and the question of Temporalitat (§83)**: The unit ends with the question "Does time itself manifest itself as the horizon of Being?" -- the question that Division Three was to answer. The entire derivation of within-time-ness from temporality remains a Dasein-side analysis; whether this derivation can be "reversed" to show that Being itself has a temporal sense is left open. This is the most consequential open question in the entire treatise. [INTERP-low] -- Linked to: BT-D2-U6-P6.

2. **Aristotle: "genuinely derived" yet ontologically insufficient (§81)**: Heidegger claims Aristotle's definition of time is "genuinely derived" and not merely "obvious," yet simultaneously insists it does not become a "problem" for Aristotle because the source of time in primordial temporality is covered over. The tension is whether "genuinely derived" means Aristotle *had* existential-ontological insight that he did not thematize, or whether the derivation is genuine only when retroactively read through the existential-ontological framework. This bears directly on the Destruktion's hermeneutic status. [INTERP-high] -- Linked to: BT-D2-U6-P3.

3. **Hegel's results agree but his orientation is "the opposite" (§§81--82)**: Heidegger's double verdict on Hegel -- agreement in results, opposition in orientation -- creates an interpretive puzzle. If Hegel's formal-dialectical analysis of time as the negation of a negation truly captures the ordinary conception in its "most radical" formulation, it is unclear how the *results* can agree with Heidegger's while the approach is "precisely the opposite." The distinction rests on whether one takes the levelled-off now-sequence as the starting point (Hegel) or as the derivative outcome of a covering-over of primordial temporality (Heidegger). [INTERP-high] -- Linked to: BT-D2-U6-P4, BT-D2-U6-P5.

4. **The infinity of time and the finitude of Dasein (§81)**: The ordinary conception holds that time is infinite; Heidegger derives this from the "they's" fleeing of Dasein's finitude and Being-towards-death. But the argument is compressed: how exactly does the inauthenticity of fleeing *produce* the thesis of infinity, rather than merely motivating a psychological preference for it? The existential-ontological derivation of infinity from inauthentic temporality needs more articulation than it receives. [INTERP-low] -- Linked to: BT-D2-U6-P2.

5. **World-time: "more Objective than any possible Object" and "more subjective than any possible subject" (§80)**: Heidegger asserts that world-time is neither subjective nor objective but prior to both -- it is what first makes possible the Being of the factically existing Self, hence "more subjective" and simultaneously the condition for entities within-the-world, hence "more Objective." Whether this dual characterization is a genuine overcoming of the subject-object dichotomy or a rhetorical straddling of it is left for the reader to decide. [INTERP-high] -- Linked to: BT-D2-U6-P1.
