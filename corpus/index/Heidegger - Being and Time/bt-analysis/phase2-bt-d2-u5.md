# Phase 2 Analysis: BT-D2-U5 (§§72–77)

## Section 1: Unit Metadata

- **unit_id**: BT-D2-U5
- **part**: Part One

- **chapter**: V — Temporality and Historicality
- **§-range**: §§72–77
- **H. page range**: H.372–404
- **Eng. page range**: 424–455
- **Unit thesis**: This unit demonstrates that Dasein's historicality (*Geschichtlichkeit*) is grounded in the ecstatic-horizonal temporality already established as the meaning of care, and that authentic historicality consists in the fateful repetition of inherited possibilities, thereby providing the existential-ontological foundation for both world-history and historiology as a science — a foundation that Heidegger traces through critical appropriation of Dilthey's life-philosophy and Count Yorck's demand for a categorical distinction between the ontical and the Historical.

## Section 2: Hierarchical Outline

### 2.1 The problem of history as an existential-ontological question (§72)
- The analytic has so far been "one-sided": oriented toward death as the futural end, leaving the other end — birth — and the "stretching-along" (*Erstreckung*) between birth and death unthematized.
- The "connectedness of life" (*Zusammenhang des Lebens*) cannot be grasped as a sequence of present-at-hand Experiences strung together in the "now"; such a model covertly imports the ontology of presence-at-hand.
- Dasein does not fill up a track with momentary actualities but *stretches itself along* as a stretching-along; both birth and death lie *in the Being* of Dasein as care.
- The specific movement in which Dasein stretches along is designated *historizing* (*Geschehen*); the ontological problem of Dasein's "connectedness" is the question of historicality.
- Historicality and within-time-ness both stem from temporality, but historicality must first be deduced from primordial temporality before the derivative character of within-time-ness is exposed (deferred to Ch. VI).
- Chapter plan announced: the ordinary understanding of history and historizing (§73); the basic constitution of historicality (§74); world-history (§75); the existential source of historiology (§76); the connection with Dilthey and Yorck (§77).

### 2.2 The ordinary understanding of history, the historicality of equipment, and Dasein as primarily historical (§73)
- Four ordinary significations of "history" (*Geschichte*): (a) what is past; (b) derivation/becoming from a past; (c) the totality of entities changing "in time" (human culture as distinct from Nature); (d) whatever has been "handed down" as tradition or heritage.
- The common thread: history is that specific historizing of existent Dasein which is past, handed down in Being-with-one-another, and still effective.
- Analysis of equipment in museums: the antiquities are historical not because they are old or present-at-hand "in the past" but because they belonged to a *world* that is no longer — the world of a Dasein that has-been-there (*da-gewesen*).
- What is primarily historical is Dasein; equipment, works, Nature are only *secondarily* historical as "world-historical" (*weltgeschichtlich*) entities belonging to the world of a Dasein that has been.
- The "past" (*Vergangenheit*) as applied to Dasein is misleading: Dasein that no longer exists has not passed away but has the character of *having-been-there* (*Da-gewesen*). The historical is determined predominantly by having-been (*Gewesenheit*), not by present-at-hand pastness.

### 2.3 The basic constitution of historicality: heritage, fate, destiny, repetition (§74)
- Anticipatory resoluteness, already established as authentic Being-towards-death, provides the clue: in resolutely coming back to itself, Dasein discloses factical possibilities of existence *in terms of the heritage* (*Erbe*) which resoluteness, as thrown, takes over.
- The handing-down of a heritage is hidden in the thrownness to which resoluteness comes back; authentic existence means choosing among inherited possibilities, not inventing ex nihilo.
- *Fate* (*Schicksal*): Dasein's primordial historizing in authentic resoluteness, in which it hands itself down to itself, free for death, in a possibility it has inherited and yet chosen.
- *Destiny* (*Geschick*): the communal historizing of a people; fateful Dasein, as Being-in-the-world in Being-with Others, co-historizes. Destiny is not a togetherness of individual fates but is guided in advance by shared Being in a common world and resoluteness for definite possibilities.
- *Repetition* (*Wiederholung*): the resoluteness that hands itself down becomes the explicit repetition of a possibility of the Dasein that has-been-there. Repetition is a reciprocal rejoinder to a past possibility, made in a moment of vision and simultaneously a disavowal of the "today." It does not bring back the past but retrieves (*wiederholt*) formerly transmitted possibilities in a futural manner.
- Authentic Being-towards-death — the finitude of temporality — is the *hidden basis* of Dasein's historicality. Dasein does not first become historical through repetition; it *is* historical as temporal, and can take itself over in its history by repeating.
- Inauthentic historicality: the "they-self" makes present its "today," evades choice, is blind for possibilities, and understands the past only as present-at-hand; its temporality has *already been stretched along* but this stretching is hidden.

### 2.4 World-history, the historicality of equipment, and inauthentic dispersal (§75)
- The historizing of history is the historizing of Being-in-the-world; Dasein's historicality is essentially the historicality of the world.
- With the existence of historical Being-in-the-world, equipment, works, institutions, Nature-as-colonized are *already* incorporated into the history of the world; these are the "world-historical" (*weltgeschichtlich*).
- World-historical entities have their own character of movement and "happening" (a ring handed over, a building destroyed) not reducible to change of location or motion of the present-at-hand.
- Inauthentic Dasein computes its history from the world-historical — the "affairs," "undertakings," and "accidents" it encounters within-the-world — and must "pull itself together" (*sich zusammenholen*) out of the dispersion and disconnectedness of its everyday dealings.
- The "connectedness" question in inauthentic historicality arises from the horizon of the "they": how Experiences of a subject get linked together. Authentic historicality — fate and repetition — constitutes a primordial stretched-along steadiness that needs no such linkage.

### 2.5 The existential source of historiology in Dasein's historicality (§76)
- Historiology (*Historie*) as a science has its existential source in Dasein's historicality, not in epistemological methodology or theory of science.
- The idea of historiology must be projected ontologically in terms of Dasein's historicality; the theme of historiology is the *possibility* of existence that has-been-there, not mere "facts."
- The historiological disclosure of the past is rooted in authentic historicality as repetition: by projecting the Dasein that has-been-there upon its ownmost possibility, historiology discloses the "quiet force of the possible."
- Nietzsche's threefold division of historiology — monumental, antiquarian, critical — is adumbrated in the historicality of Dasein; his division is not accidental but grounded in temporality.
- Authentic historiology is necessarily a critique of the "Present" (*Gegenwart*); the ground on which historiology is founded is *temporality* as the existential meaning of care.

### 2.6 The connection with Dilthey and Count Yorck (§77)
- Dilthey's researches, divided into three domains (theory of the humane sciences; history of sciences of man, society, state; psychology aimed at understanding "life itself"), converge on a single goal: to understand "life" philosophically and to secure a hermeneutical foundation in terms of life itself.
- Count Yorck's letters to Dilthey demand a sharper *generic* differentiation between the ontical and the Historical, pushing beyond Dilthey's own formulations.
- Yorck's key insights: (a) the "psycho-physical datum" is the germinal point of historicality, not abstract subjectivity; (b) behaviour and historicality are like breathing and atmospheric pressure — inseparable; (c) the separation between systematic philosophy and Historical presentation is "essentially incorrect"; (d) the "fundamental aim of the philosophy of life" is tied to the question of historicality.
- Heidegger appropriates Yorck's demand: the question of historicality is an *ontological* question about the Being of historical entities; it cannot be worked out unless one has first clarified the meaning of Being in general through the existential-temporal analytic of Dasein.
- The preparatory analytic thus serves Dilthey's and Yorck's project by providing the fundamental-ontological ground they lacked.

## Section 6: Tensions & Open Questions

1. **Fate, destiny, and the political (§74)**: Heidegger designates the communal historizing of a *Volk* as "destiny" (*Geschick*) and ties it to shared resoluteness in Being-with Others. The text says nothing about how authentic communal destiny is to be distinguished from ideological mobilization or how the "heritage" that resoluteness takes over is critically evaluated rather than simply affirmed. The language of *Volk*, fate, and struggle operating without any normative filter is a persistent site of political-philosophical controversy. [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D2-U5-P3, BT-D2-U5-P5.

2. **The priority of having-been vs. the futural character of anticipatory resoluteness (§§73–74)**: §73 argues that the historical is determined "predominantly" by having-been (*Gewesenheit*), yet §74 grounds authentic historicality in anticipatory resoluteness, which is primarily *futural*. The text asserts that authentic Being-towards-death is the "hidden basis" of historicality and that repetition retrieves the past *futurally*. Whether having-been or the future genuinely has priority in historicality — or whether the claim is that both are equiprimordial in the ecstatic unity — is left in tension. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D2-U5-P2, BT-D2-U5-P3, BT-D2-U5-P4.

3. **Repetition as retrieval vs. the indeterminacy of the heritage (§74)**: Repetition is defined as a "reciprocal rejoinder" to a possibility of the Dasein that has-been-there, and it is simultaneously a "disavowal" of the today. But the text offers no criterion for which inherited possibilities are to be retrieved and which disavowed, beyond the formal structure of anticipatory resoluteness. The heritage is what thrownness delivers, and resoluteness is what takes it over — but no content-level guidance is given for distinguishing genuine retrieval from arbitrary or self-serving selection. [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D2-U5-P4.

4. **The relation between historicality and within-time-ness (§72)**: Heidegger insists that historicality must be derived from primordial temporality *before* the ordinary time of within-time-ness (Ch. VI) is exposed, and that historically and within-time-ness both "stem" from temporality. Yet the ordinary understanding of history (§73) operates entirely within the vocabulary of the "past," the "present," and being "in time." The degree to which the existential analysis of historicality actually succeeds in freeing itself from within-time-ness — rather than presupposing it at every step — is a methodological vulnerability the text acknowledges (H.377) but does not fully resolve. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D2-U5-P1.

5. **Dilthey appropriation: completion or transformation? (§77)**: Heidegger claims the existential analytic provides the fundamental-ontological ground Dilthey lacked. But the analytic simultaneously transforms the "philosophy of life" into an ontology of Dasein that abandons Dilthey's central category — "life" (*Leben*) — in favour of "existence" (*Existenz*). Whether this constitutes a *fulfilment* of Dilthey's project or a displacement of it into a fundamentally different register is left to the reader. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D2-U5-P7.

6. **Inauthentic historicality as default and the question of access (§§74–75)**: The text acknowledges that Dasein "proximally and for the most part" exists inauthentically and that inauthentic historicality — computing one's history from world-historical "affairs" — is the dominant mode. The analysis of authentic historicality (fate, repetition) is introduced as a structural possibility grounded in anticipatory resoluteness, but the conditions under which Dasein actually transitions from inauthentic to authentic historicality are not specified. This parallels the broader tension between structural possibility and existentiell actualization that runs through Division Two. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D2-U5-P3, BT-D2-U5-P5.
