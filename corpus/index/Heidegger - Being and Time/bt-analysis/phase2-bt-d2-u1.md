# Phase 2 Analysis: BT-D2-U1 (§§45–53)

## Section 1: Unit Metadata

- **unit_id**: BT-D2-U1
- **part**: Part One
- **division**: Division Two
- **chapter**: Preamble (§45) + Chapter I — Dasein's Possibility of Being-a-whole, and Being-towards-death
- **§-range**: §§45–53
- **H. page range**: H.231–267
- **Eng. page range**: 274–311
- **Unit thesis**: This unit establishes that the preparatory analysis of Dasein (Division One) cannot claim primordiality because it has not yet grasped Dasein as a *whole*, and then works out the existential-ontological structure of death as Being-towards-the-end — Dasein's ownmost, non-relational, certain yet indefinite possibility that is not to be outstripped — culminating in the projection of authentic Being-towards-death as *anticipation* (*Vorlaufen*), a freedom towards death that individualizes Dasein and discloses its totality.

## Section 2: Hierarchical Outline

### 2.1 Division Two preamble: the task of a primordial existential Interpretation (§45)
- Division One's result — care as the being of Dasein — is recapitulated, but the analysis is declared non-primordial because it has not brought Dasein's *wholeness* or *authenticity* into view.
- The fore-sight guiding Division One was the idea of existence as potentiality-for-Being, but this potentiality is free for authenticity or inauthenticity, and neither has been existentially determined.
- The task of Division Two is announced: bring Dasein as a whole into our grasp, determine authentic potentiality-for-Being, and show that temporality is the ontological meaning of care.
- The division's chapter plan is previewed: being-towards-death (Ch. 1), conscience and resoluteness (Ch. 2), temporality as meaning of care (Ch. 3), temporality and everydayness (Ch. 4), historicality (Ch. 5), within-time-ness (Ch. 6).

### 2.2 The aporia of Dasein's wholeness and the inadequacy of substitutes (§§46–47)
- The "ahead-of-itself" structure of care means Dasein always has something still outstanding (*Ausstand*); as long as it exists, it has not yet reached its end — and when it reaches its end, it is no longer Dasein (§46).
- The death of Others cannot serve as a substitute theme: the deceased's Being-come-to-an-end is precisely what the survivors do *not* experience; moreover, dying is in each case mine — it cannot be represented by someone else (§47).
- The non-relational and non-substitutable character of death is first established negatively: no one can take the Other's dying away from him.
- The investigation is thereby forced into a purely existential orientation toward Dasein's own death.

### 2.3 Clearing the ground: end, totality, and the demarcation of the existential analysis (§§48–49)
- The "not-yet" of Dasein is distinguished from the not-yet of entities ready-to-hand (a debt still outstanding) and from the not-yet of ripening fruit; Dasein's not-yet *belongs* to it constitutively as a mode of Being, not as an outstanding remainder (§48).
- "Ending" in the sense of stopping, finishing, or disappearing cannot characterize Dasein's death; death is not a Being-at-an-end (*Zu-Ende-sein*) but a Being-towards-the-end (*Sein zum Ende*) (§48).
- The existential analysis of death is demarcated from biology (perishing, *Verenden*), psychology and biography (typologies of dying), theology (metaphysics of death and the afterlife), and Simmel's metaphysics of death. The existential analysis is methodologically *superordinate* to all of these (§49).
- Dasein does not simply perish (*verenden*); it demises (*ableben*); but authentically it *dies* (*sterben*) — dying is the existential phenomenon proper (§49).

### 2.4 The full existential conception of death and everyday evasion (§§50–52)
- Death is sketched as belonging to the structure of care: existence (ahead-of-itself), facticity (thrownness into death), falling (fleeing in the face of death). Death is something that impends (*bevorsteht*), is not yet at one's disposal, and has been reduced to a minimum of outstanding-ness (§50).
- Anxiety in the face of death discloses Being-towards-the-end; it is grounded in care and must be exhibited in everydayness even if proximally in an inauthentic mode (§50).
- Everyday Being-towards-death is characterized as evasion: the "they" interprets death as a "case of death" that occurs publicly, belongs to nobody in particular, tranquillizes Dasein, and tempts it to cover up its ownmost non-relational possibility. The "they" does not permit the courage for anxiety in the face of death (§51).
- The full existential conception: death, as the end of Dasein, is Dasein's *ownmost possibility — non-relational, certain and as such indefinite, not to be outstripped*. Death is, as Dasein's end, in the Being of this entity *towards* its end (§52).
- Everyday certainty of death is inauthentic: it evades *Being*-certain by treating death as empirically certain ("one dies") while covering up its indefiniteness by deferring it to "sometime, but not right away" (§52).

### 2.5 Authentic Being-towards-death: anticipation as freedom (§53)
- Authentic Being-towards-death cannot evade, cover up, or give a new explanation for death; it must hold open the possibility *as* a possibility without weakening it.
- "Being towards" death is distinguished from expecting (*Erwarten*): expecting leaps from the possible to the actual, while anticipation (*Vorlaufen*) holds open the possibility and lets it reveal itself as the possibility of the impossibility of existence.
- Anticipation discloses death as *ownmost*: it wrenches Dasein from the "they-self" and reveals the factical lostness of everydayness.
- Anticipation discloses death as *non-relational*: it individualizes Dasein, lays claim to it as an individual, makes manifest that all Being-alongside and Being-with fail when one's ownmost potentiality-for-Being is at issue.
- Anticipation discloses death as *not to be outstripped*: it frees Dasein for its ownmost possibilities, shatters one's tenaciousness to whatever existence one has reached, and liberates for authentic choice among factical possibilities.
- Anticipation discloses death as *certain*: the certainty is not empirical but existential; it demands holding death for true in the mode of Being-in-the-world, not in the mode of theoretical evidence. It is more primordial than any certainty about present-at-hand entities.
- Anticipation discloses death as *indefinite*: Dasein opens itself to a constant threat arising out of its "there"; the state-of-mind that holds open this threat is anxiety (*Angst*).
- Summary formula: anticipation reveals to Dasein its lostness in the they-self, and brings it face to face with the possibility of being itself — in an impassioned *freedom towards death*, released from the Illusions of the "they," factical, certain of itself, and anxious.
- The unit closes by noting that authentic Being-towards-death has been projected only as an *ontological* possibility; whether Dasein factically demands such authenticity must be investigated through conscience and attestation (transition to Ch. 2).

## Section 6: Tensions & Open Questions

1. **The aporia of wholeness and existence (§§45–46)**: Dasein's Being is care — "ahead-of-itself" — which means it always has something still outstanding. But when it reaches its end, it is no longer Dasein. The analysis must grasp Dasein as a whole *while it exists*, which seems structurally impossible. Heidegger resolves this by redefining death as a *possibility* towards which Dasein exists, not as an event at which Dasein ceases. Whether this resolution genuinely overcomes the aporia or merely relocates it remains debated. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D2-U1-P1, BT-D2-U1-P3.

2. **Anticipation as existential or existentiell? (§53)**: Heidegger insists that the projection of authentic Being-towards-death is "existential" (ontological-structural), not existentiell (a concrete way of life). Yet the summary formula — Dasein freed from the "they," factical, certain of itself, anxious — has unmistakable existentiell resonance. The unit closes by acknowledging that whether Dasein factically *demands* such authenticity has not been established and must be investigated through conscience (§§54–60). The boundary between structural possibility and concrete imperative is porous. [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D2-U1-P7.

3. **The status of anxiety in the face of death (§§50–51, 53)**: Anxiety is identified as the fundamental state-of-mind (*Befindlichkeit*) that discloses Being-towards-death, both in the preliminary sketch (§50) and in the full projection of anticipation (§53). But everyday Dasein transforms anxiety into fear and covers it up. The question of how Dasein *comes to* anxiety — what occasions the transition from inauthentic evasion to authentic anticipation — is not answered here. The appeal to conscience in Chapter 2 is meant to address this, but there is a structural gap: if falling is an essential existentiale, how can Dasein ever authentically sustain anxiety? [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D2-U1-P6, BT-D2-U1-P7.

4. **Death as the possibility of impossibility: logical paradox or phenomenological insight? (§§50, 53)**: Death is defined as "the possibility of the impossibility of existence" and "the possibility of the absolute impossibility of Dasein." Anticipation is said to make this possibility "greater and greater" as one approaches it. This formulation has attracted extensive criticism (Adorno, Derrida): is death really a possibility that Dasein can *have*, or does the impossibility of existence destroy the very subject who would relate to it? Heidegger's resolution depends on death being understood strictly as a possibility towards which Dasein exists, never as an actuality. [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D2-U1-P5, BT-D2-U1-P7.

5. **Solipsism and the non-relational character of death (§53)**: Anticipation individualizes Dasein and discloses that "all Being-alongside the things with which we concern ourselves, and all Being-with Others, will fail us when our ownmost potentiality-for-Being is the issue." Yet Heidegger also insists that Dasein is authentically itself "only to the extent that, *as* concernful Being-alongside and solicitous Being-with, it projects itself upon its ownmost potentiality-for-Being" (H.263). The tension between radical individuation and the claim that authentic existence does not sever concern and solicitude is philosophically unresolved in this unit. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D2-U1-P7, Phase 0 Tension #2 (authenticity vs. sociality), Phase 0 Tension #3 (historicality vs. individuation).

6. **The fruit analogy: illuminating or misleading? (§48)**: Heidegger introduces the analogy of the ripening fruit to show that a "not-yet" can belong constitutively to an entity's Being. But he immediately notes that ripeness *fulfills* the fruit, whereas death does not necessarily fulfill Dasein — "even 'unfulfilled' Dasein ends." The analogy establishes formal similarity (constitutive not-yet) only to reveal material dissimilarity (fulfillment vs. non-fulfillment). Whether the analogy ultimately clarifies or obscures the specificity of Dasein's not-yet is an open question. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D2-U1-P3.
