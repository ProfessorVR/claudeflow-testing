# Phase 2 Analysis: BT-D2-U4 (§§67–71)

## Section 1: Unit Metadata

- **unit_id**: BT-D2-U4
- **part**: Part One
- **division**: Division Two
- **chapter**: IV — Temporality and Everydayness
- **§-range**: §§67–71
- **H. page range**: H.334–372
- **Eng. page range**: 383–423
- **Unit thesis**: This unit demonstrates that temporality (*Zeitlichkeit*) is the existential-temporal ground of Dasein's everyday disclosedness — understanding, state-of-mind, falling, and discourse — and extends this temporal Interpretation to Being-in-the-world as a whole: circumspective concern, the genesis of theoretical discovery, the transcendence of the world, the spatiality of Dasein, and the temporal meaning of everydayness as such. The chapter thereby concretizes the abstract thesis of §65 (temporality as the meaning of care) by exhibiting the specific ecstatic-horizonal temporalizations that constitute each structural moment of Dasein's everyday existence.

## Section 2: Hierarchical Outline

### 2.1 Preliminary sketch of the temporal Interpretation (§67)
- Recapitulation of Division One's preparatory analysis: the structures of disclosedness (understanding, state-of-mind, falling, discourse) and care must now be exhibited in their temporal constitution.
- Temporality is the primordial totality that makes possible the unity of these structures; it does not coincide with the simplicity of an ultimate element but is an *articulated* whole.
- Chapter plan: §68 (temporality of disclosedness), §69 (temporality of Being-in-the-world and transcendence), §70 (temporality of spatiality), §71 (temporal meaning of everydayness).

### 2.2 The temporality of disclosedness in general (§68)
- **(a) Temporality of understanding**: Understanding is primarily futural — it projects upon possibilities. Authentic future = *anticipation* (*Vorlaufen*); inauthentic future = *awaiting* (*Gewärtigen*). The Present corresponding to anticipation is the *moment of vision* (*Augenblick*); the inauthentic Present is *making present* (*Gegenwärtigen*). Having-been in authentic mode = *repetition* (*Wiederholung*); in inauthentic mode = *forgetting* (*Vergessen*).
- **(b) Temporality of state-of-mind**: State-of-mind (*Befindlichkeit*) temporalizes itself primarily in *having been* (*Gewesenheit*). Fear is grounded in a forgetting that awaits and makes present (bewildered making-present). Anxiety is grounded primarily in having-been, bringing Dasein back to its thrown "that-it-is" as repeatable; its Present is held on to at the ready (*auf dem Sprung*), not yet the moment of vision but the mood for a possible resolution.
- **(c) Temporality of falling**: Falling has its existential meaning in the *Present*. Curiosity is a making-present that leaps away from awaiting and retention; its ecstatical mode is *never-dwelling-anywhere*, the counter-phenomenon to the moment of vision. The Present leaps away from the authentic future, constituting the falling into lostness; yet this has its source in primordial authentic temporality.
- **(d) Temporality of discourse**: Discourse does not temporalize itself primarily in any one definite ecstasis but Articulates the disclosedness constituted by understanding, state-of-mind, and falling. Tenses and temporal "aspects" of language are rooted in the primordial temporality of concern. The problem of the temporal structure of the "is" (copula) cannot be formulated in terms of the ordinary conception of time.

### 2.3 The temporality of Being-in-the-world and the problem of transcendence (§69)
- **(a) Temporality of circumspective concern**: Concern (*Besorgen*) is grounded in the ecstatical unity of awaiting-retaining-making-present. Letting things be involved (*Bewandtnis*) is the existential structure of concern; its possibility must be sought in a mode of temporalizing. Circumspective deliberation (*Überlegung*) is the "if–then" schema bringing entities closer by interpreting the involvement-context. The as-structure (*Als-Struktur*) of understanding and interpretation is grounded in the ecstatico-horizonal unity of temporality.
- **(b) The genesis of theoretical discovery from circumspective concern**: The theoretical attitude does not arise from the mere "disappearance of praxis" but through a change-over in the understanding of Being — from readiness-to-hand to presence-at-hand. The mathematical projection of Nature (Galileo, Newton) is the paradigmatic case: it discloses something *a priori* and determines what counts as a "fact." Thematizing Objectifies entities by a distinctive kind of making-present, freeing them for "pure discovery."
- **(c) The temporal problem of the transcendence of the world**: The existential-temporal condition for the world lies in temporality's ecstatico-horizonal character. Each ecstasis has a "horizonal schema" (the "whither" of rapture): the future's schema is the "for-the-sake-of-itself," having-been's schema is "that in the face of which" / "that to which abandoned," the Present's schema is the "in-order-to." These schemata in their unity constitute worldhood. The world is neither ready-to-hand nor present-at-hand but temporalizes itself in temporality; if no Dasein exists, no world is "there." Transcendence is grounded in ecstatico-horizonal temporality, not in a subject's crossing over to an Object.

### 2.4 The temporality of Dasein's spatiality (§70) and the temporal meaning of everydayness (§71)
- Dasein's spatiality — making room, de-severance, directionality — is existentially possible only through temporality. Dasein "takes space in" (*Raum-einnehmen*) not as an extended corporeal Thing but as care. Bringing-close (de-severing) is grounded in the Present of falling, which "leaps after" spatial relationships of making-present. Temporality makes it possible for Dasein to "break into" space; space is independent of time, yet Dasein's spatiality depends on temporality.
- "Everydayness" is not the sum of "days" but the *how* of Dasein's average existence — "proximally and for the most part." It is a way *to be*, publicly manifest and determinative even when Dasein has not chosen the "they" for its hero. The ontological delimitation of everydayness requires the framework of temporality; Dasein "stretches along" temporally in the sequence of its days, pointing forward to the problematic of historicality (Ch V) and within-time-ness (Ch VI).

## Section 6: Tensions & Open Questions

1. **Discourse's temporal indeterminacy (§68d)**: Heidegger states that discourse "does not temporalize itself primarily in any definite ecstasis" but Articulates the disclosedness constituted by the other three structural moments. This raises the question of whether discourse has a genuinely *equiprimordial* status with understanding, state-of-mind, and falling, or whether it is structurally parasitic on them. The brevity of §68d relative to §68a–c reinforces this asymmetry. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D2-U4-P1.

2. **The scope of the theoretical change-over (§69b)**: Heidegger describes the genesis of theoretical discovery as a change-over in the understanding of Being, exemplified by mathematical physics. But he explicitly restricts his analysis to the case where "circumspective concern with the ready-to-hand changes over into an exploration of what we come across as present-at-hand." Whether the human sciences, history, or philosophy itself undergo an analogous change-over is left open — Heidegger notes that the existential Interpretation of science "cannot be carried out until the meaning of Being and the 'connection' between Being and truth have been clarified." [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D2-U4-P4.

3. **Horizonal schemata as the unwritten ontology (§69c)**: The horizonal schemata are introduced as the key to solving the problem of transcendence and worldhood, yet their full elaboration was projected for the unpublished Division Three ("Time and Being"). The published text provides only a sketch — "for-the-sake-of," "in-the-face-of-which," "in-order-to" — without developing the horizonal problematic into a general ontology. Whether these schemata can bear the systematic weight assigned to them is underdetermined by the text as published. [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D2-U4-P5.

4. **Temporality and spatiality: grounding without reduction (§70)**: Heidegger insists that Dasein's spatiality is grounded in temporality but that space is "independent of time." This produces an asymmetric dependence: Dasein is spatial *because* it is temporal, yet space itself is not temporal. The precise character of this dependence relation — and how it differs from Kant's treatment and from later Heidegger's notion of time-space (*Zeit-Raum*) — is left underdeveloped. [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D2-U4-P6.

5. **Everydayness as residual category (§71)**: Heidegger acknowledges that the analytic has thus far exhibited temporal structures "before" investigating everydayness existentially, and that it remains questionable whether the explication of temporality carried through so far is sufficient to delimit the existential-temporal meaning of everydayness. The section is openly transitional, admitting that everydayness can only be adequately grasped when historicality and within-time-ness have been exhibited. This raises the question of whether "everydayness" functions as a genuine existential concept or as a placeholder for the undifferentiated average. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D2-U4-P7.
