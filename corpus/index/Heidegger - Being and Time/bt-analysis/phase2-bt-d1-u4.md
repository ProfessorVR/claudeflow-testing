# Phase 2 Analysis: BT-D1-U4 (§§22–27)

## Section 1: Unit Metadata

- **unit_id**: BT-D1-U4
- **part**: Part One
- **division**: Division One
- **chapter**: III.C (The Aroundness of the Environment, and Dasein's Spatiality) + IV (Being-in-the-world as Being-with and Being-one's-Self. The "They")
- **§-range**: §§22–27
- **H. page range**: H.101–130
- **Eng. page range**: 134–168
- **Unit thesis**: This unit completes the analysis of worldhood by exhibiting the existential spatiality of Dasein (de-severance and directionality as constitutive of Being-in-the-world) and then turns to the question of the "who" of everyday Dasein, demonstrating that Dasein is essentially Being-with (*Mitsein*), that Others are encountered through solicitude (*Fürsorge*), and that the "who" of everyday existence is not the individual Self but the anonymous "they" (*das Man*) whose dominion constitutes the existentiale of publicness, averageness, and levelling down.

## Section 2: Hierarchical Outline

### 2.1 The spatiality of the ready-to-hand and of Being-in-the-world (§§22–23)
- §22 introduces the spatiality of the ready-to-hand within-the-world: equipment has closeness (*Nähe*), place (*Platz*), and belongs to a region (*Gegend*) — a "whither" that circumspective concern keeps in view as a pre-discovered totality of places.
- The region has the character of inconspicuous familiarity; it becomes visible only in deficient modes (e.g., failing to find something in its place).
- §23 exhibits Dasein's own spatiality through two existentiale: **de-severance** (*Ent-fernung*) — the active bringing-close that makes remoteness vanish — and **directionality** (*Ausrichtung*) — the oriented directedness toward regions in which things belong.
- De-severance is not measured distance but a circumspective estimation ("a good walk," "a stone's throw"); Dasein is essentially de-severant, i.e., spatial.
- Directionality is grounded in Being-in-the-world, not in subjective feelings of left and right; Kant's example of orientation in a dark room presupposes a world already familiar.
- De-severance and directionality together constitute Dasein's spatiality as a mode of Being-in, prior to any theoretical cognition of space.

### 2.2 Space and Dasein's spatiality (§24)
- The space disclosed through worldhood lacks the pure three-dimensionality of homogeneous geometric space; it is articulated by regions, places, and involvements.
- When entities are let be encountered in the way constitutive for Being-in-the-world, Dasein "gives space" (*Raum-geben*) or "makes room" (*einräumen*) — an existentiale belonging to Being-in-the-world.
- "Space is not in the subject, nor is the world in space" — space is rather 'in' the world insofar as Being-in-the-world has disclosed space; the 'subject' (Dasein), ontologically understood, is itself spatial.
- When space is discovered non-circumspectively (by pure looking), the environmental regions are neutralized to pure dimensions; the ready-to-hand becomes merely present-at-hand; the world loses its aroundness and becomes the world of Nature — extended Things deprived of worldhood.
- The Being of space is neither ready-to-hand nor present-at-hand; it cannot be reduced to *res extensa* or to *res cogitans*. The ontological problem of space requires liberation from the narrowness of traditional concepts of Being.

### 2.3 The "who" of Dasein: Being-with and the Dasein-with of Others (§§25–26)
- §25 poses the question of the "who" of everyday Dasein. The seemingly obvious answer — "I myself" — must be held in suspicion: the formal indication "I" may conceal that Dasein, proximally and for the most part, is *not* itself.
- The approach to the "who" must be phenomenological: starting from Being-in-the-world, not from an isolated subject or "I"-Thing; the Self must be interpreted existentially, not as substance or *subjectum*.
- §26 shows that Others are encountered not by theoretical cognition but *from* the world — in the work-world of equipment ("the field belongs to such-and-such a person"), along with entities ready-to-hand.
- Others are not present-at-hand Things but entities encountered as Dasein — they are "in" the world by way of Being-in-the-world; hence Dasein's world is always a *with-world* (*Mitwelt*); Being-in is Being-with (*Mitsein*); Others' Being-in-themselves is Dasein-with (*Mitdasein*).
- Being-with is an existential characteristic of Dasein even when factically no Other is present-at-hand or perceived; even Being-alone is a deficient mode of Being-with.
- Dasein's comportment toward Others is *solicitude* (*Fürsorge*), distinct from *concern* (*Besorgen*) toward equipment. Solicitude ranges between two positive extremes: (a) "leaping in" (*einspringen*) — taking over the Other's care, dominating — and (b) "leaping ahead" (*vorausspringen*) — freeing the Other for his own existentiell potentiality-for-Being.
- Everyday Being-with-one-another maintains itself in the deficient and indifferent modes of solicitude (passing one another by, not "mattering" to one another). Solicitude is guided by *considerateness* (*Rücksicht*) and *forbearance* (*Nachsicht*).

### 2.4 Everyday Being-one's-Self and the "they" (§27)
- The ontologically relevant result of the analysis of Being-with: the "subject character" of Dasein and Others is to be defined existentially — they *are* what they do.
- In everyday Being-with, Dasein stands in *subjection* (*Botmässigkeit*) to Others. The "who" is not this one or that one, not oneself, not some people, not the sum of them all — it is the neuter, *das Man* ("the 'they'").
- *Das Man* has its own ways of Being: **distantiality** (*Abständigkeit*) — the concern to maintain distance from Others; **averageness** (*Durchschnittlichkeit*) — the levelling to what is proper and common; **levelling down** (*Einebnung*) — the suppression of every exception and priority.
- These three constitute **publicness** (*Öffentlichkeit*), which controls how the world and Dasein get interpreted, is always "right" because it never gets to the heart of the matter, and obscures everything while passing it off as familiar and accessible.
- The "they" *disburdens* (*entlastet*) Dasein of its Being; by accommodating Dasein in its everydayness, it retains and enhances its dominion. Everyone is the other, and no one is himself.
- The "they" is an *existentiale* belonging to Dasein's positive constitution, not merely a negative deviation. The Self of everyday Dasein is the *they-self* (*Man-selbst*), distinguished from the *authentic Self* (*eigentliches Selbst*) which has seized upon itself in its own way.
- Proximally, Dasein is "they" and for the most part remains so; authentic Being-one's-Self is an existentiell modification of the "they."
- The closing passage states the unit's systematic yield: the everyday kind of Being — publicness, averageness, the "they" — is what proximally misses itself and covers itself up, yet makes possible an ontological Interpretation of the everyday state of Being. *Authentic Being-one's-Self* does not rest upon an exceptional condition of the subject detached from the "they"; it is rather an existentiell modification of the "they" as an essential existentiale.

## Section 6: Tensions & Open Questions

1. **Spatiality and temporality (§§22–24)**: Dasein's spatiality is exhibited as an existentiale of Being-in-the-world, yet Division Two will claim that temporality is the ontological meaning of care and thus of all existentiale. The question of how Dasein's spatiality is itself to be grounded in temporality is raised but never fully resolved in the published text (see §70, which treats it only provisionally). Heidegger himself acknowledges the difficulty: "The problematic of space (with regard to that phenomenon itself and various phenomenal spatialities) must be turned in such a direction as to clarify the possibilities of Being in general" (H.113). [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U4-P2, BT-D1-U4-P3.

2. **The status of the "they" as existentiale vs. evaluative category (§27)**: Heidegger insists that *das Man* is an existentiale — a positive ontological structure — not a moral judgment on "mass society." Yet the vocabulary of §27 (subjection, dominion, dictatorship, disburdening, levelling down, "no one is himself") carries heavy evaluative weight. The tension between the formal-ontological neutrality claimed for the analysis and the quasi-normative tone of its descriptions is a long-standing interpretive problem. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U4-P7, BT-D1-U4-P8.

3. **Being-alone as deficient mode of Being-with (§26)**: Heidegger claims that Being-alone *presupposes* Being-with and is merely its deficient mode (H.120). This structural claim is asserted rather than argued: why should solitude be derivative rather than equiprimordial? The account of how Being-with can fail to be actual while remaining constitutive is sketched via analogy to concern (circumspective concern remains constitutive even in deficient modes), but the argument depends on the strength of the analogy. [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D1-U4-P5.

4. **Leaping-ahead and the possibility of authentic community (§26)**: The positive mode of solicitude — "leaping ahead" — frees the Other for authentic care. This raises the question (not addressed here) of whether authentic Being-with-one-another is possible as a sustained communal mode, or whether authenticity remains structurally individuating. The later analysis of "destiny" (*Geschick*, §74) gestures toward authentic community, but its relation to the leaping-ahead of §26 is underdeveloped. [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D1-U4-P6.

5. **The "who" as formal indication (§25)**: Heidegger insists that "I" is only a "formal indicator" (*formale Anzeige*) that does not settle the ontological question. But the method by which the formal indication gets "filled in" — starting from the world rather than from the "I" — is itself a substantive commitment that shapes the outcome. The question is whether the suspicion of the "I" has been methodologically justified or merely declared. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U4-P4.

6. **Circumspective estimation vs. measurable distance (§23)**: De-severance is characterized in terms of circumspective estimates ("a good walk," "a stone's throw"), and Heidegger insists these are not "subjective" but reveal the world more primordially than objective measurement. Yet the relationship between existential de-severance and the mathematized space of physics remains unclear: is the latter merely "derived" from the former, or do they belong to different orders? §24 gestures at the problem but defers it. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U4-P1, BT-D1-U4-P2.
