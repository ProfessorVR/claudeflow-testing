- **Unit thesis**: This unit works out the ontological structure of worldhood by exhibiting the Being of entities encountered in everyday concernful dealings as readiness-to-hand (*Zuhandenheit*), showing how the world "announces itself" through the breakdown modes of conspicuousness, obtrusiveness, and obstinacy, clarifying the referential structure of equipment through the phenomenology of signs, and culminating in the formal determination of worldhood as the relational totality of involvement (*Bewandtnis*) and significance (*Bedeutsamkeit*) grounded in Dasein's for-the-sake-of-which.

## Section 2: Hierarchical Outline

### 2.1 The idea of worldhood and the approach through the environment (§14)

- "Worldhood" (*Weltlichkeit*) is an ontological concept designating the structure of the world as a constitutive moment of Being-in-the-world; it is an existentiale of Dasein, not a property of entities within-the-world.
- Four senses of "world" are distinguished: (1) ontic — totality of present-at-hand entities; (2) ontological — the Being of those entities (e.g., the "world" of the mathematician); (3) ontic-existentiell — the "wherein" a factical Dasein lives; (4) ontologico-existential — worldhood as such.
- Traditional ontology (Nature-ontology, Descartes' *res extensa*) misses worldhood by starting from present-at-hand Things and their properties rather than from Dasein's everyday dealings.
- The methodological decision: begin with the world of everyday Dasein closest to it — the environment (*Umwelt*) — and work out worldhood through an analysis of environmentality (*Umweltlichkeit*).

### 2.2 Equipment, readiness-to-hand, and circumspection (§15)

- The entities closest to Dasein's dealings are not objects of theoretical cognition but equipment (*Zeug*) encountered in concern (*Besorgen*). The Greeks had the right term: *pragmata* — things one has to do with in *praxis*.
- There is strictly no such thing as "an" equipment; equipment always belongs to a totality of equipment (*Zeugganzes*). Each item is essentially "something in-order-to" (*Um-zu*), constituted by its referential assignments to other equipment.
- Equipment is not grasped thematically in use; its Being manifests precisely when it withdraws from notice. The hammering does not grasp the hammer as a Thing — it has appropriated it in a way that reveals its specific "manipulability" (*Handlichkeit*).
- The kind of Being in which equipment manifests itself in its own right is **readiness-to-hand** (*Zuhandenheit*). This is not discovered by theoretical looking (*Hinsehen*) but through circumspective dealing (*Umsicht*).
- Readiness-to-hand is the way entities are "in themselves" ontologico-categorially; it is not ontologically founded on presence-at-hand but is rather more primordial.
- The work (*Werk*) bears the referential totality: it has a "towards-which" (*Wozu*) of usability and a "for-which" (*Wofür*) of materials. The work also assigns to persons (the wearer, the user), and through the work the public world and environing Nature are encountered.

### 2.3 Breakdown and the announcement of worldhood (§16)

- The world itself is not an entity within-the-world, yet it announces itself precisely when everyday dealings are disturbed. Three modes of disturbance bring the worldly character of the environment to the fore:
  - **Conspicuousness** (*Auffälligkeit*): equipment is unusable, damaged, or inappropriate — it becomes "conspicuous" and readiness-to-hand is partially suspended, revealing the "towards-this" and the workshop context.
  - **Obtrusiveness** (*Aufdringlichkeit*): something needed is missing; what is un-ready-to-hand obtrudes upon us, and the entire referential context lights up in its absence.
  - **Obstinacy** (*Aufsässigkeit*): something "stands in the way" of our concern — equipment is un-ready-to-hand not because it is unusable or missing, but because it does not belong and must be dealt with before anything else.
- All three modes bring presence-at-hand to the fore *within* what is ready-to-hand, but readiness-to-hand does not vanish; it "takes its farewell" and the worldly character of the ready-to-hand shows itself precisely here.
- The "Being-in-itself" of entities is phenomenally constituted by their inconspicuousness, unobtrusiveness, and non-obstinacy — the privative character of undisturbed readiness-to-hand. If the world is to remain phenomenally hidden, the ready-to-hand must *not* announce itself.

### 2.4 Reference, signs, and the ontology of indicating (§17)

- The phenomenon of reference (*Verweisung*) was identified in §15 as constitutive of equipment; §17 investigates it more precisely through the ontological analysis of signs (*Zeichen*).
- Signs are themselves items of equipment whose specific character lies in *indicating* (*Anzeige*). Indicating is a kind of referring, but not every reference is an indication, and not every relation is a reference. The formally general character of "relation" has its ontological source in reference.
- The example of the turn-signal (adjustable red arrow on a motor car): the sign is ready-to-hand within the equipment-context of driving and traffic; it explicitly raises a totality of equipment into circumspection and lets the worldly character of the ready-to-hand announce itself.
- A sign is not a Thing that stands to another Thing in an indicating relation; it is an item of equipment that *explicitly raises* the referential totality and worldhood into circumspective accessibility. Signs have a peculiar ontological priority: they can make the "wherein" of one's concern and involvement manifest.
- Primitive sign-use (fetishism, magic) does not refute the equipmental analysis: for "primitive" Dasein, the sign coincides with what it indicates because readiness-to-hand and Objectification are not yet differentiated — not because signs are mere Things.
- The threefold result: (1) indicating makes the "towards-which" of serviceability concrete upon the "in-order-to" of equipment; (2) indicating is itself an equipmental character belonging to a referential totality; (3) through the sign, the environment becomes explicitly accessible for circumspection. A sign *ontically* ready-to-hand indicates (*anzeigt*) the ontological structure of readiness-to-hand, referential totalities, and worldhood.

### 2.5 Involvement, significance, and the formal structure of worldhood (§18)

- The Being of the ready-to-hand is involvement (*Bewandtnis*): to say that an entity has the character of reference or assignment means it has in itself the character of "having been assigned or referred" (*Verwiesenheit*). With any such entity, there is an involvement in something — letting it be involved (*Bewendenlassen*).
- The totality of involvements is constitutive for readiness-to-hand, and is "earlier" than any single item of equipment. Involvements go back through chains of "towards-which" (*Wozu*) until they reach a "towards-which" that is no further involvement: the **for-the-sake-of-which** (*Worumwillen* / *Umwillen*), which pertains to Dasein's own Being.
- "Letting something be involved" (*Bewandtnis haben lassen*) is the *a priori* condition for encountering anything ready-to-hand. Ontologically, it means previously freeing entities within-the-world for their readiness-to-hand; ontically, it means letting things be in our factical concern as they already are.
- Understanding (*Verstehen*) is the mode in which Dasein lets itself make assignments in these relationships. Dasein "signifies" to itself: it gives itself its Being and potentiality-for-Being in terms of the "for-the-sake-of-which," the "in-order-to," the "towards-this," and the "with-which" of involvement. The relational totality of this signifying is **significance** (*Bedeutsamkeit*).
- Significance is the formal structure of the world as disclosed in Dasein's familiarity with it. Dasein, in its familiarity with significance, is the ontical condition for the possibility of discovering entities ready-to-hand in their kind of Being. Upon this structure is founded the Being of words and of language.
- Three kinds of Being must be distinguished: (1) readiness-to-hand (equipment); (2) presence-at-hand (entities discoverable through the ready-to-hand); (3) the worldhood of the world — the *existential* way of determining Being-in-the-world, pertaining to Dasein. Categories apply to (1) and (2); significance, as an existentiale, applies to (3).

## Section 6: Tensions & Open Questions

1. **Readiness-to-hand as "more primordial" than presence-at-hand (§15, §16)**: Heidegger claims that readiness-to-hand is not ontologically founded on presence-at-hand but is rather prior. Yet the phenomenal evidence for readiness-to-hand comes through its *disruption* — that is, through the very modes (conspicuousness, obtrusiveness, obstinacy) in which presence-at-hand emerges. The priority claim thus rests on a methodological reversal (the order of discovery is the inverse of the order of Being) that Heidegger asserts but does not fully argue for in this unit. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U2-P2, BT-D1-U2-P3.

2. **The status of Nature within the equipmental analysis (§15)**: Heidegger insists that "Nature" is encountered through equipment — as timber, water-power, wind-in-the-sails — and that Nature "as the power of Nature" or as present-at-hand is a derivative encounter. But the text also acknowledges that "the Nature which 'stirs and strives,' which assails us and enthralls us as landscape, remains hidden" when Nature is discovered only through readiness-to-hand (H.70). Whether this aesthetic-affective encounter with Nature can be accommodated within the equipmental analysis, or whether it signals a mode of Being that exceeds the readiness-to-hand/presence-at-hand distinction, is left open. [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D1-U2-P2.

3. **Formalization and the sign (§17)**: Heidegger distinguishes indicating from reference and reference from relation, insisting that formalization ("reading off" structure from any context) cannot ground the ontology of signs. But the analysis itself proceeds partly by formal distinction (every indication is a reference, not every reference is an indication, etc.), and the positive ontological ground of reference is deferred to §18. The status of formal distinctions within phenomenological analysis remains unclear. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U2-P4.

4. **The "a priori perfect tense" of involvement (§18, H.85)**: The claim that "letting something be involved" is an *a priori* perfect — Dasein has always already freed entities for involvement — introduces a temporal structure (prior freeing) before temporality has been thematized. Whether this "always already" is merely a structural description or an implicit temporal claim creates an internal tension with the treatise's announced order of exposition (temporality first appears in Division Two). [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D1-U2-P5.

5. **Significance, language, and the Being of words (§18, H.87)**: Heidegger claims that upon significance "is founded the Being of words and of language." This is a single sentence, offered without argument, and it is unclear how the referential-involvement structure of worldhood grounds linguistic meaning specifically. The later analysis of discourse (*Rede*, §34) will address this, but the promissory character of the claim here leaves the relation between significance and language as an open question. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U2-P5.

6. **Worldhood and "pure thinking" (§18, H.88)**: At the close of §18, Heidegger raises but does not answer the objection that his analysis "volatilizes" the substantial Being of entities within-the-world into a system of Relations. The text distinguishes three kinds of Being (readiness-to-hand, presence-at-hand, worldhood) and warns that formalization loses the phenomenal content of significance. But whether worldhood *as* a relational totality can resist the charge of idealism — especially given that it is grounded in Dasein's for-the-sake-of-which — is a tension the unit acknowledges without resolving. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U2-P5.
