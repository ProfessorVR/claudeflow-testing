## Section 2: Hierarchical Outline

### 2.1 The necessity of restating the question of Being (§1)
- The question of Being, once the animating impulse of Plato and Aristotle, has since been trivialized: treated as the most universal, indefinable, and self-evident "concept."
- Three prejudices: (a) "Being" is the most universal concept (but universality ≠ clarity); (b) "Being" is indefinable (true — but this makes the question *more* urgent, not less); (c) "Being" is self-evident (every judgment and comportment presupposes an understanding of Being, but this familiarity is precisely what conceals the question).
- The dogma that the question is superfluous actually confirms the need to restate it.

### 2.2 The formal structure of the question (§2)
- Every question has three structural moments: *das Befragte* (that which is asked about — Being), *das Gefragtes* (that which is interrogated — beings/entities, since Being is always the Being *of* some entity), and *das Erfragtes* (that which is to be found out — the meaning of Being, *der Sinn von Sein*).
- The question of Being is thus not a question about a being; it requires a distinctive mode of access that differs from knowledge of entities.
- To formulate the question properly, we must clarify how to "look at," "understand," and "conceptually grasp" Being — and this requires a prior (pre-ontological) understanding of Being.

### 2.3 The ontological priority of the question (§3)
- The sciences (history, nature, mathematics, theology) each operate within a "region" of beings whose fundamental concepts and basic structures presuppose an understanding of Being.
- The "crisis" in the foundations of the sciences signals the need for an ontological inquiry that is *prior* to any particular science.
- The question of Being is the most fundamental question: it asks about the condition for the possibility of the ontologies that underlie the sciences.

### 2.4 The ontical priority of the question: Dasein as the point of access (§4)
- The question of Being requires an entity that can *ask* it — an entity for whom its own Being is an issue. This entity is Dasein.
- Dasein has a *pre-ontological understanding* of Being: in its very existing, it comports itself toward its Being and toward Being in general.
- Dasein's double priority: (a) **ontically**, Dasein is distinguished by the fact that it exists — its Being is an issue for it; (b) **ontologically**, Dasein's existence includes an understanding of Being, making it the proper entity to interrogate.
- The analytic of Dasein is therefore the "first thing to be called for" in the working-out of the question of Being.

## Section 6: Tensions & Open Questions

1. **The hermeneutic circle (§2)**: The question of Being requires a pre-ontological understanding of Being to get started, yet this understanding is precisely what the question aims to clarify. Heidegger acknowledges this circle (H.7: "what is to be found out... is already well known to us, even if not conceptually") but defers its treatment. This is a foundational tension for the entire project. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-Intro-U1-P2, BT-Intro-U1-P3.

2. **Dasein-centrism vs. the question of Being itself (§§2, 4)**: The question asks about the meaning of Being *in general*, but the access point is Dasein's particular understanding. Whether this access is a genuine path to Being as such or a structural limitation is left open. §4 insists that Dasein's priority is both ontical and ontological, but the movement from Dasein's understanding *to* Being itself (projected as Division Three) is never accomplished. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-Intro-U1-P3.

3. **Pre-ontological understanding: resource or obstacle? (§§2, 4)**: The pre-ontological understanding is what makes the question of Being *possible*, but it is also characterized as "vague and average" (H.5). Whether this understanding is a reliable guide or a source of distortion is not resolved here. The later analysis of everydayness and fallenness will sharpen this tension. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-Intro-U1-P2, BT-Intro-U1-P3.
