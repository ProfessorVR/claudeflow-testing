# Phase 2 Analysis: BT-D1-U5 (§§28–31)

## Section 1: Unit Metadata

- **unit_id**: BT-D1-U5
- **part**: Part One

- **chapter**: V.A (pt 1) — Being-in as Such: The Existential Constitution of the "There"
- **§-range**: §§28–31
- **H. page range**: H.130–148
- **Eng. page range**: 169–188
- **Unit thesis**: This unit opens the thematic analysis of Being-in as such by establishing that Dasein *is* its disclosedness ("there") and then working out the two equiprimordial existentialia that constitute this disclosedness — state-of-mind (*Befindlichkeit*), which discloses Dasein in its thrownness, and understanding (*Verstehen*), which discloses Dasein in its projection upon possibilities — with fear analyzed as a concrete mode of state-of-mind.

## Section 2: Hierarchical Outline

### 2.1 The task of a thematic analysis of Being-in (§28)
- Prior analyses addressed the world and the "who" of everyday Dasein; the investigation now turns to Being-in as such, the phenomenon that was methodologically deferred.
- Being-in is not the present-at-hand insideness of one thing "in" another; it is an essential kind of Being of Dasein itself, not a *commercium* between subject and object.
- Dasein *is* its "there" (*Da*): in the expression "there" Heidegger has in view the essential disclosedness (*Erschlossenheit*) of this entity — Dasein is its disclosedness.
- The "there" is constituted equiprimordially by state-of-mind (*Befindlichkeit*), understanding (*Verstehen*), and discourse (*Rede*); the present chapter (V.A) takes up the first two plus fear as a concrete mode.
- The analysis is existential, not a description of present-at-hand properties; characteristics are ways to be, not categorical attributes.

### 2.2 State-of-mind as the disclosure of thrownness (§29)
- *Befindlichkeit* is the ontological-existential term for what is ontically familiar as mood (*Stimmung*); Dasein always already has a mood, even the pallid lack of mood is a way of being attuned.
- State-of-mind has three essential characteristics: (1) it discloses Dasein in its thrownness (*Geworfenheit*) — the "that it is and has to be" which is veiled in its "whence" and "whither"; (2) it has already disclosed Being-in-the-world as a whole, making it possible first of all to direct oneself toward something; (3) it discloses the worldhood of the world in the mode of circumspective concern, constituting Dasein's openness to being affected by what it encounters.
- Thrownness names the facticity of Dasein's being delivered over to its Being; this is not the factuality of a *factum brutum* present-at-hand, but a characteristic of Dasein's Being that has been taken up into existence.
- Moods disclose prior to and beyond cognition and volition; even the purest *theoria* has not left all moods behind.
- Aristotle's investigation of *pathe* in *Rhetoric* II is identified as the first systematic hermeneutic of the everydayness of Being-with-one-another; the ontological tradition since Aristotle has failed to advance the basic interpretation of the affective life.
- State-of-mind not only discloses thrownness but is itself the existential kind of Being in which Dasein surrenders itself to the "world" — a pointer toward the phenomenon of falling.

### 2.3 Fear as a mode of state-of-mind (§30)
- Fear is analyzed from three structural vantage points: (1) that in the face of which we fear (*das Wovor*), (2) fearing as such, and (3) that about which we fear (*das Worum*).
- The fearsome is something encountered within-the-world that has the character of being threatening: it is detrimental, comes from a definite region, draws close, and yet may pass by — "it is fearsome."
- In fearing as such, the threatening is freed and allowed to matter; fearing discovers the fearsome beforehand in its fearsomeness and does not first ascertain a *malum futurum*.
- That about which fear fears is Dasein itself — the entity whose Being is an issue for it; fear discloses Dasein as endangered and abandoned to itself, always revealing Dasein in the Being of its "there."
- Variations: fearing-for (Others), fearing-about (one's Being-with), alarm (*Erschrecken*), dread (*Grauen*), terror (*Entsetzen*); all point to Dasein's "fearfulness" (*Furchtsamkeit*) as an existential possibility.

### 2.4 Understanding as projection upon possibilities (§31)
- Understanding (*Verstehen*) is equiprimordial with state-of-mind as a basic existentiale constituting the Being of the "there."
- Understanding is not one cognitive capacity among others (not "explaining" as opposed to another mode); it is a fundamental mode of Dasein's Being as potentiality-for-Being (*Seinkönnen*).
- Dasein is primarily Being-possible; existential possibility is sharply distinguished from empty logical possibility and from the contingency of the present-at-hand. Possibility as existentiale is the most primordial and ultimate positive way in which Dasein is characterized.
- Dasein is thrown possibility through and through — delivered over to itself as potentiality-for-Being, constantly projecting upon or waiving its possibilities.
- The existential structure by which understanding always presses forward into possibilities is *projection* (*Entwurf*): Dasein has always already projected itself and is projecting as long as it is. Projection is not a plan or deliberate arrangement but the kind of Being in which Dasein *is* its possibilities as possibilities.
- Understanding is either authentic or inauthentic; in every case it pertains to Dasein's full disclosedness as Being-in-the-world, and its diversion in inauthenticity is an existential modification of projection as a whole.
- Understanding constitutes Dasein's existential "sight" (*Sicht*): circumspection (*Umsicht*) of concern, considerateness (*Rücksicht*) of solicitude, sight upon Being as such (*Sicht auf das Sein als solches*), and transparence (*Durchsichtigkeit*) as self-knowledge.
- "Intuition" and "thinking" are derivatives of understanding, not the reverse; even the phenomenological "intuition of essences" (*Wesensschau*) is grounded in existential understanding.
- The existential meaning of Dasein's understanding of Being cannot be fully clarified except on the basis of the Temporal Interpretation of Being — a forward reference to Division Two.

## Section 6: Tensions & Open Questions

1. **Cognition vs. mood as primordial disclosure (§29)**: Heidegger claims that state-of-mind discloses Dasein in its thrownness "prior to all cognition and volition, and *beyond* their range of disclosure" (H.136). Yet Dasein also "can, should, and must, through knowledge and will, become master of its moods" (H.136). The exact ontological relationship between the priority of mood and the legitimate role of cognition is not resolved; the claim is that cognition has its existential-ontological Constitution *in* the state-of-mind of Being-in-the-world, but how this "in" works structurally is left underdeveloped. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U5-P2, BT-D1-U5-P7.

2. **Thrownness as veiled yet disclosed (§29)**: State-of-mind discloses the "that it is," yet this "that it is" is precisely what is "veiled in its whence and whither" (H.135). Disclosure and concealment are thus simultaneous in thrownness. The text gestures toward this paradox — moods disclose "in the manner of an evasive turning-away" — but whether this constitutive concealment is a feature of mood as such or of its falling-everyday mode remains an open question until §§35–38 (falling) and §40 (anxiety). [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U5-P3.

3. **Fear vs. anxiety: anticipatory structural gap (§30)**: Fear is analyzed as a mode of state-of-mind, but Heidegger notes (H.140 fn.) that the earlier editions mistakenly cited §39 rather than §40 for the forthcoming analysis of anxiety. The structural contrast between fear (which has a definite intraworldly object) and anxiety (which has none) is not yet drawn; fear's function in this unit is partly as a placeholder that will be surpassed. Whether the fear-analysis is genuinely autonomous or merely propaedeutic for anxiety is a question the text itself raises by deferral. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U5-P4.

4. **Possibility as "higher than actuality" (§31)**: Heidegger asserts that "higher than actuality stands possibility" (H.38, recalled at H.143–144) — existential possibility is the "most primordial and ultimate positive" characterization of Dasein. This reversal of the traditional priority of actuality over possibility (Aristotle's *energeia* over *dynamis*) is stated rather than fully argued. The claim that Dasein is "never more than it factically is" yet is "existentially that which, in its potentiality-for-Being, it is *not yet*" (H.145) introduces an internal tension between factical limitation and existential excess that the text acknowledges but defers to the Temporal Interpretation. [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D1-U5-P5, BT-D1-U5-P6.

5. **Authenticity and inauthenticity of understanding (§31)**: Heidegger states that understanding is "either authentic or inauthentic" and that the "in-" of inauthentic "does not mean that Dasein cuts itself off from its Self and understands 'only' the world" (H.146). Inauthentic understanding is still understanding of the whole of Being-in-the-world, only as an "existential modification of projection as a whole." But the criteria for distinguishing authentic from inauthentic projection are not given here; the distinction is announced structurally without phenomenal content, which will arrive only through the analysis of conscience and resoluteness in Division Two. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U5-P5.
