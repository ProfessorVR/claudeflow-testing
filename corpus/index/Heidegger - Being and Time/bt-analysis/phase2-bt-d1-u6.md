# Phase 2 Analysis: BT-D1-U6 (§§32–34)

## Section 1: Unit Metadata

- **unit_id**: BT-D1-U6
- **part**: Part One

- **§-range**: §§32–34
- **H. page range**: H.148–166
- **Eng. page range**: 188–210
- **Unit thesis**: This unit works out the existential structures of interpretation, assertion, and discourse. Interpretation is the development of the possibilities projected in understanding, operating through the fore-structure (fore-having, fore-sight, fore-conception) and the "as"-structure. Assertion is shown to be a derivative mode of interpretation in which the hermeneutical "as" is levelled down to the apophantical "as." Discourse (*Rede*) is then established as an equiprimordial existentiale with state-of-mind and understanding, constituting the Articulation of intelligibility that is the existential-ontological foundation of language.

## Section 2: Hierarchical Outline

### 2.1 Understanding and interpretation: the fore-structure and the as-structure (§32)

- Understanding projects Dasein upon possibilities; interpretation is the working-out of those possibilities, not the acquiring of information about what is understood.
- In circumspective concern, the ready-to-hand is interpreted as "something for something" — the "as"-structure (*Als-Struktur*) constitutes interpretation and makes what is understood explicit.
- Every interpretation is grounded in a tripartite fore-structure: fore-having (*Vorhabe*), fore-sight (*Vorsicht*), and fore-conception (*Vorgriff*). These are not contingent prejudices but the existential conditions of interpretation as such.
- Meaning (*Sinn*) is formally defined as the "upon-which" of a projection in terms of which something becomes intelligible as something; meaning is an existentiale of Dasein, not a property of entities.
- The hermeneutic circle: understanding and interpretation always operate within a circle (pre-understanding conditions what is interpreted), but this circle is not a *circulus vitiosus* — it is the positive expression of Dasein's existential fore-structure. The decisive task is to come into the circle in the right way.

### 2.2 Assertion as a derivative mode of interpretation (§33)

- Assertion (*Aussage*) has three interconnected significations: (1) pointing-out (*Aufzeigung* — apophansis: letting an entity be seen from itself), (2) predication (*Bestimmung* — giving a definite character to a subject), (3) communication (*Mitteilung* — sharing what has been pointed out with Others in a Being-towards).
- The "as"-structure undergoes a decisive modification in assertion: the hermeneutical "as" of circumspective interpretation is levelled down to the apophantical "as" of predicative determination.
- In assertion, what was ready-to-hand in the fore-having becomes something present-at-hand "about which" the assertion is made; the "as" is cut off from the totality of involvements and restricted to the uniform plane of what is merely present-at-hand.
- Assertion cannot disown its ontological origin in interpretation; it retains the fore-structure (fore-having, fore-sight, fore-conception) even when this origin is concealed.
- The history of logic since Aristotle has taken the *logos* as present-at-hand (the Being-present-at-hand-together of words), veiling the phenomenon of the "as" and collapsing into a formal "theory of judgment." The copula problem and the synthesis/diaeresis structure of the *logos* are traced back to the concealment of the hermeneutical "as."

### 2.3 Discourse and language (§34)

- Discourse (*Rede*) is existentially equiprimordial with state-of-mind and understanding; it is the Articulation of intelligibility. Discourse underlies both interpretation and assertion and is more primordial than either.
- What gets Articulated in discourse is the "totality-of-significations" (*Bedeutungsganze*), which can be dissolved into significations (*Bedeutungen*). The totality-of-significations is put into words as language (*Sprache*).
- Four structural items constitute discourse: (a) what the discourse is about, (b) what is said-in-the-talk as such (*Geredetes*), (c) communication (*Mitteilung*), and (d) making-known (*Bekundung*).
- Hearing (*Horen*) and keeping silent (*Schweigen*) are essential existential possibilities belonging to discourse. Hearing is constitutive for discourse and is Dasein's existential way of Being-open as Being-with for Others. Hearkening (*Horchen*) is phenomenally more primordial than acoustic perception.
- Keeping silent authentically Articulates the intelligibility of Dasein in a primordial manner; it gives rise to genuine potentiality-for-hearing and does away with idle talk.
- The Greeks defined the essence of man as *zoon logon echon* ("something living which has reason/discourse"); Heidegger argues this covers up the phenomenal basis that Dasein is the entity that talks. Grammar and the science of language are oriented toward assertion and the ontology of the present-at-hand; a more primordial doctrine of signification must be rooted in the ontology of Dasein.

## Section 6: Tensions & Open Questions

1. **The hermeneutical "as" vs. the apophantical "as": derivation or co-origination? (§33)** Heidegger argues assertion is a "derivative mode" of interpretation, with the apophantical "as" arising from a levelling-down of the hermeneutical "as." Yet his own analysis shows assertion retains the full fore-structure of interpretation — it has fore-having, fore-sight, fore-conception. If the structure is retained, what exactly is "levelled"? The claim of strict derivation sits uneasily with the structural continuity Heidegger himself acknowledges. [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D1-U6-P4.

2. **Meaning as existentiale vs. meaning as understood by the sciences (§32, H.151–153)** Meaning is defined as the "upon-which" of projection, an existentiale of Dasein — "only Dasein can be meaningful or meaningless." Yet the sciences, historiology, and philological interpretation all operate with notions of meaning. Heidegger insists his concept is not to be confined to the "content of judgment," but the relationship between existential meaning and the meaning operative in ontic disciplines remains underdeveloped. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U6-P1, BT-D1-U6-P2.

3. **The circle: existential vindication vs. methodological cash-value (§32, H.152–153)** The hermeneutic circle is declared a positive possibility of the most primordial kind of knowing. But Heidegger's concrete prescription — "to come into it in the right way" by working out fore-structures "in terms of the things themselves" — is programmatic rather than methodologically detailed. How one distinguishes productive from distortive fore-structures is left open, deferred to the concrete analyses of everydayness and authenticity. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U6-P3.

4. **Discourse, language, and the ontological status of words (§34, H.161–162)** Language is the totality of words in which discourse gets expressed; it is an entity within-the-world that can come across as ready-to-hand or present-at-hand. But discourse is said to be "more primordial" than language. The precise ontological relationship — whether language is a mere derivative externalization of discourse or has constitutive force of its own — is underdetermined. Heidegger acknowledges at the end of §34 that the investigation has been designed "merely to point out the ontological 'locus'" of language, deferring fuller treatment. [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D1-U6-P5.

5. **Keeping silent as authentic discourse (§34, H.164–165)** Heidegger asserts that keeping silent Articulates intelligibility "in so primordial a manner" that it gives rise to genuine potentiality-for-hearing. But the mechanism by which silence accomplishes positive Articulation — rather than simply refraining from idle talk — is not elaborated. The concept anticipates the analysis of conscience as a "silent call" (§§56–57) but at this stage remains promissory. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U6-P6.
