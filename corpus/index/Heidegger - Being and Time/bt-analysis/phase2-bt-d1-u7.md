# Phase 2 Analysis: BT-D1-U7 (§§35–38)

## Section 1: Unit Metadata

- **unit_id**: BT-D1-U7
- **part**: Part One
- **division**: Division One
- **chapter**: V.B — The Everyday Being of the "There", and the Falling of Dasein
- **§-range**: §§35–38
- **H. page range**: H.167–180
- **Eng. page range**: 210–224
- **Unit thesis**: This unit exhibits the three constitutive phenomena of Dasein's everyday disclosedness — idle talk, curiosity, and ambiguity — and demonstrates that their interconnection reveals a basic kind of Being belonging to everydayness which Heidegger calls *falling* (*Verfallenheit*). Falling is not a moral or theological judgment but a positive existential characteristic: Dasein's absorption in the publicness of the "they," characterized structurally by temptation, tranquillizing, alienation, and the downward plunge (*Absturz*).

## Section 2: Hierarchical Outline

### 2.1 Idle talk as fallen discourse (§35, H.167–170)

- Discourse (*Rede*), as an existentiale, has the possibility of becoming idle talk (*Gerede*): a groundless passing-along of what has been said, in which language is no longer rooted in a primordial relationship to entities.
- Idle talk communicates not by appropriating the entity talked about but by "gossiping and passing the word along" (*Weitersagen*); what is said-in-the-talk takes on an authoritative character and spreads in ever wider circles.
- Idle talk closes off rather than discloses: it perverts disclosing (*Erschliessen*) into closing-off (*Verschliessen*), since it discourages new inquiry by presenting everything as already understood.
- Idle talk is not confined to speech but extends to writing ("scribbling," *Geschreibe*); the average understanding "will never be able to decide what has been drawn from primordial sources and how much is just gossip" (H.169).
- The "they" prescribes one's state-of-mind and determines what and how one "sees" (H.170); Dasein "keeps floating unattached" (*in einer Schwebe*) in the uprooting of its Being-in-the-world.

### 2.2 Curiosity as fallen seeing (§36, H.170–173)

- Heidegger traces the phenomenon of curiosity back to the Greek and Augustinian recognition of a "desire to see" (*concupiscentia oculorum*) that belongs essentially to Dasein's Being.
- Aristotle's *Metaphysics* A.1 ("all men by nature desire to know") is interpreted not as a cognitive thesis but as an existential observation about the care-structure: "the care for seeing is essential to man's Being" (H.171).
- When circumspection (*Umsicht*) is released from practical concern, it becomes free for "just looking" — curiosity seeks novelty, never tarries with what is close, and leaps restlessly from one thing to the next.
- Three constitutive characteristics: (1) *not tarrying* alongside what is closest; (2) *distraction* (*Zerstreuung*) by new possibilities; (3) *never dwelling anywhere* (*Aufenthaltlosigkeit*) — curiosity is everywhere and nowhere.
- Curiosity and idle talk reinforce each other: "what one 'must' have read and seen" is delivered over to idle talk, and both modes drag each other along in their uprooting.

### 2.3 Ambiguity as fallen understanding (§37, H.173–175)

- In everyday Being-with-one-another, everything looks as though it were genuinely understood, yet nothing is grasped authentically: this is ambiguity (*Zweideutigkeit*).
- Ambiguity extends to the world, to Being-with-one-another, and to Dasein's Being towards itself; it infects Dasein's projective understanding so that "what everyone has surmised and scented out" has already been decided in advance.
- Ambiguity produces a distinctive temporal dynamic: genuine action "comes too late" because idle talk has already surmised and discussed everything in advance; what is genuinely new is "out of date as soon as it emerges before the public" (H.174).
- Ambiguity does not arise from deliberate disguise or distortion; it is structurally implied in Being-with-one-another "as thrown Being-in-the-world" (H.175) — publicly hidden, it belongs to the "they."

### 2.4 Falling and thrownness: the movement of Dasein's everydayness (§38, H.175–180)

- Idle talk, curiosity, and ambiguity are interconnected existential characteristics that together reveal the basic kind of Being belonging to everydayness: *falling* (*Verfallenheit*).
- Falling designates Dasein's absorption in the "they-world" — it has "fallen away" from authentic potentiality-for-Being and "fallen into the 'world'" (H.176). This is not a fall from a purer state; inauthenticity is itself a "positive" possibility of Dasein.
- Four structural moments of the movement of falling: (1) *temptation* (*Versuchung*) — idle talk presents the possibility of losing oneself as constant temptation; (2) *tranquillizing* (*Beruhigung*) — the supposition that everything is "in the best of order"; (3) *alienation* (*Entfremdung*) — Dasein drifts towards a concealment of its ownmost potentiality-for-Being; (4) *self-entangling* (*Verfängnis*) — all three moments compound into entanglement.
- These four together characterize the *downward plunge* (*Absturz*): Dasein plunges out of itself into the groundlessness and nullity of inauthentic everydayness, a movement characterized by *turbulence* (*Wirbel*).
- Falling is not evidence against Dasein's existentiality but rather the "most elemental evidence *for* Dasein's existentiality" (H.179): Dasein *can* fall only because Being-in-the-world understandingly with a state-of-mind is the issue for it.
- Heidegger insists the analysis makes no ontical assertion about the "corruption of human Nature"; it is prior to any claim about *status corruptionis* or *status integritatis* (H.180). Falling is conceived ontologically as a kind of motion, not as a moral defect.

## Section 6: Tensions & Open Questions

1. **Idle talk as positive phenomenon vs. closing-off (§35)**: Heidegger insists that idle talk is a "positive" existential phenomenon and not to be taken in a "disparaging" signification (H.167). Yet the entire analysis describes it as *closing off*, *groundless*, a perversion of disclosure into concealment. The evaluative neutrality claimed at the outset sits uneasily with the phenomenological characterization that follows. This tension recurs in the question of whether the entire existential analytic can maintain its claimed neutrality regarding authenticity and inauthenticity. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U7-P1, BT-D1-U7-P5.

2. **Falling and moral neutrality (§38)**: Heidegger explicitly disclaims any moral or theological evaluation: the analysis is "prior to" claims about corruption or integrity (H.180). Yet the vocabulary — falling, plunging, groundlessness, alienation, turbulence, entanglement, nullity — carries unmistakable evaluative resonance. Whether an existential-ontological description can genuinely remain neutral while deploying such language is a persistent interpretive question. The disclaimer about *status corruptionis* may itself function rhetorically to foreclose a reading that the text's own language invites. [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D1-U7-P5, BT-D1-U7-P6.

3. **The relationship between fallen modes and their authentic counterparts (§§35–37)**: Idle talk is fallen discourse, curiosity is fallen seeing, ambiguity is fallen understanding — but the authentic counterparts are not exhibited here. Discourse (*Rede*, §34), understanding (*Verstehen*, §31), and state-of-mind (*Befindlichkeit*, §29) were analyzed earlier, but their authentic modes (silence, moment of vision, anxiety) appear only later. This creates an asymmetry: the fallen modes are analyzed in isolation from their authentic counterparts, raising the question of whether falling can be adequately characterized without reference to what it falls *from*. Heidegger's own answer — that there is no "purer" state from which Dasein falls (H.176) — intensifies rather than resolves this tension. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U7-P4, BT-D1-U7-P5.

4. **Curiosity and wonder (§36)**: Heidegger traces curiosity to Aristotle's *thaumazein* tradition but then treats everyday curiosity as a fallen phenomenon. The relationship between the philosophical wonder that gives rise to ontology and the restless curiosity that characterizes everydayness is left unexplored. Are they the same tendency in different existentiell modalities, or structurally distinct? The text does not say. [INTERP-high] — Linked to: BT-D1-U7-P2.

5. **Thrownness and falling (§38, H.179)**: The section title links falling and thrownness, and the closing pages assert that "turbulence makes manifest that the thrownness which can obtrude itself upon Dasein in its state-of-mind has the character of throwing and of movement" (H.179). This suggests falling is not merely an existentiell tendency but is structurally entailed by thrownness itself. If so, falling is not contingent but necessary — which puts further pressure on the claim that authentic existence is a genuine possibility rather than a merely formal one. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: BT-D1-U7-P6, BT-D1-U7-P7.
