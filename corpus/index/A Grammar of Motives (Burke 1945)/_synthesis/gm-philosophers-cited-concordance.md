## Tier-A Philosophers (full schema)

### Aristotle (384-322 BCE)
- **Primary locus**: Ch. "Act" (Aristotle and Aquinas), book pp. 227-274; act-and-potency pp. 252-262; Index lemma pp. 519-520 enumerates >25 sub-loci.
- **Units**: GM-02, GM-03, GM-05, GM-06, GM-07, GM-08, GM-09, GM-10, GM-11.
- **Pentad term**: Act (with secondary purpose-stress via final cause).
- **Burke exegetical move**: Aristotle is Burke's preferred lexicon for act-philosophy: hylomorphism, act-potency, four causes, entelechy, and the energeia/dynamis pairing are mined as the substrate from which all dramatistic act-vocabulary radiates; Burke repeatedly returns to *Nicomachean Ethics*, *Metaphysics*, *Physics*, *Rhetoric*, and *Poetics* for technical terms he then translates into pentadic apparatus.
- **Depth**: sustained-analysis.

### Aquinas (1225-1274)
- **Primary locus**: Ch. "Act" co-protagonist with Aristotle, pp. 227-232 and 247-262 (realist-family / nominalist-aggregate; actus-status pp. 41-43).
- **Units**: GM-03, GM-06, GM-07, GM-09, GM-11.
- **Pentad term**: Act (Christian-realist appropriation of Aristotelian act-potency).
- **Burke exegetical move**: Burke treats Aquinas as the medium through which Aristotelian act-philosophy enters Christian theology, especially the actus/status doctrine, the realist understanding of substance, and the linking of grace to act; the *Summa* is treated as a structural exemplar of dialectical-dramatic terminology.
- **Depth**: sustained-analysis.

### Spinoza (1632-1677)
- **Primary locus**: Ch. "Scene (Philosophic Schools)", pp. 137-152; contextual paradox pp. 24-26; substance-definition p. 50; pantheism pp. 72-77.
- **Units**: GM-01, GM-02, GM-03, GM-06, GM-07, GM-08, GM-09, GM-11.
- **Pentad term**: Scene (materialism transcended via pantheism; substance defined by contextual placement).
- **Burke exegetical move**: Spinoza is Burke's paradigm of scene-philosophy at its most dialectically refined: substance = that-by-which-defined-by-what-it-is-not, the *Ethics*' active/passive verb-structure, the three forms of knowledge, and the pantheist circumference are excavated as Scene-term machinery; Burke aligns Spinoza's negation-formula with his own concept of dialectical substance.
- **Depth**: sustained-analysis.

### Plato (c. 428-348 BCE)
- **Primary locus**: Ch. "Dialectic in General", pp. 402-443 (Socratic transcendence pp. 420-430); Justice p. 173; Jowett pp. 421-423; Platonic love p. 425; *Phaedrus* p. 427.
- **Units**: GM-02, GM-03, GM-05, GM-06, GM-07, GM-09, GM-11.
- **Pentad term**: Purpose / Dialectic (idealist forms; ultimate-term ascent).
- **Burke exegetical move**: Plato is the paradigm of Socratic-dialectical transcendence; the upward path from particulars through stages of merging-and-dividing toward an ultimate-term is read as the dialectical archetype of all motive-theory; the *Phaedrus* and *Symposium* furnish Burke's image of the dialectician-as-statesman.
- **Depth**: sustained-analysis.

### Hegel (1770-1831)
- **Primary locus**: Ch. "Agent in General" pp. 198-200 (idealism after Kant); pp. 46-47; 200-204 (philosophy of history); 281-282 (Marx-reversal); 312.
- **Units**: GM-01, GM-02, GM-06, GM-07, GM-08, GM-09.
- **Pentad term**: Agent (collective-historical Geist) + Dialectic (Aufhebung as merger-and-division paradigm).
- **Burke exegetical move**: Hegel furnishes the dialectical syntax that Burke generalizes as merger-and-division (the four-master-tropes apparatus is partly a Hegel-derivative); the cunning-of-reason, *Insofern*, and World-Spirit are recoded into pentadic ratios; Marx's inversion of Hegel is itself thematized as a dialectical move.
- **Depth**: sustained-analysis.

### Kant (1724-1804)
- **Primary locus**: Ch. "Agent in General" pp. 185-197; moral transcendence pp. 192-197; intuition p. 188; idealism after Kant p. 198; on merger pp. 404-405.
- **Units**: GM-02, GM-03, GM-09, GM-10.
- **Pentad term**: Agent (transcendental subject as Agent-idealization); also Purpose (categorical imperative).
- **Burke exegetical move**: Kant is the hinge from scene-philosophy to agent-philosophy: the transcendental apparatus (forms of intuition, categories, schematism, the noumenal-phenomenal split) is reframed as the philosophical-school featuring Agent; the antinomies pre-figure Burke's own antinomies-of-definition; the *Critique of Pure Reason* and the moral-philosophy are mined for ratio-structure.
- **Depth**: sustained-analysis.

### Hobbes (1588-1679)
- **Primary locus**: Ch. "Scene", pp. 131-137 (materialist; *Leviathan*; Endeavor/Appetite/Aversion); also in Constitutions chapter.
- **Units**: GM-04, GM-08.
- **Pentad term**: Scene (materialist-mechanist reduction; motion as substrate).
- **Burke exegetical move**: Hobbes inaugurates the modern scene-philosophy lineage: a thoroughgoing materialism that reduces motive to motion (Endeavor-Appetite-Aversion); *Leviathan* is treated as a constitutional-political application of scene-philosophy in which the sovereign is the personified scenic ground.
- **Depth**: sustained-analysis.

### Leibniz (1646-1716)
- **Primary locus**: Ch. "Agent" pp. 184-185 (monadology, pre-established harmony); cross-chapter pp. 35, 147, 177, 225, 311-312, 400.
- **Units**: GM-02, GM-06, GM-07, GM-08, GM-13.
- **Pentad term**: Agent (windowless monad as agent-substance) + scientism-of-Leibniz (Purpose).
- **Burke exegetical move**: Leibniz figures as transitional between scene and agent: the monad is read as proto-agent (each monad an internally-driven principle); pre-established harmony is treated as a dialectical solution to mind-body that bequeaths the calculus-of-pure-thought lineage Burke ties to logical positivism.
- **Depth**: sustained-analysis.

### Marx (1818-1883)
- **Primary locus**: Ch. "Agent" pp. 209-214 (dramatist grammar for Marxism; class-action); dialectical materialism pp. 200-202; class-concept pp. 215-218; Hegel-reversal pp. 281-282.
- **Units**: GM-01, GM-05, GM-07, GM-08, GM-09, GM-13.
- **Pentad term**: Agent (collective-class agent); scene-act ratio via dialectical materialism.
- **Burke exegetical move**: Burke supplies a dramatist grammar for Marxism: class is read as a dramatistic agent (not a sociological aggregate), the *Manifesto* as a representative anecdote, and dialectical materialism as a scene-stressed dialectic that nonetheless requires an agent-term (proletariat) to consummate; the Hegel-inversion is dramatized as a pentadic shift.
- **Depth**: sustained-analysis.

### William James (1842-1910)
- **Primary locus**: Ch. "Agency and Purpose" pp. 62-66 (act as locus of motives; way of creation); pragmatism pp. 275-277, 282-283; cash-value p. 286; mystic moment pp. 287-298.
- **Units**: GM-00, GM-03, GM-07.
- **Pentad term**: Agency (pragmatist instrumentalism) + Purpose (cash-value of ideas).
- **Burke exegetical move**: James is Burke's chief twentieth-century interlocutor for the act-as-locus-of-motives thesis; the pragmatist circumference, the two principles of truth, and the *Varieties of Religious Experience* are read as agency-purpose philosophy *par excellence*; Burke deploys James as the rhetorical pivot from scene to act.
- **Depth**: sustained-analysis.

### Bergson (1859-1941)
- **Primary locus**: Ch. "Agency and Purpose" pp. 295-296 (negation; purposiveness); pp. 152, 412 (*Creative Evolution*).
- **Units**: GM-04, GM-07, GM-09.
- **Pentad term**: Act (élan vital as creative-act principle); Agency.
- **Burke exegetical move**: Bergson is enlisted for the purposiveness-in-the-negative theme and for treating biological-creative process as scene-act fusion; *Creative Evolution* is cross-read with Darwin to extract a counter-mechanist motive-vocabulary.
- **Depth**: illustrative.

### Plotinus (c. 204-270)
- **Primary locus**: Ch. "Agency and Purpose" pp. 293-294 (Neo-Platonist purpose); index pp. 30, 34.
- **Units**: GM-02, GM-07.
- **Pentad term**: Purpose (Neo-Platonist emanation-and-return; ultimate-term).
- **Burke exegetical move**: Plotinus is the Neo-Platonist exemplar of purpose-philosophy: emanation downward from the One and ascent of soul back to the One is treated as the archetypal purpose-trajectory, with Plotinus serving as bridge between Plato and Christian mystical theology.
- **Depth**: illustrative.

### Darwin (1809-1882)
- **Primary locus**: Ch. "Scene" pp. 152-158; pp. 291 (mysticism and materialism); pp. 431-432.
- **Units**: GM-01, GM-04.
- **Pentad term**: Scene (evolutionary-biological scene).
- **Burke exegetical move**: Darwin is the modern materialist scene-philosopher; natural selection furnishes Burke's example of a scene-act ratio in which the agent (organism) is wholly scene-derived; *Origin of Species* is read as a representative anecdote whose generalization Burke contests.
- **Depth**: sustained-analysis.

### Lucretius (c. 99-55 BCE)
- **Primary locus**: Ch. "Scene" pp. 159-164; *De Rerum Natura* p. 162; atoms-swerving p. 160.
- **Units**: GM-04.
- **Pentad term**: Scene (atomistic materialism; clinamen as proto-agency).
- **Burke exegetical move**: Lucretius furnishes the Epicurean variant of scene-philosophy: atoms-and-void plus the *clinamen* (swerve) as the residual proto-agency embedded in materialist scene; Burke contrasts Lucretius's Epicurean materialism with Stoic providential materialism.
- **Depth**: illustrative.

### Coleridge (1772-1834)
- **Primary locus**: Ch. "The Problem of the Intrinsic" pp. 470-471; cross-chapter pp. 14, 34, 192, 233, 296, 299, 325, 338, 359-360, 368-371, 408, 411-412.
- **Units**: GM-01, GM-02, GM-03, GM-06, GM-08, GM-09, GM-10, GM-11, GM-12.
- **Pentad term**: Act / Agent (the imagination as dramatistic-creative faculty).
- **Burke exegetical move**: Coleridge is Burke's primary literary-theoretical interlocutor: *Biographia Literaria*'s primary/secondary imagination, the doctrine of organic form, the pantisocracy project, and Coleridge's dialectic of negation all furnish a Romantic-poetic vocabulary that translates onto pentadic ratios; the Keats and Moore chapters extend Coleridgean reading-methods.
- **Depth**: sustained-analysis.

### Santayana (1863-1952)
- **Primary locus**: Ch. "Agent" pp. 214-223 (sustained treatment); skepticism pp. 216-217; cross-ref pp. 299, 313.
- **Units**: GM-04, GM-05, GM-06, GM-07, GM-09.
- **Pentad term**: Agent (essences as agent-ideal terms) + Purpose.
- **Burke exegetical move**: Santayana receives a full philosophical-schools treatment as a hybrid: skeptical-materialist scene (animal faith) plus Platonist essences, yielding a doubled grammar that Burke reads as the ideal mid-twentieth-century scene-agent attempt at integration.
- **Depth**: sustained-analysis.

### Korzybski (1879-1950)
- **Primary locus**: Ch. "Act" pp. 238-241; cross-chapter pp. 57, 173, 247, 251, 293, 317, 440, 511; Appendix D critique.
- **Units**: GM-02, GM-05, GM-06, GM-07, GM-08, GM-09.
- **Pentad term**: Act (general-semantics critique; structural-differential as a reductive anecdote).
- **Burke exegetical move**: Korzybski is Burke's running critical foil: the Structural Differential is treated as a non-representative anecdote that misrepresents meaning by reducing it to a mechanical apparatus; Burke nonetheless adopts Korzybski's emphasis on terminological awareness and his critique of identity-A=A logic.
- **Depth**: sustained-analysis.

### Berkeley (1685-1753)
- **Primary locus**: Ch. "Agent" pp. 177-181; cross-ref pp. 40, 97, 108.
- **Units**: GM-02, GM-03, GM-05.
- **Pentad term**: Agent (subjective idealism; *esse est percipi*).
- **Burke exegetical move**: Berkeley is featured as the British-empiricist passage from Locke's representationalism to subjective idealism; *esse est percipi* reframes the agent as constitutive of perceived scene; Burke reads the divine perceiver as a transcendental rescue from solipsism.
- **Depth**: illustrative.

### Hume (1711-1776)
- **Primary locus**: Ch. "Agent" pp. 181-184 (skepticism; on cause and effect); experience p. 182; cross-ref pp. 186, 415.
- **Units**: GM-03, GM-05.
- **Pentad term**: Agent (skeptical empiricism; bundle-self).
- **Burke exegetical move**: Hume is the empiricist pivot: causation as customary expectation, the bundle-theory of self, and the *Treatise*'s destabilization of substance-vocabulary are treated as preparation for Kant's transcendental rescue.
- **Depth**: illustrative.

### G. H. Mead (1863-1931)
- **Primary locus**: Ch. "Act" pp. 236-238 (vocal gesture; biologism); cross-ref p. 279; *Philosophy of the Act*.
- **Units**: GM-00, GM-06, GM-07.
- **Pentad term**: Act (social act; symbolic-interactionist agency).
- **Burke exegetical move**: Mead supplies Burke with the social-act doctrine in which symbolic communication (vocal gesture) is constitutive of the self; *Mind, Self, and Society* and *Philosophy of the Act* anchor Burke's dramatism in pragmatist social-psychology.
- **Depth**: illustrative.
