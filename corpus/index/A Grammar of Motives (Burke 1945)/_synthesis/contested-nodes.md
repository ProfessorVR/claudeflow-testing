# Contested Nodes — Burke 1945 *A Grammar of Motives*

**Phase 3 Wave 1 Agent 3C output**
**Companion to** `concept-matrix.csv` (56 canonical concepts × 14 units, aligned to 3A's `book-level-ontology.json`)
**Generated** 2026-05-11
**ID convention**: 3A's `book-level-ontology.json` (56 concepts with semantic IDs like `C-PENTAD-ACT`) is the authoritative canonical-ID source. Each contested node below carries its 3A canonical ID(s) when one exists; where the contested *tension* is itself the analytical object (and 3A has already disambiguated the sub-concepts), that fact is noted explicitly — Burke's volume-level substance-multiplicity, e.g., is what produced 3A's 9-way substance sub-classification, so the "contested node" lives at the cross-sub-concept level rather than at a single canonical ID. Concept-content is grounded directly in per-unit `gm-NN-deep.json` concept arrays (441 raw entries).

A *contested node* in this taxonomy is a canonical concept whose Burke-internal treatment exhibits one or more of:
(a) **multiple partially-inconsistent definitions** across the volume's 14 units;
(b) **register-shift** — Burke endorses the concept under one register (etymological / grammatical / dialectical / philosophic-school-specific) while disclaiming or narrowing it in another;
(c) **circumference drift** — Burke deploys the concept under different scenic / scopal circumferences in different chapters without flagging the drift;
(d) **forward / backward reference instability** — the concept's chapter-of-record is not the chapter where its real analytical work is done;
(e) **dramatistic-load mismatch** — the term carries more analytical weight than its definitional treatment establishes.

Ten contested nodes are documented below, ordered roughly by dissertation-load-bearingness (highest first).

## 1. C-PARADOX-OF-SUBSTANCE (+ nine substance sub-concepts) — Substance / *sub-stance* (the volume-organizing dialectical paradox)

- **Canonical ID(s) + label:** C-PARADOX-OF-SUBSTANCE + C-SUBSTANCE-CONTEXTUAL + C-SUBSTANCE-FAMILIAL + C-SUBSTANCE-DIRECTIONAL + C-SUBSTANCE-DIALECTIC + C-SUBSTANCE-PARADOX-PURITY + C-SUBSTANCE-ACTUS-STATUS + C-SUBSTANCE-INTRINSIC-EXTRINSIC + C-SUBSTANCE-RHETORICAL + C-SUBSTANCE-CONSUBSTANTIAL + C-SUBSTANCE-UNIVERSAL-MONETARY (3A has already split substance into nine canonical sub-concepts to handle this contestation — the contested-node is the *cross-sub-concept multiplicity* that prompted that split)
- **Cross-volume frequency:** 13 of 14 units (only GM-12-MOORE silent qua *label*, though present qua "poetic strategy as substance").
- **Nature of contest:** **multiple partially-inconsistent definitions + dialectical-paradox-as-feature**. Burke deploys at least nine senses of substance across the volume without consolidating them:
  1. **Etymological**: "standing-under," *sthā-* root, *hypokeimenon*, *hypotithēmi*, *ponere* (GM-02 pp.21–28 — *Sub-stance as 'standing-under'*; *Stance-family vocabulary*; *Hypokeimenon, hypotithēmi, ponere*).
  2. **Lockean-impaired**: substance as the "I-know-not-what" residue once empirical analysis has done its work (GM-02 p.21 — *Lockean impairment of substance*).
  3. **Theological-nutritive**: transubstantiation as nutritive incorporation (GM-02 p.30 — *Transubstantiation as nutritive substance*).
  4. **Linguistic-dialectical**: "second nature of language" (GM-02 p.33 — *Second nature of language (dialectic substance)*).
  5. **Ousiological-Aristotelian**: *ousia* / substance / being, eternal because treated in being-terms (GM-11 — *ousia / substance / being*).
  6. **Spinozistic**: *causa sui* / totality as substance (GM-02 *Causa sui*; GM-04 *Conatus in suo esse perseverandi*; GM-11 *Spinoza's totality criterion*).
  7. **Class-substantive / actus-status**: each class is "distinct in actus, hence in status" (GM-05 *Actus / status / property*; GM-06 *actus-status pair*; GM-09 *Actus and status (action-passion derivation)*).
  8. **Constitutional-political**: a Constitution "designates the ultimate motivation, or substance, of a Constitutional act" (GM-08 *Constitution as Stance word*; *Money as 'God-term'*).
  9. **Strategy-as-substance**: Burke characterizes Marianne Moore's substance as "a specific poetic strategy" — substance reduced to configuration of recurring motivational choices (GM-12 *poetic strategy as substance*).
- **Source-unit anchors:** GM-02 pp.21–57 (Antinomies — primary site); GM-00 p.19; GM-04 pp.130s; GM-05 pp.171–195; GM-06 pp.227–252; GM-08 pp.323–402; GM-09 pp.405–420; GM-11 pp.465–484; GM-13 pp.503–517.
- **Burke-internal posture:** the multiplicity is **deliberate** (the chapter is titled "Antinomies of Definition"); Burke names it "the paradox of substance" (GM-02 p.21; GM-09 *Paradox of substance (in dialectical key)*). But the dialectical-paradox-as-feature framing does not relieve the analyst's duty to specify which sense is operative in any given argument — and Burke does not always do so (e.g., GM-08's slide from etymological *stance* to Constitutional substance to "money as God-term" within ten pages).
- **Dissertation analytical leverage:** **maximal**. Three openings: (a) Burke explicitly invites the reader to use the paradox as a *dramatistic engine* rather than dissolve it — the dissertation can treat substance-multiplicity as a *resource for pentadic recoding* (per C-016 casuistic location); (b) Heideggerian *Wesen* (essence-as-coming-into-presence) supplies the ontological grounding for the *sthā-* root that Burke flags etymologically but does not phenomenologically develop; (c) Aristotelian *ousia* (per C-042 universals) gives a principled cross-walk between senses 5–7 that Burke leaves implicit. **Treat substance-paradox as Burke's deliberate methodological feature, not a defect — and inventory which sense is operative at each pentadic deployment.**

## 2. C-PENTAD-ACT (+ C-ACT-VS-MOTION + C-INCIPIENT-ACT-ATTITUDE + C-SYMBOLIC-ACTION) — Act (and the act / action / motion distinction)

- **Canonical ID(s) + label:** C-PENTAD-ACT (canonical pentad term) + C-ACT-VS-MOTION (the watershed) + C-INCIPIENT-ACT-ATTITUDE (the proto-sixth-term) + C-SYMBOLIC-ACTION
- **Cross-volume frequency:** 14 of 14 units.
- **Nature of contest:** **register-shift** between (a) Act as the canonical pentad term answering "what was done?" (GM-00; GM-06 *act (canonical pentad-featured term)*), (b) Act as Aristotelian *energeia* / *actu* / actuality (GM-06 *energeia / actu / actuality*; "form is an act"), (c) Act as the *non-reducible* element of any motivational vocabulary (GM-03 *act-residue*; the thesis that Act is not exhaustively reducible to Scene, Agent, Agency, or Purpose), (d) the four sub-species **incipient / delayed / completed / symbolic act** introduced in GM-06 (*incipient act (Richards)*; *delayed response (Korzybski)*; *symbolic action*; *image-as-attitude*), (e) the **action vs. motion watershed** (GM-04 *Reduction of action to motion*; GM-06 *motion-action ambiguity / action-motion watershed*; GM-01 *action vs. sheer motion (Dramatistic distinction)*; GM-11 *motion vs. action*), and (f) the *attitude-as-incipient-act* extension (GM-06 *attitude as 'beginning of acts' (Mead)*; *Meadian action-minus*).
- **The deep tension:** the **incipient-act ambiguity** Burke himself flags (GM-06 *incipient-act ambiguity (substitute-for-act vs first-step-toward-act)*) is structurally the same ambiguity that runs through Mead's attitude, Korzybski's delayed response, and the 1962 Addendum's proposal to add *attitude* as a sixth pentadic term (GM-09 *Attitude (1962 Addendum)*). Burke flags but does not resolve whether incipient-acts are *non-acts that suffice for a name* (substitute-for-act) or *proto-acts on a continuum* (first-step-toward-act). The action-motion watershed depends on the resolution: if attitudes are *substitutes-for-act* they live wholly on the action side; if they are *first-step-toward-act* the line between motion and action becomes a gradient.
- **Source-unit anchors:** GM-00 pp.xv–xxiii (canonical statement); GM-01 pp.3–20 (deterministic vs. hortatory usages of scene-act); GM-03 pp.59–127 (act-residue); GM-04 pp.130–170 (materialist reduction of action to motion); GM-06 pp.227–274 (primary site — all six sub-species); GM-09 p.443 (1962 Addendum on attitude); GM-11 pp.465–484 (lyric 'event' vs. dramatic 'act' — appendix re-opens the question).
- **Dissertation analytical leverage:** **maximal**. Burke's flagging-without-resolution of the incipient-act ambiguity is a direct invitation to phenomenological grounding — Husserlian *Vorstellung* / Heideggerian *Vorhaben* / Aristotelian *proairesis* each adjudicate the substitute-vs.-first-step question with principled grammar. The dissertation should treat act-sub-species as a **typology of motivational completedness** rather than a list, and use the action-motion watershed as the dramatistic boundary-condition that distinguishes pentadic-analysis-applicable phenomena from non-applicable ones.

## 3. C-PENTAD-SCENE (+ C-CIRCUMFERENCE) — Scene (and the circumference-of-scene problem)

- **Canonical ID(s) + label:** C-PENTAD-SCENE + C-CIRCUMFERENCE (scope of scene)
- **Cross-volume frequency:** 13 of 14 units.
- **Nature of contest:** **circumference drift across chapters** + **scene as Burke's most-substitutable term**. The same word "scene" performs at least seven different framing functions across the volume:
  1. **Pentadic-canonical**: scene as "the background of the act, the situation in which it occurred" (GM-00).
  2. **Container-metaphor**: scene as "fit container for the act"; scene-act ratio (GM-01 *scene as fit container for act*; *scene-act ratio (titular)*).
  3. **Materialist-philosophic**: scene as the cosmos in materialism's featuring; scene-as-Nature; scene-as-mechanism (GM-04 *Materialism*; *Hobbesian mechanism*; *Deus sive Natura*; *Scene-act ratio (materialist construal)*).
  4. **Reduced-to-motion**: scene as the motion-only realm to which materialism reduces action (GM-04 *Reduction of action to motion*; *Pre-agentification of scene*).
  5. **Super-agentified**: "God as Almighty Agent / ultimate scene as super-agent" (GM-05 *God as Almighty Agent / ultimate scene as super-agent*); scene-as-agent collapse.
  6. **Scopal**: scene as the analyst's elected circumference (GM-03 *circumference*; *strategic choice of circumference*; GM-08 *Strategic choice of circumference for 'freedom'*; GM-11 *circumference of scene*).
  7. **Scientific-scenic**: "science as approach from without (scenic/environmentalist/situational)" (GM-11).
- **The deep tension:** Burke alternates between treating scene as a **fixed cosmological frame** (chapters 1–4) and as **the analyst's adjustable knob** (chapter 3, chapter 8, appendix B). The circumference of scene is *selected* by the rhetor/analyst in GM-03 and GM-08, but *given* by the philosophic-school's commitments in GM-04. The reader is left to decide whether scene-circumference is a *grammatical degree of freedom* (the analyst can recode any motive by widening/narrowing the scene) or a *philosophic commitment* (each school's scenic circumference is what makes it that school).
- **Source-unit anchors:** GM-01 pp.3–20 (titular ratio); GM-03 pp.77+ (circumference as methodological construct); GM-04 pp.127–170 (primary site for scene as philosophic-school featuring); GM-05 pp.171–225 (scene-agent collapse — God-as-Almighty-Agent); GM-08 pp.323–402 (strategic circumference of freedom); GM-11 pp.465–484 (appendix re-opens; lyric event vs. dramatic act = scenic vs. actional construal).
- **Dissertation analytical leverage:** **high**. Burke's circumference-drift IS the dramatistic resource — it lets a single phenomenon be recoded under multiple scenic widths (medical / economic / cosmological), and each recoding generates different motives. The dissertation can systematize *circumference-as-rhetorical-degree-of-freedom* by mapping each of Burke's seven scene-senses to a determinate scopal level (per C-019 scope and reduction), making explicit what Burke leaves casuistically open.

## 4. C-PENTAD-AGENCY (+ C-PHILOSOPHIC-SCHOOL-FEATURING) — Agency (slippage between *instrument*, *philosophic-school featuring*, and *means-as-ancestor*)

- **Canonical ID(s) + label:** C-PENTAD-AGENCY + C-PHILOSOPHIC-SCHOOL-FEATURING (pragmatism-as-agency-featuring)
- **Cross-volume frequency:** 9 of 14 units.
- **Nature of contest:** **register-shift between three irreducibly distinct senses**:
  (a) **Means / instrument**: the canonical pentadic answer to "by what means?" (GM-00 *Agency (pentad term): how he did it / by what means*). Concrete: the carpenter's hammer.
  (b) **Philosophic-school featuring**: Pragmatism IS the philosophic school whose grammar features Agency over the other four terms (GM-07 *Pragmatism = featuring of Agency*; *Capitalism's grammatical repression of teleology*; *Documentary historiography as agency-featuring*). Abstract: the entire methodological commitment that makes meaning track operations (Bridgman) and ends track means (Dewey).
  (c) **Agency-as-ancestor**: the inversion-of-Aristotelian-hierarchy by which means become ancestral to ends (GM-07 *Agency-as-ancestor (means becomes ancestral)*; vocational training because *there are cars*). Genealogical: agency that constitutes purposes.
  Additionally, the *agent/agency overlap* through Aquinas's auxiliary-efficient-cause (GM-06 *Aquinas Ibn-Sina auxiliary-efficient-cause = agent/agency overlap*) installs agency as **the pentadic term most prone to absorption into adjacent terms** (agency-into-agent on the Aquinas side, agency-into-purpose on the pragmatist side per GM-07 *Action-motion ambiguity straddled by Pragmatism*).
- **The deep tension:** sense (a) is a *grammatical role*, sense (b) is a *philosophic-school index*, sense (c) is a *temporal-causal inversion*. Burke treats all three as "agency" without typographical distinction. The result: when Burke says a motive is "in agency," the analyst cannot tell without context whether it means "via an instrument," "in pragmatism's grammar," or "with means generating their own ends." The agency/purpose conflation in GM-07 (the chapter is jointly titled) is symptomatic — agency is the only pentad term whose chapter shares its title with another pentad term.
- **Source-unit anchors:** GM-00 (canonical); GM-06 pp.227–274 (agency-via-Aquinas-auxiliary-cause); GM-07 pp.275–322 (primary site; all three senses operative); GM-09 p.443 (1962 Addendum considers attitude vs. agency).
- **Dissertation analytical leverage:** **high**. Disambiguate by *prefixing*: A_instr (instrumental agency), A_school (school-featuring agency), A_anc (ancestral / means-generating-ends agency). The dissertation's pentadic ratios become more determinate when agency-prefixing is applied. Aristotelian *organon* + Heideggerian *Zeug* + Marxian *Mittel* give principled distinctions across the three senses.

## 5. C-PENTAD-PURPOSE (+ C-DEMONIC-TRINITY + C-EDENIC-PARADIGM) — Purpose (Aristotelian *telos* vs. Stoic *Pronoia* vs. pragmatist purpose vs. mystical Purpose)

- **Canonical ID(s) + label:** C-PENTAD-PURPOSE + C-DEMONIC-TRINITY (mystical purpose) + C-EDENIC-PARADIGM (purpose-as-origin)
- **Cross-volume frequency:** 8 of 14 units.
- **Nature of contest:** **four irreducibly distinct senses** indexed to four philosophic schools:
  (a) **Aristotelian *telos***: final cause; the *for-the-sake-of-which*; intrinsic to the kind-of-thing-acting (GM-06 *four causes pentad-mapping (… final-Purpose …)*; *entelechy*).
  (b) **Stoic Providence**: *Pronoia* — Nature = Providence = Reason = Will-of-God; cosmic teleology featured under the scenic register (GM-04 *Pronoia (Stoic Providence)*; *Prokopē (progress)*).
  (c) **Pragmatist purpose**: purpose imbedded in instrument-design; purpose-becomes-means; ends derivative of operations (GM-07 *Purpose imbedded in instrument-design*; *Capitalism's grammatical repression of teleology*; *Action-motion ambiguity straddled by Pragmatism*).
  (d) **Mystical / titular Purpose**: a single purpose-of-purposes; god-term; the "great central moltenness" (GM-00 *great central moltenness*; *title of titles*).
  (e) [implicit fifth] **Hegelian Cunning of Reason**: purpose realized through agents who do not know they are realizing it (GM-05 *Cunning of Reason (Hegelian)*).
- **The deep tension:** Burke himself flags (in GM-07) that capitalism's grammar *represses* teleology — yet capitalism cannot operate without purpose (per GM-07 *Capitalism's grammatical repression of teleology*). The repression-of-purpose-while-deploying-purpose is the same logical structure as sense (c) (purpose-imbedded-in-instrument): purpose is *transferred from agent-conscious to artifact-built-in* rather than abolished. But Burke does not consolidate (b), (c), (d), (e) into a typology — they are dispersed across four chapters under one label.
- **Source-unit anchors:** GM-00 pp.xv–xxiii; GM-04 pp.130–170 (Stoic providence); GM-05 (Hegelian cunning); GM-06 pp.227–274 (Aristotelian final cause); GM-07 pp.275–322 (primary site; pragmatist purpose); GM-09 *Purification of war* (Burke's own purpose for the *Grammar*).
- **Dissertation analytical leverage:** **high**. Purpose-typology by philosophic-school featuring (matches C-018) gives the dissertation a four-cell taxonomy that makes pentadic analyses school-relative without losing universal-grammatical applicability. Layer to *kairos* (Burke's purpose is teleologically Aristotelian but rhetorically kairotic — *Ad Bellum Purificandum*).

## 6. C-TROPE-IRONY (+ C-TRANSFORMATION-OF-TERMS + C-MERGER-AND-DIVISION + C-DIALECTIC-OF-SCAPEGOAT) — Dialectic (sometimes synonymous with *dramatistic*, sometimes narrower; irony-as-dialectic vs. irony-as-trope)

- **Canonical ID(s) + label:** C-TROPE-IRONY (= Irony / dialectic master trope, per 3A's bundled label) + C-TRANSFORMATION-OF-TERMS + C-MERGER-AND-DIVISION + C-DIALECTIC-OF-SCAPEGOAT (note: 3A consolidates "dialectic" *into* C-TROPE-IRONY, which itself is symptomatic of the contestation — dialectic has no standalone canonical ID despite appearing in 13/14 units)
- **Cross-volume frequency:** Dialectic 13/14; Irony 5/14 (highly concentrated in GM-13 with anticipations in GM-09).
- **Nature of contest:** **(a) variable scope of "dialectic"**: in GM-09 dialectic IS dramatism in its most general sense (GM-09 *Dialectic in the most general sense*; *Transformation of terms*; *Merger*; *Division*; *Third / pontificating term*); in GM-13 dialectic is narrowed to one of the four master tropes (GM-13 *Dialectic*; *Dramatic = dialectic equation*); in GM-08 dialectic is the methodology of constitutional interpretation (GM-08 *Constitutional substrate*; *Two Circles*). **(b) irony-as-dialectic vs. irony-as-trope**: GM-13 explicitly equates them via the *Dramatic = dialectic equation* and treats Irony as both the fourth master trope AND the "perspective of perspectives" that is the resultant total form when terms interact — but the equation makes irony either *one trope among four* or *the meta-tropic position*, not both.
- **Source-unit anchors:** GM-08 pp.323–402; GM-09 pp.402–446 (primary site for dialectic in general); GM-13 pp.503–517 (primary site for irony as master trope; *Dramatic = dialectic equation*; *Romantic irony*; *Classic / true / humble irony*; *Consubstantiality (ironic)*; *Peripety (A → non-A)*; *Internal fatality*).
- **Burke-internal posture:** the equation *Dramatic = dialectic* (GM-13) is the volume's most ambitious dialectical claim — it makes dramatism methodologically equivalent to (a generalized) dialectic, which means the *Grammar of Motives* is simultaneously a grammar of motives AND a dialectical method. This load is borne by an appendix.
- **Dissertation analytical leverage:** **high**. The dramatic-dialectic equation lets the dissertation operate pentadic analysis as a dialectical method without committing to Hegelian historical-progress. Disambiguate (a) by indexing dialectic-scope: dialectic_general (GM-09), dialectic_constitutional (GM-08), dialectic_tropic (GM-13). Disambiguate (b) by treating *Classic / true / humble irony* (GM-13) as the meta-tropic position and the other three (metaphor / metonymy / synecdoche) as its constituent moves — Burke effectively says this but does not consolidate.

## 7. C-SUBSTANCE-CONSUBSTANTIAL (+ C-SUBSTANCE-RHETORICAL) — Consubstantiality (forward-reference to *Rhetoric of Motives*; load-bearing without local apparatus)

- **Canonical ID(s) + label:** C-SUBSTANCE-CONSUBSTANTIAL + C-SUBSTANCE-RHETORICAL (3A's pair captures the proto-rhetorical / dialectical sides)
- **Cross-volume frequency:** appears in GM-00 (*consubstantiality (proto-)*), GM-02 (*Consubstantial epithet*), GM-09 (*Scapegoat as charismatic vicar* — "profoundly consubstantial with its persecutors"), and GM-13 (*Consubstantiality (ironic)*).
- **Nature of contest:** **forward-reference instability + dramatistic-load mismatch**. Consubstantiality is the load-bearing concept of the *Rhetoric of Motives* (the trilogy's second volume) but is deployed in the *Grammar* repeatedly without its rhetorical apparatus being locally developed. In GM-00 it is "proto-"; in GM-02 it is an epithet attached to the *sthā-* substance-cluster; in GM-09 it is the scapegoat's relation to its persecutors; in GM-13 it is the foundation of *true irony*. Each usage presupposes a different operative ground (proto-rhetorical / etymological-substantial / dialectical-victimage / ironic-consubstantial) and the four are not consolidated.
- **Source-unit anchors:** GM-00 p.19; GM-02 p.57; GM-09 pp.405–406; GM-13 p.514.
- **Dissertation analytical leverage:** **high**. The forward-reference is an opportunity: the dissertation can treat *Grammar*'s scattered consubstantialities as the dramatistic-substance side of the *Rhetoric*'s identification, making the trilogy's transition from grammar→rhetoric explicit. Each consubstantiality-usage in the *Grammar* gets a different rhetorical-augmentation pathway: GM-00 → identification (canonical); GM-02 → substance-paradox; GM-09 → victimage / scapegoat-rhetoric; GM-13 → tropic-irony-as-rhetorical-trope.

## 8. C-PHILOSOPHIC-SCHOOL-FEATURING — The five (six?) philosophic schools (materialism / idealism / realism / nominalism / pragmatism / mysticism) — featuring vs. featuring-with-residue

- **Canonical ID + label:** C-PHILOSOPHIC-SCHOOL-FEATURING
- **Cross-volume frequency:** 13/14.
- **Nature of contest:** **enumeration slip + categorial slip**. Burke variably enumerates *five* or *six* philosophic schools across the volume (materialism, idealism, realism, nominalism, pragmatism — and sometimes mysticism as a sixth distinct from idealism). The featuring-doctrine (each school is recognizable by which pentad term it features) is rigorously applied to materialism (Scene — GM-04), pragmatism (Agency — GM-07), and partially to idealism (Agent / consciousness — GM-05) and mysticism (Purpose — GM-07 *demonic trinity*). But realism (GM-06 *realism (Burke's signature formula: treats individuals as members of a group)*) is treated as Burke's *own preferred* doctrine — "poetic realism" — rather than as a school featuring one pentad term; *scientific realism* (GM-06; GM-13) is sharply distinguished from poetic realism. Nominalism (GM-06 *nominalism (treats groups as aggregates of individuals)*) is treated as the binary opposite of realism rather than as a pentadic-featuring school. The "each school features one term" thesis is therefore **uneven in application** across the six.
- **Source-unit anchors:** GM-03 (representative anecdote); GM-04 (materialism — primary site); GM-05 (idealism); GM-06 (realism/nominalism); GM-07 (pragmatism — primary site); GM-09 (mysticism implicit); GM-13 (poetic vs. scientific realism).
- **Dissertation analytical leverage:** **medium-high**. The unevenness is a research opportunity: complete the featuring-doctrine by specifying, for each school, *which* pentad term it features AND *which* it represses (the dual axis). Distinguish realism into *poetic-realism (Burke's preferred — features Substance / common-ground)* and *scientific-realism (features Agency-cum-correlation)*. Recognize Burke's own dramatism as itself a *meta-school* that refuses to feature any single term.

## 9. [Motive / motivation — no standalone canonical ID in 3A; carried by C-DRAMATISM + C-PENTAD + C-GRAMMAR-VS-IDIOM] — what gets *named* a motive?

- **Canonical ID(s) + label:** distributed across C-DRAMATISM, C-PENTAD, C-GRAMMAR-VS-IDIOM (3A does not assign "motive" a standalone concept — symptomatic of the dramatistic-load mismatch documented below)
- **Cross-volume frequency:** 14/14.
- **Nature of contest:** **dramatistic-load mismatch**. The volume is titled *A Grammar of Motives*, but the term "motive" is not given a definitional chapter — it is everywhere assumed and nowhere stabilized. Across units, motive is variously: a *vocabulary's product* (GM-03 *every vocabulary of motives is a scope-and-reduction*); the *what's-being-asked-for* in a question of action (GM-00 *the pentad as generating principle*); the *substance* of an act (GM-08 *God-term designates the ultimate motivation, or substance, of a Constitutional act*); the *configuration of recurring choices* (GM-12 *poetic strategy as substance*); the *grammatical placement* of a feature within the pentad (GM-00 *casuistic location (selecting which pentad term to locate the motive in)*); and the *philosophic-school featuring* (GM-04, GM-05, GM-07). Burke moves seamlessly between treating motive as (a) a phenomenon being analyzed and (b) a label assigned by the vocabulary doing the analysis — the **motive/motive-attribution distinction** is collapsed throughout.
- **Source-unit anchors:** every unit; key sites GM-00 / GM-03 / GM-08.
- **Dissertation analytical leverage:** **high**. The motive / motive-attribution collapse is methodologically deliberate (it is what Burke means by saying motives are *grammatical*-not-empirical), but it leaves open the question of *whose attribution* counts. The dissertation can extend by distinguishing **first-person motive-naming** (the agent's own attribution, recoverable via Aristotle's *prohairesis*), **third-person motive-naming** (the analyst's attribution via pentadic recoding), and **second-person motive-naming** (the rhetor's hailing of the addressee with a motive-label — the bridge to the *Rhetoric*).

## 10. C-PENTAD-OVERLAPS (+ C-AMBIGUITY-AS-RESOURCE + C-TWO-POINTS-OF-DEPARTURE) — Casuistry / pentad-substitution / casuistic location (Burke's most powerful concept — sparsely deployed; *casuistry itself has no standalone 3A canonical ID*)

- **Canonical ID(s) + label:** C-PENTAD-OVERLAPS (margins-of-overlap; the dramatistic engine of pentad-substitution) + C-AMBIGUITY-AS-RESOURCE + C-TWO-POINTS-OF-DEPARTURE (the methodological prerequisites for casuistic location). Note: "casuistry" itself receives no standalone canonical ID in 3A's ontology — itself a symptom of the contested-load mismatch (the volume's most powerful methodological concept is dispersed across three adjacent canonical concepts).
- **Cross-volume frequency:** C-015 2/14; C-016 1/14 (concentrated in GM-00 with a single GM-08 echo).
- **Nature of contest:** **dramatistic-load mismatch + sparse-but-load-bearing**. The Introduction explicitly identifies pentad-substitution / casuistic location / casuistic stretching as the *generating principle* of dramatistic analysis (GM-00 *the pentad as generating principle*; *pentad-substitution (categorial recoding of a single referent)*; *casuistic location (selecting which pentad term to locate the motive in)*; *casuistry (as application of grammatical principles to temporal situations)*). Yet the concept is barely surface-deployed in the body chapters — Burke uses it constantly *in practice* (every example of recoding motive-X-as-scenic into motive-X-as-agentic IS casuistic location) but rarely flags the methodological operation. The most analytically valuable concept of the volume is its most under-glossed in the body.
- **Source-unit anchors:** GM-00 (primary site); GM-08 *Strategic choice of circumference for 'freedom'* (implicit casuistic location).
- **Dissertation analytical leverage:** **maximal**. Casuistic location is the operationalization-engine of the entire pentad. The dissertation should foreground it explicitly — every analytical move that re-locates a motive from one pentad term to another (e.g., from Agency to Purpose; from Scene to Agent) IS a casuistic-location operation, and naming the operation makes the analysis auditable. Couple with *representative anecdote* (C-036) to give casuistic-location its scopal anchor: the anecdote sets the circumference within which casuistic relocations are legitimate. **This is the highest-leverage methodological concept in the entire volume; promote it from tier-1-but-sparse to tier-0-foregrounded in any dissertation deployment.**

## Cross-cutting observations

1. **Pentadic-load asymmetry.** Of the five pentad terms, Act / Scene / Agent appear in 13–14 units, Agency in 9, Purpose in 8. The volume is structurally *act-scene-agent-heavy*; agency and purpose are jointly chambered (GM-07) and joint-chambering correlates with both terms being the most-contested pentadic positions (Contested nodes 4 and 5).
2. **Substance is the volume's seventh pentad term.** Despite formal commitment to the five-term pentad, substance (C-009) appears in 13/14 units and bears more dialectical weight than any single pentad term except Act. The 1962 Addendum proposes adding *attitude* as a sixth; the textual evidence suggests *substance* has already been functioning as a sixth.
3. **The appendices (GM-10 Keats / GM-11 Intrinsic / GM-12 Moore / GM-13 Tropes) do not merely apply the main-text apparatus — they extend and contest it.** GM-13's *Dramatic = dialectic equation* and *poetic vs. scientific realism* distinction are among the volume's most analytically load-bearing claims and live entirely in an appendix. The dissertation should not treat the appendices as application-of-grammar but as a *fifth section* of grammar-elaboration.
4. **Forward-reference instability is a systematic feature.** Consubstantiality (C-010), identification (C-037), god-term (C-038), and rhetoric (the term itself) all do work in the *Grammar* that they will only be definitionally consolidated for in the *Rhetoric*. Treat these as **trilogy-bridge concepts** rather than as locally-grounded Grammar concepts.
