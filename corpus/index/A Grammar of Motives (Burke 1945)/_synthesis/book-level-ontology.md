# Book-Level Ontology — Kenneth Burke, *A Grammar of Motives* (1945)

**Phase 3 Wave 1 Agent 3A synthesis** — 14 unit deep JSONs (GM-00 through GM-13) consolidated into 56 canonical CONCEPT nodes + 12 canonical POSITIONs.

**Plan reference**: `plans/grammar-of-motives-burke-1945-analysis.md` v1 (2026-05-11), §5 Phase 3 Wave 1 3A.

## (i) Overview of ontology architecture

Burke's *Grammar of Motives* is a single, recursively self-consistent terminological apparatus distributed across fourteen units. The book-level ontology must therefore be organized not as a flat list of concepts but as a layered apparatus with five concentric strata: (1) the **pentad** itself — Act, Scene, Agent, Agency, Purpose — as five irreducible terms of motive-statement; (2) the **ratios** — ten dyadic combinations of the pentad terms, each reversible, expanded by the 1962 Addendum into twenty when order is preserved; (3) the **methodological apparatuses** that work the pentad and ratios at scale — representative anecdote, circumference, action-vs-motion distinction, symbolic action, incipient act/attitude, philosophic-school featuring; (4) the **dialectical apparatuses** that handle transformations of the pentad in time and under pressure — transformation of terms, merger and division, dialectic of the scapegoat, temporizing of essence, Socratic transcendence, dissolution of drama; (5) the **four master tropes** — metaphor, metonymy, synecdoche, irony — operating as a parallel apparatus to the pentad. The substance vocabulary developed in GM-02 cross-cuts all five strata.

This stratification is not Burke's own division; it is the dedup result of consolidating ~470 unit-level concept entries into 56 canonical CONCEPTs. Tier-1 (45 concepts) carries the load-bearing apparatus across the volume; Tier-2 (8 concepts) covers chapter-central concepts that ramify into ≥2 chapters; Tier-3 (3 concepts) covers distinctive apparatuses developed in a single chapter but elevated to volume-relevance (Edenic paradigm, demonic trinity, dissolution-of-drama-as-four-paths). Every concept node carries (a) a ≤60-word paraphrastic gloss, (b) tier assignment, (c) first-introduced unit, (d) developed-across-units array, (e) pentad alignment, (f) source-unit concept IDs it consolidates.

## (ii) The pentad core (five terms)

Burke's five terms are the apex anchors. **Act** (C-PENTAD-ACT) answers *what was done?* On the scholastic-realist construal Burke develops in GM-06, an act is an *actualization* — the bringing of a potentiality (*dynamis*) into realization (*energeia*) in accordance with a form proper to its kind. Distinguishable from mere motion (which lies in the realm of instruments); admits a spectrum (attitude, incipient act, delayed act). Burke names a "modicum of novelty" in every genuine act (GM-03) that cannot be relocated under any other pentad term — the irreducibility-of-act thesis.

**Scene** (C-PENTAD-SCENE) answers *where/in what situation?* Names the background or containing environment within which an act occurs. As containing-principle, scene fixes the *circumference* within which motive may be located. Spinoza's universal substance is the maximal-circumference instance; the behaviorist laboratory is the minimal.

**Agent** (C-PENTAD-AGENT) answers *who did it?* Names the constituting subject — the doer of the act. Admits subdivision into co-agents (friends) and counter-agents (enemies, GM-00) and generalization into super-agents — church, race, nation, Spirit, class (GM-05). Idealism is recognizable not by surface vocabulary but by its derivation of scene/act/agency/purpose from properties of the agent.

**Agency** (C-PENTAD-AGENCY) answers *how? by what means?* Names instruments, methods, mediating media. Paradigmatic in tools and machines; reducibly extended through the pragmatist nesting of file/hand/brain/educational-system (GM-00).

**Purpose** (C-PENTAD-PURPOSE) answers *why?* Names the end, telos, motivating-toward-which. The term most subject to violent disagreement among motive-attributors and most susceptible to dissolution (GM-07): pure Purpose collapses into the paradox of substance (docta ignorantia, mystic oxymoron) and survives implicitly even where formally eliminated.

Two structural features of the pentad-core are book-load-bearing. First, the **common ground / pentad-overlap thesis** (C-PENTAD-OVERLAPS): the five terms participate in a common substance whose role as their causal ancestor makes them transformable into one another at every point where their fields overlap. The "great central moltenness" figure (GM-00) — distinctions arise from a central moltenness where all is merged — and the fingers-and-palm metaphor (terms distinct at the extremities merge in the palm of the hand) name this insight. Second, the **transcendental-not-formal claim** (GM-07): the five terms are necessary forms of talk-about-experience rather than forms of experience itself; the Grammar analyzes language, not 'reality.'

## (iii) The ratio catalog (10 dyads + higher-order)

The ten dyadic ratios are catalogued in GM-01 (book p.15) and developed unevenly there, with full per-ratio specifications recovered by Phase 2 across the volume. All ten are reversible (GM-06's expansion to twenty), and the 1962 Addendum (GM-09) sharpens that order-of-terms matters: scene-act and act-scene are not equivalent.

The **scene-act ratio** (C-RATIO-SCENE-ACT) is the titular and most-developed ratio. Scene-quality implies act-quality (Marxist "objective situation"; military "terrain determines tactics"; Coleridge's Pantisocracy); convertibly, act constructs scene (Lavinia's command nails the shutters and the closed scene then commands her). The **scene-agent ratio** (C-RATIO-SCENE-AGENT) is positional in the strong sense: the contents of a divine container synecdochically share in its divinity (Wordsworth's "beauteous evening" sonnet; Carlyle on the Arabs and the Arabian country); the naturalist novelist's embarrassment (indicting bad conditions by their brutalizing effect produces characters not worth saving) shows the ratio's strong claims. The **act-agent ratio** (C-RATIO-ACT-AGENT) is temporal/sequential rather than positional: agents author acts; acts make or remake their agents (the sobering influence of the Presidency; judicial robes transforming the wearer). Burke's signature example deploys it competitively against scene-act: was Russian wartime resistance produced by socialism (scene-act) or by Russian patriotism (act-agent)? Ratio-selection is rhetorically loaded.

The remaining seven ratios — **scene-agency** (Biddle's accumulated technologies becoming scenic), **scene-purpose** (Hegel's Reason as Grundprinzip of history), **act-agency** (Burke's way-words: *hodos*/tao/yoga as act-words sliding into met-*hodos* as agency-word), **act-purpose** (the Aeschylean Erinyes→Eumenides total transformation), **agent-agency** (Seurat pointillism where medium becomes common element relating agent to scene), **agent-purpose** (needs as property of agents), and **agency-purpose** (the chapter-titular dyad of GM-07, the dialectical hinge between Pragmatism and Mysticism) — are individually shorter but operationally complete.

Higher-order combinations matter for applied-dramatism: the **Edenic paradigm** (mutual triadic ratification of scene-act + act-agent + scene-agent producing total transformation, GM-01); Burke's **Biddle four-term cascade** (agencies-become-scenic → scenic-agencies-motivate-acts → unsatisfied-needs-return-motive-to-agent); the **picturesque conflict** of ratios (fools in wise situations: Chaplin, Empson's pastoral). The principle generalizes: every applied-dramatism analysis names which ratio (or ratio-combination) is in operation and how the analyst's choice of ratio deflects attention from competing motive-loci.

Two methodological qualifications soften the ratio apparatus. Ratios may be read as **principles of selectivity** rather than thoroughgoing causal relationships (GM-01 closing): a situation "votes for" appropriate temperaments and acts rather than producing them ex nihilo; "mute inglorious Miltons" are unselected seeds. And every ratio is **a formula indicating a transition from one term to another** (GM-06): the mediated necessarily differs from the immediate as translation differs from original. Every ratio has potential-to-actual form.

## (iv) Substance vocabulary (Tier-1 selections from GM-02)

GM-02 develops the substance vocabulary as a distinct analytical apparatus alongside the pentad. The chapter's **paradox of substance** (C-PARADOX-OF-SUBSTANCE) is the master irony: the word designates what a thing intrinsically is, but etymologically refers to what stands beneath or supports it from outside (sub-stance, *hypostasis*, the Indo-Germanic *stā-* root). Every substance-claim covertly imports a contextual reference into an intrinsic claim. Locke's "something I know not what" exposes the incoherence; Burke's dramatistic move is to study the term's embarrassments rather than banish it.

From this paradox Burke derives a taxonomy of substance-types whose Tier-1 members map onto the pentad: **contextual / geometric substance** (C-SUBSTANCE-CONTEXTUAL) anchors Scene (Spinoza's universal substance; *omnis determinatio est negatio*); **familial substance** (C-SUBSTANCE-FAMILIAL) anchors Agent and Act-via-entelechy (Aristotelian entelechy as in-rebus realism; Plato's Forms as ante-rem realism; mediaeval universalia debate); **directional substance** (C-SUBSTANCE-DIRECTIONAL) anchors Purpose and Agency ("Where are you going?" as motive-question); **dialectic substance** (C-SUBSTANCE-DIALECTIC) is the over-all category subsuming the others by treating motive in terms of verbal/symbolic action.

Cross-cutting these, **paradox of purity** (C-SUBSTANCE-PARADOX-PURITY) names the asymptote at which any term intensified to absolute purity flips into its dialectical antithesis (Hegel's pure being = not-being; Aristotle's God as Pure Act = eternal repose; Plotinus's negative-theological One). **Actus / status** (C-SUBSTANCE-ACTUS-STATUS) is the chapter's most book-load-bearing pair: act vs. state, motion vs. rest, drama vs. lyric, faith-as-act vs. knowledge-as-state. It tracks the philological drift by which active terms (aretē, virtus, hero) become state-terms ("L'état c'est moi") and articulates the substance vocabulary with the Act-Agent ratio of the pentad. **Intrinsic / extrinsic substance** (C-SUBSTANCE-INTRINSIC-EXTRINSIC) is generalized in GM-11 as the scope-of-text question; **rhetorical substance** (C-SUBSTANCE-RHETORICAL) names the working tactic by which "substantially," "essentially," "in principle" let speakers assert identity at unfalsifiable generality; and **consubstantial substance** (C-SUBSTANCE-CONSUBSTANTIAL) is the epithet-mechanism by which calling a man "brother" proclaims consubstantiality while calling him "bastard" attacks his familial authorship — Burke's forward-anchor to the consubstantiation/identification apparatus of A Rhetoric of Motives (1950).

Finally, **universal motive / monetary substance** (C-SUBSTANCE-UNIVERSAL-MONETARY) names the cross-volume thread by which money operates as the secular analogue of the One God — a universal motive supplying believers with a common motivational ground. GM-03's long monetary-reduction case study, GM-07's "Catholic act, Protestant agent, Scientist scene with money across," and GM-08's money-as-God-term-of-constitutional-frame are all instances of this single thread.

## (v) Methodological apparatuses

Three Tier-1 methodological apparatuses are the volume's most transferable construct after the pentad itself.

The **representative anecdote** (C-REPRESENTATIVE-ANECDOTE, GM-03) is the compact case that condenses the analytical pattern Burke will then expand into a full terminology — the form "in conformity with which the vocabulary is constructed." Drama is Burke's representative anecdote for human motives; the behaviorist conditioned reflex is the negative example (animal anecdote cannot represent linguistic-symbolic motives). GM-13 sharpens the contrast: a representative anecdote is *synecdochic* (part-for-whole), a reductive anecdote is *metonymic* (lower-order substitute); Otto Neurath's isotypes and Korzybski's Structural Differential are named reductive misrepresentations. GM-08 deploys the representative-anecdote requirement at document-corpus scale: the U.S. Constitution itself is chosen as master anecdote because constitutions are "substantival" and explicitly enact human wills.

The **circumference** (C-CIRCUMFERENCE, GM-03) is the analyst's elected scope-of-scene — the size of the contextual frame within which an act is located. Burke's methodologically signature claim: selection of circumference "is in itself an act, an act of faith." Different circumferences yield different motives for the same act (Mitya vs. Alyosha Karamazov inhabit identically the same scene but interpret it with different circumferences). The historical narrowing-of-circumference (God's law → natural law → environment → monetary scene) is Burke's structural account of secularization, and GM-11 generalizes it to the scope-of-text problem (intrinsic-vs-extrinsic as a meta-question about which circumference the analyst draws around "the work itself").

The **intrinsic / extrinsic distinction** (C-SUBSTANCE-INTRINSIC-EXTRINSIC + GM-11's elaboration) is not a primitive ontological cut but a scene-act ratio meta-question. The historical drift across Aristotle (intrinsic = entelechy of genus) → Aquinas (intrinsic = abilities/habits, extrinsic = God/Devil) → Spinoza (only the totality is intrinsic) → modern science (intrinsic abolished; everything extrinsic) demonstrates the boundary's instability. Burke's dramatistic resolution is the **poem-as-act / constitutive-act-surviving-as-object** framework: a poem is a constitutive act enacted by a poet in a particular temporal scene, surviving as an objective structure examinable in other scenes by other agents. Internal-structure analysis (intrinsic-pole) and act-scene ratio analysis (extrinsic-pole) are complementary, both dramatistic.

Two additional Tier-1 apparatuses round out the methodological layer. **Symbolic action** (C-SYMBOLIC-ACTION, GM-06) is language considered as a mode of action rather than information; a poem is the symbolic act of its maker, which the reader re-enacts in reading. **Incipient act / attitude** (C-INCIPIENT-ACT-ATTITUDE) is the proto-sixth pentad term — Richards' "preliminary adjustments, getting ready to act," Mead's "beginnings of acts," Korzybski's "delayed response" all converging on the recognition that an attitude can be substitute for an act OR first step toward an act. The realm of the incipient is the realm of symbolic action par excellence. Keats's Urn-as-"fair Attitude" is the summational lyric instance (GM-10); the 1962 Addendum (GM-09) explicitly proposes Attitude as a sixth pentad term, fully formalized only in A Rhetoric of Motives (1950).

## (vi) Dialectical apparatuses

GM-09 ("Dialectic in General") supplies the volume's master dialectical apparatuses; GM-08 ("Dialectic of Constitutions") supplies the applied-dramatism worked example at document-corpus scale.

**Transformation of terms** (C-TRANSFORMATION-OF-TERMS) is the master apparatus: tracking the disposition and trans-position of terms across motive-statements — the way ancestral, derivative, and bridge terms migrate, merge, split, or get re-keyed under dialectical pressure. The pentad itself is a transformation-vocabulary in miniature; GM-00 presents the wakes-of-launches metaphor, the casuistic locations (the hero-file example's four candidate motive-locations), and the dramatistic pliancy through which "A may become non-A" by descent to logical-substance ground. GM-09 generalizes this into a method.

**Merger and division** (C-MERGER-AND-DIVISION) are the two fundamental dialectical operations (Plato, *Phaedrus*). Merger = comprehension of scattered particulars in one idea; division = carving an idea at the joints. Most concrete dialectical events are mergers atop division or divisions atop merger; a "third / pontificating term" (continuity, polarity, synthesis) is usually generated to mediate. Per genus et differentiam is the classical instance: merger via genus + division via differentia. Scotist *haecceitas* (thisness) is the third stage merging genus, differentia, and individuality.

The **dialectic of the scapegoat** (C-DIALECTIC-OF-SCAPEGOAT) is the three-step ritual by which a charismatic vicar absorbs and carries off the collective iniquities of a community: (1) original merger between iniquitous and chosen vessel; (2) ritualistic division alienating the shared element onto the vessel; (3) new merger of the purified community defined in dialectical opposition to the sacrificial offering. The scapegoat is *consubstantial* with persecutors — its curative efficacy depends entirely on that prior consubstantiality. Hitlerite Anti-Semitism is the cleanest negative example; Socrates as scapegoat of transcendence is the closing application that interlocks scapegoat-dialectic with Socratic-transcendence.

**Temporizing of essence** (C-TEMPORIZING-OF-ESSENCE) is the pun on sequence: the logically prior can be expressed as the temporally prior, and vice versa. Essence becomes narrated as a "going back" to origins (Freud's primal horde, Plato's Meno-recollection, original sin); historicist and essentialist vocabularies are convertible. Ibsen's *Peer Gynt* is the worked literary case (the protagonist's search for true self dramatized as going back to maternal/familial origin). The apparatus is the chapter's strongest cross-link to GM-13 — temporizing-of-essence operates as synecdoche dialectically (part-for-whole = origin-for-essence).

**Socratic transcendence** (C-SOCRATIC-TRANSCENDENCE) is the upward dialectical move: a new motive discovered en route; the position at the end is reached by transformation, and the position at the start can only retrospectively be seen in light of the new motivation. Burke's signature reading: a Platonic dialogue is an *act* of transcendence (Phaedrus' five levels), not just a plea for one. The Plotinian Upward Way to the Primal-Principle is the mystical instance; Korzybski's higher-and-higher levels of generalization is the modern semantic instance; mathematics as imagery-transcending dialectic is the Faraday-unified-energy-formula instance (Title of Titles / god-term).

**Dissolution of drama** (C-DISSOLUTION-OF-DRAMA) names the dramatistic perspective's structural self-abolition: it contains a point at which it abolishes itself in its own enunciation, by four canonical paths: drama dissolves into lyric state; action reduced to motion (scientism); philosophies *of* dramatism (Burke's own move: coordinates derived from drama yet used non-dramatically); philosophies of super-drama (monotheistic god as super-person becomes impersonal). This is the closing tension of the Grammar.

## (vii) The four master tropes

GM-13's Appendix D supplies a parallel analytical apparatus to the pentad. The four master tropes — **metaphor / perspective** (C-TROPE-METAPHOR), **metonymy / reduction** (C-TROPE-METONYMY), **synecdoche / representation** (C-TROPE-SYNECDOCHE), **irony / dialectic** (C-TROPE-IRONY) — are instruments for the discovery and description of "the truth," not figures of speech. Their literal-realistic substitutions are perspective, reduction, representation, dialectic. Any one trope fully developed generates the other three.

Burke's load-bearing asymmetry: synecdoche/representation is two-way (part-for-whole and whole-for-part); metonymy/reduction is one-way (quality-to-quantity only, in the restricted sense). The "noblest synecdoche" is microcosm-macrocosm identity (Leibniz's monadology); reduction is the metonymic move proper to scientific realism (the materialist mirror of the synecdochic move proper to poetic realism). Burke's distinction between poetic vs. scientific behaviorism — both translate spiritual to bodily, but the poet treats body as figure/symbolization while the scientist treats body as substantial reduction — is the dramatistic diagnosis of where motive-talk goes wrong when it crosses the action-motion watershed.

The classic-vs-romantic irony distinction is the chapter's load-bearing methodological move. True/classic/humble irony is grounded in consubstantiality with the "enemy" — Flaubert's "Madame Bovary, c'est moi"; Mann's "Judas psychology"; Newton observing the criminal, "that man is going to prison for me"; Falstaff's gloriously sympathetic distortion of religious values (against Tartuffe's mere hypocrisy). Romantic/Pharisaic irony is the artist superior to the rejected role; Burke reads Allen Tate's interpretation of Eliot's *Waste Land* seduction scene as misidentifying Pharisaic irony as humble irony. The over-all formula of irony: **A → non-A** (the peripety), the strategic moment of reversal that places the essence of drama and dialectic in the structure of internal fatality.

The tropes operate as a parallel apparatus to the pentad in a five-by-four interaction matrix (made explicit in GM-13's Phase-2 extension): each pentad term can be operated on by each trope. Metaphor places perspectives on the pentad terms (act seen as conquest; scene as marketplace); metonymy reduces them to lower-order substrates (act reduced to behavior; agent reduced to body); synecdoche elects representative instances (one act standing-for-all-acts; avatar standing for player); irony deploys all five terms together in peripety (the act returns as its reversal; the purpose that arose in time perishes in time).

#### 3. Act (pentad term)
- **canonical_name**: Act (pentad term)
- **definition**: The pentad term answering 'what was done?' On the scholastic-realist construal Burke develops in GM-06, an act is an actualization (energeia) — the bringing of a potentiality into realization in accordance with a form proper to its kind. Distinguishable from mere motion (which lies in the realm of instruments); admits a spectrum (attitude, incipient act, delayed act) and is reciprocally related to potency. Featured by Realism (Aristotle/Aquinas).
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-00, GM-01, GM-02, GM-03, GM-06, GM-09, GM-10, GM-11
- **centrality**: core

#### 4. Scene (pentad term)
- **canonical_name**: Scene (pentad term)
- **definition**: The pentad term answering 'where/in what situation?' Names the background, setting, situation, or containing environment within which an act occurs and an agent moves. As containing-principle for action, scene fixes the circumference within which motive may be located. Featured by Materialism (Hobbes, Spinoza, Darwin, Lucretius, Stoicism). Spinoza's universal substance is the maximal-circumference instance.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-00, GM-01, GM-02, GM-03, GM-04, GM-06, GM-08, GM-10, GM-11
- **centrality**: core

#### 5. Agent (pentad term)
- **canonical_name**: Agent (pentad term)
- **definition**: The pentad term answering 'who did it?' Names the constituting subject — the doer of the act, from which (on idealist construal) scene, act, agency, and purpose are derived. Admits subdivision into co-agents and counter-agents and generalization into super-agents (church, race, nation, Spirit, class). Featured by Idealism (Berkeley, Hume, Leibniz, Kant, Hegel) — and by Marxism as a hybrid 'idealistic materialist' (class-as-collective-agent under materialist surface).
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-00, GM-01, GM-02, GM-05, GM-08, GM-09, GM-10, GM-12
- **centrality**: core

#### 6. Agency (pentad term)
- **canonical_name**: Agency (pentad term)
- **definition**: The pentad term answering 'how? by what means?' Names the instruments, methods, and mediating media through which an act is performed. Paradigmatic in tools, machines, and the pragmatist nesting of file/hand/brain/educational-system. Featured by Pragmatism (James, Dewey, Mead, Peirce, Bridgman) — the modern, science-driven inversion of Aristotle's subordination of agency under final cause: 'means become ancestors of ends.'
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-00, GM-01, GM-07, GM-08
- **centrality**: core

#### 7. Purpose (pentad term)
- **canonical_name**: Purpose (pentad term)
- **definition**: The pentad term answering 'why?' Names the end, telos, motivating-toward-which of an act. The term most subject to violent disagreement among motive-attributors. Featured by Mysticism (Plotinus, Plato's Good=One, Bonaventura, Christian mystics) — recognizable not by mere unity but by 'unity of the individual with some cosmic or universal Purpose.' Most susceptible to dissolution: pure Purpose collapses into the paradox of substance (docta ignorantia) and survives implicitly even where formally eliminated.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-00, GM-01, GM-07, GM-08, GM-09
- **centrality**: core

#### 8. Scene-act ratio
- **canonical_name**: Scene-act ratio
- **definition**: The dyadic ratio whereby the quality of the scene implies (contains, calls forth) a correspondingly qualified act, and convertibly the act demands or constructs a scene of corresponding quality. Reversible; usable deterministically or hortatorily. The chapter-titular ratio of GM-01 'Container and Thing Contained' and the prediction-engine of dramatistic analysis (Burke's deployment in the Keats Ode worked example, GM-10).
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-00, GM-01, GM-03, GM-04, GM-08, GM-10, GM-11
- **centrality**: core

#### 9. Scene-agent ratio
- **canonical_name**: Scene-agent ratio
- **definition**: The dyadic ratio whereby person and place stand in synecdochic correspondence: the quality of a place implies a corresponding quality of inhabitant; the contents of a divine container share in its divinity. Positional in the strong sense (scene contains agent). Worked at length in nineteenth-century idealist 'Einklang zwischen Innen- und Aussenwelt' and in the naturalist-novelist embarrassment (brutalizing scene yields brutal characters).
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-00, GM-01, GM-04, GM-08, GM-10, GM-11, GM-12
- **centrality**: core

#### 10. Scene-agency ratio
- **canonical_name**: Scene-agency ratio
- **definition**: The dyadic ratio between scene and the instrumental means employed in an act. The scene affords/constrains which agencies are available; conversely, new agencies (technologies, methods) accumulate to become scenic — Burke's Biddle example: railroad, telegraph, telephone, automobile, radio 'having in their accumulation become scenic' motivate political acts.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-01, GM-07, GM-08
- **centrality**: core

#### 11. Scene-purpose ratio
- **canonical_name**: Scene-purpose ratio
- **definition**: The dyadic ratio between scene and the purpose toward which an act tends. A scene affords particular purpose-structures (quest-givers, eschatological horizons, providential cosmos); conversely a purpose may project a scene proportioned to it. Hegelian Reason-as-Grundprinzip is its paradigm idealist deployment; the Stoic cosmic-providential plan is its materialist (pronoia) deployment.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-01, GM-04, GM-07
- **centrality**: core

#### 12. Act-agent ratio
- **canonical_name**: Act-agent ratio
- **definition**: The dyadic ratio between an act and the agent who performs it. NOT a strict containment relation: 'the agent does not contain the act, though its results might be said to pre-exist virtually within him.' Temporal/sequential: agents author acts; acts can in turn make or remake the agent. Burke's signature examples: 'sobering influence of the Presidency,' judicial robes, Russian wartime resistance (act-agent vs scene-act as competing readings). Scholastic-realist primary ratio: 'every thing acts according as it is in act' (Aquinas).
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-00, GM-01, GM-02, GM-06, GM-09
- **centrality**: core

#### 13. Act-agency ratio
- **canonical_name**: Act-agency ratio
- **definition**: The dyadic ratio between act and the instrumental means employed to perform it. Burke's way-words discussion (hodos / tao / yoga / dike → met-hodos as method-as-means) is the chapter's clearest formulation of the act/agency boundary instability: an act-word (way of life) becomes an agency-word (means of doing) by a subtle slide from being to doing.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-01, GM-06, GM-07
- **centrality**: core

#### 14. Act-purpose ratio
- **canonical_name**: Act-purpose ratio
- **definition**: The dyadic ratio between act and the purpose toward which it tends. An act is consummated only insofar as it realizes the purpose for the sake of which it is done; conversely a purpose is articulate only insofar as it admits expression in a concrete act. The Aeschylean Erinyes→Eumenides transformation is Burke's paradigm of act-purpose total transformation.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-01, GM-06, GM-09
- **centrality**: core

#### 15. Agent-agency ratio
- **canonical_name**: Agent-agency ratio
- **definition**: The dyadic ratio between an agent and the instrumental means available to (or characteristic of) that agent. Agent-character implies a corresponding set of fit agencies; conversely, habituated agencies constitute the agent's character. The Seurat-pointillism case (book p. 8) is a borderline instance where the medium becomes the common element relating agent to scene.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-01, GM-05, GM-07
- **centrality**: core

#### 16. Agent-purpose ratio
- **canonical_name**: Agent-purpose ratio
- **definition**: The dyadic ratio between an agent and the purpose toward which the agent's action tends. Burke locates 'needs' as 'a property of agents,' making agent-purpose the relation between an agent's needs/wants/aspirations and the purposes they generate. Distinguishes 'primal needs' (proper to agent) from 'new needs' (which may best be treated as scenic).
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-01, GM-05, GM-07
- **centrality**: core

#### 17. Agency-purpose ratio
- **canonical_name**: Agency-purpose ratio
- **definition**: The dyadic ratio between the instrumental means and the purpose for which they are employed. Agency tends to acquire its own teleology under sustained use ('philosophy of means'); purpose conversely shapes which agencies are admissible. The chapter-titular ratio of GM-07; the dialectical hinge between Pragmatism (Agency-feature) and Mysticism (Purpose-feature). Burke's 'Mysticism of Means' critique (Huxley pacifism) is its most worked instance.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-01, GM-04, GM-07
- **centrality**: core

#### 18. Reversibility of ratios (10 → 20)
- **canonical_name**: Reversibility of ratios (10 → 20)
- **definition**: Burke's principle (GM-01, GM-06) that each ratio operates in both directions: scene-act reads either as scene-implies-act or as act-demands-scene; the ten dyadic ratios expand to twenty reversible ratios. 'A ratio is a formula indicating a transition from one term to another'; the mediated necessarily differs from the immediate, as translation differs from original. The 1962 Addendum (GM-09) sharpens: order of terms matters (scene-act ≠ act-scene).
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-01, GM-06, GM-09
- **centrality**: core

#### 19. Ratios as principles of determination / selectivity
- **canonical_name**: Ratios as principles of determination / selectivity
- **definition**: Burke's strong formulation: the ratios are 'principles of determination' — the analytical engine of dramatism, not merely a catalogue. Softened reading: principles of selectivity (a situation 'votes for' appropriate temperaments and acts rather than producing them ex nihilo; 'mute inglorious Miltons' as unselected seeds). The choice between competing ratios is rhetorically consequential — ratio-selection deflects attention from one motive-locus to another.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-01, GM-04, GM-08, GM-09
- **centrality**: core

#### 20. Metaphor / perspective (master trope)
- **canonical_name**: Metaphor / perspective (master trope)
- **definition**: The device for seeing something in terms of something else — bringing out the thisness of a that. Literal-realistic substitution: perspective. To consider A from the point of view of B is to use B as a perspective on A. Reality is not dissolved by multi-perspectivism; it is established by it. Degrees of being correspond to variety-of-perspectives-supportable (plants > minerals; animals > plants; men > animals).
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-13
- **centrality**: core

#### 21. Metonymy / reduction (master trope)
- **canonical_name**: Metonymy / reduction (master trope)
- **definition**: Conveying an incorporeal or intangible state in terms of the corporeal or tangible ('heart' for 'emotions'); the 'archaicizing' device. Reduction is the one-direction (quality-to-quantity) special case proper to scientific realism. Two senses: poet's metonymy as figure/symbolization vs. scientific behaviorist's metonymy as substantial reduction. The trope underwrites materialism's scene-featuring.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-03, GM-08, GM-13
- **centrality**: core

#### 22. Synecdoche / representation (master trope)
- **canonical_name**: Synecdoche / representation (master trope)
- **definition**: Convertibility between two terms in an integral relationship: part for whole, whole for part, container for contained, sign for signified, cause for effect, genus for species, and all reversals. The 'noblest synecdoche' is microcosm-macrocosm identity (Leibniz). Two-way (unlike metonymy's one-way). Burke extends to before/after, implicit/explicit, name/narrative, disease/cure, hero/villain, active/passive. Underwrites every representative-anecdote choice.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-08, GM-12, GM-13
- **centrality**: core

#### 23. Irony / dialectic (master trope)
- **canonical_name**: Irony / dialectic (master trope)
- **definition**: Arises when one tries, by the interaction of terms upon one another, to produce a development that uses all the terms. From the standpoint of this total form ('perspective of perspectives'), no participating sub-perspective is precisely right or precisely wrong; all are voices integrally affecting one another. True/classic/humble irony is grounded in consubstantiality with the 'enemy' (Flaubert, Mann, Newton, Falstaff); romantic/Pharisaic irony is grounded in superiority. The over-all formula: A → non-A (the peripety).
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-09, GM-13
- **centrality**: core

#### 24. Paradox of substance
- **canonical_name**: Paradox of substance
- **definition**: The structural irony at the root of all substance-talk: the word designates what a thing intrinsically is, but etymologically refers to something outside the thing that stands beneath or supports it (sub-stance, hypostasis, stā- root). Every substance-claim covertly imports a contextual reference into an intrinsic claim. The master-irony of GM-02 from which the substance-vocabulary taxonomy is derived; recapitulated as a dialectical staple in GM-09.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-02, GM-06, GM-07, GM-09, GM-11
- **centrality**: core

#### 25. Contextual / geometric substance
- **canonical_name**: Contextual / geometric substance
- **definition**: Definition by placement of a thing within its context, location, or surroundings. To define (de-finire) is to mark boundaries, requiring reference to what is outside the thing. Maximalist version: Spinoza's universal substance, where each object is determined by the totality (omnis determinatio est negatio). Geometric subspecies: Euclidean-Spinozistic implicit-relation calculus.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-02, GM-04
- **centrality**: core

#### 26. Familial (tribal, ancestral) substance
- **canonical_name**: Familial (tribal, ancestral) substance
- **definition**: Defining a substance by ancestral cause — biological descent or spiritualized analogue (covenant, founder, eponym, creation). Includes Plato's archetypal Forms (each thing imitating its eponym in heaven) and Aristotle's entelechy (the individual as actualization of its class). Mediaeval universalia debate (ante rem / in re / post rem) is its scholastic refraction.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-02, GM-06
- **centrality**: core

#### 27. Directional substance
- **canonical_name**: Directional substance
- **definition**: Substance characterized by motion toward, by 'the way,' by direction-from-within. Strongly futuristic and purposive ('Where are you going?' rather than 'Where are you from?'). Includes the cluster motion / movement / cause / emotion / moment as variant glosses on motivation.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-02
- **centrality**: core

#### 28. Dialectic substance
- **canonical_name**: Dialectic substance
- **definition**: The over-all substance category: substance arising from the systematic contemplation of antinomies — recognition that we necessarily define a thing in terms of something else, where the something else is the other (the antagonist, the agon). The dramatistic super-category that subsumes contextual / familial / directional / geometric substance-types.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-02
- **centrality**: core

#### 29. Paradox of purity (pure substance flips to its negation)
- **canonical_name**: Paradox of purity (pure substance flips to its negation)
- **definition**: When a substance is purified by abstraction to its absolute, it tends toward its negation: God as 'pure person' is impersonal; pure being is not-being (Hegel); pure escape is perpetual imprisonment in escape; Aristotle's God as Pure Act = eternal repose. Plotinus's negative theology arrives at abstract Oneness by negating all material attributes.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-02, GM-06, GM-07
- **centrality**: core

#### 30. Actus / status (action-state dyad)
- **canonical_name**: Actus / status (action-state dyad)
- **definition**: Burke's load-bearing dyadic reduction of the tragic triad (poiema/pathema/mathema): act vs. state, motion vs. rest, drama vs. lyric, faith-as-act vs. knowledge-as-state. Maps onto potentiality/actuality and tracks the philological drift by which active terms (aretē, virtus, hero) become state-terms ('L'état c'est moi'). Articulates the substance vocabulary with the Act-Agent ratio of the pentad.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-02, GM-06, GM-09
- **centrality**: core

#### 31. Intrinsic / extrinsic substance
- **canonical_name**: Intrinsic / extrinsic substance
- **definition**: The dialectical pairing of motivations-within-the-agent against motivations-derived-from-scene. Hegel pairs Matter (extrinsic, gravity-externally-referred) with Spirit (intrinsic, Bei-sich-selbst-sein). The poles invert structurally when an intrinsic principle is operationalized through external means. The intrinsic must not be confused with the unique. GM-11 generalizes this as the scope-of-text question and resolves it via the poem-as-act framework.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-02, GM-03, GM-11
- **centrality**: core

#### 32. Rhetorical substance
- **canonical_name**: Rhetorical substance
- **definition**: The ambiguity of substance as a deployable resource of persuasion. 'Substantially,' 'essentially,' 'in principle,' 'in the long run' allow a speaker to assert identity at a level of generality that cannot be falsified by particulars. Positivists who outlaw substance reimport it covertly under 'in-principle' and 'universally valid.'
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-02
- **centrality**: important

#### 33. Consubstantial substance (epithetic, identificatory)
- **canonical_name**: Consubstantial substance (epithetic, identificatory)
- **definition**: Substance as it operates in epithets and identification-tactics: to call a man brother is to proclaim consubstantiality; to call him bastard is to attack his familial 'authorship.' An epithet assigns substance doubly — characterizes the object and implies an action toward it, thus serving as motive. Forward-anchors the consubstantiation/identification apparatus of A Rhetoric of Motives (1950).
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-00, GM-02, GM-09, GM-13
- **centrality**: core

#### 34. Universal motive / monetary substance (money as god-term)
- **canonical_name**: Universal motive / monetary substance (money as god-term)
- **definition**: When a substance becomes a universal motive, it operates as a master scene supplying believers with a common motivational ground. Monotheism and the monetary motive are structurally parallel; money 'transubstantiates' from agency (medium of exchange) into ground/purpose of action — the 'capitalist psychosis.' GM-08 develops money as God-term of constitutional frames ('where one can volunteer, there one is free').
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-02, GM-03, GM-07, GM-08
- **centrality**: core

#### 35. Representative anecdote
- **canonical_name**: Representative anecdote
- **definition**: The compact case that condenses the analytical pattern Burke will then expand into a full terminology; the form 'in conformity with which the vocabulary is constructed.' A good representative anecdote is to a vocabulary of motives what a paradigm is to a research program — its scope-limiter and its generative seed. Drama is Burke's representative anecdote for human motives; behaviorist conditioned reflex is the negative example. GM-13 contrasts representative vs. reductive anecdote.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-03, GM-08, GM-13
- **centrality**: core

#### 36. Circumference (scope of scene)
- **canonical_name**: Circumference (scope of scene)
- **definition**: The analyst's elected scope-of-scene; the size of the contextual frame within which an act is located. Burke's most transferable methodological construct after the pentad itself. Selection of circumference 'is in itself an act, an act of faith.' Different circumferences yield different motives for the same act. The historical narrowing-of-circumference (God's law → natural law → environment → monetary scene) is Burke's structural account of secularization.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-03, GM-08, GM-11
- **centrality**: core

#### 37. Action vs. motion (Dramatistic distinction)
- **canonical_name**: Action vs. motion (Dramatistic distinction)
- **definition**: Any verb with connotations of consciousness or purpose falls under 'act'; stumbling over an obstruction is not act but mere motion; the basic Dramatistic unit is 'the human body in conscious or purposive motion.' Behaviorism truncates drama by reducing action to motion. The watershed-moment dividing dramatist from operationalist vocabularies (GM-06): no instrument can record action ('ideas') except insofar as the subject-matter is reduced to motion.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-00, GM-01, GM-04, GM-06, GM-09, GM-11
- **centrality**: core

#### 38. Symbolic action
- **canonical_name**: Symbolic action
- **definition**: Language considered as a mode of action (poetic) rather than information (scientific); a poem is the symbolic act of its maker, which the reader re-enacts in reading. Symbolic action is the realm of the incipient/attitudinal par excellence — actual conflicts can be transcended there, with results sometimes fatal, sometimes felicitous. Burke's central concept for analyzing literary works and rhetorical performances as motivated acts.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-06, GM-10, GM-11, GM-12
- **centrality**: core

#### 39. Incipient act / attitude (proto-sixth-term)
- **canonical_name**: Incipient act / attitude (proto-sixth-term)
- **definition**: An incipient act is a perception/attitude that contains the form of a not-yet-actualized act — Richards' 'preliminary adjustments, getting ready to act' or Mead's 'beginnings of acts.' Fundamentally ambiguous: an attitude can be SUBSTITUTE for an act OR FIRST STEP toward an act. Forward-anchors the sixth pentad term Burke adds in the 1962 Addendum (GM-09) and develops fully in A Rhetoric of Motives (1950). Keats's Urn-as-'fair Attitude' is the summational lyric instance.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-01, GM-06, GM-07, GM-09, GM-10, GM-11
- **centrality**: core

#### 40. Featuring of a term (philosophic-school diagnostic)
- **canonical_name**: Featuring of a term (philosophic-school diagnostic)
- **definition**: Burke's diagnostic move: each philosophic school selects one of the five terms as Ausgangspunkt (generating principle) and develops a vocabulary suited to give that term full expression, with the other four slighted or placed in its perspective. Materialism features Scene; Idealism features Agent; Realism features Act; Pragmatism features Agency; Mysticism features Purpose. The diagnostic is featuring, not exclusivity — every philosophy deploys all five terms.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-04, GM-05, GM-06, GM-07
- **centrality**: core

#### 41. Grammar vs. philosophic idiom (universal vs. open)
- **canonical_name**: Grammar vs. philosophic idiom (universal vs. open)
- **definition**: Burke's signature two-level claim: a statement of the grammatical principles of motivation can lay claim to universal validity (every motive-attribution must reference some background, etc.), but the choice of any particular philosophic idiom filling in those principles (God / nature / history / means of production for Scene) is open to question and varies with cultural situation. A philosophy is a casuistry of the pentadic resources.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-00, GM-04, GM-07, GM-09
- **centrality**: core

#### 42. Transformation of terms (master dialectical apparatus)
- **canonical_name**: Transformation of terms (master dialectical apparatus)
- **definition**: Master apparatus of GM-09 and pervasive throughout the Grammar: tracking the disposition and trans-position of terms across motive-statements; the way ancestral, derivative, and bridge terms migrate, merge, split, or get re-keyed under dialectical pressure. Burke's signature methodological move — pentad as a generating principle whose transformations, permutations, and combinations produce the full variety of motive-statements.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-00, GM-09
- **centrality**: core

#### 43. Merger and division
- **canonical_name**: Merger and division
- **definition**: The two fundamental dialectical operations (Plato, Phaedrus): merger = comprehension of scattered particulars in one idea; division = carving an idea at the joints. Most concrete dialectical events are mergers atop division or divisions atop merger; a 'third / pontificating term' (continuity, polarity, synthesis) is usually generated to mediate. Per genus et differentiam is the classical instance: merger via genus + division via differentia.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-09
- **centrality**: core

#### 44. Dialectic of the scapegoat
- **canonical_name**: Dialectic of the scapegoat
- **definition**: Three-step ritual by which a charismatic vicar absorbs and carries off the collective iniquities of a community: (1) original merger — iniquities shared between iniquitous and chosen vessel; (2) ritualistic division — alienation of the shared element onto the vessel; (3) new merger — purified community defined in dialectical opposition to the sacrificial offering. The scapegoat is consubstantial with persecutors. Hitlerite Anti-Semitism is the cleanest negative example; Socrates as scapegoat of transcendence is the closing application.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-09
- **centrality**: core

#### 45. Temporizing of essence
- **canonical_name**: Temporizing of essence
- **definition**: The pun on sequence: the logically prior can be expressed as the temporally prior, and vice versa. Essence becomes narrated as a 'going back' to origins (Freud's primal horde, Plato's Meno-recollection, original sin); historicist and essentialist vocabularies are convertible. Translation rule: when an apparently empirical-historical claim is discredited as fact, ask whether it was really a statement about essence. Ibsen's Peer Gynt is GM-09's worked case.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-09
- **centrality**: core

#### 46. Socratic transcendence (Upward Way)
- **canonical_name**: Socratic transcendence (Upward Way)
- **definition**: The upward dialectical move: a new motive discovered en route; the position at the end is reached by transformation, and the position at the start can only retrospectively be seen in light of the new motivation. Burke's signature reading: a Platonic dialogue is an ACT of transcendence (Phaedrus' five levels), not just a plea for one. Plotinus's journey 'to the Primal-Principle' is the mystical instance; Korzybski's higher-and-higher levels of generalization is the modern semantic instance.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-07, GM-09
- **centrality**: core

#### 47. Pentad overlap / common ground / margins of transformability
- **canonical_name**: Pentad overlap / common ground / margins of transformability
- **definition**: The five pentad terms participate in a common ground or substance, whose role as their causal ancestor makes them transformable into one another at every point where their fields overlap. The 'great central moltenness' / alchemic alembic figure (GM-00): distinctions arise from a central moltenness where all is merged. The fingers-and-palm metaphor: terms distinct at the extremities merge in the palm of the hand. This pliancy is what licenses grand-style metaphysical reduction-to-one (being / action / motion as titular over-all terms).
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-00, GM-02
- **centrality**: important

#### 48. Ambiguity as resource (not defect)
- **canonical_name**: Ambiguity as resource (not defect)
- **definition**: Burke's methodological signature: where logical positivism and symbolic logic seek terms free of ambiguity, the Grammar wants terms that clearly reveal the strategic spots at which ambiguities necessarily arise, because the areas of ambiguity are exactly where transformations among the terms take place. Ambiguities are alchemic opportunities, not defects.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-00
- **centrality**: important

#### 49. Consistent / compensatory points of departure
- **canonical_name**: Consistent / compensatory points of departure
- **definition**: A pentad term used as 'principle' admits two opposite developmental directions. Consistent: derive consequences from the term's nature ('reasons by a therefore'). Compensatory: depart toward the term's near-antithesis ('reasons by a however'). Lets a thinker keep a god-term while diverging from it ('naturalism' begun as man-in-nature becoming name for technology-and-money).
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-02
- **centrality**: important

#### 50. God-term
- **canonical_name**: God-term
- **definition**: The over-all summarizing term in a vocabulary — the universal title to which all less general terms are related as parts to whole. 'Being,' 'God,' 'money,' 'freedom' are Burke's leading examples. Designates 'the ultimate motivation, or substance, of a Constitutional frame' (GM-08). Faraday's unified-energy formula as 'perfect god-term' / Title of Titles (GM-09).
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-03, GM-08, GM-09
- **centrality**: important

#### 51. Ground (background / reason double-usage)
- **canonical_name**: Ground (background / reason double-usage)
- **definition**: The philosophical term 'ground' carries the double sense of background (scene) and reason (cause). Hegelian idealism exploits this by positing Reason as the Grundprinzip — at once mover of history and substance of which history is made. Leveraged in GM-10's reading of the theological controversy about whether God willed the good because it is good, or the good is good because God willed it.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-01, GM-10
- **centrality**: important

#### 52. Pathetic fallacy / motion-action ambiguity (extended)
- **canonical_name**: Pathetic fallacy / motion-action ambiguity (extended)
- **definition**: Ruskin's term (attribution of human emotions to non-human objects: 'the trees were gay, the mountains mourned') interpreted by Burke as tracking the scene-agent (or LYRIC) ratio. Burke extends to cover the broader 'action-motion ambiguity' (motion-personalization-as-act): 'the wheat tossed in the wind' is motion; 'the wheat tossed its head in the wind' is action. The watershed moment dividing dramatism from operationalism.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-06
- **centrality**: important

#### 53. Poiema / pathema / mathema (tragic-dialectic triad)
- **canonical_name**: Poiema / pathema / mathema (tragic-dialectic triad)
- **definition**: Burke's Greek-grammar triplet of action / suffering / learning. Greek proverb ta pathemata mathemata ('the suffered is the learned'). The tragic pattern: action (poiema) calls forth a counter-assertion from its context that the agent suffers (pathema); from the suffered comes knowledge (mathema) that transcends the original act. Christian martyrdom: act-of-self-sacrifice identical with sufferance; pathema (temptation) + poiema (resistance) + mathema (understanding) = martyrdom as new action-passion level.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-02, GM-06
- **centrality**: important

#### 54. Edenic paradigm (total acts producing total transformations)
- **canonical_name**: Edenic paradigm (total acts producing total transformations)
- **definition**: If an agent acts in keeping with his nature (act-agent), changing his scene (scene-act), and likening agent to scene (scene-agent), the result is a state of mutual conformity — an Edenic paradigm applicable only to total acts producing total transformations. Theological notions of creation and re-creation approach this limit (Striegel's reading of Christ's work on the Cross; Aeschylus' Erinyes becoming Eumenides).
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-01
- **centrality**: peripheral

#### 55. Demonic trinity (erotic + urinary + excremental cluster)
- **canonical_name**: Demonic trinity (erotic + urinary + excremental cluster)
- **definition**: Burke's image-cluster for the cloacal-mystical: three bodily principles vicariously substitutable for one another (rain ↔ urination ↔ erotic-purpose); 'thinking of the body' as condition of poetic transcendence; ecclesia super cloacam. Mystic thoroughness requires recognition of fundamental taboos at the very moment of their transcendence.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-07
- **centrality**: peripheral

#### 56. Dissolution of drama (four ways)
- **canonical_name**: Dissolution of drama (four ways)
- **definition**: The dramatistic perspective contains a point at which it abolishes itself in its own enunciation. Four canonical paths: (1) drama dissolves into lyric state; (2) action reduced to motion (scientism); (3) philosophies OF dramatism — Burke's own move — coordinates derived from drama yet used non-dramatically; (4) philosophies of super-drama — monotheistic god as super-person becomes impersonal.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: GM-09
- **centrality**: peripheral
