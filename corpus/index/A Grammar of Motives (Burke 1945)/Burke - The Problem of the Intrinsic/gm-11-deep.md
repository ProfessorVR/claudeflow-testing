## (i) Overview

Burke's second appendix essay opens by addressing a polemic — the Chicago Neo-Aristotelian critique of "Coleridgean" criticism, mounted by R. S. Crane, Norman Maclean, and Elder Olson in three coordinated *University Review* essays — and ends by reframing that polemic as a deeper methodological problem internal to dramatism itself: the problem of how to specify what counts as "the work itself" versus its environing context. The local target is a debate about lyric analysis; the load-bearing payload is a generalized methodological clarification of the intrinsic/extrinsic distinction that has been working through the *Grammar* since GM-02 (the substance vocabulary) and GM-03 (circumference and scope-of-scene). The appendix is the volume's most explicit methodological meta-statement on how the pentad apparatus relates to other critical methods, and on how the choice of scene-scope underwrites every other choice an analyst makes.

The essay's structure is five unlabeled roman-numeral sections. Section I introduces three levels of motive-talk (Rhetoric, Symbolic, Grammar) using the doctrine of the "eternity of art" as a test case, and identifies the grammatical reading as the entry-point into the intrinsic problem. Section II runs a four-stage historical dialectic — Aristotle, Aquinas, Spinoza, modern science — showing that the very meaning of "intrinsic" has been redrawn at each stage until it has nearly inverted into its opposite. Section III turns to Crane's prefatory note and dismantles the inductive/deductive opposition on which the Neo-Aristotelian polemic rests. Section IV runs Burke's own dramatistic counter-analysis of Maclean's Wordsworth-sonnet reading using the scene-agent ratio, and introduces the load-bearing distinction between rest-as-cessation-of-motion and rest-as-end-of-action. Section V concedes that Olson's actual analysis of Yeats is dramatistic in practice, develops the lyric-event versus dramatic-act distinction, and resolves the whole methodological tangle by proposing that the poem be treated as a constitutive act surviving as an objective structure — a framing that licenses both intrinsic-pole (internal-structure) and extrinsic-pole (act-scene ratio) analyses as complementary dramatistic modes.

## (ii) Intrinsic/extrinsic as scope-of-text question

Burke's central reframing is that the intrinsic/extrinsic distinction is not a primitive ontological cut between text and context but a scene-act ratio meta-question about scope of scene. Every critical method, whether or not it acknowledges this, tacitly draws a line between what it will count as "the work itself" (the intrinsic scene) and what it will count as merely "environing context" (the extrinsic scene). The choice of where to draw the line is a grammatical commitment with rhetorical and symbolic consequences, and the choice is historically variable.

The argument runs in four historical stages. (1) Aristotle anchors intrinsicality in *ousia* (being) and the *entelechy* (that which contains its own aim). A being's principle of motion and perfection is internal — but Burke's careful reading insists that this internality is located in the genus, not in bare individuality. The intrinsic principles of a being are variations of principles common to the whole family or *genos*; the world itself is eternal, and there is no creator-ground "behind" the order of beings. The intrinsic-scope is small (the individual qua kind-member) but tight. (2) Aquinas inherits Aristotelian *ousia* but Christian Genesis forces him to engraft an extrinsic ground onto the apparatus: substance is now constituted both by the creature's intrinsic principles (abilities, habits) and by the creature's derivation from God (God and the Devil as extrinsic motives). Burke flags in a footnote the productive ambiguity here — an ancestral God who has descended his substance into the creature is not wholly extrinsic; the logical completion of Aquinas's framework would be pantheism, and Aquinas resists this completion by explicitly classifying God as extrinsic. (3) Spinoza takes Aristotle's "in itself" criterion (Spinoza's *id quod per se concipitur*) at its word and applies it rigorously. The result is dialectically explosive: any finite being is defined, limited, and negated by its surroundings; only the totality of being meets the criterion for genuine intrinsicality. Locke's parallel etymological observation — that "substance" originally names that which supports a thing from outside — reinforces the near-reversal. The most intrinsic statement we can now make about a thing is a statement about its place in the whole world; intrinsic and extrinsic have nearly traded meanings. (4) Once Spinoza's theological side of the equation is dropped, the totality-criterion collapses into secular historicism, and modern empirical science (par excellence the approach "from without") formally abolishes the substance category. The gerontologist's promise to increase the body's "intrinsic resistance" by chemical dosage is Burke's reductio: chemical dosage is extrinsic agency, and insofar as the intervention works it may do so by decreasing rather than increasing intrinsic resistance — parallel to "scientific" warming-by-heating rather than warming-by-cold-tolerance. Andras Angyal's *Foundations for a Science of Personality* is the limit case: a science of personality must translate the dramatist concept "personality" into non-personal organism/environment terms, becoming necessarily extrinsic and thereby self-undermining as a "science of personality" in any non-incongruous sense.

The methodological pay-off is that the question of intrinsicality is not "what is internal to the work?" — that question presupposes a settled scope. The real question is "what scene-boundary does the analyst draw?" Three answers are stable: (a) the work as an individual qua kind-member (Aristotelian); (b) the work as a creature of an authorial creator (Aquinian, with the pantheist instability built in); (c) the work as a moment in a totalizing context (Spinozistic, sliding into historicism). A fourth option — the work as a constitutive act surviving as object — is Burke's dramatistic alternative, developed in Section V.

## (iii) Burke's examples

Burke deploys six worked examples to anchor the methodology. Three are literary: Wordsworth's sonnet "It is a beauteous evening," handled at length via the scene-agent ratio; a passage from Wordsworth's "Tintern Abbey," handled via the action/motion distinction; and Keats's "Ode on a Grecian Urn," handled via the attitude-as-arrested-act framework. Two are critical-historical: the Chicago Neo-Aristotelian programme itself, and Yvor Winters's pseudo-reference diagnostic illustrated on Eliot's "Gerontion." One is methodological-diagnostic: the contrast between contemplating one Station of the Cross (lyric) and traversing all fourteen Stations (dramatic). A seventh, Andras Angyal's *Foundations for a Science of Personality*, anchors the extrinsic-method limit case.

The Wordsworth sonnet is the central case. Maclean reads the poem as a three-part structure — octave, mid-line turn, sestet — producing a "spiritual and religious translation of the evening." Burke approves the analysis and the particularity but argues it would be sharper if explicitly framed as a scene-agent ratio. The octave establishes the scene-quality (calm, holy, tranquil, with the broad sun sinking and the gentleness of heaven brooding); the sestet introduces the agent (the Child walking with the speaker); the agent's quality is so imbued with the divine quality of the containing scene that she possesses this quality unawares, by the simple fact of having it as her ground. This is the methodological signature of Burke's scene-agent ratio: scene-quality contains agent-quality, just as in drama the scene-quality contains the act-quality (Shakespearean storm or darkness as setting for sinister act; Hardy's landscape as source of motivation). The lyric, having no overt action, instead identifies agent and scene via mood-imagery.

The "Tintern Abbey" passage lets Burke run his action/motion distinction. "The motion of our human blood" is bodily motion, suspended while "a living soul" — mental action — is attained; "the power of harmony" is another synonym for the rest of potential action. The state of arrest is the level of mental action transcending the level of bodily motion. The Grecian Urn ode is glossed as a parallel split: bodily passion versus mental action, agitation arrested into its transcendent counterpart, with the Urn's "fair Attitude" as the summational moment.

The Stations of the Cross diagnostic is structurally important: concern with the totality of the fourteen-Station progression is dramatic; pause-and-contemplation at any one Station, in itself, is lyric. The distinction is not between different art-objects but between different acts of attention to the same object — which means the dramatic/lyric distinction is itself a scope-of-scene choice, parallel to the intrinsic/extrinsic choice.

## (iv) Relation to circumference and substance vocabulary

GM-11 is the methodological complement of GM-03 (Scope and Reduction) and GM-02 (Antinomies of Definition). GM-03 develops the concept of circumference: every scene-statement is a circumference-choice (the act might be circumscribed within the household, the city, the nation, the universe, the cosmos), and the choice of circumference determines what counts as scene and what counts as background-of-scene. GM-11 generalizes circumference from spatial-temporal scenic scope to text-scope: the choice of intrinsic-boundary is a circumference-choice applied to the text itself. The Aristotelian intrinsic-scope (individual as kind-member) is a tight circumference; the Spinozistic intrinsic-scope (totality of being) is a maximally wide circumference; modern science's intrinsic-scope (the category abolished) is no circumference at all. Burke's dramatistic resolution (poem as constitutive act surviving as object) is a circumference set at the boundary of the act qua enactment, with the surrounding scene admitted at the act-scene ratio level rather than absorbed into the intrinsic-scope.

GM-02's substance vocabulary develops a typology of substance-distinctions, including the contextual or directional substance — the substance of a thing constituted by what surrounds it. GM-11 historicizes this substance-distinction by showing that the intrinsic/extrinsic application of the directional substance is itself unstable, and offers the dramatistic resolution that substance is best treated neither as wholly intrinsic (Aristotelian) nor wholly directional (Spinozistic-modern-scientific) but as the surviving-constitution of an act — the act's structure as objectively examinable plus the act's enactment-in-scene as scenically grounded.

These three chapters together form a methodological core. GM-03 sets up the circumference machinery for scene-talk; GM-02 sets up the substance machinery for being-talk; GM-11 unifies them by showing that every analytic method's choice of intrinsic-scope is simultaneously a circumference-choice (about scope of scene) and a substance-choice (about whether substance is internal or directional). The cross-link is what makes the intrinsic/extrinsic apparatus methodologically generalizable beyond literary criticism.

## (v) Implications for textual analysis

Several decision rules can be extracted from the essay. First: if an analysis treats only the relation of parts to one another and to the whole, with no reference outside the work, it is operating intrinsically — in being-terms, *sub specie aeternitatis*. This is legitimate and useful but insufficient for full motive-analysis of the human realm. Second: if an analysis reduces the work to its conditions of production (biographical, sociological, ideological, economic, neurotic, dietary, climatic), it is operating extrinsically — legitimate but liable to neglect the structure of the act itself. Burke's quip that the New Critical and Chicago objection to extrinsicism is correct as far as it goes — recent science has indeed offered the poem as "exemplar" of political exigencies, neurosis, physique, diet, climate, cultural movements, and economic classifications — anchors the legitimate intrinsic-pole concern.

Third: if an analysis uses motion-vocabulary (cause/effect, stimulus/response, function/environment), it cannot yield statements about the intrinsic. Motion-talk produces only extrinsic statements about human affairs because motion belongs to the physical realm and action to the human realm. Fourth: if an analysis uses action-vocabulary (act/scene/agent/agency/purpose, attitude, motive), it can address the intrinsic, but only by treating the work as a constitutive act whose enactment survives as an object. Fifth: an ostensibly inductive analysis that risks no prospective generalizations is prophecy-after-the-event. Real induction must specify what to expect from the class and why, not merely improvise translations of unique poems into unique conceptual paraphrases. Burke's diagnostic that Aristotelianism has historically been prone to casuistic rationalization-after-the-fact is an internal warning to dramatism itself, not only to the Chicago school.

Sixth, and most generative: single-work intrinsicality is insufficient. The terms of one poem are qualified by their use in other poems by the same poet; individual works are stages or stations of a more comprehensive act, and statements about that more-comprehensive-act are also statements about each work's intrinsic nature. This deflates the sui-generis dogma without abandoning attention to the individual work, and provides the dramatistic warrant for oeuvre-level critical analysis (which is also the methodological signature of GM-12, the Marianne Moore appendix immediately following).

Seventh, the dramatistic resolution: poem as constitutive act surviving as object. Crane's dichotomy of poem-as-exemplar versus poem-as-object suppresses the third option of poem-as-act. The pentad pries it open. The poem is enacted by a poet in a particular temporal scene and survives as an objective structure examinable in other temporal scenes by other agents. Internal-structure analysis (intrinsic-pole) treats the surviving constitution; act-scene ratio analysis (extrinsic-pole) treats the poem's enactment in its original and subsequent scenes. Both are dramatistic; neither is dispensable.

Eighth, the closing warning: pure-intrinsic criticism, logically completed, demands separated forms without admixture of matter — Aquinas's angels each being both genus and individual at once. The Neo-Aristotelians' simultaneous commitment to lyric uniqueness and lyric-as-kind tracks this Scotist *haecceitas* pattern, perhaps as an unconscious return to scholasticism by way of modern empirical science. Burke's diagnosis is that the search for the intrinsic, if pursued to its completion, becomes an angelology of poems — pure forms subsisting without admixture of the matter of scenic ground.

## (vi) Dissertation deployment: game-as-text and the scope-of-text question

The intrinsic/extrinsic apparatus of GM-11 is the most directly portable methodological frame in the entire *Grammar* for the dissertation's game-as-text problem. The question "what counts as the game itself?" is precisely Burke's scope-of-text question, and the historical instability of "intrinsic" across Aristotle/Aquinas/Spinoza/modern-science maps onto the game-studies field's parallel instability across candidate intrinsic-scopes for games.

Five candidate intrinsic-scopes for a game can be enumerated, each tracking one of Burke's historical stages. (a) **The executable software artifact alone** — the rules, systems, assets, and code as shipped — is the Aristotelian individual-as-kind-member intrinsic-scope. The game's *entelechy* is the rule-system's internal logic; what counts as "the game" is what counts as "this code in this build." This is the formalist/ludological intrinsic-scope (Aarseth, early Bogost; cybertext as a formal object). Its limitation: it ignores designer intent, player practice, modding, livestream culture, and historical reception. (b) **The software plus designer intent** — paratext, design documents, GDC talks, post-mortems, developer interviews — is the Aquinian intrinsic-balanced-with-extrinsic scope. The creator-ground is engrafted onto the artifact-intrinsic. This is the auteurist intrinsic-scope. Its limitation: the Aquinian ambiguity recurs — is the designer wholly extrinsic, or partly internalized as the artifact's creator-substance? (Authorial-intent debates inherit this ambiguity directly.) (c) **The software plus player-community practice** — metagame, speedrun routes, social norms, common builds, viable strategies — extends the scene-agent ratio: the game's being is partly constituted by agent-comportment within it. This is the practice-turn intrinsic-scope (Calleja's PIM; Sicart on play; community-of-practice ethnography). Its limitation: it blurs into Spinozistic totalism if extended to include all player communities and the wider cultural scene. (d) **The software plus livestream/streaming ecosystem** — Twitch chat, YouTube let's-plays, Discord servers, fan wikis, esports broadcasts — approaches the Spinozistic totality criterion. The game becomes its full scenic ground. The intrinsic dissolves into the extrinsic; the game becomes coextensive with its culture. (e) **Modder-modified versions and mod ecosystems** — Skyrim with mods, the Half-Life mod scene, the Minecraft modpack ecosystem — instantiates the more-comprehensive-act-of-stages framework: individual mods are stations of a larger act of which the base game is the originary enactment. This requires the corpus-level methodology Burke argues for against sui-generis.

The dramatistic resolution for games is the game-as-act framework, analogous to Burke's poem-as-constitutive-act-surviving-as-object. The game is a constitutive act enacted by designers in a particular temporal scene (the development period, the studio, the production conditions) and survives as an objective structure (the shipped artifact) examinable in other temporal scenes by other agents (players in various historical, cultural, and technical contexts). Each playthrough is the game's re-enactment by new agents in new temporal scenes. The artifact survives as constitution. Intrinsic analysis treats the surviving constitution — the rule-system, the design grammar, the procedural rhetoric of the shipped object. Extrinsic analysis treats the re-enactment — the player's incorporation, the community's metagame, the streamer's performance, the modder's intervention. Both are dramatistic; neither is dispensable; and the false choice between formalist game studies (intrinsic-only) and contextualist game studies (extrinsic-only) dissolves.

Three further moves are directly portable. The two-senses-of-rest distinction sharpens game-studies analyses that conflate cessation-of-motion with end-of-action: engagement-metric studies that treat input-frequency (motion) as if it were act-of-playing (action) are running motion-vocabulary on action-vocabulary subject-matter — a category error parallel to the Andras-Angyal-style science-of-personality. The attitude-as-incipient-act-or-substitute-for-act category, anchoring the *Rhetoric of Motives*' sixth pentad term (Attitude), is directly portable to game-user comportment: many game-affordances elicit attitude without enacting (the held-but-not-pressed button; the camera-pan toward a quest-marker the player chooses not to pursue; the menu-open contemplation; the "thinking face" before a build-decision in a roguelike). Attitude-as-incipient-act maps onto Calleja's affective-involvement dimension; attitude-as-substitute maps onto the spectator-mode/let's-play viewer who absorbs the game's attitude-purveying imagery without enacting it. The comprehensive-act-of-stages framework licenses treating a developer's oeuvre — FromSoftware's Souls lineage, Nintendo's Mario lineage, an indie auteur's catalog, a long-running live-service title's seasonal-content history — as a more-comprehensive-act of which individual titles or seasons are stations; statements about the comprehensive act are also statements about each title's intrinsic nature.
