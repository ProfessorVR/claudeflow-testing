## (i) Chapter overview

Burke's Introduction is the load-bearing master statement of *dramatism*. In nine pages of continuous prose, with no titled subsections and only paragraph-break transitions for structure, Burke opens the trilogy of *Motives* by naming the five terms that will organise every subsequent page: *Act, Scene, Agent, Agency, Purpose*. He immediately offers them as the "generating principle" of the investigation, glosses each by the interrogative it answers (what / when-or-where / who / how / why), and insists that any rounded motive-statement must answer all five questions. He then states the methodological signature that distinguishes the Grammar from logical-positivist or symbolic-logical projects: dramatism wants terms that *reveal* the spots at which ambiguity necessarily arises rather than terms that abolish ambiguity, because ambiguities are exactly the loci of transformation among the five terms. He gives two metaphors for that transformability — the wakes of two launches resolving into a single generating-principle of design (book p. xvi) and the fingers merging into the palm of the hand (book p. xxii) — together with a third, the alchemic "great central moltenness" out of which distinctions arise as congealed surface-crusts (book p. xix). He demonstrates pentad-substitution via a series of worked micro-cases (war, voting, machines, the body in portraiture versus materialistic medicine) and via the fully-worked hero-villain-co-agent-file fable that demonstrates four casuistic locations (agent / scene / co-agent / counter-agent) for the same act. He distinguishes a Grammar of motives (concerned with the terms alone — the terministic substrate that may claim universal validity) from philosophic idioms (each particular school's casuistic filling-in of the grammatical principles — open to question, varying with cultural situation). And he closes with the programmatic claim that the subject of motivation is a *philosophic* one, not ultimately to be solved in terms of empirical science. The chapter is the canonical source from which every later deliverable in this pipeline — the Pentad Operationalization Manual, the ratio catalog, the substance vocabulary, the philosophic-school mapping — inherits its vocabulary and its motivating distinctions.

## (ii) The pentad — five terms in full

### Act ("What was done?")

Burke defines *Act* as the term naming what took place, in thought or deed. It is the action-content of any motive-statement, and it admits both subdivision (collective acts subdivisible into individual acts) and metaphysical foregrounding as the titular term of an entire philosophic idiom. Burke gives the example of the hero's bond-breaking in the fable (book p. xx), where the act-of-bond-breaking is the central referent that all four casuistic locations re-locate the motive of. He then gives war as a collective Act subdivisible into many individual acts (p. xx). And in the pragmatist section he notes that Mead "called his pragmatism a philosophy of the act" (p. xxi) — a remarkable case in which an agency-foregrounding philosophy (Mead's instrumentalism) nonetheless takes Act as its titular ancestor. The question this term answers is "what was done?"; this is the term whose substantive depth Burke will undertake exegetical work on in Part 2.III (GM-06), via the Aristotelian/Thomistic vocabulary of substance and actus/potentia.

### Scene ("When or where was it done?")

*Scene* is the blanket name for the background or situation in which an act occurred. Burke explicitly designates it a "blanket term for the concept of background or setting in general, a name for any situation in which acts or agents are placed" (book p. xvi). It is also the term in whose blank Burke first demonstrates the grammar-vs-idiom distinction: one thinker uses "God" as his term for the ultimate Scene; another uses "nature"; another uses "environment," "history," or "means of production" (p. xvii). Examples Burke offers in the Intro: the room of confinement in the hero-file fable (p. xx); the industrial scene constituted by the vast accumulation of machines (p. xx, simultaneous with their being Agencies in their individual function); war as Scene for the inducted soldier — the situation motivating the nature of his training (p. xx); the body as scenic "purely objective material" in materialistic medicine (p. xx, simultaneous with its being Agent-property in portraiture). The question this term answers is "when or where was it done?"; the philosophic school Part 2.I (GM-04) will diagnose as Scene-featuring is materialism.

### Agent ("Who did it?")

*Agent* names the person or kind of person who performed the act. Burke gives the term immediate analytic depth in two directions. First, it admits subdivision into *co-agents* (friends who modify and so partly motivate the act) and *counter-agents* (enemies whose opposition is its precondition); both subtypes are demonstrated in the hero-file fable. Second, it gathers under itself the personal properties assigned motivational value: "ideas, the will, fear, malice, intuition, the creative imagination" (book p. xx). Examples in the Intro: the hero of the fable (p. xx); the voter alongside voting as act (p. xx); the war-god as super-agent in mythology (p. xx); the body as agent-property in portraiture (an expression of personality, p. xx). The question this term answers is "who did it?"; the philosophic school Part 2.II (GM-05) will diagnose as Agent-featuring is idealism, extending into Marxism via the class-as-agent reading.

### Agency ("How did he do it?")

*Agency* names the means or instruments by which the agent performed the act. Burke gives it immediate operational reach via the file in the fable (the file as the means of bond-breaking, book p. xx), then expands it via the pragmatist nesting at p. xxi: the file is an instrument, the hand holding the file is an instrument, the brain guiding the hand is an instrument, and the educational system that taught the methods and shaped the values is also an instrument. The pragmatist reduction of motivation to agency thus nests instruments within instruments without limit. Burke also names machines as obvious Agencies — though in their vast accumulation they constitute the industrial Scene, demonstrating once again the pentad-overlap. The question this term answers is "how did he do it?" or "by what means?"; the philosophic school Part 2.IV (GM-07, first half) will diagnose as Agency-featuring is pragmatism. Burke names Dewey and Mead explicitly in the Intro (p. xxi), with Dewey paraphrased on intelligence-as-instrument embodied in scientific method, and Dewey's "experience" / "nature" identified as his act/scene equivalents — with the proviso that Dewey emphasises overlap-between-them via "the idea of continuity" (the quoted phrase from Dewey's *Intelligence and the Modern World*, book p. xxi-xxii).

### Purpose ("Why?")

*Purpose* names the end for the sake of which the act was performed. Burke notes immediately that this is the term most subject to violent disagreement among motive-attributors: "men may violently disagree about the purposes behind a given act, or about the character of the person who did it, or how he did it, or in what kind of situation he acted" (book p. xv) — and Purpose heads the list. Examples in the Intro: the hero's purpose of escape, served by the act of bond-breaking (p. xx); war as Purpose in "schemes proclaiming a cult of war" (p. xx); "love of freedom" as an agent-internal property that can also be read as the purpose of escape (p. xx, the recoding example). The question this term answers is "why?"; the philosophic school Part 2.IV (GM-07, second half) will diagnose as Purpose-featuring is mysticism, with Plotinus and Bonaventura as paradigm interlocutors. The Intro previews this via the heretical-Christian case at p. xxi (Judas-worship: betrayal-as-precondition-of-Crucifixion-as-precondition-of-redemption) — a counter-agent location whose intelligibility nonetheless depends on a cosmic-Purpose ordering.

## (iii) Pentad grammar

Three metaphors carry the Intro's account of how the five terms relate.

**The wakes-of-launches metaphor** (book p. xvi). Burke recalls an aerial photograph from the *Road to Victory* exhibit at the Museum of Modern Art: two launches proceed side by side on a tranquil sea, their wakes crossing and recrossing in "almost an infinity of lines." Despite the intricacy of the tracery, the picture conveys an impression of great simplicity, because one immediately perceives the generating principle of its design. The pentad, used as a generating principle, ideally provides "a kind of simplicity that can be developed into considerable complexity, and yet can be discovered beneath its elaborations." This metaphor anchors Burke's structural claim: the five terms are not five static categories but a generative engine whose permutations and combinations produce the full variety of human motive-attribution. The intricacy is real; the simplicity is also real; the achievement is in seeing both at once.

**The fingers-and-palm metaphor** (book p. xxii). Burke gives a second figure for pentad-relationality, this one specifying *how* the terms transform into one another. The five terms are like fingers, distinct at their extremities but merging in the palm of the hand. To go from one finger to another without a leap, "you need but trace the tendon down into the palm of the hand, and then trace a new course along another tendon." The figure makes pentad-transformability mechanically intelligible: every transition is a descent to the common substrate and a re-emergence on a different terminal. Burke uses this metaphor to explain, immediately, how "no great dialectical enterprise is necessary if you would merge the terms, reducing them even to as few as one." The grand-style metaphysical method — bringing a doctrine to a head in some over-all titular term (being-in-general, action-in-general, motion-in-general, development-in-general, experience-in-general) and then "deducing" the others from it across margins of overlap — exploits exactly this fingers-and-palm structure. Dramatism makes the structure explicit while refusing to causally privilege any one finger as the palm's true source.

**The great-central-moltenness / alchemic-alembic metaphor** (book p. xix). The most generative of the three. "Distinctions, we might say, arise out of a great central moltenness, where all is merged. They have been thrown from a liquid center to the surface, where they have congealed. Let one of these crusted distinctions return to its source, and in this alchemic center it may be remade, again becoming molten liquid, and may enter into new combinations, whereat it may be again thrown forth as a new crust, a different distinction." The figure does three things at once. First, it gives an ontology of distinctions: they are emergent precipitates from a fluid substrate, not primitives. Second, it gives a method: to transform A into non-A, one does not leap, but descends to "the ground of its existence, the logical substance that is its causal ancestor, and on to a point where it is consubstantial with non-A; then we may return, this time emerging with non-A instead." This is the Intro's seed of the consubstantiation apparatus that will be developed fully in *A Rhetoric of Motives* (1950) as identification. Third, it gives a justification for the ambiguity-as-resource methodological signature: ambiguity is exactly the *moltenness* in which terms are still consubstantial; without ambiguity, transformation would be impossible.

The fourth and most consequential structural feature of the pentad-grammar is **the universal-validity-vs-philosophic-idiom distinction** (book pp. xvi-xvii). A statement of the grammatical principles of motivation may lay claim to universal validity / complete certainty: every coherent motive-statement *must* reference some background (Scene), some actor (Agent), some means (Agency), some end (Purpose), and some action-content (Act). This is a necessary-condition claim about intelligibility, not a contentful ontological claim. But the choice of any one philosophic idiom to fill in those grammatical principles — God or nature or environment or history or means-of-production for Scene — is open to question, varies with cultural situation, and is what arguments over motivation are really about. The two-level structure is what gives the *Grammar* its title and its analytic posture: a grammar describes the rules under which statements get made, not the empirical truth of what is stated. Burke insists, in a striking concession at p. xvii, that "even a cultural situation extending over centuries is a 'case,' and would probably require a much different philosophic idiom as its temporizing calculus of motives than would be required in the case of other cultural situations." Philosophic idioms are not eternal; they are temporizing calculi for cases.

## (iv) Ratio seeds

Although the full development of the ten dyadic ratios is reserved for GM-01 (Container and Thing Contained), all ten are seeded in this Introduction by the worked examples Burke deploys. The seeds:

- **Scene-act** is seeded by the hero-file fable's option-(ii) casuistic location: "nothing is surer to awaken thoughts of escape in a man than a condition of imprisonment" (book p. xx) — situation determining act.
- **Scene-agent** is seeded by the war-as-Scene-for-inducted-soldier example (the situation motivates the nature of the agent's training, p. xx) and by the industrial-scene-of-accumulated-machines case (the scene's motivational properties shape the agents within it).
- **Scene-agency** is seeded by the machines-as-agency-yet-also-industrial-scene case (p. xx): the scene determines which instruments are available; instruments in accumulation constitute scene.
- **Scene-purpose** is seeded by the heretical-Judas case at p. xxi: the cosmic-Scene of providence reframes the act of betrayal as ordered toward divine Purpose, a mystical-Scene-Purpose ordering.
- **Act-agent** is seeded by the hero-file fable's option-(i) casuistic location: "love of freedom" as a personality-trait integral to the agent — the act-of-escape predicated on the agent's character (p. xx).
- **Act-agency** is seeded by the file-as-agency-of-bond-breaking pairing (p. xx); the pragmatist nesting of file/hand/brain/educational-system at p. xxi extends this seed indefinitely.
- **Act-purpose** is seeded explicitly: "break his bonds (act) in order to escape (purpose)" (p. xx) — the chapter's clearest act-purpose pairing.
- **Agent-agency** is seeded by voting-as-politician's-agency (p. xx): the voters themselves are agents at one register and agency at another; agent-agency interchange.
- **Agent-purpose** is seeded by the gathering of personal properties under Agent (will, malice, intuition, creative imagination at p. xx) — these are motive-laden properties at the agent-internal register.
- **Agency-purpose** is seeded explicitly via Dewey at p. xxi: intelligence as instrument (agency) embodied in scientific method — the means proportioned to the end of inquiry.

All ten ratios thus have textual seeds in the Intro; GM-01 will develop them into operational tools, with Ibsen's *Enemy of the People* and O'Neill's *Mourning Becomes Electra* as the paradigm worked-cases.

## (v) The grammatical-not-empirical claim

The chapter's closing two pages (book pp. xxii-xxiii) deliver the programmatic statement that anchors every subsequent Phase 2 chapter and every Phase 3 deliverable. Burke names his method "dramatism" — a titular word, deliberately chosen — because dramatism invites one to consider motives in a perspective developed from the analysis of drama, "treating language and thought primarily as modes of action." This is the foundational ontological commitment of the method. Then he specifies the method as *synoptic* but explicitly not in the historical sense. A purely historical survey of motive-theory would require "no less than a universal history of human culture," because every judgment, exhortation, admonition, every view of reality, every intention or expectation involves assumptions about motive. The synoptic move is instead structural: the *Grammar* offers "a system of placement," and should enable, "by the systematic manipulation of the terms, to 'generate,' or 'anticipate' the various classes of motivational theory." The reduction is synoptic *while still permitting* appreciation of scope and complexity. Burke is explicit that dialectical and metaphysical concerns are not being imported into a subject that might otherwise be free of them; rather, the work makes clear how dialectical and metaphysical issues necessarily figure in motivation. The chapter closes on the load-bearing assertion that "the subject of motivation is a philosophic one, not ultimately to be solved in terms of empirical science."

This closing claim positions the *Grammar* against four foils, all of which receive their negative-definitional treatment elsewhere in the chapter. (1) Against symbolic logic and logical positivism (named at p. xviii): the *Grammar* takes ambiguity as resource, not defect. (2) Against grand-style metaphysics (named at p. xxii): the *Grammar* makes the pentad-pliancy explicit and refuses to causally privilege any one term. (3) Against implicit-No-Metaphysics / "X is nothing but Y" unmasking (also p. xxii): the *Grammar* refuses the reduction to atomic constituent as much as the reduction to titular over-all term. (4) Against behaviorist stimulus-response motivational reduction (p. xx): the *Grammar* registers the behaviorist analysis as the under-act limit-case — voting cast without adequate knowledge of consequences may be "sheer motion (what the behaviorists would call a Response to a Stimulus)" — but treats this as a demotion rather than as the basic case.

## (vi) Cross-pipeline bridges

**Anticipatory: Calleja-PIM (the dissertation's reciprocal pipeline).** The five pentad terms map onto Calleja's six PIM dimensions in a five-to-six anticipatory projection that this Intro establishes the canonical vocabulary for. *Scene* maps to Spatial + Kinesthetic (the navigable virtual environment plus the macro-Scene of the player's physical room). *Act* maps to Ludic + Narrative + Kinesthetic (the action-content of choices, the event-structure of session, and the act-as-physical-input). *Agent* maps to Shared, with the dissertation-utility move of treating the player-avatar-character triple as a question of which agent-position holds at which moment (Burke's co-agent/counter-agent subdivision at p. xx is structurally homologous to the player-versus-NPC distinction). *Agency* maps to Kinesthetic-controls + Ludic-rules-as-means; the pragmatist nesting of file/hand/brain/educational-system at p. xxi is the proto-typical model for this mapping — the controller, the hand pressing it, the cognitive schema interpreting it, the rule-system that scaffolds the cognition are all instruments in a nested chain. *Purpose* maps to Ludic-goals + Narrative-arc + Affective-regulation; the violent-disagreement-over-purpose at p. xv anticipates the heterogeneity of player-purpose (mastery / flow / social / progression / affect-regulation) that the dissertation will need to disambiguate. None of these mappings is exegetical (Burke 1945 cannot cite Calleja 2011); all are anticipatory-projection.

**Exegetical-forward: Aristotle Complete Works + Phantasia Cluster + Bowin AMT.** The Intro previews via the substance-and-common-ground figure at p. xix — terms participate in a common substance — that Part 2.III (GM-06) will undertake the realism-features-Act exegesis of Aristotle and Aquinas. This is the pipeline's *only* exegetical cross-pipeline bridge to existing corpus indices. The Act-as-titular-term work of GM-06 will anchor directly in *energeia/dynamis* (Aristotle *Metaphysics* Theta, *Physics* III) and *actus/potentia* (Aquinas *Summa* I q.3). Within the Intro itself, the Aristotelian anticipation is present in (a) the common-substance figure (which maps to Aristotelian *ousia*) and (b) Burke's repeated use of the word "act" with proper-noun emphasis throughout — every occurrence of "Act" is already proto-Aristotelian.
