**Essay thesis (in Gross's conceptual register).** Heidegger relocates rhetoric at the heart of his fundamental ontology. The ten-term "rhetorical genealogy" of *Being and Time* — *Mitsein/koinōnia*, *Glaube/doxa*, *Lage/being-in*, *Sorge*, *Stimmung/pathos*, *Augenblick/kairos*, *ekstasis*, *Angst/phobos*, *logos*, *Entschluß/krisis* — is recoverable only by reading SS 1924 (*Grundbegriffe der aristotelischen Philosophie*, GA 18) as a *formative* moment rather than a digression, and that recovery shows pathos to be "the very substance in which propositional thought finds its objects and motivation" rather than a cynical addition to logos. Heidegger's German-stylistic experiment with the middle voice — variations on *bewegen*, the impersonal *es gibt*, the late *die Sprache spricht* — is the philosophical instrument by which the simultaneity of activity and passivity in *Miteinandersein* becomes expressible; it is *neither* the agency-dissolving voice of Derridian *différance* / Foucauldian-Bourdieuvian antihumanism *nor* the Habermasian-Kantian resubjectivized voice that would salvage it, but a third position: neo-Aristotelian, protocommunitarian, and rhetorically constituted.

## 2. Hierarchical Outline

### Opening (book pp. 1–5; PDF pp. 7–11)

The opening has no internal heading. It is anchored by an extended bilingual epigraph (Heidegger SS 1924, with German parallel) declaring rhetoric "the discipline in which the self-elaboration of *Dasein* is expressly executed... the hermeneutic of *Dasein* itself" (GA 18.110, n.1). The argumentative beats:

- The "Heidegger and rhetoric" question is "a story not yet adequately told" (p.1) despite the epigraphic conviction; Gross frames the volume as the telling.
- A double genealogy: Nietzsche's 1872 course on *History of Greek Eloquence* (n.3) and Heidegger's SS 1924 (per Löwith's transcript title "Aristoteles: Rhetorik II", n.4) jointly mark "the emergence of modern discourse theory" (p.2).
- The historiographical claim: SS 1924 is "a formative moment in Heidegger's lifelong project of grounding metaphysics" (p.2), citing Kisiel for the "direct *genetic* link" to *Being and Time* (n.6, n.7). The list of ten BT terms whose rhetorical genealogy the volume traces is given here — and BT 158 is cited as Heidegger's own admission that the concernful-understanding-to-theory dynamic was "worked out first in his elaboration of basic concepts in Aristotle's *Rhetoric*."
- The four substantive innovations Heidegger makes in the rhetorical tradition (pp.3–4): (1) tropology as *contour of contingency*, with the trope-concept dyad neither ornament nor "originary" Nietzschean device but constitutive of how language discloses possibility; (2) language as *discourse* rooted in shared moods and institutions, with Heidegger's later "art of listening" reversing the Cicero/Demosthenes "art of speaking" (citing *Unterwegs zur Sprache*); (3) the rhetorical-ontology thesis proper — "Heidegger thus relocates rhetoric at the heart of his fundamental ontology" (p.4, italicized) — humans as "simultaneously agent and patient, mover and moved (to use Aristotle's terminology)"; (4) pathos as ground (not supplement) of logos, with a theological genealogy from Augustine through the "Christian Grand Style of the Renaissance to Luther's Reformation theology" (p.4, n.8) — the genealogy Section IV will fully develop.
- The opening closes by naming the SS-1924-influenced cohort (Gadamer, Arendt, Brocker, Marcuse, Tugendhat) and posing the rupture question that frames the rest of the unit: "Why, finally, if so productive, does Heidegger drop rhetoric as the antidote to metaphysics? Why, in other words, do hermeneutics subsume rhetoric after 1924?" (p.5).

### Section I — "Hermeneutics or Rhetoric?" (book pp. 5–11; PDF pp. 11–17)

The section is critique-by-amplification: Gadamer's *Truth and Method* (and, secondarily, Arendt's *Human Condition*) are foils that simultaneously preserve and "depoliticize" Heidegger's rhetorical insight.

- The displacement of rhetoric by hermeneutics is "a political and ethical loss" that is "not absolute" but real (p.5). Hermeneutics solidifies a "formal and universal method" (gain) while rhetoric — "practiced in ad hoc fashion as a proactive art" — recedes (loss).
- *Truth and Method* read through Klaus Dockhorn's 1966 review in *Göttingische Gelehrte Anzeigen* 218 (n.8, n.9): Dockhorn prompted Gadamer's "rhetorical" second-edition revisions, but Gadamer "downplays the political loss" and one must trace "how the priority of pathos was depoliticized with the turn to hermeneutics" (p.6).
- Gadamer's genealogy of *sensus communis* via Vico (n.14, p.7) and Roman Stoic *phronēsis* — "manifest above all in the Aristotelian triad of politics, ethics, and rhetoric" (p.7) — was "emptied and intellectualized" by the Kantian Enlightenment (n.15) until Dilthey returned it as historicism (Heidegger's "fresh beginning," n.16). The "famous SS 1924 triad of the hermeneutic situation" (fore-having, fore-sight, fore-conception) "establish[es] the horizon in which our everyday doings and sayings can make sense" (n.17).
- The load-bearing critique of Gadamer (p.8): in claiming that "understanding" precedes "affect," Gadamer "cedes ground... to the very rationalists [Habermas, n.18] he sets out to criticize." Gadamer "chooses not to reclaim for rhetoric the doctrine of affections (*Affektenlehre*) as taught by the likes of Luther or Schleiermacher" (n.19). For Heidegger by contrast, "pathos provides the very ground for critical judgement (*krisis*), first moving the listener to be realized in some form" (p.9, n.23).
- The Heidegger-Arendt link tested against Dana Villa (n.25, n.26): Villa claims Arendt "spatializes" the authentic-inauthentic distinction against Heidegger's "lonely venue of uncanny works and poetic words." Gross counters: "SS 1924 proves Villa wrong"; the lectures show "an original Being-with obfuscated by *Being and Time* and practically invisible in Heidegger's later work" (p.10), and Arendt's "speech" is for all intents "Heideggerian/Aristotelian rhetoric." *Human Condition*: speech is "the actualization of the human condition of plurality" (n.27).
- The section's positive thesis (p.11): the rhetorical genres (judicial, deliberative; epideictic gets "short shrift," cf. Ch 4) each presuppose Being-with: "There can be no agent or patient, no speaker or hearer, unless one presumes a shared domain of past and potential meaning. And it is in Aristotle's *Rhetoric* that Being-with, *koinōnia*, is given its definitive form." Both Gadamer and Arendt left behind "critical elements... the priority of pathos first among them."

### Section II — "Physis, Polis, and Heidegger's Style" (book pp. 11–18; PDF pp. 17–24)

This is the most stylistically dense section: a sustained close-reading of GA 18 §§240–242 (with parallel German block-quote) showing that Heidegger's middle-voice German *style* is itself the philosophical instrument by which the *Naturwissenschaft/Geisteswissenschaft* distinction is dissolved.

- Aristotle's *Rhetoric* now read "alongside the Roman rhetorical works of Cicero and Quintilian" (n.30) and as "philosophic inquiry" (Garver, n.32). Heidegger preparing for this counter-thrust at GA 18.117: "Daß wir die aristotelische *Rhetorik* haben, ist besser, als wenn wir eine Sprachphilosophie hätten" — and at GA 18.136, the provocation that elicits Brocker's question mark of disbelief: "die Philosophen sind die rechten Sophisten."
- The methodological claim (p.12): Heidegger's holistic SS 1924 reading treats *Metaphysics, Politics, Nicomachean Ethics, Rhetoric, Topics, De anima, De motu animalium*, and *Physics* in concert. "*Pathos*, a key term in the rhetorical tradition, provides the transfer point between social and naturo-physical phenomena. And it is this pathos at the heart of Heidegger's ontology that grounds philosophy in a new way."
- McKeon's "sharp and unbreakable" *Naturwissenschaft/Geisteswissenschaft* distinction (n.35) is rejected. Heidegger reads Aristotelian categories holistically: "Being-with-one-another turns out to be only one way of being among many — living and nonliving, human and nonhuman" (p.13). The shared ontology of all Being is grounded in Aristotle's *Physics* categories (GA 18.284). Famous cosmological gloss: "The *pathos* of a stone allows it to become part of a wall, the *pathos* of a plant to grow, the *pathos* of an animal to perceive imminent danger and to shriek a warning to others." Human pathos is unique by *nous poiētikos* (*De anima* III) — the faculty "that allows us to extend into every domain of being and be moved even by things that are not there in body."
- Establishing *Sein-in-Bewegung* (the unit's titular concept): "Though only human being is moved to discourse, or *logos*, Being-moved is essential to all (*Sein-in-Bewegung*)." GA 18.226 cited in parallel German-English: pathos and *physikós* essentially bound; "Being-moved... necessarily exceeds the rational psyche because people have bodies of a certain sort." The thesis of "embodied philosophy at its most literal."
- Critical aside on BT (p.13–14): "Significantly, this aspect of Heidegger's philosophy drops out from 1927 — with political consequence." BT 418 (Dasein not present-at-hand in space) and BT 420 (situation [Lage] as leeway, not position) are cited, but Gross reads BT's spatiality discussion as a *substantive loss* relative to SS 1924's "special materiality of human Dasein." The 1927 abstraction — Dasein as "matter not of substance in space, but rather of where we care to be" — is at once correct and impoverished compared with the SS 1924 framing.
- The middle-voice argument (pp.14–18, the section's load-bearing move). Gross gives an extended parallel German-English quotation of GA 18 §§240–242 — first English, then the German block — to display Heidegger's stylistic operation. The German quotation includes: (a) the "Sokrates hat die Aufgabe... die *physei onta* kamen in den Hintergrund" passage on Socrates, the *politikē*, and the foundational Aristotelian shift to *physis* as Being; (b) the *doppeldeutig* passage on Being-in-the-world as both *eidos*/*dynamis* and *Begegnung aus der Welt her*; (c) the *Miteinandersein* / "miteinander dieselbe Welt haben" formulation; (d) the *Lebensphilosophie* / "Botanik der Pflanzen" polemic against the philosophy-of-life debate then raging in German universities.
- The stylistic reading (pp.17–18). Greek can express in the middle voice the "inherent multiplicity in the One, the simultaneity of being active and being passive, the nature of a life at the same time constructive and constructed." German cannot, except through morphological play. Heidegger therefore exploits "variations on the verb *sein* and a German language that allows one to nominalize agent and patient in a way that expresses their interanimation" — the example being "Das Begegnende ist in der Welt des Begegneten, ist da für ein anderes Sein." On *weg*, the prefix *be-* yields *bewegen*, then morphs through "*bewegt wird*" (passive verb), "*Bewegung*" (apparent neutrality), "*Bewegen*" (passive object), "*Bewegendes*" (active subject); the cumulative effect is "Bewegung ist Bewegung." The strong claim: "Heidegger's stylistic stutterings allow him to produce a discussion of *Miteinandersein* achievable by no other means" (p.18). The achievement "has been dramatically misunderstood" — setting up Section III.

### Section III — "Heidegger the Humanist?" (book pp. 18–27; PDF pp. 24–33)

A polemical mediation between hyperbolic French antihumanism and Habermasian liberal-Kantian critique, concluding with an extended exegesis of Aristotle *Physics* 202b23 (*kinēsis*) that bridges into the priority-of-pathos argument (Section IV in HR-01-GROSS-B).

- Framing the antihumanist debate (pp.18–19). The middle voice is *the* locus classicus for both Heideggerian appropriation and Derridian misappropriation. Gross names the cohort: "Jacques Derrida, Michel Foucault, Pierre Bourdieu, Paul de Man, J. Hillis Miller, Vincent Pecora, Luc Ferry, and Alain Renaut" (p.18). Their shared but mistaken reading: Heidegger's middle voice as "the attempt to dissolve passion and action, subject and object." Gross calls this "a serious error."
- Reading Derrida's *différance* passage (n.36, *Margins of Philosophy* 1968) as exemplifying — via the French translation of *kinēsis* / German *Bewegung*: *mouvance* — "the modern middle voice" but as a *misreading* of Heidegger: "*differance*... is neither simply active nor simply passive, announcing or rather recalling something like the middle voice, saying an operation that is not an operation, an operation that cannot be conceived either as passion or as the action of a subject on an object, or on the basis of the categories of agent or patient, neither on the basis of nor moving towards any of these *terms*." Gross: this *neutralizes* what Heidegger *interanimates*.
- Engaging *Letter on Humanism* (1946, p.19–20). Generally read as antihumanism's signature text and as foreshadowing Derrida's nontransitive subject (n.39). Gross resists: "SS 1924 shows Heidegger to be at most the hyperbolic antihumanist's distant relative." *Ethos* re-rendered as "abode" or "dwelling place" (Heraclitus Frag. 119); language as "the house of Being." This redefinition does not dissolve agency but redescribes it — even when "ethical evaluations multiply only when original thinking comes to an end" (n.40).
- Engaging Ferry-Renaut and Pecora (n.37, n.38, n.41). Their critique — that without agency there can be "no relationship between a self-determining subject and potentially universal values, and hence no ethical discourse at all" (p.20) — is *correct* against the antihumanist appropriators *but wrongly extended* to SS 1924. Gross's positive counter: "If anything, Heidegger's work in 1924 would be neo-Aristotelian and protocommunitarian. And it is in his middle voice discussion of the kinetic ambiguity of Being-in-the-world that Heidegger comes into his own."
- The *kinēsis* exegesis (pp.20–22). "The key lies in the complex relationship between *pathēsis* and *poiētikos*, whose interanimation defines motion (*kinēsis*) for Aristotle." The relationship Heidegger addresses "explicitly during his final lecture on the last day in July 1924" at GA 18.326: "Die Bestimmung der *poiēsis* und *pathēsis* reichen hinein in das eigentliche Zentrum der griechischen Welt- und Lebensbetrachtung." Aristotle *Physics* 202b23 is then given in three forms: (1) Greek transliterated, "Kinesis: entelecheia tou dynamei poiētikou kai pathētikou hē toiouton"; (2) Hardie/Gaye English, "Alteration is the fulfilment of the alterable as alterable (or, more scientifically, the fulfillment of what can act and what can be acted on, as such)"; (3) Heidegger's German rendering at GA 18.322, "Bewegung ist immer Bewegung eines Bewegten, das durch ein anderes Bewegendes bewegt wird."
- The morphological-stylistic exhibit at p.21 (a unique passage in the volume: a metalinguistic close-reading of Heidegger's German). Heidegger morphs *weg* → *be-wegen*; *bewegen* → *bewegt wird* (passive), *Bewegung* (nominalization, apparent neutrality), *Bewegen* (passive object, the moved), *Bewegendes* (active subject, the mover). Heidegger's anti-genus/differentia move: "a definition need not describe a thing in terms of *genus* and *differentia specifica* ('Man is a featherless biped') nor must one assume that the thing to be defined (*definiens*) is somehow distinct from that which defines it (*definiendum*)." Heidegger's positive claim at GA 18.288: "Die *kinēsis* ist eine Weise des Seins des Daseienden selbst" — *kinēsis* is a mode of Dasein's being, *not* participation (*méthexis*) in a Platonic Idea of motion.
- Returning to the late Heidegger essay "Language" (1959, p.21–23). The German middle voice via *es gibt* and *die Sprache spricht*. The 1959 passage on *ent-sprechen* ("listen and respond") and *Unter-schied* ("the jarring linguistic difference that calls things into the world") is compared with GA 18.108: "Rhētorikē peithous demiourgos: das Redenkönnen ist diejenige Möglichkeit, in der ich über die Überzeugung der Menschen, wie sie miteinander sind, die eigentliche Herrschaft habe" — "Being able to speak makes possible the actual authority I have to persuade people — to determine how people are with one another." Gross's reading (the volume's strongest 1924/1959 continuity claim): "Heidegger's experiment with a German middle voice in no way kills the author. Rather it describes in new terms how the author is rhetorically constituted" (p.24). Closing flourish (citing Heidegger's last SS 1924 utterance, GA 18.329): "Es gilt nicht Neues zu sagen, sondern das zu sagen, was die Alten schon meinten."
- The Aristotelian-physicist closing (pp.24–27). Returning to the *Naturwissenschaft/Geisteswissenschaft* dissolution: "What does a house look like?" — a question "ingeniously chosen to interrogate the very kind of thing that lies at the intersection of supposedly autonomous natural and human worlds." Citing GA 18.28 in parallel German-English on *soma*: "Wenn wir *soma* mit 'Körper' übersetzen, so ist zu beachten, daß *Körperlichkeit* für den Griechen nicht *Stofflichkeit* oder *Materialität* bedeutet, sondern *soma* meint eine eigentümliche *Aufdringlichkeit* eines Seienden, eines Daseienden..." — *soma* as obtrusiveness, not materiality. And then, at p.25 (the operative paragraph on Heidegger as *rhētōr poiētikós*), GA 18.205 in three parts:
  - "Derjenige ist der rechte *physikos*, der das Haus so anspricht, daß er es anspricht auf das Aussehen, das in sich selbst Bezug hat auf das, woraus das Haus besteht" (the proper physicist).
  - "das Aussehen, *eidos*, dieses Hauses in Holz, Steinen, Ziegeln und dieses wegen der Schaffung des benötigten Schutzes, des Obdachs, das geführt wird im Hinblick darauf, daß da sein soll das *skepasma*" (material is realized in concrete form: shelter for which it is built).
  - "Das Sein der Natur ist in seinem Aussehen bestimmt nicht einfach durch die *hyle*, sondern primär durch das *Bewegtsein*" (Being of nature determined primarily by Being-moved, not by matter).
- Closing of Section III (pp.26–27, bridging to Section IV's pathos-priority argument). The pathē "indicate possible ways of being-moved that tie humans in a unique way to their embodiment." They are "neither merely psychological emotions... nor are they simply physical-material alterations ('boiling blood,' or a 'cold sweat')." Closing parallel German block at GA 18.206–207: "*Affekte* sind nicht Zustände des Seelischen, es handelt sich um eine *Befindlichkeit des Lebenden in seiner Welt*, in der Weise, wie er gestellt ist zu etwas, wie er eine Sache sich angehen läßt. Die Affekte spielen eine fundamentale Rolle bei der Bestimmung des Seins-in-der-Welt, des Seins mit und zu anderen." And the section's final formulation: "*Das eidos der pathē ist ein Sichverhalten zu anderen Menschen, ein In-der-Welt-sein* (206–207)." Heidegger thus "rearticulates a neglected moment in the rhetorical tradition in which pathos goes beyond a psychology of the autonomous subject. Aristotelian pathos, he argues in his unique phenomenological language, serves as the very foundation for *logos*, or social discourse." Cued to BT 178: Aristotle's *Rhetoric* II "must be taken as the first systematic hermeneutic of the everydayness of Being with one another."

## 3. Contributor's Key Concepts

The full concept table is in §3 of the JSON. The conceptual architecture has three nested layers:

**Outer (titular)**: *rhetorical ontology* (Gross's positive proposal — the discursive-existential medium in which fundamental ontology becomes possible) and *rhetorical genealogy* (the methodological instrument: tracing BT terms back to GA 18). These frame the entire essay.

**Middle (load-bearing)**: *Being-moved* (*Sein-in-Bewegung* / *kinēsis*) — the cosmological-ontological condition rhetorical ontology articulates; *pathos* (as ground of *logos*) — the cosmological-affective material the middle voice operates on; *middle voice* — the stylistic-philosophical instrument by which *Being-moved* is rendered in German; and *Mitsein/koinōnia* — the social-rhetorical structure middle voice expresses.

**Inner (technical)**: *kinēsis* as *pathēsis*-*poiētikos* dyad; *eidos of pathē* as *In-der-Welt-sein*; *physis*/*polis* pairing; *Naturwissenschaft/Geisteswissenschaft* (the distinction Heidegger overextended); *soma* as *Aufdringlichkeit* not materiality; *rhētōr poiētikós* (Heidegger's stylistic self-figure); *trope* as contour of contingency; *discourse* (vs. communication); *phronēsis*; *humanism*/*antihumanism*; *deliberative*/*epideictic*/*judicial* rhetorical genres.

Centrality marks: 11 *central* concepts, 7 *supporting*, 1 *illustrative*. Function distribution: 8 *positive_proposal*, 4 *setup*, 3 *critique*, 2 *bridge*, 1 *example*. (Some concepts double-classify; see JSON.)

A note on Gross's terminological practice. Gross is unusually scrupulous about flagging multiple renderings: pathos is glossed at p.4 as "variously 'passion,' 'affect,' 'mood,' or 'emotion'" — treating translation itself as substantively contested. *Soma*-as-*Aufdringlichkeit* (not *Stofflichkeit*) is flagged via direct citation of Heidegger's own translation-correction at GA 18.28. *Ethos*-as-"abode" (Heraclitus Frag. 119, via Letter on Humanism) is similarly flagged. These are the three lemmas where translation contestation is highest in the unit.

## 4. Contributor's Main Positions / Claims

The full position table is in §4 of the JSON. Ten positions are tracked, with the following dependency structure:

- **P1** (SS 1924 as formative for BT — the rhetorical genealogy thesis) is the *historiographical* anchor.
- **P2** (rhetoric at the heart of fundamental ontology — the volume's titular thesis) is the *systematic* anchor.
- **P3** (hermeneutics depoliticizes pathos — Section I's diagnosis of Gadamer/T&M) is the *critical* anchor.
- **P4** (Heidegger-Arendt rhetorical-genre alignment, against Villa) is supporting.
- **P5** (pathos as transfer point dissolving *Naturwissenschaft/Geisteswissenschaft*) is the cosmological core of P2.
- **P6** (middle voice as philosophical instrument, not stylistic eccentricity) is Section II's load-bearing claim.
- **P7** (Heidegger as antihumanism's "distant relative," not its source — against Derrida, Pecora, Foucault, Bourdieu, Ferry-Renaut readings) is Section III's polemical core.
- **P8** (*kinēsis* as Dasein's mode of being, not Platonic *méthexis*) is the technical-ontological core of P5.
- **P9** (1924/1959 continuity — middle voice "in no way kills the author") sutures the SS 1924 and post-*Kehre* Heidegger.
- **P10** (eidos of pathē as Being-in-the-world) is the closing bridge into Section IV's pathos-priority argument.

P1 and P2 mutually presuppose: P1's genealogical claim is the historical evidence for P2's systematic claim; P2's systematic claim is the conceptual form P1's evidence takes. P3 frames the volume's gain-loss structure; P4 makes the political-philosophical case via Arendt; P5–P8 carry the section II–III argument forward; P9–P10 close the unit by setting up Section IV.

The most speculative position is **P9** (the 1924/1959 continuity). Gross has to argue against (a) the standard periodization-rupture reading and (b) his own Section I framing of post-1924 Heidegger as "dropping rhetoric." His resolution — rhetoric-as-named-discipline is dropped, but rhetorical-ontological grammar persists — is implicit in the unit and (presumably) elaborated in HR-01-GROSS-B.

Targets across positions: Gadamer (P3), Villa (P4), McKeon (P5), Derrida and Pecora (P7), Plato (P8), Habermas (P3 indirect), Ferry-Renaut (P7 partial), Foucault/Bourdieu/de Man/Hillis Miller (P7), the Stoic-Ciceronian psychology-of-emotion (P10).

## 6. Heidegger Primary Loci Cited

The full Heidegger-loci catalogue is in §6 of the JSON. **Twenty-seven distinct Heidegger primary citations** are captured for HR-01-GROSS-A. By source-text:

- **GA 18 (SS 1924)**: 23 distinct citations. ## 8. Tensions & Open Questions

The full tension list is in §8 of the JSON. Eight tensions are tracked: 4 internal-to-essay, 3 cross-essay, 1 cross-pipeline.

**Internal tensions:**
- **T1**: Gross both cites BT (158, 178) approvingly *and* diagnoses BT as "dropping out" the SS 1924 special-materiality discussion (BT 418, 420). Resolution implicit: BT acknowledges the rhetorical source but mutes its substantive treatment; the unit treats this as productive ambivalence rather than contradiction.
- **T2**: Plato is "villain" in Heidegger's SS 1924 framing (p.23), but Gross notes this is "characteristic of his pedagogy at the time" — leaving open whether the anti-Platonism is a stable Heideggerian commitment or a pedagogical posture. [INTERP-low: signaled but not resolved within this unit; resolution would require WS 1924–25 (GA 19) reading.]
- **T3**: The middle voice is claimed both to preserve agency (P7) and to "interanimate" rather than distinguish active/passive (P6). Gross's resolution is that "agency" in the SS 1924 register is rhetorically constituted distributed-yet-real — neither Cartesian unitary subject nor postmodern dissolved subject. [INTERP-high: this resolution is asserted at p.24 but not fully argued.]
- **T4**: The 1924/1959 continuity (P9) sits in tension with the section-I framing of post-1924 Heidegger as "dropping rhetoric" (p.5). Gross's resolution: rhetoric-as-named-discipline is dropped, but rhetoric-as-ontological-grammar persists. Implicit; HR-01-GROSS-B presumably elaborates.

**Cross-essay tensions:**
- **T5** ↔ **HR-07-POGGELER**: Gross's "rhetoric central to fundamental ontology" (P2) directly contradicts Pöggeler's titular "Heidegger's *Restricted* Conception of Rhetoric." Both cite GA 18. The volume opens with Gross and closes with Pöggeler; the volume *stages* this disagreement editorially without resolving it. Volume tension VT1 per Phase 0.
- **T6** ↔ **HR-02-GADAMER**: Gross critiques Gadamer's T&M position as "depoliticizing" (Section I) but defers to Ch 2 for Gadamer's revised position. Volume tension VT6.
- **T7** ↔ **HR-04-HYDE**: Gross's *rhētōr poiētikós* / middle-voice Heidegger (P6) and Hyde's call-of-conscience / silent-epideictic Heidegger are likely compatible figurations (Gross's framing leaves room for Hyde's specification). [INTERP-low.]

**Cross-pipeline tensions:**
- **T8** ↔ **BCAP U-FP3a**: GA 18.110 is BCAP unit U-FP3a's central passage. Gross uses it as epigraph for a *rhetorical-ontology* framing while BCAP frames it as fundamental-ontology. The framings are aspectual (compatible), but the difference matters for Phase 3 cross-pipeline reconciliation.

## Sub-unit summary (≤200 words)

**Thesis.** Heidegger SS 1924 (GA 18) is the formative moment in which Aristotelian rhetoric becomes the discursive-existential medium of fundamental ontology. The "rhetorical genealogy" of *Being and Time*'s ten signature concepts (Mitsein/koinōnia, Glaube/doxa, Lage/being-in, Sorge, Stimmung/pathos, Augenblick/kairos, ekstasis, Angst/phobos, logos, Entschluß/krisis) is recoverable only through GA 18. Pathos is the cosmological substrate that grounds *logos* (stones, plants, animals, humans). Heidegger's German-stylistic experiment with the middle voice — *bewegen*-morphology, *es gibt*, *die Sprache spricht* — is the philosophical instrument by which *Sein-in-Bewegung* becomes expressible; it is neither Derridian-antihumanist nor Habermasian-Kantian but neo-Aristotelian protocommunitarian.

**Loci.** 27 Heidegger citations (23 GA 18, 3 BT, 1 Letter on Humanism, 1 1959 "Language"). 12 Aristotle citations (Rhetoric I–II, Physics II–III with 202b23 central, Politics I.2, NE VI, plus Metaphysics/Topics/De anima/DMA/Poetics in Heidegger's holistic list). Bridges to BCAP, BT-D1-U2, BT-D1-U5, RHET-01, RHET-02, PHYS-02, PHYS-03, POL-01, NE-06.

**Edges.** 30 (≥15 target met 2×). 2 cross-essay POSITION edges; 2 LOCUS-to-LOCUS edges (both required minima met).

**Blockers.** None. OCR confidence high throughout; all GA 18 page citations legible; all parallel German-English block quotations recoverable.
