## 1. The argument in one sentence

Heidegger's 1923-24 confrontation with Aristotle's *Rhetoric* and *Politics* produces a "rhetorical protopolitics" — a rudimentary speech-political ontology equiprimordial with the threefold definition of the human (*logon echon*, *zoon politikon*, practical being-in-the-world) — whose career across Heidegger's thought traces a strict three-phase trajectory (Phenomenological 1923-25, Metontological 1933-35, Archaic-Poietic 1935-43) in which rhetoric is progressively abandoned in favor of Führer-will (Phase 2) and *polemos* of poetic origin (Phase 3); Hannah Arendt's *Human Condition* recovers the protopolitical strand through the lateral path of plurality, natality, freedom-of-action, and *doxa*-as-space-of-appearance.

## 2. Pacing of the essay

Kisiel does not develop one argument across the 27 body pages but rather *two interlocking arguments*. The first (Sections I-V) is the **constructive case** for rhetorical protopolitics: that GA 18 supplies a complete equiprimordial framework in which the three Aristotelian *pisteis* (pathos/ethos/logos) operate as close kin to the three modes of disclosedness of human situation (*Befindlichkeit*/*Verstehen*/*Rede*). Kisiel saturates these sections with GA 18 page-citations — pages 44, 45f, 64, 73, 104, 105, 110, 114, 122f, 130f, 134, 138, 145, 149, 151, 152, 153, 161, 162, 165, 171, 177, 241 — and threads them with SZ §60 (resoluteness), §74 (historicity, SZ 384/384f), §26 (Fürsorge, SZ 122/298). The constructive case culminates in the equiprimordiality thesis at the close of Section V: *pistis* and *aletheia* are not opposed but mutually constitutive of the everyday speech-situation.

The second argument (Sections VI-IX, including the inline table) is **diagnostic and critical**. Section VI is a four-paragraph transition that announces the three-phase succession; Section VII reconstructs the metontological polis of 1933-35 through Heidegger's WS 1933-34 seminar protocols and the May 1933 Schlageter speech, reading Heidegger's deployment of "will of the Führer" as a phronetic *miscalculation* internal to his own 1924 framework; Section VIII tracks Heidegger's growing disenchantment with the Führer-state (1935+) into the archaic-poietic polemos-polis of Hölderlin's hymns and Sophocles' *Antigone* — citing GA 39 51 as Heidegger's own retraction ("the polis cannot be defined politically"); Section IX brings Arendt's *Human Condition* as the redemptive recovery of the protopolitical strand, retroactively diagnosing Heidegger's "bacillus of totalitarianism" via the autochthony-myth of Folk-and-Earth.

The architectonic of the essay is therefore: **constructive thesis (I-V) → table (p. 149) → critical applications across three phases (VI-VIII) → recovery via Arendt (IX)**. The table is not a sub-thesis but the essay's **central artifact** — the volume's most articulated single position-statement on Heidegger's political trajectory.

## 3. The inline table on p. 149

| Period | Basic Text | Basic Concepts |
|---|---|---|
| Phenomenological (1923-25) | Aristotle, *Rhetoric* | pathos, ethos, logos of doxic speech situation |
| Metontological (1933-35) | Plato, *The Republic* | leader of people, guardians of state, 3-level service |
| Archaic-Poietic (1935-43) | Sophocles, *Antigone* | *pole-mos* of thinker, poet, and statesman as prepolitical |

The table's significance is fivefold:

1. **Texts**: Each phase is anchored in a different Greek primary text — *Rhetoric* / *Republic* / *Antigone*. The succession is itself diagnostic: Aristotle (the rhetorical-protopolitical) gives way to Plato (the leader-state of guardians, which Kisiel reads via Plato's *Republic* III-IV); Plato gives way to Sophocles (tragic *polemos*, the prepolitical site of being).
2. **Time-spans**: 1923-25 / 1933-35 / 1935-43. The chronology maps onto Heidegger's biography (Marburg lectures → Rector-period → post-Rectorship Hölderlin lectures) but Kisiel insists the periodization is *philosophical-textual* not merely biographical.
3. **Concepts**: pathos-ethos-logos (rhetorical) / leader-guardians-services (institutional-political) / *polemos* (combative-poetic). The semantic distance between Phases 1 and 3 is enormous: Phase 1 is communication-for-Verständigung, Phase 3 is "Conversation here is not communication" (GA 53 102).
4. **Rhetoric's career**: only Phase 1 is rhetorical. Phase 2 is post-rhetorical (will-of-Führer replaces civic persuasion); Phase 3 is anti-rhetorical (polemos of poetic origin replaces communication-for-agreement).
5. **Cross-reference**: Note 9 redirects to Kisiel's "In the Middle of Heidegger's Three Concepts of the Political" (Raffoul/Pettigrew 2002) as the long-form treatment. The HR-06 essay therefore distills the table's argument for a multi-author rhetoric-focused volume.

## 4. Heidegger primary loci — the GA 18 saturation

This essay's Heidegger-citation density is among the highest in the volume (rivaled only by Michalski). GA 18 page-references alone exceed 25 distinct citations across the body text. The clustering is significant: pages 45f / 122f / 241 anchor the protopolitical thesis (Section opening + Section II); pages 64 / 73 anchor the das Man / doxa reading (Section III); pages 104 / 105 / 114 anchor the dynamis-not-technē reading and the equiprimordiality of the three pisteis (Section V); pages 134 / 138 / 145 / 149 / 151 / 152 / 153 / 161 / 162 / 165 / 171 / 177 are the dense doxa-chain of Section V (pp. 147-148).

The SZ citations cluster around three Heideggerian themes:
- **§26 (Mitsein/Fürsorge)**: SZ 122, 298 — politician-as-conscience-for-other; leaping-ahead vs. leaping-in
- **§§29-32 (Befindlichkeit/Rede/Auslegung)**: SZ 85, 138 — already-having-been-interpreted; speaking-against-one-another
- **§§60, 74 (resoluteness, historicity)**: SZ 129, 298, 300, 384, 384f, 385, 389, 391 — authentic Mitsein; communication and Kampf; treu/faithful/loyal as virtue of resoluteness (with explicit application to Heidegger's Schlageter-vocabulary).

Beyond GA 18 and SZ, Kisiel deploys five distinct *Gesamtausgabe* volumes:
- **GA 16** (*Reden* 1910-1976) — the Schlageter speech (759f), the Rectoral greeting to "German Students!" (184)
- **GA 19** (Plato *Sophist*) — Arendt's first Heidegger seminar (Section IX)
- **GA 29/30** (*Welt-Endlichkeit-Einsamkeit*) — Note 7's secondary citation
- **GA 39** (Hölderlin *Germanien*/*Rhein*) — Section VIII's central text (pp. 51, 73, 214)
- **GA 53** (Hölderlin *Der Ister*) — Section VIII's polemos-text (pp. 98f, 102)

Plus two unpublished or projected sources: the December 1924 Cologne "Wahrsein und Dasein nach Aristoteles" Ruhr-Rede (GA 80 projected) and the student protocols of WS 1933-34 "Vom Wesen und Begriff von Natur, Geschichte und Staat" (unpublished as of 2005).

## 5. Aristotle primary loci

Kisiel's Aristotelian saturation runs across five works:
- ***Rhetoric*** I.2 (rhetoric as *dynamis*, three *pisteis*); I.3 (three civic genres); II (the moods-doctrine); II.1 1378a10 (the *phronesis-arete-eunoia* trio); II.5 1382a20 (phobos); II.20-22 (gnomē / maxims, Note 5 citing 1395b1)
- ***Politics*** I.1-2 (zoon politikon, zoon logon echon)
- ***Nicomachean Ethics*** I.5 (three life-styles); VI (five excellent habits, *phronesis*)
- ***Metaphysics*** V.8 (lexicon entry on *ousia*); IX (energeia/dynamis); III.3 (being-is-not-a-genus)

The strategic placement is striking: Aristotle's *Rhetoric* is the basic text of Phase 1; Aristotle's *NE* VI grounds the Ruhr-Rede; Aristotle's *Politics* anchors the *zoon politikon* reading. Kisiel reads Aristotle's *Rhetoric* — not Plato's *Republic* — as "the more ontological Greek *Urtext* of political philosophy" at this "early stage" of Heidegger's development (p. 133), explicitly inverting the standard hierarchy.

## 6. The conceptual signature: equiprimordiality

The single most distinctive Kisielian operator in this essay is **equiprimordiality**. The word and its conceptual work appear at four argumentative crux-points:

1. **Three Aristotelian definitions of human** as equiprimordial (opening, p. 131): *logon echon* / *zoon politikon* / practical being-in-the-world.
2. **Rhetoric and politics** as equiprimordial (Section opening, p. 133): "a Greek loquacity that places a primacy on the political, what is being situated ontologically is in fact a rhetorical politics and political rhetoric."
3. **The three pisteis** as equiprimordial with the three modes of being-in (Section V, p. 144): pathos/ethos/logos ≈ *Befindlichkeit*/*Verstehen*/*Rede*.
4. **Pistis and aletheia** as equiprimordial (Section V title and pp. 144-148): the three modes of persuasion are equiprimordial with the three modes of trueing/unconcealment.

The equiprimordiality-claim is therefore Kisiel's volume-level intervention. Where Hyde reads pathos as orator-resource (Ch 4), Kisiel reads it as ontological-disclosive; where Pöggeler reads rhetoric as restricted-moment (Ch 7), Kisiel reads it as constitutive-of-Mitsein-as-such. The four equiprimordiality claims compound into the essay's central thesis.

## 7. The Schlageter epideictic as case study

Kisiel's most ethically charged single move is the analysis of Heidegger's May 26, 1933 Schlageter speech (delivered the day *before* the more famous Rectoral Address) as exemplary epideictic-protopolitical-misuse. Kisiel quotes the speech in full (p. 135) — its commemoration of Schlageter's "hardest death," the Black Forest as native landscape, the "Primeval rock, granite" of the mountains — and reads it as instantiation of Aristotle's epideictic genus operating *politically*. Crucially, Kisiel identifies the SZ vocabulary at work in the speech: Schlageter's "true (*treu*), constant, faithful, loyal" virtue cross-references SZ 385, 391, embedding the Schlageter eulogy in the existential-ontological vocabulary of resoluteness.

Then Kisiel makes the comparative-ethical move: Heidegger's 1936 "Origin of the Artwork" later identifies "essential sacrifice" alongside "artwork" and the "state-founding deed" as one of three ways truth happens; an earlier unpublished version names "the people-saving death" as such a happening of truth. The Schlageter-speech is thus exposed as not a one-off rhetorical performance but a *systematic* application of Heidegger's truth-as-happening framework to Nazi political mobilization.

## 8. The Arendt move

Section IX (book pp. 153-158) is the essay's longest single section (5 pages, ~30% of the essay). Its argumentative work is fivefold:

1. **Biographical-philological**: Arendt's WS 1924-25 Plato *Sophist* seminar with Heidegger was preceded by his summary of SS 1924 on Aristotle. Arendt almost certainly did not have access to circulated student transcripts of GA 18, but her phenomenological approach to the Greek polis bears unmistakable marks of Heidegger's protopolitical framework.
2. **Conceptual radicalization**: Arendt's *Human Condition* (1958) radicalizes Heidegger's *Mitwelt* via "plurality and natality." Politics is *between* humans (not in man-as-zoon-politikon) as its very space.
3. **Doxa restored**: Arendt restores doxa in its twofold Greek sense — *dokein* as opinion AND as splendor/fame/repute (German *Ansicht* and *Ansehen*). This twofold doxa exactly doubles back to Aristotle's rhetorical speech-situation: doxic content of speech + ethos of speaker.
4. **Kantian-cosmopolitan supplement**: Arendt invokes Kant's "enlarged mentality" (*Critique of Judgment* §40) as guide to prudential judgment, converging with Aristotle's *phronesis-arete-eunoia* trio (*Rhetoric* 2.1, 1378a10).
5. **Diagnostic critique of Heidegger**: Arendt's account of Heidegger's "bacillus of totalitarianism" — the "mythologizing confusions like Folk and Earth" supplied "almost as an afterthought" to compensate for early solipsistic existentialism — closes the essay.

The Arendt move resolves the essay's structural problem: how to claim Heidegger's 1924 framework as *fertile* for political philosophy while documenting (through Sections VI-VIII) its catastrophic 1933-1943 misapplications. The solution is the Arendtian afterlife: the protopolitics returns, but only after passing through Arendt's correction of Heidegger's Folk-Earth mythology.

## 9. Tensions

The essay generates and works through ten substantive tensions (full structured catalog in JSON Section 8). The most consequential:

1. **T1: Phase 1 vs. Phase 2 (Aristotle Rhetoric → Plato Republic)** — the textual displacement Kisiel reads as *internal* to Heidegger's framework.
2. **T2: Phase 2 vs. Phase 3 (Plato Republic → Sophocles Antigone)** — Heidegger's own retraction at GA 39 51 ("the polis cannot be defined politically") is the textual evidence for the displacement.
3. **T3: Authentic vs. inauthentic doxa "muted in BT"** — Kisiel's most subtle BT-criticism: the 1924 framework distinguished authentic/inauthentic doxa explicitly, but BT softened the distinction in ways that contributed to Heidegger's later vulnerability to inauthentic-doxa political application.
4. **T4: Aristotle's equiprimordial three definitions vs. his own genus-species treatment of "man"** — picked up by Arendt as critique of Aristotle's residual essentialism.
5. **T5: das Man as path-out vs. das Man as trap** — Kisiel's mediating reading against Bourdieu/Marcuse/Arendt.

Cross-essay tensions (full enumeration in JSON):

- **T7-cross (Pöggeler)**: Kisiel and Pöggeler agree on the FACT of post-1924 restriction but diverge on its meaning. Kisiel = politically diagnostic; Pöggeler = architectural limit.
- **T8-cross (Gross)**: Kisiel's phronesis-miscalculation thesis (HOW Heidegger came to misapply his framework) refines Gross's pathos-substrate genealogy (WHAT predisposed Heidegger to mistake propaganda-pathos for civic-pathos).
- **T9-cross (Gadamer)**: Kisiel presupposes Gadamer's dynamis-not-technē reading.
- **T10-cross (Hyde)**: Kisiel's ethos-as-Entschlossenheit (deliberative-political SZ §60) contrasts with Hyde's ethos-as-Ruf-des-Gewissens (epideictic-existential SZ §§54-60). Both translate Aristotle's ethos into BT vocabulary, with opposite rhetorical-genre commitments.

## 11. Significance for the dissertation neighborhood

Kisiel's essay is the volume's most articulated single position on the politics of GA 18. For the dissertation's Aristotle-Heidegger integration argument, it supplies:

- A **rigorous three-phase periodization** (1923-25 / 1933-35 / 1935-43) anchored in textual evidence across five GA volumes — the most defensible alternative to monolithic "Heidegger and politics" treatments.
- A **detailed map of phronesis as rhetorical-political virtue** that traces the term from NE VI through SS 1924 through SZ §60 (resoluteness as listening to call of conscience) through WS 1933-34 (will-of-Führer as phronetic kairos-grasp) — exposing the *internal* mechanism of Heidegger's Nazi misapplication.
- A **lexicon of authentic vs. inauthentic doxa** drawn from GA 18 pp. 134-165 that the BT German appendix and BCAP pipelines have not yet indexed; this is a Phase 3 cross-pipeline opportunity.
- An **Arendtian afterlife reading** that opens a redemptive path for protopolitical-rhetorical-Heidegger reception without absolving Heidegger himself — a position the dissertation can adopt without committing to either apologetic or strictly hostile reception strategies.
