## 1. Volume Overview

**The editorial project.** The volume's central claim is staked in Gross's titular phrase "the pathos of Heidegger's rhetorical ontology" and developed across forty-five pages of dense Introduction (book pp. 1–45) that braids four interrelated arguments: (i) that "Heidegger and rhetoric" is a story not yet adequately told (p. 1); (ii) that the SS 1924 lecture course *Grundbegriffe der aristotelischen Philosophie* (GA 18) is a *formative* moment rather than a digression — "a direct *genetic* link" from rhetoric to *Being and Time* (p. 2); (iii) that pathos (variously rendered "passion," "affect," "mood," or "emotion") is "the very condition for the possibility of rational discourse" (p. 4) and not a cynical addition to logos; and (iv) that the rhetorical genealogy of BT's signature concepts — *Mitsein/koinōnia*, *Glaube/doxa*, *Lage/being-in*, *Sorge*/care, *Stimmung/pathos*, *Augenblick/kairos*, *ekstasis*, *Angst/phobos*, *logos*/articulation, *Entschluß/krisis*, *Überlegung/* deliberation — has been "lost" with the displacement from rhetoric to hermeneutics (p. 2). Kemmann's role is institutional and bibliographic: he conducted the Gadamer interview (1999–2001), translated his own German bibliographic apparatus into English, and compiled the seven-page Selected Bibliography organized into "Rhetoric Defined in Heidegger's Work" (eight specific lecture-course/book loci where Heidegger explicitly defines rhetoric), "Published and Unpublished Works by Heidegger around SS 1924," and "Secondary Literature."

**The seven essays as a constellation.** The volume opens with Gross's strong centrality thesis ("Heidegger thus relocates rhetoric at the heart of his fundamental ontology," Intro p. 4) and closes with Pöggeler's countertitle "Heidegger's *Restricted* Conception of Rhetoric" — an architectural disagreement built into the volume's frame. Within that frame the contributors form three loose sub-clusters. (a) The *positive amplification* cluster — Gadamer's testimony that rhetoric is *dynamis*, not *technē* (Ch 2 p. 49: "Aristotle defined rhetoric (1.2) as *dynamis*. This definition is to be retained even though Aristotle often designates it as *technē*. This designation is incorrect, whereas *dynamis* is the correct definition"); Hyde's bridging of Aristotelian *epideictic* and Heidegger's *Ruf des Gewissens* (Ch 4 pp. 81–104); and Struever's reading of *Alltäglichkeit* as "timefulness" rather than ordinariness, with rhetoric as the politically and temporally indispensable life science (Ch 5 pp. 105–130). (b) The *philological-methodological* cluster — Michalski (GA 18's editor) on Heidegger's procedure as philology rather than philosophy (Ch 3 pp. 65–80), and Kisiel on the same lectures as the genesis of a "rhetorical protopolitics" with Arendtian afterlife (Ch 6 pp. 131–160). (c) The *negative case* — Pöggeler's claim that Heidegger's later "topology of being" leaves rhetoric behind, the rhetorical strand thus being a circumscribed and ultimately abandoned moment (Ch 7 pp. 161–172). The constellation is not centripetal but productively oppositional: every contributor cites SS 1924 (GA 18) but their accounts of what GA 18 *means* for the trajectory of Heidegger's thought diverge sharply.

**Relation to the existing pipelines.** This volume is the most cross-pipeline-rich text in the corpus index. (i) BCAP (GA 18) is *the* primary reference: every contributor cites it, with footnote density highest in Michalski (who edited GA 18) and Kisiel; Gross's Introduction quotes GA 18 page numbers in roughly twenty places; Gadamer's interview footnotes are largely GA 18 page references. (ii) BT is a continuous secondary reference: Hyde's whole essay is structured around BT §§54–60 (call of conscience, with H-pages 289, 316, 318, 342) read against Aristotle's *Rhetoric* I; Gross builds his pathos-genealogy from BT §§29–30 (*Befindlichkeit/Furcht*); Kisiel uses SZ §§29, 60, 74 (resoluteness, historicity). (iii) The Aristotle pipeline is engaged through the *Rhetoric* (most cited — by Gross, Gadamer, Hyde, Struever, Kisiel, and Pöggeler), the *Nicomachean Ethics* (Hyde, Struever, Kisiel), the *Politics* (Gross, Struever, Kisiel), the *Physics* (Gross, Struever, Kisiel), *De anima* (Hyde, Struever, Pöggeler), and *De motu animalium* and *De partibus animalium* (Gross, Struever — this volume is unique in the corpus for citing the latter zoological treatises). (iv) Rickert's pipeline neighborhood is engaged indirectly: the Marburg Neo-Kantianism (Cohen, Natorp, Hartmann) that Heidegger reacted against forms part of the volume's biographical scenery (Gadamer's interview is a direct source). (v) Uexküll has no overlap.

**Significance for the dissertation's Aristotle-Heidegger integration argument.** All seven essays are dissertation-load-bearing. Gross's pathos-as-substrate thesis (Intro IV: "The Priority of Pathos") and Hyde's BT-§55-as-epideictic-ontology argument are direct material for a dissertation chapter on Heideggerian appropriation of Aristotelian *pathē*; Struever's *Alltäglichkeit*-as-timefulness reading bridges the Aristotelian *Physics* (motion, *kinēsis*) to BT Division I in a way no other secondary source in the corpus does; Kisiel's protopolitical reading is the most rigorous single-essay treatment of the SS 1924 → SZ → 1933 trajectory available in English; Gadamer's interview is *the* primary witness to Heidegger's pedagogical practice in 1923–24; Michalski's philological framing constrains how Heidegger's translations can legitimately be read; and Pöggeler's restriction thesis cannot be ignored — any centrality claim must be argued *through* it. Genuinely surprising about the volume: (a) the editor (Gross) and the closing contributor (Pöggeler) are in *direct titular opposition*, and the volume keeps that disagreement open rather than resolving it editorially; (b) the editorial Subject Index functions as a pre-curated trilingual (English/German/Greek) glossary of nearly eighty entries, complete with rhetorical/Aristotelian cross-references absent from BT's German appendix.

## 4. Gross Introduction Split-Point Decision

**Section structure (re-confirmed)**: Opening (5 pp.) + I (6 pp.) + II (7 pp.) + III (9 pp.) + IV (14 pp.) + Notes (4 pp.).

| Sub-unit | Sections | Body pages | Pages |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HR-01-GROSS-A** | Opening + I + II + III | book 1–27 / PDF 7–33 | **27** |
| **HR-01-GROSS-B** | IV + Notes | book 27–45 / PDF 33–51 | **18** |

**Decision**: Split between Section III ("HEIDEGGER THE HUMANIST?") and Section IV ("THE PRIORITY OF PATHOS"), at book p. 27 / PDF p. 33. HR-01-GROSS-A = 27 body pages; HR-01-GROSS-B = 14 body pages of Section IV + 4 pages of notes = 18 pages.

**Rationale**.
1. **Argumentative coherence.** Sections I–III form a single unit: I introduces the hermeneutics-vs-rhetoric problem (using Gadamer + Arendt as foils), II works through Heidegger's *physis/polis/Bewegung* style and the deconstruction of Naturwissenschaft/Geisteswissenschaft via Aristotle's *Physics*, III addresses the politics of poststructuralism (middle voice, Derrida) and Heidegger's stylistic appropriation of *kinēsis*. Section IV is a discrete extended treatment of *pathos* with its own Aristotelian-Roman-Lutheran-Heideggerian arc (Aristotle's *pathē* in the *Rhetoric* → Roman compromise → Luther's *Affektenlehre* → Heidegger's *Befindlichkeit*-as-pathos → political consequences and Heidegger's 1933). This is the longest single section and stands on its own as a sustained argument.
2. **Page balance.** A-side 27 / B-side 18 is uneven by raw count, but counting only body argumentative density (excluding the four pages of notes attached to B), the practical analysis load is 27 vs. 14, with B's heavier theoretical concentration (the pathos-genealogy is the volume's most-cited single thesis) compensating for lower page count.
3. **Cross-reference cleanliness.** Notes 1–34 are referenced primarily from Sections I–III; Notes 35–49 are referenced primarily from Section IV. Splitting at the III/IV boundary keeps note clusters with their argumentative origin.

**Alternative considered and rejected**: split at I/II boundary (book p. 11) would give A = 11 pp. and B = 34 pp., wildly unbalanced. Split at II/III boundary (book p. 18) would give A = 18 pp. and B = 27 pp., balanced numerically but argumentatively wrong: II→III is a continuous discussion of Heideggerian style (II is on syntax; III is on the politics of that syntax via Derrida/middle voice). A three-way split (A: opening+I, B: II+III, C: IV+Notes) is rejected as over-fragmenting; Phase 2 analysis is more coherent at two passes.

**Phase 2 unit definitions**:
- **HR-01-GROSS-A**: book pp. 1–27 / PDF 7–33; opening + I + II + III; thesis = "rhetorical genealogy of BT, with humanism, middle voice, and *physis/polis* style as its deconstructive vehicles."
- **HR-01-GROSS-B**: book pp. 27–45 / PDF 33–51; IV + Notes; thesis = "the priority of pathos: pathos as the ground of logos, traced from Aristotle through Christian theology to Heidegger's 1924 SS lectures and the political afterlife in the Nazi turn."

**Compiler**: Ansgar Kemmann. **Pages**: book 177–183 / PDF 183–189 (7 pages). **Layout**: opens with "KEY TO ABBREVIATIONS" (GA = Gesamtausgabe; SS = Summer Semester; WS = Winter Semester), then three labeled sections.

### Section A: "RHETORIC DEFINED IN HEIDEGGER'S WORK" (book p. 177–178 — gold)

The compiler's note: "The term *rhetoric* can be found in at least eight places. Seven of those come from the Marburg period." This is a ready-made cross-pipeline bridge index. The eight loci are:

1. **Lecture manuscript 1923/24, "Wahrsein und Dasein nach Aristoteles"** (unpublished) — "Rhetorik" identified as the *Fundamentallehre* vom *Logos*, "die ursprüngliche Logik" (3), following *Rhetoric* 1.2–3.
2. **Lecture Course SS 1924, "Grundbegriffe der aristotelischen Philosophie"** = **GA 18**. Multi-page set:
   - "Die Auslegung des Daseins des Menschen hinsichtlich der Grundmöglichkeit des Miteinandersprechens am Leitfaden der Rhetorik" (103–267)
   - "Der konkrete Beleg für die Ursprünglichkeit des Sehens [des *Logos*] ist die ganze 'Rhetorik'" (61)
   - **"Die Rhetorik ist nichts anderes als die Auslegung des konkreten Daseins, die Hermeneutik des Daseins selbst" (110)** — *the volume's most-cited single passage; epigraph for Ch 1 and Ch 5*
   - "Besinnung über das Sprechen" (113)
   - "Leitfaden" für die Bestimmung der Stellung der "wissenschaftliche[n]" Begriffsbildung im Dasein des Menschen (123f).
3. **Lecture Course WS 1924/25, "Plato: Sophistes"** = **GA 19**. Aristotelian rhetoric (200, 219, 337–339, 350–351); Sophistic rhetoric (16, 219, 294, 306–310); Plato on rhetoric, *Phaedrus* (307–351, 625–629).
4. **Lecture Course SS 1925, "Prolegomena zur Geschichte des Zeitbegriffs"** = **GA 20**. Rhetoric (346f).
5. **Lecture Course WS 1925/26, "Logik: Die Frage nach der Wahrheit"** = **GA 21**. *De interpretatione* 17a (130).
6. **Lecture Course SS 1926, "Grundbegriffe der antiken Philosophie"** = **GA 22**. Rhetoric as teaching of Protagoras (86); rhetoric as textbook of Aristotle (146).
