## Tier A (figures cited by ≥3 contributors)

### Aristotle (cited by all 7 essays)

**Works cited by unit**:

- **HR-01-GROSS-B**: Rhetoric 1354a (Rhet. I.1 — straightedge analogy); Rhetoric II (pathē as condition of krisis, p.30); Rhetoric 1382a20 (phobos); Rhetoric II.5 typology (pp.36–37 — nine kinds of fearsome people in three categories); Politics (bios politikos, p.40)

**Volume role**: source-of-everything. Aristotle is read via Heidegger's GA 18 reading; the Bröcker/Schalk variants at GA 18 110 are themselves the volume's editorial fault-line (DG9).

**Controversy**: rhetoric as dynamis vs. technē (DG2); Aristotle's three definitions of human as equiprimordial (Kisiel) vs. genus-species (DG-implicit); Aristotle Rhetoric I.1 (denigration of pathos) vs. Rhetoric II (pathē as condition of krisis) — internal-to-Aristotle disagreement Heidegger and the contributors navigate; whether Aristotle's biology (De motu animalium, De partibus animalium) intricates with Rhetoric in GA 18 207-219 (Struever's coinage).

**Cross-pipeline link**: Aristotle pipeline (entire); particularly tight overlap with BCAP units U-FP3a (GA 18 §§13–14) and Rhetoric II.5 reception in BT pipeline §§29–30.

**Works cited by unit**:

- HR-01-GROSS-B: Gorgias 459c (p.27)

**Volume role**: foil to Aristotle in nearly every essay. Plato's anti-rhetoric (Gorgias, Phaedrus, Sophist) is the position Aristotle (and Heidegger reading Aristotle) overcomes. **Controversy**: Plato as villain (Gross's Heidegger framing) vs. Plato as partial-acknowledgement of rhetoric (Hyde Phaedrus 260d-e); Plato's Symposium as origin of Gadamer's hermeneutics vs. Plato Sophist as Heidegger's anti-rhetorical sharpener (Pöggeler) vs. Plato Republic as Heidegger's Nazi-period source (Kisiel three-phase Phase 2).

**Cross-pipeline link**: no Plato pipeline yet; Plato Sophist via GA 19 cross-links to BCAP/post-BCAP units (WS 1924-25).

**Works cited by unit**:
- HR-01-GROSS-A: Truth and Method (extensively — n.11–22); Philosophical Hermeneutics
- HR-01-GROSS-B: Truth and Method (via Dockhorn's 1966 review)
- HR-03-MICHALSKI: Philosophische Lehrjahre (1977); Sechs Briefe an Martin Heidegger aus der Marburger Zeit (1999); 'Werner Jaeger, Aristoteles' review in Gesammelte Werke 5; Grondin biography (1999)
- HR-04-HYDE: Heidegger's Ways; Philosophical Hermeneutics; T&M 2nd rev. ed. xxxviii
- HR-06-KISIEL: implicit testimony on Heidegger's 1923-24 Aristotle seminars
- HR-07-POGGELER: Wahrheit und Methode; 'Rhetorik, Hermeneutik und Ideologiekritik'; Heidegger und die Griechen

**Volume role**: Ch 2 author (interview) + theoretical interlocutor + living witness; Heidegger's student 1923-24, intellectual antagonist post-1933, lifelong interlocutor.

**Controversy**: DG6 (hermeneutic displacement of rhetoric — substantive loss vs. continuous engagement); DG2a (Gadamer's internal correction: Heidegger 'missed' the technē side); DG5c (productive middle on pathos — bewährt). Pöggeler treats Gadamer as ALLY (Gadamer 'had to admit hermeneutics has older roots in rhetoric').

**Cross-pipeline link**: no Gadamer pipeline; T&M as bridge text to hermeneutics-rhetoric tradition.

### Edmund Husserl (cited by Gross-A, Gadamer, Michalski, Hyde-background, Struever, Kisiel-background, Pöggeler)

Heidegger's teacher-foil; phenomenological background that Heidegger eventually breaks from via the Aristotle-rhetoric route. Gadamer p.49, p.53 documents Heidegger's strategic positioning between Marburg and Freiburg. Michalski p.67, p.76 reads Heidegger as 'recasting the reflexive phenomenology of Husserl as a hermeneutic phenomenology.' Pöggeler n.1 (Husserl Festschrift 1929). Volume consensus background; minimal controversy.

---

### Wilhelm Dilthey (cited by Gross-A, Michalski, Struever, Pöggeler)

Heidegger's hermeneutic-of-life proximate source; the Geisteswissenschaft-vs-Naturwissenschaft predecessor whose distinction Heidegger 'overextended' but who also returned the rhetorical sensus communis to philosophy. **Works**: Einleitung in die Geisteswissenschaften (Pöggeler n.4); Weltanschauung und Analyse des Menschen seit Renaissance und Reformation (Gross n.8, Pöggeler n.36, Struever). For Gross, Dilthey returns rhetoric to philosophy but overextends the natural/human partition; for Pöggeler, Dilthey's Renaissance-and-Reformation analyses inform Heidegger's pathos-via-Christian-tradition reading; for Struever (via Makkreel/Rodi), Dilthey's realist temporality (Innewerden, not Vorstellen) is Heidegger's antecedent.

---

### Cicero (cited by Gross-A, Gross-B, Gadamer, Hyde, Struever)

Roman rhetorical tradition's primary source. **Works**: De inventione, De oratore (1.8.32, 2.44.186-187, 2.44.189, 2.52.214, 3.12, 3.14.55, 3.16.61), De officiis (1.6.19, 1.7.22), De legibus, Orator (33.118). For Gross, the Roman 'compromise' (logos + pathos by sheer fiat) Heidegger rejects; for Hyde, the Roman recovery that keeps rhetoric joined to the heart and to civic moral duty (Section V); for Gadamer, the classical-philology study material that prepared him for Heidegger's rhetorical Aristotle.

**Controversy**: Cicero as Roman compromise critiqued (Gross) vs. Cicero as positive Roman recovery (Hyde); the dispute concerns whether Roman rhetoric is the right antidote to Heidegger's late-Heidegger restriction.

---

### Quintilian (cited by Gross-A, Gross-B, Gadamer, Hyde, Struever-with-Hobbes)

Roman rhetorical tradition (continued). **Works**: Institutio oratoria — 6.1.7 (reluctant defense), 6.2.29 (phantasia definition), 6.2.31 (murder-scenario example), n.28 (Gross). Gadamer's school study material. For Gross, the canonical phantasia-account Heidegger reverses (Roman fantasy-model — fear as imagination-driven, individual-mentation-first).

---

### Augustine (cited by Gross-A, Gross-B, Pöggeler)

Christian rhetorical-theological tradition. **Works**: De civitate Dei (p.33); 'Pelagianische Schriften' (p.33); the Augustinian timor castus / timor servilis distinction. For Gross, a stage in the patristic-medieval pathē-genealogy culminating in Luther; for Pöggeler, the Christian-theological scaffolding underneath Heidegger's seemingly Aristotelian fear-analysis (Rhet. II.5).

---

### Martin Luther (cited by Gross-A, Gross-B, Michalski, Hyde-via-Gross, Kisiel, Pöggeler)

The Affektenlehre tradition's culmination (Gross's central genealogy). **Works**: Sermo de poenitentia; Tischreden (especially the 'Dialectica docet, Rhetorica movet' formula and the 'rhetoricatur' formulation of how the Holy Ghost is 'rhetorized' when the past Passion of Christ becomes belief). Per Dockhorn, Luther's principle 'Belief realizes itself in affect — must complete itself in affect — because reason is incapable of making past and future present' grounds Heidegger's hermeneutics of facticity. For Michalski, Luther's sola scriptura principle is Heidegger's hermeneutic model applied to Aristotle (sui ipsius interpres). For Kisiel, Luther is invoked at Heidegger's Rectoral-Address-period to legitimate Prussian-Lutheran leadership ethos ('Si gesturus principatus ut sciam rem esse populi non meam privatam').

**Controversy**: Luther as central genealogical bridge from medieval theology to Heidegger (Gross) vs. Luther as principle of textual interpretation (Michalski sola scriptura) vs. Luther as Heidegger's political-philosophical legitimation source (Kisiel WS 1933-34) — three distinct Luther-receptions in the same volume.

---

### Hannah Arendt (cited by Gross-A, Gross-B, Gadamer, Struever, Kisiel)

Heidegger's student → political-philosophy heir; Kisiel's Ch 6 co-titular figure. **Works**: The Human Condition (1959) — 175, 177, 183, 187, 197; Was ist Politik? (Piper 1993) — ii, 48-52; Between Past and Future (Viking 1968) — 154f, 263, 220, 241, 84; Essays in Understanding 1930-1954 (Harcourt 1994) — 1-23, 428-447, esp. 442; 'Philosophy and Politics' Social Research 57 (1990); 'A Conversation with Günter Gaus' (1964); 'What is Existenz Philosophy?' Partisan Review 13 (1946).

**Controversy**: DG4a — is Arendt the redemption of the protopolitical project Heidegger abandoned (Kisiel), or its translation/transformation into post-1945 modernity? Gross reads Arendt as already-rhetorical-Heideggerian; Villa's reading (critiqued by Gross) takes the Heidegger-Arendt relation as ruptural. Gadamer's biographical anecdote ('an interesting, talented Jewess') illustrates Heidegger's rhetorical-pedagogical dominance.

---

### Klaus Dockhorn (cited by Gross-A, Gross-B, Struever)

Single-source pivot for the Gadamer T&M discussion. **Work**: review of Wahrheit und Methode in Göttingische Gelehrte Anzeigen 218 (1966), pp.169-206 — extensively cited (Gross n.8, n.48; Struever n.4). Dockhorn shaped the Lutheran-rhetorical-theological genealogy Gross develops. For Struever, the rhetoric-as-formation thesis (Heidegger sees rhetoric not as teaching-manual tradition but as 'second formation, indeed alternate formation to philosophy').

---

### Friedrich Nietzsche (cited by Gross-A, Gross-B, Michalski, Struever, Pöggeler)

Multiple roles. **Works/loci**: 'mobile army of metaphors' (On Truth and Lies — Gross n.3); 1872 'History of Greek Eloquence' lecture (Löwith's transcript title; Michalski p.68); Birth of Tragedy §15 (Pöggeler n.5); Heidegger Nietzsche I 48-49 (Gross-B n.49 — pivotal for diagnosis of 1933 turn); Heidegger's 1936-37 Nietzsche lectures; Heidegger's 1938 essays; Pöggeler's own Friedrich Nietzsche und Martin Heidegger (n.39).

**Controversy**: Nietzsche as alternative-origin foil (Gross) vs. Nietzsche as Heidegger's rhetorical antecedent natural philologist (Michalski) vs. Nietzsche as the figure Heidegger 'fails to honor' on rhetoric (Pöggeler) vs. Nietzsche as Heidegger's bridge to Nazi-period thought (Gross-B). Pöggeler treats Nietzsche as the figure Heidegger uses to cap the metaphysical tradition AND the figure whose rhetoric-attentive side Heidegger fails to honor.

---

### G.W.F. Hegel (cited by Gross-A implicit, Michalski, Pöggeler)

Background figure; final formulator of metaphysical thinking in the principle of reason (Dilthey via Pöggeler n.4); critiqued by Heidegger in the scholastic logos-tradition (Michalski; GA 18 9f, 15f, 335f — Porphyry/Boethius/Kant/Hegel as data of Wirkungsgeschichte). Pöggeler's own Hegel work (Hegels Idee einer Phänomenologie des Geistes 1993; Idealismus und Romantik n.39).

---

### Giambattista Vico (cited by Gross-A, Struever, Pöggeler)

Anti-Cartesian rhetorical-humanist tradition. **Works**: De nostris temporis studiorum ratione (1708, published 1947 by Helmut Küpper) — Pöggeler n.14; New Science — Pöggeler n.20; Liber metaphysicus — Pöggeler n.20; Pöggeler's own 'Vico und die humanistische Tradition' n.20. For Struever (p.125), Vico exemplifies 'timely figurative strategies, the creative fudging of language in legal fictions' — rhetorical-temporal practice in legal fiction. For Gross, Gadamer's primary recovery-source for sensus communis-as-phronesis. For Pöggeler, the topics-tradition exemplar Heidegger 'in conversation' with Pöggeler 'would not reconsider' — Vico 'wrote in Latin and Italian, therefore following the Roman transformation of the legacy of the ancients.'

**Controversy**: Vico as the topics-tradition exemplar Heidegger REJECTS (Pöggeler P3); for Pöggeler the rejection is conclusive evidence of Heidegger's restriction.

---

### Rudolf Bultmann (cited by Gadamer, Michalski, Kisiel implicit, Pöggeler)

Marburg theological neighborhood SS 1924. For Gadamer (p.50), Heidegger's successful collaborator ('I tried to bring the two together, but that failed completely, whereas it worked with Bultmann and Heidegger'). For Michalski (p.65), parallel kerygma-project to Heidegger's Destruktion: 'striving in corresponding fashion to release the authentic kerygma of the New Testament from the distorting conceptuality of Greek philosophy.'

---

### Paul Natorp (cited by Gadamer, Michalski, Pöggeler)

Marburg Neo-Kantian backdrop. For Gadamer, Heidegger's elder colleague who recommended Heidegger to Marburg (n.2); Gadamer's first doctoral supervisor; 'still had contact with him for another year and a half' after the call. The logos-as-reason doctrine Heidegger breaks via logos-as-legein. Gadamer p.53: 'we have not done enough with this, namely to understand the influence the later Natorp had on the younger Heidegger.'

---

### Nicolai Hartmann (cited by Gadamer, Michalski, Pöggeler)

Marburg colleague. The well-meaning Aristotle-reader whose reading 'did not actually transfer' (Gadamer pp.47, 49-50, 53) — 'carefully read Aristotle' but it 'did not actually transfer to his own thinking'; Heidegger by contrast made Aristotle 'suddenly come alive.' Succeeded Scheler in Cologne.

---

### Michel Foucault (cited by Gross-A, Pöggeler; with Index of Names support)

Rhetorical genealogy tradition (with Bourdieu et al.); antihumanist appropriator of Heidegger Gross opposes (cohort listing p.18: Foucault, Bourdieu, de Man, Hillis Miller). Pöggeler Index of Names: pp.2, 3, 18, 20 (multiple essay references). Tier A status borderline; included for cohort-significance.

---

### Jacques Derrida (cited by Gross-A, Gross-B, Pöggeler via Nietzsche-as-rhetorician link)

**Works**: Margins of Philosophy (1968 / Bass trans. Chicago 1982) — n.36 (Gross-A) / Note 36 (Gross-B). The 1968 Margins-of-Philosophy treatment of differance/mouvance is the primary 'misreading' of Heideggerian middle voice — Derrida's middle voice 'neutralizes' rather than 'interanimates' (Gross critique). Pöggeler treats Derrida as Nietzsche's rhetorical inheritance line.

## Tier B (figures cited by exactly 2 contributors; selected single-contributor figures included for thematic / Phase 0 coherence)

### Auerbach (Erich) — Pöggeler + Kisiel implicit
*Mimesis* — topology in literature. For Pöggeler, the rhetorical-literary topology Heidegger left behind.

### Curtius (Ernst Robert) — Pöggeler (sole; Index lists pp.162-163 across two essays)
*Europäische Literatur und lateinisches Mittelalter* (n.7) — topoi of researching a place (Toposforschung). Counter-Cartesian humanism Heidegger did not pursue.

### Max Scheler — Gadamer + Kisiel + Pöggeler
**Works**: Heidegger's 1928 obituary (GA 26); Ressentiment work (Pöggeler's own 'Ressentiment und Tugend bei Max Scheler' n.34). Phenomenologist; Heidegger's critical foil; subject of de partibus animae/animal typo-correction anecdote (Gadamer pp.52-53). For Pöggeler, the ressentiment-virtue analyst Heidegger leaves behind when he 'ceases any longer to set passions and feelings over against virtues.'

### Pierre Bourdieu — Gross-A + Kisiel
*The Political Ontology of Martin Heidegger* (Stanford 1991), 79. 'Heideggerian habitus'; political ontology. The sociological critic of Heidegger's das Man whom Kisiel partly accepts and partly redirects.

### Jürgen Habermas — Gross-A + Pöggeler
**Works**: Gross n.18 ('Habermas leading the charge'); Pöggeler n.16 (Theorie und Praxis: Sozialphilosophische Studien). Communicative-rationality target of 'rhetorical' critique. For Gross, the Enlightenment-rationalism foil to whom Gadamer's T&M cedes ground; for Pöggeler, ALLY (Habermas agrees with Hennis that practical philosophy could establish ties to rhetoric).

### Paul Ricoeur — Gross-A + Pöggeler
Hermeneutical neighborhood.

### Walter Bröcker — Gross-B (n.7 implicit) + Gadamer + Pöggeler
SS 1924 student transcripts (= Karl Löwith's per Gadamer). Bröcker poetry-rejection anecdote (Gadamer p.56) secures Gadamer's claim that 'poetic interpretation was [Heidegger's] weakest area.' Source of Bröcker variant at GA 18 110 (Gross sides with Bröcker against Michalski-Schalk — DG9).

### Karl Löwith — Gross-A (n.4) + Gross-B (n.4) + Gadamer + Kisiel + Pöggeler
**Work**: Löwith transcript of SS 1924 — 'Aristoteles: Rhetorik II', DLA Marbach no. 75.7452. Auditor of SS 1924; primary documentary evidence for SS 1924's character. Heidegger 'just before Löwith died, wrote him such a wonderful letter that Löwith and his wife were completely charmed' (Gadamer p.51).

### P. Christopher Smith — Hyde + Struever (n.8)
*The Hermeneutics of Original Argument: Demonstration, Dialectic, Rhetoric* (Northwestern 1998) 23, 27-29, 214. Direct rhetorical-Heideggerian neighborhood. For Hyde, the enthymeme as 'radically redirected' by Heidegger toward emotional take-up; for Struever, the incarnate-argument framing supporting anti-foundationalism.

### Pierre Aubenque — Hyde + Struever
**Works**: *Le problème de l'être chez Aristote* (PUF 1994); 'Logos et pathos' in *Corps et âme: sur le De anima* (Vrin 1996, 37-49). French Aristotle-and-rhetoric reading. For Struever, the 'rhetorized psychology' / 'practical anthropology' gloss; calls *Rhetoric* a 'practical anthropology.'

### Gorgias — Gross-A (n.46) + Hyde
*Encomium of Helen* — 'all who persuade do so by molding a false argument.' For Hyde, the Sophistic position Aristotle overcomes; for Gross, the rhetorical-art Plato dismissed.

### William Blattner — Struever (13 distinct citations)
*Heidegger's Temporal Idealism* (Cambridge UP 1999). Struever's main interlocutor for time; supplies manifold-temporality framework Struever partially corrects (Blattner reads BT as eroding Alltäglichkeit; Struever recovers SS 1924's political Alltäglichkeit-as-timefulness — DG10).

### Vincent Pecora — Gross-A (n.37) + Gross-B (n.37)
'Ethics, Politics, and the Middle Voice' *Yale French Studies* 79 (1991), 203-230. Middle voice / antihumanism. Pecora's neoliberal critique of Heidegger's middle voice overreads continuity between SS 1924 and apathetic antihumanism (Gross critique).

### Luc Ferry & Alain Renaut — Gross-A (n.38, n.41) + Gross-B (notes 38, 41)
*French Philosophy of the Sixties: An Essay on Antihumanism* (Amherst 1990), 22. Neo-Kantian critics of French antihumanism. Their critique applies TO the antihumanist appropriators (Foucault/Bourdieu/Derrida), not TO Heidegger directly (Gross's calibration).

### Herbert Marcuse — Gross-A + Kisiel (n.5-6)
Marcuse-Olafson interview, *Graduate Faculty Philosophy Journal* 6 (1977): 28-40. Heidegger's student → critical theorist. For Kisiel, confirms sociological diagnosis of das Man as Weimar premonition.

### Searle / Sperber / Grice — Gross-A + Gross-B
Speech-act theory cluster (Grice, Searle, Habermas, Sperber). The analytic-pragmatic tradition that has lost what early Heidegger had in 1924 (Gross-B's depoliticization-by-hermeneutics target).

### Ernst Tugendhat — Gross-A (n.23) + Gross-B (p.5)
Heidegger's student → philosopher of language; one of the SS-1924-influenced cohort named alongside Gadamer and Arendt.

### Damascenus / Gregory of Nyssa / Nemesius / pseudo-Dionysius — Gross-B (p.33) [single-contributor cluster]
Christian theological tradition (patristic pathē). For Gross, historical-genealogical waypoint between Aristotle and Luther. **Works**: *De fide orthodoxa* II (Damascenus, p.33); the others appear as a constellation. Listed for group-coherence per Phase 0.

### Thomas Hobbes — Gross-A (p.12) + Struever (p.113, n.13)
First English Aristotle's *Rhetoric* translator (1637). For Struever, Hobbes recognized 'an attribution of an emotion is performative, a political deed, not a description of inner state' — proto-Heideggerian sociality of passions (via Strauss, *Political Philosophy of Hobbes* Chicago 1984).

### Peter Ramus — Gross-A (p.4) [single-contributor; tradition-significance]
The Ramist tradition Heidegger dissociates from; Ramus + Port Royalists + Dumarsais + Fontanier + British Elocutionists = the trope-as-ornament tradition Heidegger departs from.

### Stanley Fish — Struever (n.44) [single-contributor; methodological-significance]
*The Trouble with Principle* (Harvard 1999); 'Boutique Multiculturality.' Struever's contrast: Fish's 'inspired adhoccery' fails because it does not take temporality seriously enough.

### C.S. Peirce — Struever (n.3, n.24) [single-contributor; thematic-significance]
'Reply to the Necessitarians' *Monist* 3 (1893): 526-570; 'Fixation of Beliefs'; 'How to Make Our Ideas Clear.' Pragmatism parallels with Heidegger's revisionism; for Struever, Peirce's anti-mechanism is parallel to Heidegger's rhetorical revisionism.

### Friedrich Schleiermacher — Gross-A (n.19) + Pöggeler
Hermeneutic tradition. For Gross, one of the Affektenlehre-tradition figures Gadamer 'chooses not to reclaim'; for Pöggeler, Gadamer 'had to admit hermeneutics did not originate during the Reformation, thence universalized by Schleiermacher — it had older roots in rhetoric.'

### Heraclitus — Gross-A + Kisiel + Pöggeler
**Citations**: Heraclitus Frag. 119 (via *Letter on Humanism*, Gross p.19); older Heraclitean sense of ethos (Kisiel p.146); Heidegger's 1962 Greece-trip identification with Heraclitus (Pöggeler p.171). Source for Heidegger's ethos = 'abode/dwelling place' equation in *Letter on Humanism*.

### Friedrich Hölderlin — Gadamer + Kisiel + Pöggeler
The poet Heidegger turned to in his later phase. **Works**: GA 39 *Hölderlins Hymnen 'Germanien' und 'Der Rhein'* (WS 1934-35) — 214, 51, 73; GA 53 *Hölderlins Hymne 'Der Ister'* (SS 1942) — 98f, 102. For Kisiel, instantiates the Archaic-Poietic polis-phase (1935-43); for Gadamer, Heidegger's later poetics yielded mixed results; for Pöggeler, Hölderlin replaces Aristotle/rhetoric in the topology of being.
