# Contested Concepts — *Heidegger and Rhetoric* (Gross & Kemmann eds., SUNY 2005)

**Pipeline phase**: Phase 3A supplement (contested-nodes section)
**Generation date**: 2026-05-10
**Source inputs**: 8 Phase 2 unit JSONs

This document records concepts whose **meaning** (not just emphasis) shifts across contributors. A concept is "contested" if (a) at least three contributors hold readings that are not mere specifications of a single shared concept, AND (b) the readings entail different categorial consequences for at least one downstream claim (genre-assignment, BT-trajectory direction, political valence, etc.).

Six core volume concepts qualify.

---

## 1. Pathos — Cognitive Resource vs. Ontological Substrate vs. Protopolitical Disposition vs. Restriction-Moment

**Phase 0 ID**: VT5
**Locus contention**: GA 18 117, 122, 165, 168, 169, 171, 207, 232, 246-263, 262

| Contributor | Reading | Categorial consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **HR-01-GROSS-A/B** | Pathos as pre-cognitive **ontological substrate** — "the very substance in which propositional thought finds its objects and motivation" (Intro p. 4); cosmologically primary (stones, plants, animals, humans, p. 13); the orator dissolved into pre-cognitive Befindlichkeit. The volume's "priority of pathos" thesis (Gross-B titular). | Pathos cannot be merely a rhetorical resource the orator deploys — its register is pre-individuated, the ground OUT of which logos arises (GA 18 262: "the ground upon which language grows"). |
| **HR-02-GADAMER** | Pathē as **fundamental possibilities by which Dasein primarily orients itself** (GA 18 117) — but ***bewährt*** (warranted), preserving argumentative function. The bewährt caveat is the volume's most careful navigation of cognitive-ontological. Speaking is not "first": Sich-Befinden / Betroffensein precedes articulation. | Pathos has argumentative power not via rational demonstration but via the witness of being-affected — neither pure-cognitive-resource nor pure-pre-cognitive-substrate but a productive middle. |
| **HR-04-HYDE** | Pathē as **both cognitive-rhetorical resources** the orator deploys (the orator must "put hearers into the right frame of mind", Rhet. 1377b24/GA 18 163) **AND ontologically primordial existentialia** (Stimmung/Befindlichkeit per BT 179-235). The orator's intentional structure is preserved. | The call of conscience can be both silent ontological-event AND structurally epideictic — the orator's intentional structure is preserved in the call's ontological-rhetorical form. Hyde's chapter is itself epideictic (P8), performing what it argues for. |
| **HR-05-STRUEVER** | Pathē as **Befindlichkeiten oriented to others** (GA 18 207, 232) — "intrinsically social, interpersonal, rather than simply physiological." Heidegger attributes them to whole person, not just soul (122, 192, 197). "Das eidos der pathē ist ein Sichverhalten zu anderen Menschen, ein In-der-Welt-Sein." | Pathos is social-dispositional (not psychological-physiological); supports Gross's substrate-reading while specifying its inter-personal-orientational structure. Aligns with antifoundationalism (mind not at all pure, GA 18 202). |
| **HR-06-KISIEL** | Pathos as **one of three pisteis equiprimordial with aletheia** — close kin to three modes of disclosedness of human situation (Befindlichkeit/Verstehen/Rede); GA 18 165 "ethos and pathos are constitutive for legein itself." Speech finds deepest roots in mood/pathos (GA 18 177). The "not-yet-logos appeal to passions — alogon of orexis — is itself a kind of speech." | Pathos as protopolitical disposition — equiprimordial in the rhetorical-truth nexus; more ontological than Hyde's reading, building on Gross. |
| **HR-07-POGGELER** | Heidegger's gloss **pathos = Befindlichkeit is the MOMENT OF RESTRICTION**: the rhetorical pathos the orator deliberately deploys is dissolved into existential attunement. "The pathetic / pathic is relegated to attunement (Befindlichkeit) and mood (Stimmung), and rhetoric's claim on pathos is dissolved into the existential structure of being-in-the-world." | Negative case: neither cognitive-resource (Hyde) nor ontological-substrate (Gross) is preserved — Heidegger's substitution dissolves both. Constitutive of the "restricted conception" titular thesis. |

**Synthesis-attempt**: Gadamer's bewährt-caveat is the volume's most articulated middle: pathē are pre-articulate ground AND warranted grounds-for-judgment. Hyde maintains the orator-side intentional structure while making pathos ontologically primordial; Struever specifies pathos as social-dispositional (whole-person, anti-Cartesian); Kisiel locates pathos in the pisteis-aletheia equiprimordial register. Pöggeler's negative case stands apart: the substitution itself IS the loss.

## 2. Alltäglichkeit — Timefulness vs. Ordinariness vs. Restriction-to-Everydayness

**Phase 0 ID**: VT8
**Locus contention**: GA 18 110, 138; BT 178 (= H.138); BT §27

| Contributor | Reading | Categorial consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **HR-05-STRUEVER** | **Timefulness** (Struever's coining against "ordinariness"). The catalytic agent of temporality, the solvent of eternity, primary qualification of Miteinandersein. "If Blattner is correct that *Alltäglichkeit* is later eroded by the philosophical program of BT and slides into everydayness, ordinariness, inauthenticity, in the 1924 lectures the extraordinary emphasis on Hellenic political rhetoric as originary matrix is at the same time an emphasis on *Alltäglichkeit* as timeful, or the solvent of eternity" (p. 125). | Alltäglichkeit retains its political-rhetorical richness; the BT slide-into-ordinariness is corrigible by reading SS 1924 forward. Rhetoric colonizes BT from within precisely because Alltäglichkeit is timeful, not ordinary. |
| **HR-01-GROSS-A/B** | The **everydayness Aristotle's Rhetoric II hermeneutically discloses** (BT 178); positively framed; doxa as "the way we have life in time, Alltäglichkeit" (GA 18 138). Gross does not coin a separate translation; reads with the standard rendering but in positive valence. | Alltäglichkeit is the ontologically-rich layer Heidegger ought to have preserved post-BT (his failure to do so contributes to depoliticization). |
| **HR-04-HYDE** | The **everyday in which call of conscience interrupts complacent interpretations**. Aligned with positive Gross reading; Heidegger has BOTH a negative pole (das Man as "mass-like, crowd-like, herd-like" BT 165) AND positive pole (BT 167: "publicness belongs to Dasein's positive constitution"). | Authorizes the rhetorical work of the orator who modifies public moods AND the silent showing-forth of conscience that interrupts everyday inauthenticity. |
| **HR-06-KISIEL** | The **rudimentary ground of rhetorical protopolitics** — everyday speech-situation IS the political situation of Mitsein. Quotes BT 178 / GA 18 110 as load-bearing in the protopolitical reading. | Alltäglichkeit-as-protopolitical-ground enables the Arendtian recovery (Section IX); without it, the public space of plurality-and-natality cannot be reached. |
| **HR-07-POGGELER** | **Restriction** — BT §29's confining-the-political-to-everydayness as the moment when rhetoric's deliberative-public scope is surrendered to das Man. "Does Heidegger assume the political in general to be confined to everydayness and hence just to the businesslike dealings of the they?" (p. 168). | Negative case: Alltäglichkeit IS the restriction. The confinement of the political to everydayness/das Man is the substantive content of "restricted conception of rhetoric." |

**Synthesis-attempt**: Five contributors read Alltäglichkeit positively; Pöggeler reads it as the moment of restriction. The translation contestation (timefulness vs. ordinariness, Struever's coinage) operates within the positive cluster. Pöggeler's reading is structurally distinctive: not contesting the translation but reading the same passage as restrictive rather than constitutive.

## 3. Call of Conscience — Primordial Epideictic vs. Moment-of-Restriction vs. Ethos-as-Resoluteness

**Phase 0 ID**: VT8-related (Hyde-Pöggeler engagement)
**Locus contention**: BT §§54-60 (H.289, 314, 316, 318, 342); GA 18 (per Hyde + Taminiaux + Gadamer testimony on 1923 NE seminar)

| Contributor | Reading | Categorial consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **HR-04-HYDE** | **Call of conscience IS the ontological-primordial form of EPIDEICTIC** — a silent showing-forth (epideixis) of the heart of human existence. BT 318: "Conscience discourses solely and constantly in the mode of keeping silent." Hyde's signature thesis (P1): BT §§54-60 operationalize Aristotle's epideictic genus (Rhet. I.3, 1358b1ff) in the existential register. Etymological warrant: phronesis-phrēn (heart) / syn-eidesis / con-scientia. Taminiaux 1992 (p. 141): Heidegger said "phronesis is Gewissen set in motion." | Grounds the BT-existentialia analysis in Aristotle Rhet. I.3/I.9 + NE VI; positions the call as structurally analogous to Aristotle's epideixis (showing-forth what is). Phase 4 BCAP/BT cross-link directly required. |
| **HR-07-POGGELER** | **BT §§54-60 is precisely WHERE RHETORIC IS LOST, not preserved**. Heidegger 'ceases any longer to set passions and feelings over against virtues' — leaves behind Aristotle and Scheler. Phronesis as Gewissen (1922 Natorp-Bericht) is the conceptual hinge that channels rhetorical-pathic register into ethical-existential register. The Augustinian timor castus/timor servilis distinction is the Christian-theological scaffolding under Heidegger's seemingly Aristotelian fear-analysis. | Negative case: the conscience-block is exactly where rhetoric's deliberate use of pathos as resource is dissolved into existential attunement. The Hyde reading (epideictic-primordial) is therefore a misreading of the substantive content. |
| **HR-06-KISIEL** | **Ethos translated into Entschlossenheit** (Section V) — speaker's display of conviction (GA 18 171); resoluteness = receptive response to call of conscience; speaker's resoluteness brings others to same resolution = kairos = moment of decision in polity/policy. Phronimos = statesman whose responsiveness to crisis-situation generates prudent counsel; phronesis as action of resoluteness receptive to call of conscience (NE VI). | Deliberative-political register (SZ §60), not epideictic-existential register (SZ §§54-60). The call of conscience is read as the deliberative resoluteness-moment, not as the silent showing-forth. |

**Synthesis-attempt**: Hyde and Kisiel both read BT call of conscience as rhetorically structured but disagree on genre (epideictic vs. deliberative); Pöggeler reads the same block as where rhetoric is lost rather than preserved. The three readings are textually grounded; the choice reveals each contributor's rhetorical-genre preference and assessment of Heidegger's-trajectory direction. Phase 4 BT/BCAP cross-link should preserve the three-way structure.

## 4. Continuity of Heidegger 1924 → 1927 → post-1933 — Continuity vs. Rupture vs. Selective-Restriction

**Phase 0 ID**: VT3
**Locus contention**: GA 18 SS 1924 (entire); BT 1927 (esp. §§29-30, §§54-60, §65, §74); Letter on Humanism 1947; Aus der Erfahrung des Denkens 1947; GA 39 / GA 53

| Contributor | Reading | Categorial consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **HR-01-GROSS-A/B** | **Genetic line (SS 1924 → BT) but substantive narrowing**: BT loses the political-rhetorical dimension Heidegger had at GA 18; the 1933 Nazi turn is the political consequence of this depoliticization. Section IV's BT 344 / BT 434 readings document the substantive narrowing. | Rhetoric-as-named-discipline is dropped, but rhetoric-as-ontological-grammar (middle voice, agent/patient interanimation) persists into late writings (1959 "Language" essay). The drop is selective. |
| **HR-02-GADAMER** | **Continuity-with-late-restriction** — SS 1924 → SZ §29 (Befindlichkeit/Mitsein) is continuous (Gadamer's first-person testimony); post-1933 poetics-only turn was "his weakest area" (p. 56). Gadamer aligns with continuity-side on GA 18 → SZ §29 but acknowledges restriction at the level of post-1933 Heidegger. | Partial alignment with Pöggeler on the late-Heidegger pivot to poetics; full alignment with Gross on the GA 18 → BT continuity. Phase 3 should record Gadamer as "continuity-with-late-restriction," not pure-continuity. |
| **HR-03-MICHALSKI** | **Methodologically authoritative continuity** — SS 1924's philological-hermeneutic-phenomenological identification is a constitutive moment for BT, not a station Heidegger eventually leaves. "Finally wound up being systematically drafted in Sein und Zeit, published in 1927" (p. 76). | Heidegger's philology in SS 1924 is the operative element of hermeneutic phenomenology that BT 1927 then systematically drafts. No rupture. |
| **HR-04-HYDE** | **Continuity-with-late-critical-pivot** — rhetoric carries through into BT (§§54-60), but Heidegger's later poetic turn leaves rhetoric's ontological status under-acknowledged. Hyde's Section V is the critical pivot: "a sincere concern for the truth and goodness of rhetoric disappeared from view" (p. 99). | Continuity holds for BT but breaks down post-1947 (call of Being / poiesis replaces epideixis / rhetoric). Heidegger never asks the difference between epideixis and poiesis. |
| **HR-05-STRUEVER** | **Rhetoric colonizes BT from within** — strong continuity thesis. Alltäglichkeit-as-timefulness is the catalytic SS 1924 contribution; Heidegger "may have thought he had effaced rhetoric in his new philosophical program of *Being and Time*" but "rhetoric effectively colonized his philosophy from within" (p. 117). | Continuity is structural (not merely topical): the temporal-political structure of GA 18 IS BT's temporality. SS 1925 GA 20 echoes confirm the continuity into 1925. |
| **HR-06-KISIEL** | **Three-phase succession**: Phenomenological (1923-25, Aristotle's Rhetoric) / Metontological (1933-35, Plato's Republic) / Archaic-Poietic (1935-43, Sophocles' Antigone). Only Phase 1 is rhetorical; the metontological-Führer phase is Heidegger's phronetic miscalculation; the polemos-phase is post-rhetorical. | Rhetoric is RESTRICTED to Phase 1 (agreement with Pöggeler on the fact of restriction) but the *arc* of restriction is itself politically diagnostic (agreement with Gross's depoliticization thesis). Kisiel's diagnosis: Heidegger applied his 1924 framework to inauthentic-doxa Nazi-rhetoric. |
| **HR-07-POGGELER** | **Restriction** — Marburg engagement is bounded; topology of being (1947+) supplants rhetoric in mature thought. Pöggeler tracks BACKWARD from 1947 → 1957 → Marburg → final position. The displacement is itself rhetorically traceable but is conclusive: language becomes "original institution of being in violent words of poetic origin" rather than "means of communication for sake of agreement, rhetoric" (GA 53 102). | Negative case: the architectural-temporal restriction. Pöggeler's reading direction (backward-from-1947) is itself argumentative: opening with 1947 frontloads the topology-of-being concept that displaces rhetoric. |

**Synthesis-attempt**: Six contributors read GA 18 → BT as continuous; one (Pöggeler) reads as restricted; one (Kisiel) makes the rupture three-phase. The pattern: forward-readers (Gross, Hyde, Struever, Michalski) trace 1924 → BT with substantive continuity; backward-reader (Pöggeler) tracks 1947 → Marburg with bounded-episode reading; Gadamer + Kisiel occupy productive middles (continuity-with-late-restriction; three-phase succession). The volume's architecture (open-with-continuity, close-with-restriction) makes the disagreement structural.

## 5. BT 178 / GA 18 110 Reception — Load-Bearing Endorsement vs. Architectural Silence

**Phase 0 ID**: VT9
**Locus contention**: GA 18 110 ("Rhetorik ist nichts anderes als die Auslegung des konkreten Daseins, die Hermeneutik des Daseins selbst") + BT 178/H.138 (Aristotle's Rhetoric II as "first systematic hermeneutic of the everydayness of Being-with-one-another")

| Contributor | Reading | Categorial consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **HR-01-GROSS-A/B** | **Load-bearing positive endorsement** (Intro p. 1 epigraph). Gross's centrality thesis depends on this passage as Heidegger's own self-statement. | Provides Heidegger's authoritative endorsement of rhetoric's foundational role in his hermeneutic project. |
| **HR-03-MICHALSKI** | **Central GA 18 passage in philological exegesis** — Heidegger's identification of rhetoric with hermeneutic of Dasein (GA 18 110, 113, 117, 139) is the load-bearing claim for the prefiguration thesis: Aristotle's Rhetoric prefigures hermeneutic-phenomenological philology. | The passage establishes the volume's most-cited single Heideggerian claim about rhetoric. |
| **HR-04-HYDE** | **Heidegger's BT 178 primary-text endorsement licenses the epideictic-conscience reading**. P2 premise (a): BT 178 statement is a primary-text endorsement of Aristotle's Rhetoric as hermeneutic of everydayness. | Heidegger's own published self-statement of what Aristotle Rhet. II ontologically means; load-bearing for the epideictic-conscience extension. |
| **HR-05-STRUEVER** | **Volume's master identification** — both epigraphs read together (GA 18 110 + BT 178). "The continuity is identification, not analogy." Both anchor the rhetoric-colonizes-BT thesis. | The two passages establish identity (not analogy) between SS 1924's rhetoric-as-hermeneutic-of-Dasein and BT's rhetoric-as-first-systematic-hermeneutic-of-everydayness. |
| **HR-06-KISIEL** | **Central to protopolitical reading** — GA 18 110 (and the parallel BT §29) grounds the protopolitical thesis that rhetoric is equiprimordial with the everyday speech-situation of Mitsein. | Both passages license the volume-wide protopolitical extension. |
| **HR-07-POGGELER** | **DOES NOT FOREGROUND GA 18 110**; the absence is itself significant per HR-07-POGGELER-T2 and Phase 0 VT9. Instead reads BT §29 (which contains substantively similar content) as the moment of RESTRICTION — Heidegger CONFINES the political to everydayness (das Man), thereby surrendering the deliberative-public dimension Aristotle's Rhetoric had named. | Negative case: BT 178 is restrictive, not constitutive. Pöggeler's contrastive omission of the GA 18 110 epigraph is itself a rhetorical-argumentative move. |

**Synthesis-attempt**: Six contributors use GA 18 110 / BT 178 as positive evidence; Pöggeler's contrastive silence is a position. The structural disagreement of VT1 is partially executed at the level of which Heidegger-quotations the contributors foreground — Pöggeler's restriction thesis is partly constituted by his selective non-citation of the passage Gross/Struever use as load-bearing epigraph.

## 6. Heidegger's Politics 1933 — Phronetic Miscalculation vs. Consequence of Depoliticization vs. Proportional Acknowledgment vs. Silence

**Phase 0 ID**: VT4
**Locus contention**: 1933 Rectoral Address; May 26, 1933 Schlageter speech; Letter on Humanism 1947; 1949 "agriculture/extermination" equation (per Wolin); GA 39 51 (Heidegger's self-critique of Nazi enthusiasts)

| Contributor | Reading | Categorial consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **HR-06-KISIEL** | **Phronetic miscalculation** — Heidegger applied his 1924 framework to the Nazi cause he saw as kairos-grasping. Explicit treatment of metontological polis + Schlageter speech + Rectoral Address + Führerstaat (Section VII). "Heidegger's 'will' is conditioned wholly in terms of Aristotle's phronesis: 'will deliberatively grasps the situation in the fullness of its time, in it the kairos is at work.'" Phronetic miscalculation is internal to Heidegger's own 1924 framework. | The Nazi turn is internally diagnostic of failure within Heidegger's own framework — neither contingent nor wholly exogenous. The framework is salvageable; the 1933-35 application is not. |
| **HR-01-GROSS-B** | **Consequence of depoliticization-by-hermeneutics** — the 1933 'resolute Freeman → resolute nation → German Führer' progression follows from BT-era abandonment of rhetorical-political resources Heidegger himself discovered in 1924. 1930s Heidegger on Nietzsche (Nietzsche I 48-49): passion 'displays self-composed superiority characteristic of great will' — the body politic subordinated to the great-will. | The shift from rhetorical-pluralism to great-will-superiority is the rhetorical/political consequence of the hermeneutic turn. Not strict syllogism but structural ascription. |
| **HR-02-GADAMER** | **Heidegger 'erred' politically (p. 55)** but defends with proportional acknowledgment: 'He was the sole university president who occupied a German professorial chair and who did not retain his presidency for even one year.' Footnote 31 cites Gadamer's own "Über die politische Inkompetenz der Philosophie." | Heidegger's political mistake is real (worth acknowledging "at every opportunity") but the case-handling-deficiency is the failure of others to factually note the brevity. Not exoneration but proportion. Does not connect to rhetoric explicitly. |
| **HR-05-STRUEVER** | **Briefly acknowledged**: "the later relation between Heidegger's philosophy and his politics is a troubling one. My claim is simply that rhetorical hermeneutics enabled him to give lucid, deft accounts of basic political elements in SS 1924" (p. 126). | Politically calibrated demurral — affirms rhetoric's positive 1924 contribution; brackets the troubling later relation. |
| **HR-04-HYDE** | **Connected to missing rhetoric's ontological status** — 1949 "agriculture/extermination camp" equation morally-rhetorically inappropriate (Wolin 1990 Politics of Being, 168); absent Cicero-tradition (De oratore, Orator, De officiis, De legibus) would have filled the moral-rhetorical vacuum. P7 connects textual claim (missing rhetoric) to political claim (moral failure) more rhetorically than logically. | Politics-and-rhetoric are bound at the moral level; missing rhetoric's ontological status correlates with moral failure. |
| **HR-07-POGGELER** | **Treated obliquely** — Heidegger "unable to confront the published reports of his actions in 1933" (p. 171) is mentioned but not developed. Letter on Humanism's polemic against "dictatorship of publicness" (Öffentlichkeit) is read for poetic-elemental Kehre, not political evasiveness. Pöggeler's silence on Heidegger's politics IS itself a position. | Negative case: the topology of being supplants both rhetoric AND politics. The opening locates 1947 as 'troubled year' but indictment is occasion, not substantive theme. |
| **HR-03-MICHALSKI** | Does not engage Heidegger's politics directly in the essay; the philological-hermeneutic framing is methodologically neutral. | Phase 4 cross-link will need to flag this as Michalski's structural silence on the political dimension. |

**Synthesis-attempt**: Six contributors with distinct accounts of the rhetoric/politics relation; Kisiel's three-phase table on book p. 149 is the most articulated single position. Gross and Kisiel are mutually reinforcing (per HR-06-KISIEL-T8-cross): Kisiel's phronesis-miscalculation thesis explains WHY Heidegger applied his framework to the Nazi cause; Gross's pathos-genealogy explains WHAT predisposed Heidegger to mistake propaganda-pathos for civic-pathos. Pöggeler's relative silence is structurally distinctive — the topology of being supplants both rhetoric AND politics.

## 7. Das Man / Publicness — Negative Pole vs. Positive Pole vs. Restriction-Site

**Locus contention**: BT §27 (das Man); BT §35 (Gerede); GA 18 64, 73; SZ 298, 384

This concept is contested at a structural level beyond the 6 enumerated above. Brief summary:

| Contributor | Reading |
|---|---|
| **HR-04-HYDE** | BOTH negative pole (das Man as "mass-like, crowd-like, herd-like" BT 165) AND positive pole (BT 167: "publicness belongs to Dasein's positive constitution"). Positive pole authorizes the rhetorical work of the orator. |
| **HR-06-KISIEL** | das Man as path OUT (via SZ 298 resolute openness + SZ 384 communication-and-struggle) AND as Bourdieu/Marcuse trap. Both readings textually grounded. |
| **HR-03-MICHALSKI** | das Man as "average understanding" — the mode in which Dasein comports itself in everyday self-expression; philology investigates this. |
| **HR-07-POGGELER** | das Man = the restriction site. Heidegger "treats publicness only as the mode of being for the they" — the restriction of the political-deliberative to inauthentic everydayness. |

## Notes on Methodology

- **Selection criterion**: A concept is "contested" if at least three contributors hold readings that are not mere specifications of a single shared concept, AND the readings entail different categorial consequences for at least one downstream claim.
- **Source**: All readings drawn from contributors' own concept-tables and position-statements in Phase 2 unit JSONs. No analyst-framework imports.
- **Phase 0 cross-reference**: Six of the seven contested concepts map directly to Phase 0 VT-tensions (VT3-VT5, VT8-VT9, plus VT4 for politics).
- **Phase 4 implications**: Each contested concept is a candidate for cross-pipeline tension resolution at Phase 4 (when the dissertation framework arrives) — different categorial consequences mean different chapter-structuring implications.
