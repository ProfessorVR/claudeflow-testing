**Deliverable**: Phase 3C (NOVEL — most distinctive deliverable of the HR pipeline)

**Compilation date**: 2026-05-10
**Disagreement count**: 11 (DG1-DG11)

## Methodological preamble

The volume *Heidegger and Rhetoric* is unusually engineered: it opens with Daniel Gross's centrality thesis ("Heidegger thus relocates rhetoric at the heart of his fundamental ontology") and closes with Otto Pöggeler's restriction thesis ("Heidegger's Restricted Conception of Rhetoric"). The two essays cite the same primary texts (GA 18 SS 1924; *Being and Time* 1927; *Letter on Humanism* 1947) and reach opposite verdicts about rhetoric's place in Heidegger's mature thought. That titular framing is not a marketing accident — the editors have staged a productive disagreement at the volume's architectural level. The remaining five chapters (Gadamer interview, Michalski, Hyde, Struever, Kisiel) populate the space between, each calibrating its own position relative to the centrality-restriction axis while introducing its own further disagreements with the others.

The disagreement map below extracts 11 distinct disagreements (DG1–DG11) from the Phase 2 analyses of the 8 unit JSONs and Phase 0's tension list. Eight of the eleven map to Phase 0 tensions VT1–VT8 (the original required-disagreement list); three (DG9, DG10, DG11) surface from Phase 2 cross-essay reading at the level of philological detail, translation, and rhetorical-genre choice. Each entry names the disagreement axis, the contributors on each side with page citations, a substantive synthesis explaining *why the disagreement is productive*, and a dissertation-relevance note flagging the actionable lever.

A note on type: I have flagged two "by-omission" disagreements (DG4d — Pöggeler's silence on Heidegger's 1933 commitment; DG8a — Pöggeler's silence on the GA 18 110 epigraph cluster) where one contributor's failure-to-engage is itself a substantive position. Two sub-disagreements (DG2a — Gadamer's internal correction; DG6a-b — Gross's editorial dissent against Michalski + Affektenlehre) are nested within the larger DGs to capture finer-grained productive splits. The most direct intra-volume engagement (DG6 — Gross interrogates Gadamer, Gadamer responds via interview) is structurally unique in the volume.

## DG1: Centrality vs. restriction (volume master tension)

**Phase 0 mapping**: VT1
**Type**: frame-disagreement

The volume's titular tension. **Position A (centrality)** is held by six contributors — Gross (Intro + Section IV), Hyde, Struever, Kisiel, and Gadamer (with qualifications). Heidegger's SS 1924 reading of Aristotle's *Rhetoric* is constitutively formative for *Being and Time*; the GA 18 110 identification ("Rhetorik ist nichts anderes als die Auslegung des konkreten Daseins, die Hermeneutik des Daseins selbst") is a load-bearing positive endorsement of rhetoric as ontological medium; ten BT signature terms (Mitsein/koinonia, Glaube/doxa, Lage, Sorge, Stimmung/pathos, Augenblick/kairos, ekstasis, Angst/phobos, logos, Entschluss/krisis) have rhetorical genealogy. **Position B (restriction)** is held by Pöggeler alone in the closing essay. Heidegger's serious engagement with rhetoric is circumscribed to the Marburg lecture courses 1923–28; rhetoric is *absent* from *Aus der Erfahrung des Denkens* (1947), the *Letter on Humanism* (1947), *Der Satz vom Grund* (1957), and the turn to Hölderlin/Heraclitus. The mature "topology of being" (*Topologie des Seyns*), the Geviert (fourfold), and the post-1947 poetic-cosmic place-figure replace rather than continue the rhetorical engagement.

The productive depth here is not that the contributors cite different texts — they cite the *same* texts (GA 18, BT, *Letter on Humanism*) and reach opposite verdicts. The asymmetry is in tracking direction. Pöggeler tracks **backward** from 1947 (post-Marburg, post-rhetoric) and finds the Marburg engagement a bounded episode that the topology of being supplants. The other six contributors track **forward** from 1924 (Marburg, rhetorical) and find the SS 1924 → BT genetic line constitutive. Pöggeler concedes in his fifth position that even when hidden, Heidegger "touches the rhetorical tradition again and again" — the hiddenness is itself the productive layer. The two positions are not symmetric: the centrality cluster makes constitutive claims that the restriction thesis can dent but not refute (the GA 18 → BT trajectory *is* textually attested); the restriction thesis makes architectural claims that the centrality cluster cannot easily absorb (the topology of being, the Geviert, the turn to poetics *are* post-rhetorical).

Gross-B anticipates the synthesis in his Section IV (book p.41): *rhetoric-as-named-discipline is dropped in late Heidegger, but rhetoric-as-ontological-grammar persists*. The depoliticization-by-hermeneutics thesis predicts Pöggeler's restriction reading as a *symptom* of the post-1924 trajectory.

**Dissertation relevance**: DG1 is the master productive disagreement for any dissertation arguing a sustained Aristotelian-rhetorical strand in Heidegger. The dissertation cannot evade Pöggeler's restriction claim — the textual evidence for the post-Marburg shift (Topologie, Geviert, Hölderlin-turn) is real. The cleanest strategy: distinguish rhetorical-as-named-discipline (which Heidegger drops) from rhetorical-as-ontological-grammar (which the GA 18 → BT trajectory continues into post-1933 Heidegger, however attenuated). The methodological point is that scholarly readings of Heidegger are tracking-direction-sensitive — the choice of starting-point (1947 vs. 1924) constitutes the verdict.

## DG2: Rhetoric as *dynamis* vs. as *technē*

**Phase 0 mapping**: VT2
**Type**: internal-Aristotelian-disagreement-with-volume-consensus

The volume's consensus on rhetoric-as-*dynamis* is unusually strong. **Position A (dynamis)**: Gadamer (Ch 2 p.49: "Heidegger said it: that designation [of rhetoric as *technē*] is incorrect... *dynamis* is the proper definition"), Struever (Ch 5 p.108: rhetoric is "not a *technē*" but *dynamis tou theoresai*), Kisiel (Ch 6 p.146: "rhetoric is first of all not an art but a power, *dynamis*"), Gross (Intro p.6 partial). All four cite Aristotle *Rhetoric* I.2 + GA 18 114 as authority. Rhetoric-as-*dynamis* is universal human capacity, aligns rhetoric with *energeia* rather than productive-poietic craft, and protects rhetoric from instrumentalization. **Position B (technē)** has no defender in the volume. The absence is the productive layer: *technē* is the post-Aristotelian-tradition default (Hellenistic, medieval, Ramist school discipline) Heidegger and Gadamer repudiate. The Subject Index's distribution confirms the asymmetry: *dynamis* citations 16, 21, 49, 60, 84, 106, 108, 111, 112, 116, 120, 146 (twelve pages, six contributors) vs. *technē* citations 49, 61, 106, 108, 115, 118, 139 (seven pages, each as the rejected term).

**Sub-disagreement DG2a (Gadamer's internal correction)**: Gadamer adds a subtle qualification at p.49: "Heidegger was still using *technē*, until he began emphatically using the concept of *energeia*. And so he associated the concept of *dynamis* with the concept of *energeia*, and missed the other one." The "missed" *technē*-side is the productive-poietic dimension that Aristotle himself preserves — Heidegger's choice protects rhetoric from instrumentalization but at the cost of a productive-poietic dimension that fuller Aristotle-reading would also need to recover.

**Dissertation relevance**: DG2 is load-bearing for any dissertation that wants to define rhetoric ontologically (in Heideggerian register) without collapsing it into a hermeneutic capacity-for-listening that loses rhetoric's productive-active dimension. Gadamer's DG2a correction is the dissertation-actionable lever: a recovered rhetorical-ontology must preserve *both* *dynamis* (capacity, universal) *and* a recovered productive sense of *technē*-as-poiesis (not as Ramist handbook-craft, but as Aristotelian making-of-arguments).

## DG3: Continuity vs. rupture in Heidegger 1924–1927–post-1933

**Phase 0 mapping**: VT3
**Type**: trajectory-disagreement-with-three-distinct-positions

The volume stages four distinct positions on the same axis. **Position A (strong continuity)** is held by Gross-A, Hyde, Struever, and Michalski: SS 1924 GA 18 is genetically formative for BT 1927; BT §§29-30 explicitly cite Aristotle *Rhetoric* II via SS 1924; BT 178 endorses Aristotle *Rhetoric* II as "first systematic hermeneutic of everydayness of Being-with-one-another"; BT's manifold-temporality, *Befindlichkeit*, *Sorge*, *Sein-zum-Tode* all have SS 1924 rhetorical-Aristotelian genesis. **Position B (continuity-with-substantive-loss)** is Gross-B's reading: BT inherits SS 1924 genetically but loses the rhetorical-political dimension; Heidegger "dropped his earlier convictions that public speaking could authentically emerge from political community" (book p.40); *Befindlichkeit* replaces pathē but with conceptual loss; the 1933 turn is the political-philosophical consequence of having lost the rhetorical-political resources Heidegger himself discovered in SS 1924. **Position C (three-phase succession)** is Kisiel's mediating reading: Heidegger's three concepts of polis form a strict succession — Phenomenological (1923-25, Aristotle's *Rhetoric* — pathos/ethos/logos), Metontological (1933-35, Plato's *Republic* — leader-guardians-services), Archaic-Poietic (1935-43, Sophocles' *Antigone* — polemos) — in which only the first is rhetorical. The displacement is rhetorically traceable (GA 53 102: language as "original institution of being in violent words of poetic origin" rather than "means of communication for sake of quick easy agreement, rhetoric"). **Position D (rupture / abandonment)** is Pöggeler's reading: post-1947 Heidegger turns to Hölderlin/Trakl/Heraclitus; the Geviert crystallizes a poetic-cosmic place-figure, not a rhetorical-argumentative one; Heidegger's 1958 letter to Pöggeler explicitly brackets "historical connotations" of "topology" — i.e., refuses the rhetorical-topical tradition.

Gross-B's continuity-with-loss reading is the most nuanced: he affirms genetic continuity with Kisiel (a vs. Pöggeler) but joins Pöggeler in flagging substantive loss (b vs. Hyde-Struever-Michalski). Kisiel's three-phase model is the volume's most articulated mediating position — affirms continuity (rhetoric *is* in Phase 1) while affirming displacement (rhetoric is *not* in Phases 2-3). The four positions can be productively triangulated: Gross-B + Kisiel + Pöggeler agree on substantive post-1924 attenuation; Hyde, Struever, Michalski track only the SS 1924 → BT line and do not foreground the post-BT trajectory. The disagreement is generative because the underlying textual record *is* staged: BT 178 / GA 18 110 affirm rhetoric; *Letter on Humanism* distances from rhetoric; *Aus der Erfahrung des Denkens* introduces topology. The textual data supports *both* continuity (1924 → BT) *and* rupture (BT → late Heidegger).

**Dissertation relevance**: DG3 is the structural map for any dissertation that traces Heideggerian rhetoric through the corpus. Kisiel's three-phase table (p.149) is the most useful single artifact: it disciplines the diachronic reading by naming the textual basis of each phase. Gross-B's depoliticization-thesis adds the political-philosophical stakes. Pöggeler's restriction-thesis prevents complacent continuity-readings. The four positions are compatible at different layers.

## DG4: Politics — protopolitical vs. tragic-acknowledgement vs. erred-but-disconnected vs. silent

**Phase 0 mapping**: VT4
**Type**: multi-position-disagreement-with-by-omission

Four distinct positions span the volume. **Position A (affirmative protopolitics)** is Kisiel's (and Struever's partial) reading: SS 1924 develops a "rhetorical protopolitics" equiprimordial with the threefold definition of human; the Nazi turn is a *phronetic miscalculation INTERNAL to Heidegger's own 1924 framework* — Heidegger mistook the Trommelnder-Führer-rhetoric for kairos-grasping civic rhetoric. The framework is salvageable; the 1933-1935 application is internally diagnostic of failure. Arendt's *Human Condition* recovers the protopolitical strand. **Position B (tragic-acknowledgement)** is Gross's (especially Section IV) reading: rhetoric is political through-and-through; Heidegger's 1933 turn is not biographically contingent but the political-philosophical consequence of having lost the rhetorical-political resources he himself discovered in SS 1924. The body politic gets subordinated to "the so-called great will" in Heidegger's 1930s Nietzsche reading. **Position C (erred-but-disconnected)** is Gadamer's (and Hyde's partial) reading: Heidegger "erred when he did this" (the rectorate); the political mistake created distance between Heidegger and Gadamer but does *not* impeach the philosophy. Gadamer concedes the political error but does not connect it to rhetoric specifically; footnote 31 refers to Gadamer's own "Über die politische Inkompetenz der Philosophie" for the longer treatment. Hyde's Section V (epideictic-conscience absent, agriculture/extermination equation) is closer to Gross's tragic-acknowledgement but operates at a more specifically rhetorical-ethical register. **Position D (silent — by-omission)** is Pöggeler's reading: 1947 is mentioned as "troubled year" (Heidegger "indicted on account of his National Socialist entanglement") but the indictment is the *occasion* for *Aus der Erfahrung des Denkens*, not its substantive theme. Heidegger's "unable to confront the published reports of his actions in 1933" is mentioned (p.171) but not developed. The silence is itself a position: the topology of being supplants both rhetoric *and* politics.

The most productive tension is between Kisiel's protopolitics-as-framework-consequent reading and Gadamer's erred-but-disconnected reading. Gross-B sits structurally with Kisiel on framework-consequent but offers a different mechanism (depoliticization-by-hermeneutics rather than phronesis-miscalculation). Pöggeler's silence frames the volume's closing — the contributor whose "restricted conception" thesis would seem most amenable to political diagnosis declines to develop it. The disagreement is productive because it stages the very question dissertation work on Heidegger's politics must engage: is the Nazi turn philosophically consequent or biographically contingent?

**Dissertation relevance**: The Kisiel-Gross synthesis — Nazi turn as framework-consequent (Kisiel) for reasons of depoliticization-by-hermeneutics (Gross) — is the most defensible scholarly position the volume itself articulates. Pöggeler's silence is dissertation-actionable: if a "restricted conception" thesis declines to develop the political consequence, then the centrality cluster has more dissertation-purchase on the political stakes.

## DG5: Pathos as cognitive resource vs. ontological substrate

**Phase 0 mapping**: VT5
**Type**: register-disagreement-with-productive-middle

Four positions on one axis. **Position A (cognitive-rhetorical resource)** is Hyde's reading: Aristotle's pathē are deliberate rhetorical resources for the orator; pathē are constituents of judgment (*krisis*), not noise. The Heideggerian *Ruf des Gewissens* operates structurally as primordial epideictic rhetoric. Hyde keeps the orator's intentional structure intact: pathē are "rooted in the emotional fabric of a person's existence" *and* deployed by the orator. **Position B (ontological substrate)** is Gross's reading: pathos is "the very substance in which propositional thought finds its objects and motivation" (Intro p.4) — pre-cognitive ontological substrate. Without pathos, "no traffic between inside and outside, self and Other, individual and community." Gross dissolves the orator into pre-cognitive *Befindlichkeit*: pathos is the structural condition for logos, not a resource logos deploys. **Position C (productive middle — *bewährt* pathos)** is Gadamer's reading: pathē are "fundamental possibilities by which Dasein primarily orients itself" (GA 18 117) — the ground of logos — but pathos is *bewährt*: warranted, not arbitrary. This protects the thesis from collapse into pure-affective relativism. *Betroffensein* (being-affected) is the pre-articulate condition Gadamer claims as his own advance beyond Heidegger. The *bewährt* caveat occupies the productive middle: pathē are pre-articulate (Gross-side) but argumentatively warranted (Hyde-side). **Position D (pathos relegated to attunement — LOSS)** is Pöggeler's reading: Heidegger's gloss in GA 18 (ethos = *Haltung*; pathos = *Befindlichkeit*) is the moment Heidegger SUBSTITUTES an existential-ontological category for the rhetorical category, thereby *losing* the pathos-as-rhetorical-resource that the orator deliberately deploys. Pöggeler's reading forecloses both Hyde's epideictic preservation and Gross's substrate-reading: Hyde's BT-§§54-60 conscience-analysis is precisely where rhetoric is *lost*, not preserved.

Hyde's cognitive-resource reading and Gross's ontological-substrate reading are not incompatible at different layers (Hyde: rhetorical-genre-level; Gross: existential-ontological-level), but they yield different downstream commitments. Gadamer's *bewährt*-caveat is the most defensible middle. Pöggeler's reading is the structural antagonist of both Hyde and Gross.

**Dissertation relevance**: DG5 is critical for any dissertation that uses pathos as a load-bearing concept in a Heideggerian register. The translation pathē → *Befindlichkeit* is the dissertation's most consequential terminological move. Hyde's reading allows preservation of rhetorical-genre distinctions (epideictic, deliberative, forensic) at the existential-ontological level. Gross's reading produces a more ambitious ontological claim (pathos as constitutive of community-as-such) but at the cost of the orator's distinct intentional structure. Gadamer's *bewährt*-caveat is the most defensible middle position. Pöggeler's loss-thesis is the negative case the dissertation must address.

## DG6: Hermeneutic displacement of rhetoric (gain or loss?)

**Phase 0 mapping**: VT6
**Type**: intra-volume-direct-engagement (Gross interrogates Gadamer; Gadamer responds via interview)

The volume's most direct intra-volume engagement. **Position A (substantive loss)** is Gross's reading: the displacement of rhetoric by hermeneutics — exemplified by Gadamer's *Truth and Method* — entails a "political and ethical loss" that "depoliticizes" the priority of pathos. Gadamer "downplays" the loss (Intro p.5); Gadamer "cedes ground... to the very rationalists [Habermas] he sets out to criticize"; Gadamer "chooses not to reclaim for rhetoric the doctrine of affections (Affektenlehre) as taught by the likes of Luther or Schleiermacher." **Position B (continuous engagement)** is Gadamer's interview position: at the *conceptual level*, rhetoric and hermeneutics are coextensive — "In truth, rhetoric is the overarching concept for everything that has to do with the other" (p.60); "It is the same dynamis!" (p.60). At the *institutional-pedagogical level*, Gadamer admits a displacement: "I no longer felt that I was at the apex of my philological knowledge" (p.62); he never held a class on rhetoric.

**Sub-disagreement DG6a (Bröcker vs. Schalk transcript variant at GA 18 110)**: Gross's Note 1 sides with Bröcker's reading ("aristotelischen Rhetorik") against Michalski's Klostermann edition (which follows Schalk's "aristotelischen Logic"). The variant is conceptually consequential and Gross flags it against the volume's editor (Michalski himself contributes Ch 3). See DG9 below for the elevated treatment.

**Sub-disagreement DG6b (Gadamer "missed" Affektenlehre reclamation)**: Gross's Section IV develops Luther's Affektenlehre as the secret history of the hermeneutics of facticity (via Dockhorn 1966). Gadamer's interview neither affirms nor denies this Lutheran genealogy. The disagreement is by-omission: Gross's Lutheran-rhetorical-theological genealogy is offered as the substantive recovery Gadamer declined.

The disagreement resolves at *two different levels*. At the conceptual level, Gadamer's claim and Gross's claim are compatible — both make rhetoric/hermeneutics coextensive. At the institutional-substantive level, Gross's loss-thesis stands: T&M did not develop the Affektenlehre genealogy, and the modern speech-act tradition (Grice, Searle, Habermas, Sperber) inherits an impoverished disposition. Gadamer himself partially affirms this at p.62. The most generative reading takes Gadamer's *bewährt*-pathos doctrine (DG5) as the move that PARTIALLY recovers what T&M proper had elided.

**Dissertation relevance**: DG6 is essential for any dissertation positioning itself in the Heidegger-Gadamer hermeneutic-rhetorical neighborhood. The institutional-conceptual two-level resolution is the actionable lever: conceptually align with Gadamer, institutionally side with Gross. The Affektenlehre genealogy (Dockhorn, Luther, Schleiermacher) is Gross's substantive recovery and the dissertation-actionable bridge.

## DG7: Phenomenology as philology (Michalski) vs. phenomenology as protopolitical (Kisiel)

**Phase 0 mapping**: VT7
**Type**: methodological-disagreement-over-the-same-text

Both Michalski and Kisiel cite GA 18 110 (Rhetoric-as-hermeneutic formula) but draw different categorial consequences. **Position A (phenomenology as philology)** is Michalski's reading: SS 1924 engages in philology in a rigorously academic sense — measured by enlistment of philologists (Jaeger, Diels, Spengel, Bonitz, Themistius, Simplicius, Nietzsche), application of genuinely philological methods (lexicology, etymology, authenticity criticism), and centering of the original Greek text. Heidegger "explicitly rejects the title of philosophy in favor of philology even for this region that exceeds the horizon of classical philology" (p.72). Philology is a *necessary element* of hermeneutic phenomenology. Michalski's editorial status (GA 18 editor for Klostermann 2002) gives this framing scholarly weight. **Position B (phenomenology as protopolitical)** is Kisiel's reading: Heidegger's 1923-24 confrontation with Aristotle's *Rhetoric* (and *Politics*, *NE*) produces a "rhetorical protopolitics" equiprimordial with the threefold definition of human. GA 18 is the genesis text of Heidegger's rhetorical-political project, with Arendt's *Human Condition* as the protopolitical strand's recovered afterlife. Kisiel's table on p.149 implicitly treats GA 18 as *primarily political* — the philological exercise is in service of the protopolitical-rhetorical reading, not its constraint.

The disagreement is productive because it surfaces a meta-question: is GA 18 a philological exercise that exceeds classical philology in the direction of dialectic and philosophy (Michalski), or is GA 18 a political-historical genesis text where the philological apparatus serves the protopolitical project (Kisiel)? The two readings are not strictly incompatible (Heidegger's procedure *is* both philological *and* protopolitical), but they prioritize differently.

**Dissertation relevance**: DG7 is methodologically consequential. Any dissertation citing GA 18 must declare its reading-protocol. Michalski's reading disciplines the dissertation against political-philosophical over-reading; Kisiel's reading licenses the protopolitical interpretation. The most defensible position takes Michalski's editorial authority as constraint (the philological discipline IS Heidegger's stated method, GA 18 4, 333) while accepting Kisiel's broader political implication.

## DG8: BT 178 reception — positive endorsement vs. moment of restriction

**Phase 0 mapping**: VT8
**Type**: reception-disagreement-over-load-bearing-passage

The most concentrated textual disagreement in the volume — a single passage (BT 178 / GA 18 110) is the site where centrality-cluster and restriction-thesis directly diverge. **Position A (positive endorsement)** is held by Hyde (BT 178 as anchor, cited multiple times), Struever (BT 178 as epigraph for the Alltäglichkeit-as-timefulness essay), Kisiel (BT 178 as constitutive for SS 1924 protopolitics), and Gross (BT 178 as Heidegger's own admission of the rhetorical genealogy). The Heidegger passage: "Contrary to the traditional [scholastic] orientation, according to which rhetoric is conceived as the kind of thing we learn in school, this work of Aristotle [*Rhetoric* II] must be taken as the first systematic hermeneutic of the everydayness of Being with one another." **Position B (moment of restriction)** is Pöggeler's reading: BT §29's reading of Aristotle's *Rhetoric* as "first systematic hermeneutic of everydayness" is RESTRICTIVE, not constitutive: Heidegger thereby CONFINES the political to everydayness (*das Man* / *Öffentlichkeit*), not extending it to deliberative public assembly. "Does Heidegger assume the political in general to be confined to everydayness and hence just to the businesslike dealings of the they?"

**Sub-disagreement DG8a (GA 18 110 epigraph cluster — Pöggeler's by-omission)**: GA 18, 110 — "Die Rhetorik ist nichts anderes als die Auslegung des konkreten Daseins, die Hermeneutik des Daseins selbst" — is the volume's most-cited single passage. Gross uses it as epigraph (Intro p.1); Struever uses it as epigraph (Ch 5 p.105); Hyde takes it as license (Ch 4 p.82); Kisiel reads it as protopolitical (Ch 6 p.132). Pöggeler does *not* foreground this passage in Ch 7. The by-omission disagreement is structural: the passage four contributors treat as load-bearing is the passage Pöggeler declines to engage as positive evidence.

The disagreement cannot be resolved by reading the passage harder — it requires a meta-level interpretive choice about Heidegger's interpretation of "everydayness": is *Alltäglichkeit* the rich, time-fraught, political-rhetorical mode Heidegger *discloses* (centrality reading), or is *Alltäglichkeit* *das Man* / *Öffentlichkeit* / *Gerede* — the inauthentic mode Heidegger *confines* the political to (restriction reading)? Struever's "*Alltäglichkeit* as timefulness" (P2) is the most articulated rebuttal to Pöggeler's confined-everydayness reading.

**Dissertation relevance**: DG8 is dissertation-actionable: any reading of BT 178 must address Pöggeler's restriction reading. Struever's "*Alltäglichkeit*-as-timefulness" is the strongest single rebuttal — and the dissertation can extend it by argued textual work on the BT §§27-29 cluster (*das Man* + *Befindlichkeit* + Aristotle *Rhetoric* II reception).

## DG9: Gross n.1 correcting Michalski editorially at GA 18 110 (Bröcker vs. Schalk transcript variant)

**Phase 0 mapping**: emerges as DG6a elevated; not in Phase 0 list
**Type**: philological-correction-against-volume-editor

Unique in the volume: a single textual variant becomes the site of intra-volume editorial disagreement. **Position A (Bröcker reading — "aristotelischen Rhetorik")** is Gross's: his Section IV Note 1 sides with Bröcker's transcript reading at GA 18 110: "Sofern man vergißt, nach der konkreten Funktion der aristotelischen *Rhetorik* zu fragen." This makes the passage's reference to rhetoric explicit — supporting the volume's titular thesis. **Position B (Schalk reading — "aristotelischen Logic")** is Michalski's, implicit via the Klostermann 2002 edition: Schalk's transcript reading "aristotelischen *Logic*" is the published canonical text. Michalski's own essay (Ch 3) does not thematize this variant — silence is implicit endorsement.

The variant is conceptually consequential: "forgetting to ask about the concrete function of Aristotelian *Rhetoric* / *Logic*" is a different claim. Gross's preference for Bröcker is consistent with the centrality reading; Michalski's Schalk-following is consistent with his philological-conservative methodology (DG7).

**Dissertation relevance**: DG9 is a dissertation-actionable philological note: any citation of GA 18 110 must specify which transcript reading is followed. Gross's Bröcker reading aligns with the centrality thesis but is non-canonical; Michalski's Schalk reading is canonical. Dissertation strategy: follow Michalski's canonical text and acknowledge the variant in a footnote, *or* follow Gross's preferred reading with explicit philological justification.

## DG10: Struever's "timefulness" coinage vs. Hyde's epideictic-temporal reading

**Phase 0 mapping**: newly surfaced in Phase 2
**Type**: translation-and-emphasis-disagreement

Both contributors read BT 178 as load-bearing positive endorsement (DG8a position-a) and both read pathē as temporally-existentially operative. The productive divergence is at the translation level. **Position A (Alltäglichkeit as timefulness)** is Struever's coinage: *Alltäglichkeit* means timefulness — not "ordinariness" but the timely character of practical life, the catalytic agent of temporality, the primary qualification of *Miteinandersein*. Struever explicitly contests the "ordinariness" translation, coining "timefulness" as proper rendering. Blattner's reading of *Alltäglichkeit* as eroded into "everydayness, ordinariness, inauthenticity" in BT is partially corrected. **Position B (epideictic-temporal restructuring via pathē)** is Hyde's: anger, fear, and pity are not psychological epiphenomena but operations that "restructure existential temporality and spatiality." BT 179-182, 228-235 distinguish the temporal/spatial workings of fear from anxiety — same conceptual operation as Aristotle *Rhetoric* II. Hyde does not propose "timefulness" as English rendering, working within standard "everydayness" translation.

The disagreement is generative because it surfaces a translation-stakes question: does the English rendering of *Alltäglichkeit* shape what kind of rhetorical-political work the term can do?

**Dissertation relevance**: DG10 is dissertation-actionable at the translation register. Struever's "timefulness" is the most philologically-aggressive rendering of *Alltäglichkeit* in the volume and addresses a real translation problem. Dissertation work citing BT §27 / §29 / Aristotle *Rhetoric* II should engage Struever's coinage explicitly. The dissertation can layer Struever's timefulness onto Hyde's epideictic-temporal restructuring without contradiction.

## DG11: Ethos translated into Entschlossenheit (Kisiel) vs. Ruf des Gewissens (Hyde)

**Phase 0 mapping**: newly surfaced in Phase 2
**Type**: rhetorical-genre-translation-disagreement

Both contributors translate Aristotelian ethos into a BT category and ground the translation in Heideggerian textual evidence. The divergence reveals each contributor's rhetorical-genre preference. **Position A (ethos as Entschlossenheit — deliberative-political-prohairesis)** is Kisiel's reading: Section V translates Aristotelian ethos into BT's *Entschlossenheit* (resoluteness, BT §60). The rhetorician's ethos qua *phronesis kai arete kai eunoia* (Rhet. 2.1 1378a10) becomes Heidegger's resolute Being-its-Self that enables authentic Being-with. The deliberative-political ethos is constitutive of the protopolitical project. Kisiel's reading emphasizes *prohairesis* — the deliberative-political-volitional dimension of ethos. **Position B (ethos as Ruf des Gewissens — epideictic-existential)** is Hyde's reading: ethos is translated into BT's *Ruf des Gewissens* (call of conscience, BT §§54-60). The silent call of conscience is the primordial epideictic showing-forth of the heart of human existence. *Phronesis* IS *Gewissen* "set in motion" (per Taminiaux 1992); the etymological chain *phronesis*–*phrēn* (heart) / *syn-eidesis* / *con-scientia* confirms the identity.

The choice between the two readings is a matter of which Aristotelian rhetorical genre best captures the existential-ontological work Heidegger asks of ethos. Kisiel's *Entschlossenheit* is more political-deliberative; Hyde's *Ruf des Gewissens* is more existential-epideictic.

**Dissertation relevance**: DG11 is dissertation-actionable at the rhetorical-genre level: the dissertation can mine the unresolved question of which Aristotelian genre (deliberative, forensic, epideictic) best maps onto Heideggerian existentialia. The cleanest dissertation move is to treat the three genres as differentiated rhetorical-existential registers — Hyde's epideictic-conscience reading captures one mode; Kisiel's deliberative-Entschlossenheit captures another. The Aristotelian genre-typology can structure a fuller Heideggerian rhetorical anthropology.

## Volume disagreement architecture (summary)

The 11 disagreements form a coherent architecture:

1. **DG1 (master tension — VT1)** stages the volume's centrality-restriction frame.
2. **DG2–DG3 (VT2-VT3)** stage productive disagreements about Aristotelian definition (dynamis vs. technē) and Heideggerian trajectory (continuity vs. rupture).
3. **DG4 (VT4)** disperses across four positions on Heidegger's politics — the most multi-positioned disagreement in the volume.
4. **DG5–DG6 (VT5-VT6)** stage productive disagreements about pathos (cognitive resource / ontological substrate / productive middle / lost) and about hermeneutic displacement of rhetoric.
5. **DG7 (VT7)** stages a methodological disagreement at the level of how to read GA 18.
6. **DG8 (VT8)** stages the most concentrated textual disagreement — BT 178's reception.
7. **DG9** is a unique editorial dispute (Gross against the volume's own GA 18 editor Michalski).
8. **DG10–DG11** are translation-and-genre disagreements that surface only at the Phase 2 cross-essay level.

The two by-omission disagreements (DG4d — Pöggeler silent on 1933; DG8a — Pöggeler silent on GA 18 110 epigraph cluster) cluster on the volume's closing essay. Pöggeler's restriction thesis is structurally consequential: it cannot account for the volume's most-cited single passage as positive evidence, and it declines to develop the political consequence of restriction.

The most direct intra-volume engagement is DG6 (Gross interrogates Gadamer; Gadamer responds via interview). This stages the volume's most articulated productive disagreement at the level of two-author exchange.

The dissertation-most-actionable disagreements are:
- DG1 (the master frame — every dissertation argument must position itself)
- DG3 (the trajectory map — Kisiel's three-phase table is the most useful single artifact)
- DG5 (the pathos register — translation pathē → Befindlichkeit is the consequential terminological move)
- DG8 (the BT 178 reception — any reading of BT 178 must address Pöggeler's restriction reading; Struever's timefulness is the strongest rebuttal)

The volume's value lies precisely in this staged disagreement architecture: by reading the essays *as a productive disagreement system* rather than as parallel monographs, the dissertation can mine the volume for its most distinctive theoretical-philological output.
