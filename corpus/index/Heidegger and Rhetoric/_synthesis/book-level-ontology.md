## 2. Volume Trajectory Narrative (3E)

### 2.1 Gross's framing (Introduction, HR-01-GROSS-A/B)

The volume opens with Daniel M. Gross's Introduction, which stages the volume's most ambitious centrality thesis: "Heidegger thus relocates rhetoric at the heart of his fundamental ontology" (Intro p. 4). Gross constructs a rhetorical genealogy of *Being and Time* by tracking how ten signature BT terms (*Mitsein/koinōnia*, *Sorge*, *Stimmung/pathos*, *Augenblick/kairos*, *Glaube/doxa*, *Lage*/being-in, *Entschluß/krisis*, *Angst/phobos*, *ekstasis*, *logos*) descend from the rhetorical-Aristotelian apparatus of SS 1924 GA 18, citing Heidegger's own BT 158 admission. The Introduction's first part (Gross-A) develops the positive thesis through a reading of *being-moved* (*Sein-in-Bewegung*) and the German middle voice as Heidegger's stylistic-philosophical instrument; the second part (Gross-B) develops "the priority of pathos" — pathos as the ontological substrate of logos that the Roman compromise, the Christian *Affektenlehre* tradition (via Augustine, Luther, Dockhorn), and modern speech-act theory (Searle, Grice, Habermas) progressively obscured. Gross's most controversial move is to read Heidegger's 1933 Nazi turn as the political-philosophical consequence of having abandoned, between 1924 and 1927, the rhetorical-pluralist resources he himself had discovered.

### 2.2 The middle constellation (Chapters 2–6)

The middle constellation amplifies different rhetorical registers without fully reconciling them.

**Gadamer's first-person interview (HR-02-GADAMER, Ch 2)** testifies to Heidegger's SS 1923–24 Aristotle seminars and stakes the volume's central textual claim — rhetoric is *dynamis*, not *technē* (Rhet. I.2 / GA 18 114) — through the formula "It is the same *dynamis*!" (p. 60) identifying rhetorical capacity with hermeneutic understanding. Gadamer's testimony also splits Heidegger's rhetorical practice (rhetorically masterful as orator, anti-conversational with audience), making conversation (*Gespräch*) the corrective Gadamer's hermeneutics developed.

**Michalski's editor's-eye view (HR-03-MICHALSKI, Ch 3)** reframes GA 18 as a rigorous hermeneutic-phenomenological philology that "exceeds the horizon of classical philology" (p. 72) — Heidegger "rejects the title of philosophy in favor of philology" even for the region exceeding it. Philology in Heidegger's sense = "passion for the knowledge of that which has been expressed and of its self-expression" (GA 18 4, 333) — the necessary element of hermeneutic phenomenology.

**Hyde (HR-04-HYDE, Ch 4)** develops the volume's most articulated single bridge across five Roman sections: the BT call of conscience (§§54–60) IS a primordial form of *epideictic* rhetoric — a silent showing-forth (*epideixis*) of the heart of human existence. The move grounds the BT-existentialia analysis in Aristotle Rhet. I.3/I.9 + NE VI *phronēsis* (via Taminiaux: "phronesis is *Gewissen* set in motion"). Hyde's Section V critically pivots to Heidegger's later turn from rhetoric to poetry — "a sincere concern for the truth and goodness of rhetoric disappeared from view" (p. 99).

**Struever (HR-05-STRUEVER, Ch 5)** coins the term "timefulness" to render *Alltäglichkeit* (against "ordinariness") and argues rhetoric is "not a *technē* but a *dynamis tou theoresai*" (Rhet. 1355b26 / GA 18 122) — a potentiality-for-theorizing whose temporal incompleteness "colonizes Being and Time's temporal program from within" (p. 117). Struever uniquely engages Aristotle's biological treatises (*De motu animalium*, *De partibus animalium*) as intricated with the *Rhetoric* in GA 18 207–219.

**Kisiel (HR-06-KISIEL, Ch 6)** introduces "rhetorical protopolitics" across nine sections: Heidegger's 1923–24 confrontation with Aristotle's *Rhetoric*, *Politics*, and *NE* produces a politics equiprimordial with the everyday speech-situation. But Heidegger's three polis-concepts (Phenomenological 1923–25, Metontological 1933–35, Archaic-Poietic 1935–43) document a strict succession in which only the first is rhetorical, with the metontological-*Führer* phase exposing Heidegger's phronetic miscalculation. Kisiel closes by tracking Arendt's recovery (Section IX) of the protopolitical strand Heidegger abandoned.

### 2.3 The Pöggeler closing (Chapter 7)

Pöggeler's closing chapter (HR-07-POGGELER, Ch 7) supplies the volume's countertitle and negative case. Tracking BACKWARD from 1947 (*Aus der Erfahrung des Denkens*, the introduction of "topology of being"), Pöggeler argues Heidegger's serious engagement with rhetoric is "restricted" in two senses: temporally bounded to the Marburg lectures 1923–28, and substantively curtailed (the conscious surrender of pathos as orator-resource into *Befindlichkeit* as existential attunement; the confinement of publicness to *das Man*; the failure to ask the Perelmanian justice question; the rename of the WS 1925–26 lecture course from "Aristotle: Rhetoric" to "Basic Concepts of Aristotelian Philosophy"). The mature topology of being — crystallized in the *Geviert* and the post-1953 Hölderlin-Trakl-Heraclitus engagements — supplants rhetoric; Heidegger's December 1958 letter to Pöggeler explicitly disavows the rhetorical-historical connotations of the term "topology." Yet Pöggeler concedes that even when hidden, Heidegger "touches the rhetorical tradition again and again" (Ebbinghaus on *Erörterung* as carrying contemplation through prescribed places; Ijsseling on Isocrates' panegyric). The volume thus closes by problematizing its own opening claim — making the volume itself a productive tension between Gross's forward-from-1924 centrality reading and Pöggeler's backward-from-1947 restriction reading.

- **Contested flag**: applied where contributors disagree about meaning, not just emphasis. Six concepts so flagged.

#### 1. Rhetoric
- **canonical_name**: Rhetoric
- **german_lemma**: Rhetorik
- **greek_lemma**: rhētorikē
- **translation**: rhetoric
- **definition**: The volume's titular concept. Six of seven contributors converge on the dynamis-reading drawn from Aristotle Rhetoric I.2 (1355b25) and Heidegger's GA 18 114: rhetoric is a power (dynamis) of being-human, not a technē. Gross frames rhetoric as 'relocated at the heart of Heidegger's fundamental ontology' (Intro p. 4); Struever as 'colonizing Being and Time from within' (p. 117); Pöggeler titularly opposes both — rhetoric is 'restricted' to the Marburg lectures 1923-28 and supplanted by the post-1947 topology of being. The volume thus stages the disagreement structurally: open-with-centrality (Gross) → middle constellation amplifies → close-with-restriction (Pöggeler).
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: core
- **aliases**: rhetorical ontology (Gross), rhetoric as life-science (Struever), rhetorical protopolitics (Kisiel), rhetoric as hermeneutic of Dasein (Michalski, after GA 18 110)

#### 2. Pathos
- **canonical_name**: Pathos
- **german_lemma**: Stimmung / Affekt (correlate: Befindlichkeit)
- **greek_lemma**: pathos / pathē
- **translation**: passion / emotion / mood / disposition
- **definition**: The volume's most-contested core concept. Gross frames pathos as 'the very substance in which propositional thought finds its objects and motivation' (Intro p. 4) — the ground of logos itself (HR-01-GROSS-B-P1, GA 18 262). Gadamer reads pathē as 'fundamental possibilities by which Dasein primarily orients itself' (Ch 2 p. 57, GA 18 117). Hyde treats pathē as both cognitive resources the orator deploys AND ontologically primordial existentialia (BT 179-235). Struever: pathē are 'Befindlichkeiten oriented to others' (GA 18 207, 232) — not psychological but social-dispositional. Pöggeler diagnoses Heidegger's gloss pathos = Befindlichkeit as the conceptual moment of restriction: the rhetorical pathos the orator deploys is dissolved into existential attunement. Kisiel reads pathos as one of the three pisteis equiprimordial with aletheia (GA 18 177).
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: core
- **aliases**: Stimmung, Affekt, Befindlichkeit (Heidegger's gloss), the priority of pathos (Gross IV titular), pathē as Befindlichkeiten (Struever)

#### 3. Logos
- **canonical_name**: Logos
- **german_lemma**: Rede / Sprechen
- **greek_lemma**: logos / legein
- **translation**: speech / reason / account / saying
- **definition**: Six contributors converge on Heidegger's SS 1924 logos = legein (speaking, articulating) reading, against the Marburg-Neo-Kantian logos = ratio (reason). Gadamer's foundational realization: 'logos has something to do with speaking. In Marburg's Neo-Kantianism logos was just reason' (Ch 2 p. 47). Michalski reads logos as having three structural moments — legein/legomenon/legein-and-legomenon (GA 18 75-76). Across all contributors: logos has its ground in pathē (GA 18 117) and presupposes the prior attunement (Sich-Befinden / Befindlichkeit). Hyde anchors his epideictic-conscience reading on GA 18 169 ('enthymematic-rhetorical practice is the ground and soil of the logos itself').
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: core
- **aliases**: legein, speech, saying, Rede

#### 4. Mitsein / Being-with-one-another
- **canonical_name**: Mitsein / Being-with-one-another
- **german_lemma**: Mitsein / Miteinandersein / Miteinanderdasein
- **greek_lemma**: koinōnia
- **translation**: being-with / being-with-one-another
- **definition**: The political-rhetorical community presupposed by every speech act. Gross at Intro p. 11: 'it is in Aristotle's Rhetoric that Being-with, koinōnia, is given its definitive form.' Struever names alltägliches Miteinanderreden (everyday speaking-with-one-another) as the primary structural mode Heidegger thematizes in GA 18 155. Michalski reads GA 18 as Heidegger's interpretation of concrete Dasein as Miteinandersein via the basic possibilities of speaking-with-one-another (Miteinandersprechen, GA 18 110). Kisiel makes Mitsein the rudimentary ground of his rhetorical protopolitics; Hyde anchors enthymeme's audience-completion on the Mitsein-soil. Pöggeler reads BT §29's claim about rhetoric as 'first systematic hermeneutic of everydayness of being-with-one-another' as the moment when the political is confined to everydayness (das Man), thereby restricting rhetoric's deliberative-public scope.
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: core
- **aliases**: Miteinandersein, Miteinanderdasein, Miteinandersprechen, alltägliches Miteinanderreden, koinōnia, being-with-one-another

#### 5. Befindlichkeit / Attunement
- **canonical_name**: Befindlichkeit / Attunement
- **german_lemma**: Befindlichkeit / Sich-Befinden
- **greek_lemma**: pathos (correlate)
- **translation**: attunement / state-of-mind / findingness / disposedness
- **definition**: Heidegger's BT §29 term, identified across the volume with Aristotelian pathos via GA 18 122 ('these pathē are not psychological states; they are a Befindlichkeit of the living one in his world'). Michalski makes Befindlichkeit the philologist's mode of attuned-and-comprehending being-in-the-world (befindlich-verstehendes In-der-Welt-sein, p. 75). #### 6. Alltäglichkeit / Everydayness
- **canonical_name**: Alltäglichkeit / Everydayness
- **german_lemma**: Alltäglichkeit
- **translation**: everydayness / timefulness (Struever's coining)
- **definition**: Volume-central term whose contested translation marks the Gross/Struever continuity reading vs. Pöggeler restriction reading. Struever titularly insists Alltäglichkeit means 'timefulness' (her coined term against 'ordinariness') — the catalytic agent of temporality and the primary qualification of Miteinandersein (p. 105-115). Gross reads it as the everydayness that BT 178 names rhetoric the 'first systematic hermeneutic' of. Michalski links it to Heidegger's 'as anyone' (als Man / das Man, p. 76). #### 7. Phronēsis / Practical Wisdom
- **canonical_name**: Phronēsis / Practical Wisdom
- **german_lemma**: Klugheit (Gewissen in Heidegger's gloss per Taminiaux)
- **greek_lemma**: phronēsis
- **translation**: practical wisdom / prudence
- **definition**: Aristotle NE VI virtue, central to four contributors. Gross reads phronesis as the Vico-Gadamer-Heidegger recovery against Cartesian-Kantian intellectualization. Gadamer: 'Phronēsis is a whole virtue; it is not an ability, but a being' (Ch 2 p. 61); phronesis is hexis, not technē. Hyde activates the etymological link phronesis-phrēn (heart) and cites Taminiaux 1992 (p. 141): Heidegger said 'phronesis is Gewissen [conscience] set in motion which makes an action transparent (durchsichtig).' Kisiel reads phronesis as the action of resoluteness receptive to call of conscience; Heidegger's 1933 'will' is conditioned wholly in terms of Aristotelian phronesis grasping kairos. Pöggeler: 1922 Natorp-Bericht characterization of phronesis as Gewissen is the conceptually decisive move that channels rhetorical-pathic into existential-ethical register.
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: core
- **aliases**: practical wisdom, prudence, Gewissen (Heidegger's gloss)

#### 8. Dynamis / Power-Capacity
- **canonical_name**: Dynamis / Power-Capacity
- **greek_lemma**: dynamis (specifically dynamis tou theoresai)
- **translation**: power / capacity / potentiality
- **definition**: The volume's signature Aristotelian correction. Three contributors (Gadamer, Struever, Kisiel) explicitly defend the GA 18 114 dictum that rhetoric is dynamis, not technē (Aristotle Rhet. 1355b25-26: dynamis tou theoresai, 'capacity for theorizing what is persuasive in a given case'). Gadamer makes this the central rhetorical-philosophical question of his interview (Ch 2 §II): 'Heidegger said the technē-designation is incorrect, whereas dynamis is the correct definition.' Struever specifies dynamis tou theoresai as the precise Aristotelian phrase (Rhet. 1355b26 / GA 18 122) and reads it as the temporal-incompleteness that makes rhetoric the proper instrument for politics. Kisiel: rhetoric is 'first of all not an art but a power, dynamis ... cultivated power of situational insight, phronesis' (p. 146). Volume-tension VT2: dynamis vs. technē.
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: core
- **aliases**: dynamis tou theoresai, potentiality for theorizing (Struever), power

- **german_lemma**: Augenblick (Heideggerian correlate)
- **greek_lemma**: kairos
- **translation**: timely moment / opportune moment

- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: core
- **aliases**: Augenblick, timely moment, decisive moment, meson kata praxis (Struever)

- **aliases**: Besorgen, concern, care

#### 11. Call of Conscience
- **canonical_name**: Call of Conscience
- **german_lemma**: Ruf des Gewissens
- **translation**: call of conscience
- **definition**: Hyde's titular concept, structurally central to his epideictic-conscience reading. BT §§54-60: the silent call (BT 318: 'conscience discourses solely and constantly in the mode of keeping silent') as ontologically-prior showing-forth (epideixis) of the heart of human existence. Hyde's signature thesis: the call IS a primordial form of epideictic rhetoric. Cited approvingly by Gross (Intro p. 2) and Kisiel (p. 146: ethos translated into Entschlossenheit = receptive response to call of conscience). Pöggeler reads BT §§54-60 as the moment where rhetoric is LOST, not preserved (P2 targets Hyde directly).
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: core
- **aliases**: Ruf des Gewissens, Gewissen, conscience

#### 12. Fundamental Ontology
- **canonical_name**: Fundamental Ontology
- **german_lemma**: Fundamentalontologie
- **translation**: fundamental ontology
- **definition**: Heidegger's titular project in BT. Gross's signature claim: 'Heidegger thus relocates rhetoric at the heart of his fundamental ontology' (Intro p. 4) — the volume's load-bearing centrality thesis (HR-01-GROSS-A-P2). 'Rhetorical ontology' (Gross's coining) is fundamental ontology approached through its discursive-existential medium. The thesis directly targets the conventional separation of rhetoric (as merely a sub-discipline) from ontology (as foundational). Pöggeler's countertitle (HR-07-POGGELER-P1) directly opposes: rhetoric is NOT a constitutive concept of fundamental ontology; it is restricted to the Marburg lectures.
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: core
- **aliases**: Fundamentalontologie, rhetorical ontology (Gross's coining)

#### 13. Epideictic Rhetoric
- **canonical_name**: Epideictic Rhetoric
- **greek_lemma**: epideiktikon (also epideixis as the act)
- **translation**: epideictic / display rhetoric / showing-forth
- **definition**: Aristotle's third rhetorical genre (Rhet. I.3) alongside deliberative (sumbouleutikon) and judicial (dikanikon). Hyde radicalizes the genre ontologically: epideictic is the showing-forth (epideixis) of what is, especially of the existential structure of the heart. The call of conscience is THE primordial epideictic event (HR-04-HYDE-P1). Gross briefly: 'the rhetoric of praise and blame, or epideictic, gets short shrift in Heidegger's course, as it does in Aristotle's text' (Intro p. 10-11). Kisiel reads Heidegger's May 26, 1933 Schlageter speech as an instance of epideictic operating politically (p. 135-136).
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: important
- **aliases**: epideiktikon, display rhetoric, showing-forth

#### 14. Deliberative Rhetoric
- **canonical_name**: Deliberative Rhetoric
- **greek_lemma**: sumbouleutikon
- **translation**: deliberative rhetoric
- **definition**: Aristotle's first rhetorical genre (Rhet. I.3). Per Gross's reading of GA 18: the genre that 'allows an advocate addressing a political assembly to characterize a problem, lay out concrete possibilities for future action, and forge a common opinion regarding what is to be done' (Intro p. 11, citing GA 18 124-125). Volume-cross-pipeline tension VT10: Heidegger himself foregrounds deliberative rhetoric in GA 18 §§13-14 (BCAP unit U-FP3a), while Hyde imports the epideictic emphasis.
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: important
- **aliases**: sumbouleutikon, deliberative

#### 15. Kinēsis / Being-moved
- **canonical_name**: Kinēsis / Being-moved
- **german_lemma**: Bewegung / Bewegtsein / Sein-in-Bewegung
- **greek_lemma**: kinēsis
- **translation**: motion / being-moved
- **definition**: Gross's subtitular concept and Struever's structural foundation. Heidegger's GA 18 288: 'Die kinesis ist eine Weise des Seins des Daseienden selbst' (motion is a mode of the Being of Dasein itself), reading Aristotle Physics 202b23. Gross: kinesis defined by interanimation of pathetikos (passive-undergoing) and poietikos (active-making); the German middle voice operationalizes this. Struever's signature SS 1924 syllogism: 'If life, then motion, kinēsis. If motion, then time' — kinesis 'gives the authentic Da-Charakter to Dasein, the vital place-time of Being' (p. 106-109). Pöggeler reads kinesis = poietikon + pathetikon at the close of GA 18 as 'the conceptual track for BT's poiesis-emphasis' — the moment pathos is sidelined into Befindlichkeit/Stimmung. Cross-pipeline tension VT12.
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: core
- **aliases**: Bewegung, Bewegtsein, Being-moved (Gross), motion as way-of-being

#### 16. Doxa
- **canonical_name**: Doxa
- **german_lemma**: Ansicht / Dafürsein
- **greek_lemma**: doxa / endoxa
- **translation**: belief / opinion / received view
- **definition**: Six contributors invoke doxa. Gross reads Heidegger's doxa (GA 18 149-151) as 'pretheoretical being-for' — 'authentic being-with-one-another in the world' (eigentliche Entdecktheit des Miteinanderseins-in-der-Welt), characterized by yes-saying-without-examination (Ja-sagen, GA 18 136-137) and openness (Es könnte auch anders sein). Struever: doxa is 'the way we have life in time, Alltäglichkeit' (GA 18 138); endoxa are revisable rhetorical premises. Hyde reads doxa as authentic discoveredness of Mitsein. Kisiel develops doxa twofold (via Arendt): opinion + splendor/fame (dokein = Ansicht + Ansehen) — the space of appearance.
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: core
- **aliases**: endoxa, Ja-sagen, Dafürsein, Ansicht, received opinion

#### 17. Zoon Politikon / Zoon Logon Echon
- **canonical_name**: Zoon Politikon / Zoon Logon Echon
- **greek_lemma**: zōon politikon / zōon logon echon
- **translation**: political animal / animal having speech
- **definition**: Aristotle's twin defining-concepts of human Dasein (Politics I.2). Struever: 'Grundcharakter des Daseins als zōon politikon' (GA 18 104) — human life defined by possession of language (logon echon); life fulfilled by language-use (zoē praktikē meta logon, GA 18 105). Kisiel: zōon logon echon now in textual register of newspapers (modern Dasein as newspaper-reader, p. 132) — three Aristotelian definitions read by Heidegger as equally primordial. Gross: zōon politikon implicit in Mitsein/koinonia framework (Intro p. 11).
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: important
- **aliases**: zōon politikon, zōon logon echon, political animal, animal having speech

#### 18. Sprachlichkeit / Linguisticality
- **canonical_name**: Sprachlichkeit / Linguisticality
- **german_lemma**: Sprachlichkeit
- **translation**: linguisticality
- **definition**: Gadamer's titular concept for Ch 2 Section IV. The medium 'in which mortals live in the speaking of language' (n. 17, citing 'Die Sprache' 1950). Not nominalistically reducible to words: 'pointing is also already language and the whole body speaks too' (book p. 59). 'Everything that can allow something to be understood. Therefore, hermeneutics; thereby its indefinability' (p. 60). 'Linguisticality is only an approximating concept' (p. 59). Gadamer subsumes Sprachlichkeit under rhetoric ('rhetoric is the overarching concept for everything that has to do with the other,' p. 60).
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: important
- **aliases**: linguisticality

#### 19. Hermeneutics of Facticity
- **canonical_name**: Hermeneutics of Facticity
- **german_lemma**: Hermeneutik der Faktizität
- **translation**: hermeneutics of facticity
- **definition**: Heidegger's pre-BT methodological term (GA 63, SS 1923). Gross: the rhetorical genealogy of BT terms 'historicizes' the hermeneutics of facticity that 'predated SS 1924 by at least two years' (Intro p. 2). Gross-B: Luther's Affektenlehre (per Dockhorn) 'opens the way for what would later become the hermeneutics of facticity practiced by both Heidegger and Gadamer.' Michalski: the precondition for hermeneutic phenomenology proper (BT 1927).
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: important
- **aliases**: Hermeneutik der Faktizität, hermeneutics of factical life

#### 20. Hermeneutic Phenomenology / Philology
- **canonical_name**: Hermeneutic Phenomenology / Philology
- **german_lemma**: hermeneutische Phänomenologie / Philologie
- **translation**: hermeneutic phenomenology / philology
- **definition**: Michalski's titular thesis. Heidegger 'rejects the title of philosophy in favor of philology even for this region that exceeds the horizon of classical philology' (p. 72). Philology = 'passion for the knowledge of that which has been expressed and of its self-expression' (Leidenschaft der Erkenntnis des Ausgesprochenen und des Sichaussprechens, GA 18 4, 333). Philology is the necessary element of hermeneutic phenomenology that takes account of the leading function of speech (logos) in the dominance of the interpretedness of Dasein. Hermeneutic phenomenology = Heidegger's recasting of Husserl's reflexive phenomenology by appropriation of fore-having/fore-seeing/fore-grasping. Volume tension VT7: phenomenology vs. philology, resolved synthetically by Michalski.
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: important
- **aliases**: philology, hermeneutic phenomenology, Leidenschaft der Erkenntnis

#### 21. Destruktion
- **canonical_name**: Destruktion
- **german_lemma**: Destruktion
- **translation**: destructuring / destruction (of traditional ontology)
- **definition**: Heidegger's pre-1924 multi-year project of laying bare the primordial concern of Aristotelian thinking by destructuring philosophical-theological systemicity layered upon it (Michalski p. 65). Background framing concept; sets the methodological context for Heidegger's GA 18 Aristotle interpretation as a return to the texts themselves (sui ipsius interpres).
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: peripheral
- **aliases**: destructuring, destruction

#### 22. Rhetorical Protopolitics
- **canonical_name**: Rhetorical Protopolitics
- **translation**: rhetorical protopolitics
- **definition**: Kisiel's titular thesis (Ch 6): the rudimentary politics taking shape in Heidegger's emerging fundamental ontology of 1923-24, where the everydayness of being-with-one-another (a speech-community whose telos is Verständigung) sediments a 'politics' equiprimordial with rhetoric. Speech-as-political. Not full political philosophy but the protopractical layer that frames the speech-situation as already political. Distinct from but congruent with Struever's 'politics of time' (p. 124) and Gross's 'rhetorical pluralism' (Intro V).
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: important
- **aliases**: protopolitics, rhetorical politics, rhetorical pluralism (Gross)

#### 23. Topology of Being
- **canonical_name**: Topology of Being
- **german_lemma**: Topologie des Seins
- **translation**: topology of being
- **definition**: Pöggeler's titular countertitle and structural opposite to Gross's 'rhetorical ontology.' Heidegger's mature post-1947 concept (Aus der Erfahrung des Denkens; Heidegger's Dec 12, 1958 letter to Pöggeler): 'the literal speaking of place,' 'thinking the truth' as 'the disclosure of the self-concealed (Entbergen des Sichverbergens) of being.' The place-figure that supplants both metaphysical essence-articulation and the Marburg Aristotelian-rhetorical engagement. Crystallized in the Geviert (fourfold). Heidegger 'in this term ... was not aware of historical connotations' (i.e., rhetorical topics-tradition) — explicit disavowal of rhetorical genealogy. Kisiel's third polis-concept (archaic-poietic, 1935-43) covers the same textual territory but with opposite valence.
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: important
- **aliases**: Topologie des Seins, topology, speaking of place, Geviert (correlate)

#### 24. Hexis / Settled Disposition
- **canonical_name**: Hexis / Settled Disposition
- **german_lemma**: Gefasstsein
- **greek_lemma**: hexis
- **translation**: habit / disposition / settled state
- **definition**: Aristotelian NE II concept, central to Struever's reading and Gadamer's phronesis-doctrine. Struever: hexis is the Grundbestimmung of aretē (GA 18 185); 'a potential for having; not pure duration but timebound, acquired and lost.' Hexis is dynamis, Gefasstsein (185). 'Hexis is ein Wie des pathos' (GA 18 184) — habit is a mode of pathos, the dispositional ground that makes kairos-grasp possible. Gadamer (Kemmann framing, n. 27 = GA 18 165): 'The interaction with the pathē is a question of hexis: ethos and pathē are constitutive of legein itself.'
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: important
- **aliases**: Gefasstsein, habit, disposition, settled state

#### 25. Middle Voice
- **canonical_name**: Middle Voice
- **german_lemma**: es gibt / die Sprache spricht / bewegen-morphology
- **greek_lemma**: (Greek middle voice)
- **translation**: middle voice
- **definition**: Gross's signature stylistic-philosophical concept. The Greek-grammatical voice that expresses simultaneity of activity and passivity, which Heidegger reproduces in German via morphological play (bewegen-bewegt-Bewegung morphology, es gibt impersonal, 1959's 'die Sprache spricht'). For Gross, the central stylistic-philosophical resource by which Heidegger operationalizes Being-moved (HR-01-GROSS-A-P6). Distinguished from Derrida's differance/mouvance reading (which 'neutralizes' rather than 'interanimates' active/passive). 'Heidegger's middle voice in no way kills the author. Rather it describes in new terms how the author is rhetorically constituted' (p. 24).
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: important
- **aliases**: es gibt, die Sprache spricht, Bewegtsein-morphology

#### 26. Affektenlehre / Doctrine of Affections
- **canonical_name**: Affektenlehre / Doctrine of Affections
- **german_lemma**: Affektenlehre
- **greek_lemma**: (correlate: pathē)
- **translation**: doctrine of affections / theological-rhetorical pathos tradition
- **definition**: Gross-B's signature genealogical bridge. The Christian-theological transformation of Aristotle's pathē running from Augustine, Damascenus, Gregory of Nyssa, pseudo-Dionysius, through medieval Thomas, to Luther. Per Dockhorn: Luther's Affektenlehre 'grounds Enlightenment philosophy' and opens 'the way for what would later become the hermeneutics of facticity practiced by both Heidegger and Gadamer.' The genealogical bridge from Aristotle through Christian theology to Heidegger SS 1924. Struever notes the Patristic-medieval-Stoic Affektenlehre tradition (n. 22).
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: important
- **aliases**: doctrine of affections, Lutheran Affektenlehre

#### 27. Trope
- **canonical_name**: Trope
- **german_lemma**: Begriff (concept) — Gross's pairing
- **translation**: trope / tropology
- **definition**: Gross's revisionary account of Heidegger's elocutio-equivalent. 'A trope, acting in concert with its staid manifestation as a concept (Begriff), marks the contours of contingency' (Intro p. 3). Tropes 'provide the nontheoretical distance necessary to see how we are in our everyday situations and how we are moved.' Distinguished from Nietzsche's 'originary' tropes and from rhetoric-as-ornament. The trope/concept dyad is constitutive, not decorative.
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: peripheral
- **aliases**: tropology, contour of contingency

#### 28. Restricted Conception of Rhetoric
- **canonical_name**: Restricted Conception of Rhetoric
- **translation**: restricted conception of rhetoric
- **definition**: Pöggeler's titular thesis. 'Restricted' = BOTH temporally bounded (Heidegger's rhetoric engagement is circumscribed to the Marburg lectures 1923-28) AND substantively curtailed (Heidegger 'restricted crucial dimensions of Aristotle's Rhetoric on the path to Being and Time' — failing to ask after justice; relegating pathos to Befindlichkeit; surrendering rhetoric to the 'they'). The volume's countertitle: Pöggeler closes the volume by problematizing its opening centrality claim.
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: important
- **aliases**: restriction thesis

#### 29. Disclosedness / Unconcealment
- **canonical_name**: Disclosedness / Unconcealment
- **german_lemma**: Erschlossenheit / Entdecktheit / Unverborgenheit
- **greek_lemma**: alētheia
- **translation**: disclosedness / discoveredness / unconcealment
- **definition**: Heidegger's BT term for the manner in which Dasein and beings are revealed. Struever: GA 18 138 ('doxa is the way we have life in time'); doxa as authentic Entdecktheit of Miteinandersein. Kisiel: pistis equiprimordial with aletheia (Section V titular thesis); three modes of concealment / three modes of trueing, two of which Heidegger attributes to language of rhetoric (p. 144-148). Pöggeler: late Heidegger reads truth as 'unconcealment' from 'strictly non-Platonic and non-metaphysical standpoint,' yet (per Ijsseling) the concept 'adheres strictly to the ancient Greek understanding of language' — the rhetorical tradition hidden but persistent.

- **centrality**: important
- **aliases**: alētheia, Erschlossenheit, Entdecktheit, Unverborgenheit, unconcealment

#### 30. Resoluteness
- **canonical_name**: Resoluteness
- **german_lemma**: Entschlossenheit / Entschluß
- **greek_lemma**: krisis (correlate)
- **translation**: resoluteness / decision
- **definition**: BT §60 existential structure. Gross-B: BT 344 'only authentically Being-their-Selves in resoluteness can people authentically be with one another' — but this BT-confinement (HR-01-GROSS-B-P8) is the depoliticization move. Hyde: the call of conscience summons resoluteness; 'ethics emerges as responsibility of affirming our freedom through resolute choice.' Kisiel: Heidegger's framework translates Aristotelian ethos into Entschlossenheit (the politician/speaker's resoluteness brings others to same resolution = kairos). SZ 384 'communication and struggle' (Mitteilung und Kampf) as path out of leveling Anyone.
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: important
- **aliases**: Entschlossenheit, Entschluß, decision

#### 31. Anyone / Das Man / Publicness
- **canonical_name**: Anyone / Das Man / Publicness
- **german_lemma**: das Man / Öffentlichkeit
- **translation**: the 'they' / Anyone / publicness
- **definition**: BT §27 existentiale. Kisiel: das Man = 'true bearer of the universal of averageness = our language' (GA 18 64); 'domineering prevalence of Anyone in language = doxa, the tacit major premises of rhetoric.' Hyde insists Heidegger has BOTH a negative pole and a positive pole: 'publicness belongs to Dasein's positive constitution' (BT 167). The positive pole authorizes rhetorical work of orator who modifies public moods. Pöggeler: Heidegger 'treats publicness only as the mode of being for the they' — the restriction of the political-deliberative to inauthentic everydayness.

#### 32. Jeweiligkeit / Temporal Particularity
- **canonical_name**: Jeweiligkeit / Temporal Particularity
- **german_lemma**: Jeweiligkeit / jeweiliges Dasein
- **greek_lemma**: (correlate of hekaston)
- **translation**: temporal particularity / particular-while / each-each-time
- **definition**: Struever's signature temporal translation. 'Meson für die praxis ist der kairos' (GA 18 144); 'meson is Jeweiligkeit (temporal particularity — particular while) in respect to the agent' (GA 18 201). Michalski: Heidegger refuses 'each' (jedes) as standard rendering for hekaston; 'Hekaston means what in each case obtains [Jeweiliges] insofar as I linger with it' (GA 18 32). Kisiel: 'temporally particularizing universals' (jeweilige Universalia) — peculiarly temporal universals sensitive not only to distributive each (jede) but also to varying temporal contexts (je nach dem).

- **centrality**: important
- **aliases**: particular while, each-each-time, temporal particularity, Jeweiliges

#### 33. Phobos / Fear
- **canonical_name**: Phobos / Fear
- **german_lemma**: Furcht
- **greek_lemma**: phobos
- **translation**: fear
- **definition**: Volume-central pathos illustration. Gross-B's signature reading (Section IV): fear as relational-temporal Befindlichkeit (GA 18 256-257); fear's structural feature is Möglichkeit / Möglichsein; most fearsome = the Reserved (Zurückhaltenden) and Ironic (Ironischen) (GA 18 257). Fear is 'Befindlichkeit that brings into speech' (GA 18 261). Struever: phobos has 'peculiar tense, voice, and mode' — future tense, voice subjunctive, mode the possible (GA 18 246-263). 'Phobos bouleutikous poiei — fear makes us deliberators' (Rhet. 1383a6-8). Hyde: BT §30 fear (180-182). Pöggeler reads Augustinian timor castus/timor servilis distinction as Christian-theological scaffolding under Heidegger's fear-analysis.
- **type**: concept
- **centrality**: important
- **aliases**: Furcht, phobos, fear

#### 34. Enthymeme
- **canonical_name**: Enthymeme
- **greek_lemma**: enthymēma / enthymeisthai
- **translation**: enthymeme
- **definition**: Hyde's central rhetorical-existential bridge. Rhetorical syllogism operating with few propositions wherein audience supplies missing premise (Rhet. 1356b3, 1357a15-20). Hyde reads enthymeisthai dually: cognitive 'giving consideration' AND emotional 'taking something to heart' (sich etwas zu Herzen nehmen, GA 18 128). 'Enthymeme = den Boden des logos selbst' (GA 18 169). Struever: enthymema-protaseis structure (GA 18 133-134). The enthymeme grounds collaborative deliberation in the soil/ground of the logos.

- **aliases**: enthymēma, enthymeisthai

#### 35. Showing-forth / Aufzeigen / Sage
- **canonical_name**: Showing-forth / Aufzeigen / Sage
- **german_lemma**: Aufzeigen / Sage
- **greek_lemma**: epideixis
- **translation**: showing-forth / showing-and-letting-be-seen
- **definition**: Hyde's structural bridge between Aristotelian epideixis and Heideggerian Aufzeigen. Heidegger's recurrent gloss of language as a 'showing' — early BT and later Sage — operates 'in the same structural register' as Aristotle's epideixis (the act of epideictic). 'To speak means to say, which means to show and to let [something] be seen' (Zollikon Seminars 215). Aligns Heideggerian 'showing-forth' with Aristotle's epideixis.

#### 36. Pisteis / Three Modes of Persuasion
- **canonical_name**: Pisteis / Three Modes of Persuasion
- **greek_lemma**: ethos / pathos / logos; pisteis
- **translation**: three modes of persuasion (ethos/pathos/logos)
- **definition**: Aristotle Rhet. I.2's three pisteis. - **aliases**: three modes of persuasion, ethos/pathos/logos triad
