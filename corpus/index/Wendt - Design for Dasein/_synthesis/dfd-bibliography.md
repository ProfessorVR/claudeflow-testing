# dfd — Bibliography & Interlocutor Map (synthesis)

Two layers: (1) the **full source list** lives in the unit `dfd-07-bibliography-notes.{md,json}` — **120 sources** extracted from Wendt's Bibliography (book p.166) + 205 endnotes (book p.177), grouped into 10 strands (design-theory 37, post-phenomenology 15, actor-network/artifacts 13, phenomenology 12, phenomenology-commentary 11, HCI/ubicomp 10, cognitive-science 7, language/literary-theory 6, other 6, self-citation 3). (2) Below is the **consolidated interlocutor map** — the people Wendt actually puts to work across the indexed chapters (union of all unit `interlocutors`), grouped by role. Counts comfortably exceed the ≥60 gate.

## Phenomenology (classical) — the lineage
- **Martin Heidegger** — the backbone: Dasein, being-in-the-world, ready-to-hand/present-at-hand, withdrawal, care, the equipmental whole, the thing/gathering (jug), technē/poiēsis/physis, standing reserve, enframing, The Turn, being-with. (dfd-00,01,02,03,04,05,06)
- **Edmund Husserl** — intentionality; the subject/object split Heidegger reacts against. (dfd-00,01,04)
- **Maurice Merleau-Ponty** — embodiment: body as ambiguous third party; skills as solicitations; body as a system of possible actions; "speech accomplishes thought." (dfd-00,02,04,05)
- **René Descartes** — the cogito / mind–body dualism, the book's principal antagonist. (dfd-01)

## Post-phenomenology & philosophy of technology
- **Don Ihde** — post-phenomenology; the four mediation relations; designer fallacy; multistability; amplification/reduction; double desire. (dfd-00,04,05,06)
- **Peter-Paul Verbeek** — mediation theory; co-shaping; "technology-to…"; *What Things Do*. (dfd-00,04,06)
- **Bruno Latour** — actor-network theory; actant, script, delegation; matters-of-fact→matters-of-concern; gatherings/*Dinge*; the hotel key. (dfd-03,04,06)
- **Graham Harman** — object-oriented ontology; concealment/unconcealment. (dfd-01,04)
- **Maarten Franssen** — "for-ness" of technical artifacts. (dfd-04,05)
- **Bernard Stiegler** — *Technics and Time*; Dasein's "not yet." (dfd-01)
- **R. L. Rutsky** — the "essence of technology" as dynamic process. (dfd-01)
- **Hubert Dreyfus** — embodied/contextual coping; critique of symbolic AI; knowing-how. (dfd-01,02,05)
