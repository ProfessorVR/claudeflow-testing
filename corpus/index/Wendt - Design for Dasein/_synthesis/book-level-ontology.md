# dfd — Book-Level Ontology

A synthesis of *Design for Dasein* (Wendt, 2015) **as a system**: what the book argues, how its concepts hang together, and what it yields as a design lens for the dissertation. Wendt-says throughout; the rhetorical-ontology mapping lives in `dfd-rhetorical-ontology-bridge.md`.

## 1. The thesis
Design is **embodied philosophy** — "doing philosophy with the hands" (dfd-00, p.6/pdf 8). The book's project is to make **phenomenology** the basis of a **phenomenological design thinking**: a stance that treats *experience* as the proper object of design and *praxis* (theory-informed practice) as the way design knows. Its through-line, stated in the embodiment chapter, is a move **"from a metaphysics of design to the phenomenology of design"** (dfd-05, p.133/pdf 135): stop asking what a designed thing *is* and start designing what it *does* and what it *means* in use.

## 2. The lineage (the book's spine)
Wendt runs one evolutionary arc and rides it into design:
**Husserl** (intentionality; the subject/object split) → **Heidegger** (Dasein, being-in-the-world, the tool analysis, care, the thing, the question of technology) → **Merleau-Ponty** (the body as the ground of perception and meaning) → **post-phenomenology** (Ihde, Verbeek: technologies as constitutive *mediators*) → **object studies** (Latour/ANT, Harman/OOO, thing theory). Design thinking's own evolution (Simon → Schön → Rittel-Webber → Cross → Krippendorff) is folded in (dfd-02), with Wendt's claim that **a phenomenological point of view is design thinking's next evolution** (dfd-02, p.69/pdf 71; dfd-06).

## 3. The system — eight load-bearing moves
1. **Anti-Cartesianism.** The self/world and mind/body splits are dissolved; the theory/practice and thinking/making dualisms in design are their symptoms (dfd-01, dfd-05). Three "disembodied" designer-assumptions are cleared: users are rational subjects, conscious of their goals, and think-then-act (dfd-05, p.123/pdf 125).
2. **The tool analysis.** Equipment is **ready-to-hand** (withdrawn, acted-through) until **breakdown** throws it to **present-at-hand**; these are a *continuum*, and breakdown is *revelatory*, not mere failure (dfd-01).
3. **Mediation is constitutive.** Post-phenomenology: a medium does not convey a ready world; it **co-constitutes** what is real and *amplifies while it reduces*. Ihde's **four relations** (embodiment / hermeneutic / background / alterity) replace the bare tool pair (dfd-04).
4. **Affordance & embodiment.** Objects are **perceived by what they afford before they are named** (Gibson); the body is "a system of possible actions"; meaning is **embodied**, distributed, and use-contingent (dfd-05).
5. **Meaning over utility.** **Sense** (one context) vs **meaning** (the sum of imaginable contexts); products are abandoned when they become *meaningless*, not merely unusable; the design goal is **meaning-formation**, and the proper interface goal is **transparency, not invisibility** (dfd-05, dfd-01).
6. **The problem-solution paradox.** Problem and solution **co-evolve** and must be understood together; the linear/waterfall model is a Cartesian artifact; design is **abductive** (reasoning to *what could be*) and proceeds by *thinking-through-making* (dfd-03, dfd-02).
7. **Multistability & emergence.** A technology *is what it can do* across contexts; **creative misuse** is expected; designer **intention is not primary**, and outcomes are emergent — design creates *conditions of possibility*, not guarantees (dfd-04, dfd-06).
8. **Design as care / being-in-the-world.** Design is a modern **mode of being-in-the-world** (Mitcham); "**care has to be designed into being**" (Fry); objects are **more than material** — they mediate, co-shape users, and carry an ethics (advocate preferable worlds; refuse dark patterns) (dfd-00, dfd-02, dfd-06).

## 4. Central tensions (see `tension-edges.json`)
Wendt is not a passive synthesizer. He **contests** Bonsiepe's "interface as central domain" and Simon's positivist artifact; **corrects** Heidegger (designers *can* design at the systems level; the object "things on its own"); **qualifies** Verbeek (background relations are about proximity, not only awareness) and Krippendorff (use is not simply secondary to meaning); rejects the **invisibility/intuition** ideal in favor of **transparency**; and reads the designer's intentionalism as a **designer fallacy**.

## 5. What it yields as a design lens
The book is, for the dissertation, a **design-conditions lens** for virtual environments. Its payoff is concentrated in three places:
- a **vocabulary** for how a designed environment forms meaning through situated, embodied engagement rather than transmitting representations (dfd-05);
- a **mediation typology** (Ihde's four relations + multistability) for analyzing VR/VLE interfaces (dfd-04; see manual §C);
- a set of **design principles** (manual §A) that locate on the actualization-of-desire chain: mediation at **A0/A1**, the taking-as at **A2**, meaning/*doxa*/emotion at **A3**, completed action and *hexis* at **A4** (see `dfd-rhetorical-ontology-bridge.md`).

The convergence point is **being-in-the-world**: Wendt designs *for* it; the rhetorical ontology theorizes emotion/action *as* its affective architecture. That is why *Design for Dasein* is the natural design-side complement to the Aristotelian-Heideggerian chain — and the instrument for Part III's goal of designing more effective virtual learning environments.
