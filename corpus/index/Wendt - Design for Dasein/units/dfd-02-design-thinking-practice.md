## Overview
Chapter 3 turns from "what it means to be" to "what it means to be a designer," and argues that a phenomenological point of view is the **next evolution of design thinking**. It runs in three movements: (1) *Being a Designer* — craft, the body, speech, and empathy; (2) *Thinking Like a Designer* — abduction and the four-part design-thinking process; (3) *Design Thinking and Phenomenology* — why design's claims about experience need phenomenology.

## 1. Being a Designer (p.49 / pdf 51)
- The "thinking" in design thinking exceeds the Cartesian thinking-subject: **thinking inherently involves making**; externalizing thought outside the head is "not only beneficial, but necessary"; "it is only through making that we are able to think." Prototypes exist to **explore ideas in physical space**, not merely to produce objects. Slogan: "**Design is a Daseinerly process, and Dasein is a designerly process**" (p.49 / pdf 51).
- *Being* vs. *thinking like* a designer: identity is at stake (Flusser — the shoemaker "makes a shoemaker out of himself," p.50 / pdf 52). The designer is defined against the scientist: science **discovers** what *is*; design **invents** what *could be* (p.50 / pdf 52).
- Everyone designs (Cross): design is inherent to human cognition. Buchanan, via Aristotle, names design as **forethought** — the "architectonic" / master art of making (p.51 / pdf 53). Because Dasein "exists ahead of itself," it is futural, hence designerly (p.51 / pdf 53).
- But *being a designer* is special. Heidegger's cabinetmaker's apprentice must "answer and respond" to the wood: the **essence of craft is the relatedness to materials**, not mere tool-facility (p.52 / pdf 54). For experience designers the "material" is **experience itself**; soft (critical-thinking) skills outrank hard (software) skills; principles over rules (p.52–53 / pdf 54–55).
- Capacity to design rests on two bodily capabilities — **to speak and to use the hands**. The hand makes *and* thinks (Derrida: the hand cannot be discussed "without speaking of technics"; Heidegger: thinking is a "handicraft"; the hand "designs and signs") (p.53 / pdf 55). Prototyping enacts three dimensions of craft: the hand makes the prototype, speech+hand give feedback, thinking incorporates both (p.54 / pdf 56).
- Design as **"un-throwing"** (Oosterling's reading of Dutch *ontwerpen*): an active coping with thrown-ness, illustrated by Freud's *fort-da* game (p.54 / pdf 56). Speech is a thinking mechanism (Merleau-Ponty: "speech ... does not translate ready-made thought, but accomplishes it"); user-interviewing makes speech the medium of truth-making (p.55 / pdf 57).
- **Empathy** is recast through Heidegger's *being-with*: empathy does not build the first bridge between closed subjects; it is **grounded in Being-with** and "becomes possible" only on that basis (p.56 / pdf 58). Design research is a purposeful enactment of being-with; empathic listening (silence) is its optimal form (p.57 / pdf 59).
- **Craft vs. design:** in craft societies designing is not separate from making (potter works the clay directly); design is a *modern* phenomenon born of the division of labor (Ford, Taylorism) that split planning from making (p.58–60 / pdf 60–62). Mitcham ties craft to **local materials + the body**. The remedy to alienated, divided labor is care: "Care has to be designed into being, and craft is the means" (Fry); *technē* is at once making and **knowing** (p.61 / pdf 63).

## 2. Thinking Like a Designer (p.61 / pdf 63)
- Cross's **three cultures**: sciences (natural world / controlled experiment / truth), humanities (human experience / analogy-metaphor / justice), design (artificial world / modelling-synthesis / appropriateness) (p.61–62 / pdf 63–64).
- **Abduction is the logic of design** — reasoning toward *what could be* (vs. deductive truth, inductive science); future-oriented, individual, intuitive; "the abductive suggestion comes ... like a flash" (Peirce) (p.62–63 / pdf 64–65). Design thinking **mediates** the resulting biases through prototyping, iteration, and critique, keeping the designer between the purely intuitive and purely analytical (p.64 / pdf 66).
- **Thinking through making** / embodied know-how: expertise is "tacit-ed" bodily activity, articulable only after the act (Tonkinwise; Dreyfus on the felt "on-track" sense) (p.64–65 / pdf 66–67).
- The four-part process: (1) **empathy for alternate contexts** (inductive — discover *what is*); (2) **framing** problems/opportunities (interpretive sense-making, humanities-like); (3) **ideation** of many solutions (abductive; counter **premature convergence**); (4) **validation** of executions (analytical) — prototyping as "the design equivalent of phenomenological praxis theory," observing artifacts-in-use from a present-at-hand position (p.66–69 / pdf 68–71). Schön's **reflective practice** links theory and practice into one praxical movement (p.69 / pdf 71).

## 3. Design Thinking and Phenomenology (p.69 / pdf 71)
A phenomenological point of view is the next evolution of design thinking: **wicked problems** (Rittel & Webber) demand rigorous study of human experience, which design theory lacks and phenomenology supplies (p.70 / pdf 72). Wendt cites the evolution artefacts → reflexive practice → problem-solving → sense-making → **creation of meaning** (Krippendorff) (p.70 / pdf 72), and assigns phenomenology a twofold role: (1) a basis for design's claims about experience; (2) design as the **enacting** of experience through products/services (p.71 / pdf 73). The chapter hands off to post-phenomenology (embodiment, specific devices, technology-as-mediator).

## Design principles (extracted)
- **Think through making** — externalize; prototype to *think*, not only to produce (p.49 / pdf 51).
- **Orient to your material** — for XD the material is *experience*; cultivate relatedness to it (p.52 / pdf 54).
- **Design care into being** — keep makers bound to outcomes; craft is the means (p.61 / pdf 63).
- **Mediate premature convergence** — structure ideation/critique timing (p.68 / pdf 70).
- **Validate in praxis** — observe artifacts-in-use, not theoretical objects (p.69 / pdf 71).
