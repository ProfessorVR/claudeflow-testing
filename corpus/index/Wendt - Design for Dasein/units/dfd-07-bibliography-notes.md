# dfd-07 — Bibliography + Notes (source/interlocutor digest)

**Source unit:** Thomas Wendt, *Design for Dasein: Understanding the Design of Experiences* (2015), Bibliography (book p. 166 ff.) and Notes/endnotes (book p. 177 ff.). pdf = book + 2.

This is a **source/interlocutor digest**, not an argument analysis. It catalogs the works Wendt cites, grouped loosely by intellectual strand, so the corpus/index can see who Wendt is reading and which interlocutors carry the most weight (by endnote frequency). ## What the endnotes mostly reference

The 205 endnotes are dominated by a small core of recurring interlocutors. **Heidegger** is by far the most-cited author across the notes (the 1982 *Question Concerning Technology*, 1988 *Basic Problems*, 2008 *Being and Time*, 1971/2001 *Poetry, Language, Thought*, 1976 *What Is Called Thinking?*, and 1977 *Building Dwelling Thinking* recur throughout). **Klaus Krippendorff** (1989, 2004) is the most-cited design-theory source, anchoring Wendt's "design = making sense of things" / semantic-turn discussions. **Nigel Cross** (2006, 2011) and **Rittel & Webber** (1973) carry the design-thinking / wicked-problems argument, often paired with **Buchanan** (1992, 1995), **Dorst** (2006), and **Schön** (1983).

On the philosophy-of-technology side, the notes lean heavily on the **post-phenomenological lineage** — **Verbeek** (2005a, 2005b, 2006, 2008, 2012, and the 1998 Verbeek & Kockelkoren) and **Ihde** (1990, 2002, 2008, 2009, 2010) — together with **Latour** (1992, 1994, 2008, 2009) for the mediation/agency thread and **Harman** (2011, 2013) for object-oriented tool-being. **Dourish** (2001, 2004; 2011 with Bell) supplies the embodied-interaction / ubicomp grounding, and **Dreyfus** (1991, 1992, 2007) the Heideggerian-AI and embodied-coping critique. **Merleau-Ponty** (1962) recurs for the perception/body strand, and **Flusser** (2013) and **Bonsiepe** (1999) for the design-as-projection / interface notes. Literary-theoretical "death of the author / intentional fallacy" notes cluster around **Barthes** (1971), **Foucault** (1979), and **Wimsatt & Beardsley** (1946), while **Beckett** (1995) is used repeatedly as a literary touchstone in the early notes. The endnotes are overwhelmingly citational (frequent "ibid." chains) rather than discursive — they point to sources rather than carrying substantive side-argument.
