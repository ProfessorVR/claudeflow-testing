## 1. Thesis / Aim

Ulrich introduces **"virtual rhetoric"**: because VR "functions primarily through visual feedback, virtual rhetoric
functions like an augmented form of visual rhetoric" (FE). VR persuades through **vividness and immersion**, heightening
emotional impact and bypassing analytical argument, and — via user data — customizing itself to the viewer, giving
world-creators "unparalleled influence." Because this leaves users "open to unconscious manipulation and behavioral
change," Ulrich argues for rhetorical literacy: "we can use rhetoric in virtual reality to convey arguments and change
how individuals view the real world" (FE, p.5).

## 2. Structure

Intro (pp.5–6): Snow Crash epigraph; VR's arrival; the thesis that VR is a new medium whose rhetoric can "change how
individuals view the real world." Middle (pp.6–14): virtual rhetoric as augmented visual rhetoric; automatization vs.
defamiliarization; vividness/immersion; the Bailenson & Yee "Digital Chameleons" persuasion experiment; desensitization
concerns. Close (pp.14–17): manipulation, ethics, the call for rhetorical awareness. References follow.

## 3. Key constructs

- **Virtual rhetoric (VR as persuasion)** (core, FE) — VR as a rhetorical medium that persuades its inhabitant.
- **Vividness / immersion → emotional impact, bypassing analysis** (core, FE) — the persuasive mechanism.
- **Design-as-rhetoric / authored environment** (core, P from FE) — a virtual world is *authored* to move its inhabitant; customization gives creators "unparalleled influence."
- **Unconscious manipulation / rhetorical literacy** (important, FE) — the ethical stakes and the call for awareness.

## 5. Relevance to Part III (design-as-rhetoric; the VLE as an authored, moving environment) [P]

Ulrich gives the entry its **design-as-rhetoric** frame directly: a virtual environment is authored to *move* its
inhabitant, and its vividness/immersion do rhetorical work (L1) that can "change how individuals view the real world"
(L2). For Part III this reframes the VLE not as a neutral delivery channel but as an environment **authored to
persuade/position** its inhabitant — the rhetorical counterpart to the phenomenological "how a world is opened."
Ulrich's caution (immersion can bypass analysis and manipulate) is the critical edge: authored immersion is a lever
that cuts both ways. Pairs with riv-01 (Golliher): Ulrich = the moving lever (immersion→persuasion); Golliher = the
doing lever (interactivity→agency). Together: the authored environment acts on its inhabitant by *what it lets them do*
and by *how it moves them*.
