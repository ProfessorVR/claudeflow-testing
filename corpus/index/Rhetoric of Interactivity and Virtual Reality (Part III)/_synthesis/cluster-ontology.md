## 1. Grouping decision + rationale (why one combined entry)

After reading both, the sources share a **single load-bearing thread: the built/authored environment as a rhetorical
actor on its inhabitant — "design-as-rhetoric."** Golliher analyzes how a museum exhibit is *authored* to grant or
foreclose visitor agency (interactivity vs. sensation); Ulrich analyzes how a VR world is *authored* to move and
persuade its inhabitant (virtual rhetoric via vividness/immersion). Neither is a social-presence or presence-theory
source; both are **rhetoric-tradition** analyses of how a designed environment acts on the person inside it. They divide
the labor cleanly — Golliher = the **doing lever** (interactivity → agency), Ulrich = the **moving lever** (immersion →
persuasion) — which is precisely the dissertation's design-as-rhetoric frame for a VLE. Because each is small (a thesis
and a short article) and they compose one argument, a single combined entry is more faithful and more useful than two
one-unit entries. (The Phase-1 proposal offered C1 combined vs. C2 standalone; the reading supports C1.)

## 2. Relevance to Part III

- **Golliher (doing lever):** the interactivity-vs-sensation distinction maps onto the VLE corpus's **A₃→A₄ stall** —
  a design is "not fully interactive" when it "does not demand that visitors" act; genuine interactivity gives the
  inhabitant something to *do*. Supports the Part III interactivity material and the stall remedy / design levers.
- **Ulrich (moving lever):** VR as an environment authored to persuade — vividness/immersion do rhetorical work that can
  "change how individuals view the real world." Supplies the design-as-rhetoric frame (the VLE as authored to move its
  inhabitant), with a critical edge (immersion can bypass analysis / manipulate).

#### 1. Design-as-rhetoric (the authored environment)
- **definition**: The built/designed environment (museum exhibit, VR world) as a rhetorical actor authored to position, move, or empower its inhabitant; the entry's shared thread across Golliher and Ulrich.
- **type**: THEORY-MODEL
- **units**: riv-01, riv-02
- **centrality**: core

#### 2. Interactivity vs. sensation (the doing lever)
- **definition**: Golliher's load-bearing distinction — genuine interactivity grants visitor agency/experimentation (the doing/task side), whereas sensation only simulates participation while foreclosing agency.
- **type**: CONSTRUCT
- **units**: riv-01
- **centrality**: core

#### 3. Virtual rhetoric (VR as persuasion; the moving lever)
- **definition**: Ulrich's concept — VR persuades as an augmented form of visual rhetoric, via vividness and immersion that heighten emotional impact and bypass analytical argument.
- **type**: THEORY-MODEL
- **units**: riv-02
- **centrality**: core

#### 4. Visitor / user agency and experimentation
- **definition**: What genuine interactivity affords the inhabitant — the capacity to act, manipulate, and experiment; the positive pole of Golliher's distinction and the design lever for the A₃→A₄ remedy.
- **type**: CONSTRUCT
- **units**: riv-01
- **centrality**: important

#### 5. Vividness / immersion → emotional impact (bypassing analysis)
- **definition**: Ulrich's persuasive mechanism — vividness and immersion give a sense of presence that enhances emotional impact and can bypass analytical argument, giving world-creators outsized influence.
- **type**: CONSTRUCT
- **units**: riv-02
- **centrality**: important

#### 6. False interactivity / foreclosed agency (A₃→A₄ link)
- **definition**: "Apparent" interactivity that does not *demand* visitor action — sensation/presence without afforded completion; the rhetorical statement of the VLE corpus's A₃→A₄ stall.
- **type**: FINDING-STRUCTURE
- **units**: riv-01
- **centrality**: important

#### 7. Unconscious manipulation / rhetorical literacy
- **definition**: Ulrich's ethical edge — authored immersion can manipulate unconsciously and change behavior, motivating a call for rhetorical awareness in creating and consuming virtual environments.
- **type**: CONSTRUCT
- **units**: riv-02
- **centrality**: supporting
