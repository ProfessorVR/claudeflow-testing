## The doing lever — interactivity vs. sensation (Golliher, riv-01)
- **"sensational rather than interactive"** — thesis ~pp.22–24. The core distinction: stimulus without agency ≠ interactivity.
- **"sensation for AiG is not a conduit for visitor agency"** — Abstract. Sensation forecloses agency while appearing participatory.
- **"Interactivity allows visitors to touch, smell, taste, hear, feel"** — thesis ~pp.2–4. What genuine interactivity affords — the doing/task side.
- **"apparent use of interactivity is not fully interactive, as it does not demand that visitors"** — thesis ~pp.31–33. The A₃→A₄ test: interactivity must *demand* action, not merely stimulate.

## The moving lever — virtual rhetoric / design-as-rhetoric (Ulrich, riv-02)
- **"The vividness of virtual reality can give an audience a sense of immersion, enhance the emotional impact of a message, and bypass analytical arguments"** — p.5. The persuasive mechanism of an authored immersive environment.
- **"we can use rhetoric in virtual reality to convey arguments and change how individuals view the real world"** — p.5. VR as world-changing rhetoric — the design-as-rhetoric frame.

## Suggested disposition
1. Use Golliher L4 ("does not demand that visitors [act]") as the rhetorical statement of the A₃→A₄ stall, and L3 as the
   design lever (give the inhabitant something to *do*).
2. Use Ulrich L1/L2 to frame the VLE as an environment **authored to move** its inhabitant (design-as-rhetoric), with
   Ulrich's manipulation caution as the critical edge.
3. Together: an authored environment acts on its inhabitant by *what it lets them do* (Golliher) and *how it moves them*
   (Ulrich) — the two levers of design-as-rhetoric for a VLE.
