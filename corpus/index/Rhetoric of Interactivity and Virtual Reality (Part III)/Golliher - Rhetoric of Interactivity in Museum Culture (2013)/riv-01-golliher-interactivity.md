## 1. Thesis / Aim

Golliher problematizes the "interactive" exhibits of the Creation Museum (and, by extension, mainstream science
centers): their sensory triggers are offered "under the guise of visitor empowerment," yet the exhibits are
**"sensational rather than interactive"** (FE). Sensation, far from granting agency, is "a rhetorical strategy for
imposing creationist ideologies upon visitors": "sensation for AiG is not a conduit for visitor agency, but rather a
rhetorical strategy for imposing creationist ideologies upon visitors" (FE, Abstract). Genuine interactivity, by
contrast, would grant the visitor experimentation and agency — the capacity to *do*, not merely to be stimulated.

## 2. Structure

Front matter (roman) → Introduction → "Interactivity" chapter (thesis p.12 per TOC) → "The Rhetoric of Sensation"
chapter (thesis p.22 per TOC) → analysis of the Creation Museum → conclusion. The argument's hinge is the distinction
between *interactivity* (visitor action/experimentation/agency) and *sensation* (stimulus that forecloses agency while
appearing participatory).

## 3. Key constructs

- **Interactivity vs. sensation** (core, FE) — the load-bearing distinction: genuine interactivity grants agency; sensation only simulates participation.
- **Visitor agency / experimentation** (core, FE) — what genuine interactive learning affords: the doing/task side.
- **Interactivity as rhetoric (design-as-rhetoric)** (core, FE) — exhibits are authored to move/position visitors; the built environment is a rhetorical actor.
- **False interactivity / foreclosed agency** (important, FE) — "apparent" interactivity that does not *demand* visitor action.

## 5. Relevance to Part III (the doing/task side; A₃→A₄ remedy; design levers) [P]

Golliher gives Part III its cleanest statement of the **interactivity-vs-sensation** difference, which maps directly
onto the VLE corpus's **A₃→A₄ stall** (vle-01/vle-05: environments that solicit interest/sensation but foreclose
*action* read as "just playback"). L4 is the operational test that matches the stall diagnosis: a design is "not fully
interactive" when it "does not demand that visitors" act — i.e., presence/sensation without afforded completion.
Conversely L3 names the design lever: genuine interactivity gives the inhabitant something to *do* (touch, manipulate,
experiment), the remedy the King–Salvo authors themselves reach for ("engagement alerts… quizzes… narrated modules").
Golliher supplies the *rhetorical* vocabulary for that lever: the exhibit/VLE is **authored** either to grant agency or
to foreclose it under a participatory guise — design-as-rhetoric. Pairs with riv-02 (Ulrich): Golliher = the doing
lever (interactivity→agency); Ulrich = the moving lever (immersion→persuasion).
