## Salience

This clip is the corpus's densest single run of **honor-as-motive-attribution**, and it cultivates the **ethical** pole of the protracted hexis-bundle far more than the techne pole. Within ~30 minutes the player is handed five consecutive honor-positive resolutions — retrieving the old man's horse (06–08), defending Tilly (23–24), defending Karen (24–26), sparing the witness Jimmy Brooks (28–31), and returning the commandeered horse (32) — each scored by the same off-screen engine that converts an A4 act into a number. RDR2's HONOR system is thereby legible as a **Burkean motive-attribution machine**: it reads the act and assigns it a valence, and the clip foregrounds the *ratios* that machine elides. Arthur disclaims agent-virtue twice ("I was just trying to impress the women," 07:45; "I'm not a good man... I kill people," 30:00) and Uncle re-attributes the good deed to **scene** rather than agent — "if you lot hadn't been here, I probably would have robbed them" (08:40) — so the honorable hexis on display is repeatedly shown to be *conditioned by witnesses* rather than seated in character. The **ethical fork** is the Jimmy Brooks keystone (28:38–31:00): the same orekton (gang secrecy/survival) is satisfiable by murder or by mercy, and the playthrough takes the non-lethal path — saving the man's life, then coercing his silence — a path the honor engine rewards. **Bia** structures the middle third as a rhythm of agent->patient inversions: Tilly gripped, Karen struck, then Jimmy dangling and begging "please do something" — Arthur functions as the **de-inverter** for the women (restoring their agency) and as the **inverter** of the predators (reducing them to patients), the honor score tracking which way the inversion runs. The **general store** (11:45–13:20) is the clip's purest econo-techne beat and its clearest 2D signature: a symbolic catalog standing in for provisioning, the orekton thinned to icons.
