## Salience

This clip's contribution to the protracted hexis-bundle is **techne, not ethical disposition**: five-finger fillet cultivates a **kinesthetic-rhythm dexterity** — timing, escalating-tempo nerve, the discipline of committing a press inside a narrowing window. The orekton is the escalating rhythm itself (and the wager/the besting of the rival), and the dominant Calleja channels are **kinesthetic** and **ludic**, with **affective** tension rising as tempo climbs and **spatial** involvement minimal (a single static table). It is analytically valuable as a **contrast case for the HONOR machine**: unlike most scored RDR2 acts, fillet is **wager-scored, not honor-scored**, so the Burke motive resolves to **agency** (the means/instrument) and the **agent's** skill rather than to an honor verdict — a clean specimen of techne-attribution with the moral pentad disengaged. The latent **ethical/agency fork** is the **BIA** structure: (1) the accelerating tempo is a soft *bia* — the orekton's pull strips deliberative slack and the game dictates the pace; and (2) the **fail-state is a self-inflicted agent→patient inversion** — the player's own committed act stabs the player's own on-screen body. Whether that inversion actually fires (a fail-stab) or is only courted (clean run) is the single most decision-relevant fact in the clip and **cannot be resolved from the transcript** — it must be read off the frames. The clip's value at the **2D pole** is paradigmatic: a minigame "about" the hand is delivered entirely through symbolic button-rhythm, so **phantasia must supply the felt body**, and embodiment registers as the **involuntary flinch** at each blade-near-finger stab (R2 via phantasia, not an HMD) — a high-phantasia-load exhibit with a large data-discrepancy between the felt risk and the screen's press.
