## Salience

This 29-second clip is the **disciplinary hinge** of the early hexis-bundle: before any combat, hunting, or honor-scoring, the game installs the most basic ethical-*technē* disposition it will build on — **obedience to the designed path, the hexis of *being-led***. It cultivates this not by instruction but by **recalcitrance**: the single load-bearing line, "I think you're going the wrong way," is the world rebuffing an off-trail vector and funneling the player back (Rickert). That funneling is a **soft *bia*** — a micro agent→patient inversion in which exploration is corralled and the player's free movement-vector is overridden by scene — the leash that the entire later open world will be *felt against* once it is loosed. The dominant **Burke motive is scene**: the blizzard subordinates every act, pre-empting agency before the player has earned any. There is **no honor event** here (the system is not yet live) and **no discrete A₄ act** (by design — nothing completes; the clip is pure A₀-constraint + A₁-onset). Its contribution to the protracted case is therefore *privative*: it defines the horizon's edges by enforcing them, so that the eventual open A₀ registers as freedom. **The clip is also the corpus's purest specimen of the 2D / high-*phantasia*-load pole**: the whiteout withdraws nearly all on-screen data, forcing phantasia to render the terrain, depth, and cold — and, crucially, the same whiteout *hides the leash*, letting a hard boundary read as diegetic weather. The ethical fork is not a choice but a **constraint**: the player is taught that the world has edges before being taught that acts have weight.
