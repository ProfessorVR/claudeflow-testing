## Salience

This clip is the corpus's clean specimen of a **technē-hexis** (skill-disposition) being *seeded* rather than an ethical hexis being tested. The protracted hexis-bundle it contributes to is the *hunter's eye-and-hand*: a perceptual disposition ("get your eye in," 06:38) plus a motor-craft ("good tension on the string," 07:52), explicitly framed as repeatable ("now let's try for another," 08:04) and as lifelong ("takes a lifetime of practice to master," 19:09). The full RODA arc runs once, cleanly and didactically, because Charles narrates each stage: A0 scarcity-horizon (00:58–02:10) → A1 trained perception of tracks (06:10–06:44) → A2/A3/A4 the drawn-bow kill (07:52–08:04) → harvest and provision (12:22, 19:19). The **orekton** is doubled: proximally the kill, distally the camp's survival — and the game's framing collapses the two, which is the clip's ethical pivot. There is **no ethical fork and no bia**: every act is volitional, the prey cannot consent or resist, and "it's easier when they ain't shooting back" (12:35) is the script *flagging* the absence of the agency-stakes that combat clips carry. The **honor-motive attribution** is therefore quiet but real: RDR2's motive-machine reads this killing as *provision* (scene/purpose), so the act accrues no dishonor — hunting-for-the-camp is the game's model of legitimate violence, against which later honor-scored killings will be measured. Its comparative payload for the VR-2D thesis: hunting is the LOW-flinch end of the phantasia-load spectrum, and the **Eagle-Eye track-vision** (06:10–06:44) is the purest artifact of 2D compensation — a literal vision-prosthesis the game must grant because the screen cannot deliver the tracker's trained gaze, where a VR body might begin to.
