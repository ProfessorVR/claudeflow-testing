## SALIENCE

This clip's contribution to the protracted hexis-bundle is **belonging-through-labor**: it cultivates an *ethical/affective* disposition (membership in Dutch's gang as something *earned and maintained by mundane contribution*) braided with a thin *techne* (the camp chore/haul mechanic). Where combat clips cultivate skill-hexis, "Chores" cultivates the dispositional substrate that makes the gang feel like *home* — and it does so through tedium rather than spectacle. The orekton throughout is **belonging** (recognition as a contributing member), kept drawn forward by the gang's standing scarcity/hunger. The honor-motive event is small but exact: at **02:30** "Good work, Mr. Morgan" the camp's motive-attribution machine scores a completed chore to the **agent** (Arthur-as-good-member) and **purpose** (provisioning), converting labor into worth. There is no ethical *fork* (no choice between honor-paths) and no hard **bia** (no forced agency-stripping); the only agency-relevant note is the *soft* compulsion of the chore-economy and the de-agentializing monotony thematized at **04:57** ("how boring it really is"). That boredom is the analytic payload: for the 2D/high-phantasia-load pole, the represented tedium of belonging and the player's actual idle screen-time *coincide*, so the clip is a clean phenomenological specimen of dwelling-as-duration.
