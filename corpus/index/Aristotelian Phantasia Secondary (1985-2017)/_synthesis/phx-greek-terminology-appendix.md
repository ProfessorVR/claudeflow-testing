# Greek Terminology Appendix — Phantasia Cluster (1985–2017)

*Canonical Greek lemmas attested across the eight phantasia-cluster units (PHX-01 … PHX-08). Generated 2026-05-11.*

**Coverage**: 213 canonical lemmas aggregated from 279 raw `greek_terms` entries across the eight unit JSONs. Tier-A = used by ≥3 of 8 units (n=29). Tier-B = used by 1–2 units (n=184).

**Canonicalization rule**: each raw `greek_terms.term` entry was transliterated (Greek script and Latin both accepted), normalized (`u`/`y` collapsed, diacritics stripped, *ē/ō* folded), and compound-split (entries like 'phantasma / phantasmata' or 'noein / noesis' split into atoms). Morphological variants (*phantasiai* → *phantasia*, *dunamis* → *dynamis*, *phantasmata* → *phantasma*) collapse via a hand-curated headword map; articles (*to/hē*) are stripped.

**Unit IDs**: PHX-01 Nussbaum 1985 · PHX-02 White 1985 · PHX-03 Frede 1992 · PHX-04 Caston 1995 · PHX-05 O'Gorman 2005 · PHX-06 Gonzalez 2006 · PHX-07 Hawhee 2011 · PHX-08 Papachristou 2013.

## 1. The cluster's lexical core

The cluster's Greek-terminological core is structured by three concentric rings.

**Ring 1 — the universal terms (Tier-A, n≈29).** Twenty-nine lemmas are used by three or more cluster articles. Of these, three are used by *all eight* (***phantasia***, ***phantasma***, ***kinēsis***), and one — ***energeia*** — is used by seven of eight (everyone except Nussbaum's actional-vocabulary unit). This is the cluster's true *koinē*: every article in the secondary literature on Aristotelian phantasia must define its relation to (a) the faculty (phantasia), (b) the product (phantasma), (c) the *kinēsis*-definition at *DA* III.3 428b30–429a2, and (d) the *energeia*/*kinēsis* contrast that determines whether phantasia is process or completion.

**Ring 2 — the cognate-cluster (Tier-A but bracketed by sub-debate).** Six lemmas — *aisthēsis, aisthēma, aisthētikon, doxa, hypolēpsis, pathos* — encode the cognitive map within which Aristotle locates phantasia in *DA* III.3 (the famous quintuple distinction: phantasia is neither *aisthēsis*, nor *epistēmē*, nor *doxa*, nor *nous*, nor *hypolēpsis*). Every article must place phantasia against at least four of these contrast-terms; differing weightings determine whether the article reads phantasia as 'weak sensation' (Frede's *aisthēsis tis asthenēs*), as 'quasi-belief' (Nussbaum), or as *sui generis* (Caston, Papachristou).

**Ring 3 — the rhetorical subset (Tier-A within the rhetoric papers, Tier-B globally).** A compact cluster of *lexis, pro-ommaton-poiein, enargeia, megethos, deinosis, auxesis, prepon, hyperoche* organizes the rhetoric papers (O'Gorman, Gonzalez, Hawhee). When the rhetoric subset overlaps with the philosophy-of-mind core, it does so via the *energeia*/*phantasia* couplet: phantasia in the *Rhetoric* operates as the same psychological faculty as in the *DA*, but its rhetorical force is registered through *energeia*-style and *enargeia*-effect.


## 2. Gloss-divergences: where the cluster disagrees about Greek

The most theoretically loaded lemma in the corpus is also the most translation-contested: ***phantasia*** itself. The eight units divide as follows:

- **PHX-01 Nussbaum**: 'appearance' / 'imagination' / 'the appearing-capacity.' She emphasizes the *qua*-structure: phantasia is the faculty by which an animal sees an object *as* an object of a certain sort.

- **PHX-02 White**: refuses to translate; treats as *sui generis* technical term.

- **PHX-03 Frede**: 'appearance' — the central meaning covering capacity, activity, and product. Reserves 'imagination' for the active/creative sub-sense.

- **PHX-04 Caston**: refuses to translate, but the article-title nonetheless uses 'imagination' as the thematic English label. Caston's gloss is functional: phantasia is what explains the appearance/reality gap.

- **PHX-05 O'Gorman**: 'epideictic faculty' / 'rhetorical-perceptual mediation' — a thick functional gloss specific to epideictic amplification.

- **PHX-06 Gonzalez**: 'imagination / appearance / mediating psychic faculty.' Argues for a unitary technical sense across *DA* and *Rhet.* III.1; explicitly rejects 'mere show.'

- **PHX-07 Hawhee**: 'visual faculty' / 'fuzzy distance-related judgment-feeding capacity.' Foregrounds the visual register.

- **PHX-08 Papachristou**: refuses to translate (footnote 32) on the ground that 'imagination' fails to convey Aristotle's notion adequately. The article then disambiguates *three kinds* of phantasia by Greek alone.

Three patterns emerge. (i) The **philosophy-of-mind authors** divide roughly along an *appearance-vs.-imagination* axis: Frede and Nussbaum prefer 'appearance' (with imagination as a marked subspecies); Caston and Papachristou refuse to translate. (ii) The **rhetoricians** prefer thick functional glosses tied to the rhetorical use-case (epideictic, visual). (iii) The **'refusers' (White, Caston, Papachristou)** all argue the same negative claim: that any English translation pre-empts the analysis. This is the cluster's most consequential lexical-methodological fault-line.


## 3. The Greek that anchors the cluster's signature debates

Three lemmas anchor three distinct sub-debates that map onto the cluster's three center-of-gravity loci:

First: the ***pathēma***-anchor. ***Phantasma*** as the *pathēma* (passion-trace) left behind by perception in the sense-organ is what *De Mem.* 450a–b makes explicit. The lemma ***tupos*** (imprint, stamp) — Tier-B but distinctive — extends the metaphor: phantasmata are *typoi* in the *aisthētikon*. This vocabulary anchors the *residue-reading* shared by Frede, Caston, and Papachristou.

Second: the ***energeia***-anchor. *Energeia* doubles in the cluster: as the metaphysical contrast-term for *kinēsis* (phantasia is a *kinēsis hypo tēs aisthēseōs tēs kat' energeian*, DA III.3 429a1), and as the rhetorical term for *energetic* style that brings-before-the-eyes (Rhet. III.11 1411b24). The fact that the same Greek word does both jobs is — for O'Gorman, Gonzalez, and Hawhee — not a coincidence but a clue: the rhetorical *energeia* of style *is* phantasia operating in its active mode.

Third: the ***bouleutikē / aisthētikē***-anchor. *DA* III.10–11 introduces ***bouleutikē phantasia*** (deliberative phantasia, human-only) in contrast to a merely-sensitive (***aisthētikē***) phantasia shared with animals. Papachristou's 'three kinds or grades' thesis splits this further (adding a third middle-tier *logistikē phantasia*). The relevant Greek vocabulary — *bouleusis, bouleutikē, logistikē, orexis* (with sub-terms *epithymia* and *thumos*), *prohairesis* — is concentrated in the action-theory subset of the cluster (Nussbaum, Papachristou, Gonzalez).


## 4. Top forty load-bearing lemmas (glossed)

Each gloss synthesizes the consensus *and the disagreements* across the units that invoke it.


**kinesis** *[A, n=8: 01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 06, 07, 08]* — Motion / change. Aristotle defines phantasia at DA III.3 428b30–429a2 as a *kinēsis* generated by actual sensation (*hypo tēs aisthēseōs tēs kat' energeian*). Cited by every unit. For Hawhee, kinesis becomes the link to rhetorical-vision dynamics; for Frede and Caston, the basis for the residue-reading.

**phantasia** *[A, n=8: 01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 06, 07, 08]* — The soul-power producing inner appearances (DA III.3 428a1ff.). Translation-contested: Nussbaum and Caston gloss as 'appearing-as' or 'appearance'; Frede settles on 'appearance' (covering capacity, activity, and product); White, Papachristou, and Caston refuse to translate; the rhetoricians (O'Gorman, Hawhee) emphasize the visual sense; Gonzalez argues for a unitary technical sense across DA and Rhet. III.1.

**phantasma** *[A, n=8: 01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 06, 07, 08]* — The product or content of phantasia — the 'inner appearance' itself, as distinct from the faculty (phantasia) that produces it. Treated as a *pathēma* (passion-trace) at De Mem. 450a; persists in the sense-organs in absence of object (Insomn.). Universally invoked across the cluster.

**energeia** *[A, n=7: 02, 03, 04, 05, 06, 07, 08]* — Actuality / actualization. Phantasia is the *kinēsis* generated *by sensation in actuality* (DA III.3 429a1). For Hawhee, O'Gorman, and Gonzalez, *energeia* doubles as the rhetorical term for the 'bringing-before-the-eyes' that *energetic* style accomplishes (Rhet. III.11). The pun is interpretive gold across the rhetoric subset.

**aisthesis** *[A, n=6: 01, 02, 03, 04, 06, 07]* — Sense-perception. Aristotle's contrast term for phantasia (DA III.3 428a5–16). At Rhet. 1370a28-30 phantasia is identified as *aisthēsis tis asthenēs* — 'a kind of weak sensation.' Universally invoked.

**aisthetikon** *[A, n=6: 01, 02, 03, 04, 07, 08]* — The 'perceptual part/power.' Hawhee, Frede, Papachristou treat phantasia as parasitic on the *aisthetikon*. The term grounds the 'sensitive phantasia' branch of Papachristou's tripartite reading.

**nous** *[A, n=6: 01, 02, 03, 04, 06, 08]* — Intellect. Aristotle distinguishes nous from phantasia and uses phantasmata as substrate of *noēsis* (DA III.7 431b2). The bridge-term for cluster-wide debates over whether thinking requires phantasia.

**bouleutike** *[A, n=5: 01, 03, 05, 06, 08]* — Deliberative (adjective). DA III.10–11 introduces *bouleutikē phantasia* — the human-only sub-kind. Anchors Papachristou's tripartite reading; mentioned by Nussbaum, Frede, Gonzalez, O'Gorman.

**doxa** *[A, n=5: 01, 02, 03, 05, 06]* — Opinion / belief. Aristotle distinguishes phantasia from doxa at DA III.3 428a20–24: phantasia does not entail belief; the same person can have a phantasia of the sun as a foot wide and a doxa that it is larger than the inhabited world. Central distinction-target for Nussbaum, Frede, Caston, Gonzalez, O'Gorman.

**hypolepsis** *[A, n=5: 02, 03, 04, 06, 08]* — Judgment / supposition / 'taking-up.' The umbrella term Aristotle uses at DA III.3 427b25 for the cognitive states phantasia *is not* (doxa, *epistēmē*, *nous*). Pivotal for Frede's contrast and Gonzalez's Rhet. III.1 reading.

**pathos** *[A, n=5: 02, 05, 06, 07, 08]* — Affection / passion / undergoing. Phantasia is what the soul *undergoes* as a result of perception (De Mem. 450a30); the rhetoricians press this into service as the affective ground of *ethos/pathos* rhetoric (O'Gorman, Gonzalez, Hawhee).

**phainetai** *[A, n=5: 01, 02, 03, 05, 06]* — The third-person impersonal 'it appears' — for Frede and Caston, the linguistic ground for phantasia's appearance-semantics; Nussbaum treats it as Aristotle's bridge to ordinary perceptual reports.

**phantastikon** *[A, n=5: 01, 02, 03, 04, 08]* — The 'imaginative part/power' of the soul — Aristotle's substantive nominalization at DA III.10 434a16. Papachristou's tripartite reading turns on this term; Nussbaum and White treat it as equivalent to phantasia as a *dunamis*; Frede uses it for the faculty-as-distinct-from-activity.

**logistike** *[A, n=4: 01, 03, 06, 08]* — Calculative / rational. DA III.10's parallel term for *bouleutikē*. Anchors the rational-phantasia debate.

**orexis** *[A, n=4: 01, 02, 06, 08]* — Desire (the unified desire-faculty subsuming *boulēsis*, *thumos*, *epithumia*). At DA III.10 433a–434a, phantasia drives *orexis*, which drives motion. Nussbaum and Papachristou: the locus classicus for phantasia's role in action.

**phainomenon** *[A, n=4: 01, 02, 06, 07]* — The 'apparent' — both the perceptual phenomenon and the rhetorical 'apparent good' or 'apparent pleasure.' Pivot term for the Rhetoric I.11 1370a apparatus (Nussbaum, White, Hawhee) and for SE 164a20–24 (Gonzalez).

**aisthema** *[A, n=3: 01, 02, 04]* — The product of sense-perception, parallel to phantasma as the product of phantasia. White, Nussbaum, and Caston use the term to argue phantasmata are 'just like aisthēmata' (DA III.8 432a9–10) — the basis for the supervenience reading.

**alloiosis** *[A, n=3: 01, 02, 04]* — Alteration. White's preferred reading: phantasia is a kind of *alloiōsis* of the sense-organ (DA II.5 416b32–417b16). Caston picks this up; Nussbaum disputes the implication.

**dianoia** *[A, n=3: 01, 02, 04]* — Discursive thought. Distinguished from *nous* (intuitive intellect) and from phantasia (image-having). Nussbaum, White, and Caston use *dianoia* to mark the cognitive zone where phantasia and intellect cooperate.

**dynamis** *[A, n=3: 01, 02, 08]* — Potentiality / capacity / faculty. Phantasia as a *dunamis* of the soul (DA II.3 414b14; III.10 433b29). White, Nussbaum, and Papachristou use the distinction *dunamis/energeia* to disambiguate faculty-talk from activity-talk in Aristotle's phantasia descriptions.

**eidolon** *[A, n=3: 01, 02, 03]* — Image / replica. Distinguished from phantasma in Nussbaum and White: an *eidōlon* is a likeness or copy, whereas a phantasma is a faculty-product. Frede tracks the distinction in Plato's *Republic* X reception by Aristotle.

**einai** *[A, n=3: 01, 02, 04]* — To be. Methodological lemma for DA I.1 essence/property distinction. White, Nussbaum, Caston.

**krisis** *[A, n=3: 03, 05, 06]* — Discrimination / judgment. DA III.3 427a20 — soul 'discriminates and knows.' Frede, O'Gorman, Gonzalez use *krisis* to argue phantasia involves a discriminative element.

**lexis** *[A, n=3: 05, 06, 07]* — Diction / style. The frame in which *Rhet.* III.1 1404a11 deploys 'phantasia.' Gonzalez argues *lexis-and-phantasia* is one technical complex; O'Gorman reads it as the bridge between style and ethical effect; Hawhee as visual diction.

**metaphora** *[A, n=3: 01, 02, 07]* — Metaphor. Rhet. III.11 connects metaphor to *energeia* and to setting-before-the-eyes. Nussbaum, White, Hawhee.

**phantazesthai** *[A, n=3: 01, 03, 06]* — The middle/passive verb 'to be made to appear, to have an appearance' — used by Aristotle for the inner-state side of phantasia. Cited by Nussbaum, Frede, Gonzalez.

**phos** *[A, n=3: 02, 05, 07]* — Light. Aristotle's etymological play at DA III.3 429a3–4: 'phantasia' from *phaos* (light), because without light there is no seeing. White and O'Gorman take the etymology seriously; Hawhee uses it for her visual-faculty thesis.

**pistis** *[A, n=3: 01, 04, 06]* — Belief / conviction / 'trusting-as.' For Caston, the term that makes phantasia's *non*-doxastic status precise: a phantasia carries no pistis. For Gonzalez and Nussbaum, the term that connects phantasia to rhetorical *peithesthai*.

**pro ommaton poiein** *[A, n=3: 05, 06, 07]* — 'To set before the eyes' — Aristotle's rhetorical formula for *energetic* style (Rhet. III.11 1411b24). The locus classicus for phantasia's rhetorical operation. Invoked by O'Gorman, Gonzalez, Hawhee; cross-referenced to Poet. 17 1455a22 ('*pro ommatōn tithesthai*').

**aistheton** *[B, n=2: 02, 04]* — The sense-object. Distinguished from aisthēsis (act) and aisthēma (product). Three units invoke (Nussbaum, Frede, Caston).

**amige** *[B, n=2: 02, 03]* — (PHX-02) unmixed (with matter and body) — the distinguishing mark of nous

**auxesis** *[B, n=2: 05, 07]* — Amplification. The rhetorical move enabled by phantasia in epideictic discourse. O'Gorman and Hawhee.

**eidos** *[B, n=2: 01, 02]* — Form. White and Nussbaum: phantasia transmits *eidē* from the sense-organ to the discursive mind. Aristotle's matter-form metaphysics enters phantasia-theory here.

**eikon** *[B, n=2: 01, 02]* — Image / likeness. Used at De Mem. 450b21–27 for the memory-image as image-of-the-past. White, Nussbaum invoke for the picture-of-past structure of *mnēmē*.

**enargeia** *[B, n=2: 05, 07]* — Vividness / palpable presence. The rhetorical virtue of 'bringing-before-the-eyes.' Connected by O'Gorman and Hawhee to the phantasia operative in *Rhet.* III.10–11; the rhetorical analog of phantasia's *energeia*.

**epithymia** *[B, n=2: 01, 08]* — Appetite. White and Papachristou use it for the lower phantasia branch.

**ethos** *[B, n=2: 06, 07]* — Character. Rhetoric II.1 *ethos/pathos* couplet. O'Gorman, Hawhee.

**krinein** *[B, n=2: 02, 06]* — (PHX-02) to distinguish / the distinguishing-power; the major Aristotelian division of soul-powers at III.9 432a15-17

**kritikon** *[B, n=2: 02, 06]* — (PHX-02) to distinguish / the distinguishing-power; the major Aristotelian division of soul-powers at III.9 432a15-17

**logos** *[B, n=2: 02, 04]* — Reasoned account / discourse. Method-term: White uses it for DA's argument-structure; the rhetoricians treat *logos* as the discursive bearer of phantasiai.

**megethos** *[B, n=2: 05, 07]* — Magnitude / greatness / size. O'Gorman's centerpiece: amplification (*auxēsis*) in epideictic rhetoric trades on phantasia's capacity to render *megethos* perspicuous. Hawhee picks this up.

**mneme** *[B, n=2: 06, 08]* — Memory. The cluster's auxiliary faculty: requires phantasmata as material. Gonzalez and Papachristou cite De Mem. 449b30 ff.

**phantazein** *[B, n=2: 06, 07]* — (PHX-06) Verbal cognate. Hdt. 7.10ε is the sole pre-Aristotelian candidate for 'arrogant display' coloring — González: strictly contextual, not lexicalized.

**phone** *[B, n=2: 01, 06]* — Voice / vocal sound. The medium of *Rhet.* III.1's *hypokrisis* discussion in which 'phantasia' appears. Gonzalez argues *phantasia* there is psychological-mediator, not vocal-show.

**semantikos** *[B, n=2: 01, 07]* — Signifying. The verbal-signification side of *Rhet.* III.1. Gonzalez, Hawhee.

## 6. Methodological notes

**Headword merging**: the canonicalization map collapses morphological variants (*phantasmata* → *phantasma*; *dunamis* → *dynamis*; *aisthēmata* → *aisthēma*) and lemmatizes verbal inflections (*phainetai*, *phainesthai* both → *phainetai* as headword). Compound entries written by the unit-coders as 'X / Y / Z' (e.g. 'noein / noesis / nous') are split into atoms and each atom routed to its headword. This yields a higher Tier-A count than naive deduplication of the 279 raw entries would produce.

**Coverage**: where a unit's `greek_terms` array did not include a given lemma (even though the lemma may have appeared in their prose) the lemma is not credited to that unit. This appendix therefore tracks *what each unit explicitly elevated to terminological status*, not every Greek word the unit happens to mention.

**Translation pool**: the `translation_pool` field aggregates all distinct English renderings encountered across the units. Where the unit refused translation, the gloss is preserved.

**Untranslated tradition**: of the cluster's eight units, three (White, Caston, Papachristou) explicitly refuse to translate *phantasia*; one (Hawhee) chooses a thick visual gloss; the remaining four (Nussbaum, Frede, O'Gorman, Gonzalez) use 'appearance' or 'imagination' with caveats. This is a load-bearing methodological signal: the cluster's *anglophone* secondary literature has not converged on an English rendering, after thirty years of debate.
