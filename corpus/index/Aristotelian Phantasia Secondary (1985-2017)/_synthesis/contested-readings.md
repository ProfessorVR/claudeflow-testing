# Contested Readings — Aristotelian *Phantasia* Secondary Cluster (1985–2017)

Phase 3C synthesis. Eleven interpretive theses on which the cluster's eight authors take incompatible positions. Each entry: reading, surface convergence, underlying disagreement, ≥2-unit evidence, implication.

---

## 1. *Phantasia*-as-interpretation vs. *phantasia*-as-image (the Nussbaum thesis)

**Surface convergence.** All eight authors reject the strong "image-view" caricature and concede that DA III.3's *phantasia* is bound up with *phainetai*-talk and so reaches beyond pictures.

**Underlying disagreement.** Nussbaum (PHX-01) makes interpretation primary: *phantasia* is the faculty whereby an animal sees an object *as* F, with scope "as wide as that of *phainomena*" (PHX-01 R1). White (PHX-02) and Caston (PHX-04) resist this reduction: White preserves the picture/encounter distinction at 427b21–24 (PHX-02 R2); Caston redescribes *phantasia* as quasi-perceptual content fixed by causal powers (PHX-04 R2/R3), not Wittgensteinian seeing-as. Frede (PHX-03) is closer to the image-pole, unifying the function as synthesizer/bridge (PHX-03 R2). Hawhee (PHX-07) mediates: Book II "moves closer to Nussbaum" but is "grafted onto direct perception" (PHX-07 T1, R3).

**Evidence.** PHX-01 readings R1, R3 (DA 428a1–4 *kata metaphoran* rereading); PHX-02 T2 ("picture-vs-encounter…pulls against the Nussbaum-style reading"); PHX-04 R2 (causal-powers, not interpretation); PHX-07 R3 ("grafted interpretation").

**Implication.** Three decades after Nussbaum, the cluster has neither absorbed nor displaced her thesis. It functions as the cluster's organizing axis: every later author positions in relation to it.

---

## 2. *Phantasia* supervenes on *aisthesis*

**Surface convergence.** Every author except Nussbaum treats *phantasia* as ontologically posterior to *aisthesis* (Frede's "parasitic," White's "preparation for *noein*," Caston's "echo," Papachristou's *kinesis hypo tes aistheseōs*).

**Underlying disagreement.** Frede (PHX-03 R1) makes the strong claim: *phantasia* is not a separate faculty at all, only a supervenient phenomenon. Caston (PHX-04 C25) rejects this Schofield-form: *phantasia* must be a real explanatory power with content fixed by causal powers. Nussbaum (PHX-01 R4) treats *phantasia*/*aisthesis* as "same in capacity, different in *einai*" on the *arete/dikaiosune* model — one disposition under two descriptions, not supervenience. Papachristou (PHX-08) complicates: her three-kinds taxonomy makes supervenience true at the indeterminate level but increasingly autonomous at the sensitive and deliberative levels.

**Evidence.** PHX-03 R1; PHX-04 C25; PHX-01 thesis "arete/dikaiosune model" with DA 433a31; PHX-08 R1 (tripartite-not-binary), C1.

**Implication.** "Supervenience" is doing four different jobs (eliminative for Frede, structural for Caston's opponents, modal for Nussbaum, developmental for Papachristou). Lexical convergence masks substantive divergence — exactly the diagnostic Caston warns of in his contra-Schofield argument.

---

## 3. *Phantasma* as decaying *kinēsis* (the *Insomn.*/*Mem.* model)

**Surface convergence.** Every cluster author registers that DA III.3's closing paragraph (429a1 ff.) plus the *Parva Naturalia* (Mem. 450a25 ff., Insomn. 461b29 ff.) characterize *phantasmata* as residual motions persisting after the original sense-event. White, Frede, Papachristou, Hawhee, and (in passing) Gonzalez treat this as a settled Aristotelian doctrine.

**Underlying disagreement.** Nussbaum (PHX-01) argues this doctrine governs *only* memory and dreaming; DA III.3 is internally inconsistent and its closing paragraph cannot be canonical (PHX-01 T1). White (PHX-02 R3) makes the same passages central as a positive stability-lending account. Frede (PHX-03 C5) makes persistence definitional. Caston (PHX-04 C16) accepts the decaying-motion description functionally. Papachristou (PHX-08 C3, *motus phantasiae*) integrates it via Aquinas.

**Evidence.** PHX-01 thesis 2 ("Decaying-sense passages are theoretically isolable"), T1 ("DA III.3 internally inconsistent"); PHX-02 R3 (stability-lending); PHX-03 C5; PHX-04 C16; PHX-08 C3.

**Implication.** Nussbaum is the lone dissenter on whether the decaying-motion passages bear cluster-wide canonical weight. The other seven authors' reliance on these passages constitutes a silent collective rejection of Nussbaum's textual-isolation argument.

---

## 4. Deliberative *phantasia* (DA III.11 434a7–10)

**Surface convergence.** All eight authors recognize DA III.11 434a7–10's *phantasia bouleutikē*. Frede, Nussbaum, Caston, Gonzalez, Hawhee, and Papachristou treat it as a load-bearing capacity; O'Gorman thematizes it as image-combining; White confines it to a footnote.

**Underlying disagreement.** Ross's tradition (Frede C9, O'Gorman C11, Gonzalez C8, Papachristou R5) reads 434a7–10 as *combining* *phantasmata* into a composite. Nussbaum refutes this: 434a7–10 is inferential ("*phantasia ek syllogismou*"), not pictorial. Papachristou (PHX-08 R5) splits: dual-register propositional + pictorial. Hawhee (PHX-07 R4) appropriates the "heaping" reading for rhetorical invention.

**Evidence.** PHX-01 thesis "Deliberative phantasia is not collage-of-pictures" with DA 434a7–10 and MA 7 cloak-example; PHX-08 R5 ("propositional + pictorial dual-register"); PHX-07 R4 ("heaping of phantasmata into one composite"); PHX-03 C9.

**Implication.** The deliberative-*phantasia* passage is read as inferential (Nussbaum), pictorial-combinatorial (Ross/Frede/Hawhee), or dual-register (Papachristou). The split tracks the deeper interpretation/image axis (Contested Reading #1).

## 5. The picture-vs-encounter detachment criterion (DA III.3 427b21–24)

**Surface convergence.** White, Nussbaum, Frede, and Caston all read DA 427b21–24 as marking *phantasia* off from judgment. The passage matters for the rhetoric-cluster too (Hawhee).

**Underlying disagreement.** White (PHX-02 R2) makes detachment-from-immediate-affect the *defining* difference. Nussbaum subordinates the passage: detachment is freedom-from-belief-commitment, not pictorial detachment (PHX-01 T2). Caston (PHX-04 T5) reads it functionally: *phantasia* must be free from *pistis* to do its work in higher cognition. Hawhee (PHX-07) inverts White: in the *Rhetoric* the passage is *not* about detachment but pre-judgmental sensory engagement.

**Evidence.** PHX-02 R2 ("picture-vs-encounter as litmus separating *phantasia* from judgment"); PHX-04 T5 ("Belief is bound…*phantasia* is free to image-up independently of belief"); PHX-01 T2 (two-aspect reading).

**Implication.** The same Bekker locus does opposite work for White (defining detachment) and Hawhee (defining engagement). This is the cluster's clearest case of one passage anchoring incompatible programmatic readings.

## 6. Appearance/truth in DA III.3 and the every-appearance-true thesis

**Surface convergence.** Caston, Nussbaum, and Frede converge on the *exegetical* point that DA III.3 includes a critique of the every-appearance-true thesis (Protagorean antirealism, *Met.* IV.4–5).

**Underlying disagreement.** Caston (PHX-04 R1 "Functional-explanatory reading") makes this critique the *raison d'être* of *phantasia* in DA III.3: *phantasia* is introduced to solve the problem of error without conceding Protagorean antirealism. Nussbaum acknowledges the critique but does not make it central — for her, *phantasia* explains intentionality, not error per se. Frede (PHX-03) treats falsehood as derivative from the appearance/perception dichotomy (T2). The rhetoric-cluster authors (O'Gorman, Gonzalez, Hawhee) largely bypass the appearance/truth debate, leaving Caston's signature thesis unaddressed across nearly half the cluster.

**Evidence.** PHX-04 R1, R4 (self-implication and restriction strategy); PHX-04 T1 (proper-perceptibles always true vs. animals more often in error); PHX-01 T1 (DA III.3 inconsistency); PHX-03 T1.

**Implication.** Caston's "Why Aristotle needs imagination" argument is *not* rejected by the rhetoric-cluster — it is simply not addressed. This is a structural absence: the rhetoric-cluster's silence on error is a methodological choice that itself constitutes a position.

## 7. *Rhetoric* III.1 1404a11 *phantasia* — psychological faculty or "mere show"?

**Surface convergence.** All three rhetoric-cluster authors (O'Gorman, Gonzalez, Hawhee) reject the Kennedy-tradition rendering of *phantasia* at *Rhet.* III.1 1404a11 as "mere show / ostentatiousness." All three appeal to DA III.3 to anchor the *Rhetoric*'s *phantasia* in the psychological faculty.

**Underlying disagreement.** Gonzalez (PHX-06 R1) makes this a high-stakes philological correction, with the *Sophistical Refutations* parallel and the adverbial-καί at 1404a11 (PHX-06 C3) as load-bearing evidence (PHX-06 R3). O'Gorman (PHX-05) accepts the psychological reading but shifts the question to where it functions in the *Rhetoric*'s economy (epideictic, not just *lexis*). Hawhee (PHX-07 T2) extends it: *phantasia* operates "near or at the level of direct sensory perception," not metaphorically. Nussbaum (PHX-01) — uniquely — wants 1404a11 *to* mean "mere show," because her *kata metaphoran* rereading depends on it.

**Evidence.** PHX-06 R1, R3, C3, C18 ("Freudenthal-Nussbaum genealogy of the 'mere show' reading"); PHX-05 READING:epideictic-amplification-thesis; PHX-07 T2; PHX-01 thesis "DA 428a1–4 *kata metaphoran*."

**Implication.** A single Bekker line — 1404a11 — supports four incompatible readings. Gonzalez explicitly traces the "mere show" reading to a "Freudenthal-Nussbaum genealogy" and contests it; Nussbaum needs precisely that genealogy to bracket the passage. This is the cleanest case of explicit cross-cluster contestation.

## 8. *Pro ommatōn poiein* — narrow stylistic device or *Rhetoric*-wide faculty?

**Surface convergence.** Six of eight units (PHX-01, PHX-02, PHX-05, PHX-06, PHX-07, PHX-08) treat *pro ommatōn poiein* / "bringing-before-the-eyes" as a central *phantasia*-concept.

**Underlying disagreement.** The traditional reading (implicit in DA III.3 427b18–19 commentary, e.g. Papachristou PHX-08 R6 and the *Rhet.* III.10–11 stylistic locus) treats *pro ommatōn poiein* as a stylistic device, narrowly belonging to vivid metaphor and *enargeia*. O'Gorman (PHX-05 CONCEPT:pro-ommaton-poiein, "lexical kinēsis") and Hawhee (PHX-07 C3) broaden it into a *Rhetoric*-wide rhetorical-vision faculty. Papachristou (PHX-08 R6) uses 427b18–19 to make *phantasia* primarily voluntary visual imagery — and notes the tension with Ross's six-fold list (PHX-08 T5). Nussbaum mostly avoids the rhetoric-cluster's appropriation of the term.

**Evidence.** PHX-05 CONCEPT:pro-ommaton-poiein; PHX-07 C3 ("pro ommatōn poiein") with Kennedy-scare-quotes pushback (PHX-07 T2); PHX-08 R6, T5; PHX-06 C9.

**Implication.** The rhetoric-cluster (O'Gorman, Hawhee) and the philosophy-of-mind cluster (Papachristou) all want to inflate *pro ommatōn poiein* into a faculty-marker — but for different reasons. Papachristou makes it license voluntary visual imagery; Hawhee/O'Gorman make it license non-metaphorical rhetorical perception.

## 9. *Megethos* / amplification as a *phantasia*-anchored epideictic concept

**Surface convergence.** O'Gorman, Gonzalez, and Hawhee all locate *megethos* / *auxēsis* in the *Rhetoric*'s *phantasia* economy.

**Underlying disagreement.** O'Gorman (PHX-05 READING:epideictic-amplification-thesis) makes *megethos* the *centerpiece* of *phantasia*-in-rhetoric: amplification anchored in spatial-logic-of-greatness, with the auditor as *theōros*. Hawhee (PHX-07 C12) treats it as one register among many. Gonzalez (PHX-06) does not centralize *megethos*; he focuses on *Rhet.* III.1's *lexis*/*hypokrisis* coordination. The philosophy-of-mind cluster does not register *megethos* as a *phantasia*-concept at all.

**Evidence.** PHX-05 READING:epideictic-amplification-thesis; PHX-07 C11–13 (*hyperochē*, *auxēsis*, *megethos*); PHX-06 (absent / not centralized).

**Implication.** O'Gorman's signature concept survives partially in Hawhee but is not picked up by Gonzalez or the philosophy-of-mind authors. The non-recognition is itself diagnostic: the rhetoric-cluster's distinctive contribution remains intra-disciplinary.

## 10. Intellectual memory possible in Aristotle, Yes or No?

**Surface convergence.** All cluster authors agree that Aristotle locates memory in the *aisthetikon* (Mem. 450a13). All accept that this raises a puzzle about how thought is remembered.

**Underlying disagreement.** Frede (PHX-03 C7 "denial of intellectual memory") makes Aristotle's denial categorical: memory is confined to the *aisthetikon*. Caston (PHX-04) accepts this but routes higher cognition through *phantasia* as foundational (PHX-04 R5). Papachristou (PHX-08), via Aquinas, is more affirmative: Aquinas's *Sentencia libri De anima* permits a reception-track in which *phantasmata* support a quasi-intellectual memory (PHX-08 R2). White (PHX-02 C18 "thought has no memory") accepts Frede's negative reading. Nussbaum doesn't centralize the question.

**Evidence.** PHX-03 C7; PHX-08 R2 (Aquinas as authoritative interpretive frame); PHX-02 C18; PHX-04 R5.

**Implication.** The Aquinas-reception authors (White and Papachristou) split: White accepts Frede's "no intellectual memory" thesis; Papachristou's Aquinas-mediated reading complicates it. This is one of two places where the cluster's two Thomistic readers diverge.

## 11. Aquinas-reception as legitimate philological method

**Surface convergence.** White (1985) and Papachristou (2013) both treat Aquinas's *Sentencia libri De anima* as a legitimate interpretive frame on DA.

**Underlying disagreement.** White uses Aquinas sparingly as a Thomistic-tradition resource; his core moves are textual close-reading of DA III.3–8. Papachristou (PHX-08 R2) makes Aquinas *constitutive*: the tripartite reading is *legitimated* by Aquinas's commentary on DA II. Frede, Caston, Nussbaum, O'Gorman, Gonzalez, and Hawhee do not engage Aquinas at all. Six of eight treat medieval-reception as inadmissible; two treat it as decisive.

**Evidence.** PHX-08 R2 ("Aquinas's *Sentencia libri De anima*, Liber II"); PHX-02 (Aquinas/Thomistic background pervasive but not foregrounded); silence in PHX-01, PHX-03, PHX-04, PHX-05, PHX-06, PHX-07.

**Implication.** The cluster contains a silent methodological dispute: whether medieval-reception evidence is admissible in DA exegesis. The 6-2 split — and the fact that the two affirmative authors come from a single Thomistic-philosophy formation — means the cluster's "consensus" on DA's *phantasia* is partly an artifact of selecting against medieval-reception readers.

---

## Synthetic remark

Three patterns: (1) **Disciplinary asymmetry** — the philosophy-of-mind authors contest each other across all eleven readings; the rhetoric-cluster engages the philosophy-of-mind cluster only on Readings 1 (Nussbaum thesis) and 7 (1404a11), and is silent on Readings 6, 10, and 11. That silence is a position. (2) **Nussbaum 1985 as anchor.** Six of the eleven readings (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7) are framed against or with Nussbaum; no other author functions thus. (3) **Bekker passages doing incompatible work.** DA 427b21–24, 428a1–4, 433a31, 434a7–10, and Rhet. III.1 1404a11 anchor opposing readings for different authors — the cluster's debates are not over which passages matter but over what those passages mean.
