# Cluster Ontology — Aristotelian Phantasia Secondary (1985–2017)

This synthesis distills the eight units of the Aristotelian Phantasia Secondary cluster (Nussbaum 1985, White 1985, Frede 1992, Caston 1995, O'Gorman 2005, Gonzalez 2006, Hawhee 2011, Papachristou 2013) into a canonical ontology of 41 concept nodes and 22 reading-theses. The cluster spans two disciplinary frames — analytic philosophy of mind and the rhetorical tradition — but converges on a shared lexicon and a shared text-base. Where 194 unit-level concepts proliferate through fine philological and exegetical distinctions, the canonical ontology aggressively merges surface synonyms (e.g., *kinesis hypo tes aistheseos*, *motus phantasiae*, *secondary motion*, *echo of sensation*) into single concept nodes while preserving the lines along which the cluster's four debate axes run.

## Volume-pillar concepts (Tier-1)

Nine concepts function as the cluster's structural pillars, appearing in six or more units and organizing the interpretive labor of every author.

**PHX-C-001 phantasia (the faculty/phenomenon)** is the unavoidable umbrella. All eight units thematize it; their disagreement begins precisely with whether it names a faculty (Caston, Papachristou), a phenomenon supervening on aisthesis (Frede), an aspect of perception (Nussbaum), a temporal mediator (White), or a rhetorical capacity (O'Gorman, Hawhee). **PHX-C-002 phantasma** is its product or vehicle, with eight-unit attestation; whether to read phantasmata as inner pictures, as causal-power-bearing motions in the central sense organ, as object-as-F appearings, or as image-residues, is itself the cluster's central disagreement.

**PHX-C-003 aisthesis (sense-perception)** appears in every unit because phantasia is invariably defined in relation to it: as caused by it (Frede, Papachristou), identical to it in capacity but different in being (Nussbaum, Caston), or set off from it by detachment-from-affect (White). **PHX-C-004 phantasia as kinesis caused by aisthesis-in-act** is Aristotle's own canonical definition (DA 428b10–15) and accordingly threads all eight units; the surface synonyms — *motus phantasiae*, *secondary motion*, *echo of sensation*, *decaying-sense* — collapse to one canonical concept because every author treats them as glosses on the same causal-physiological structure.

**PHX-C-005 phainesthai/phainetai** anchors the cluster lexically. Nussbaum's whole 1985 paper turns on the *phainetai-*kinship; Hawhee assembles a *phainein* verbal cluster; White, Gonzalez, O'Gorman, and Papachristou trace the etymological link to *phaos* (light) at DA 429a4–5. Only Caston downplays this lexical anchor in favor of a structural-functional analysis.

**PHX-C-006 deliberative phantasia (bouleutike/logistike)** and **PHX-C-007 perceptual/sensitive phantasia (aisthetike)** are the two species Aristotle distinguishes at DA 433b29 and 434a5–11. Seven of eight units treat them; Papachristou alone subdivides aisthetike into perceptual proper and a third indefinite tier. Around the *bouleutike* concept the cluster's rhetorical wing builds its image-combining account: Hawhee's "heaping," Wolfson's composite image, Gonzalez's audience-deliberation, and O'Gorman's auxesis-via-megethos all locate rhetorical work in DA 434a's "one phantasma out of many."

**PHX-C-008 pro ommaton poiein** (bringing-before-the-eyes) is the rhetorical/mnemonic engine in Rhet. 3.10–11 and DA 427b18–19. Five units treat it directly; for Hawhee and O'Gorman it is the principal lexical engine of rhetorical vision, for Papachristou the locus of voluntary mental imagery, for Nussbaum a marginal note, for Gonzalez a hinge to Mem. 449b30.

**PHX-C-009 same-in-capacity/different-in-being** is the formal mode of non-identity Aristotle uses at DA 433a31 and De Insomn. 459a15 to relate phantasia and aisthesis. Six units interpret it; the divergent readings — one capacity under two descriptions (Nussbaum, Frede), two distinct nonidentity-types (Caston), supervenience (Frede in another mood, Papachristou) — already split the cluster along Axes 1 and 2.

**PHX-C-015 thinking is never without a phantasma** (DA III.7–8) is the eighth pillar that every unit treats, because it dictates the relation of phantasia to nous. White's organizing thesis, Frede's bridging-function, Caston's foundational-substrate reading, Nussbaum's symbolic-vehicle thesis, O'Gorman's rhetorical-order parallel, and Papachristou's calculative-phantasia analysis all build on this doctrine.

## Chapter-central concepts (Tier-2)

Twelve concepts organize the chapter-level architecture of subsets of units. **PHX-C-010 phantasia-and-orexis** is central for the action-explanation wing (Nussbaum, Caston, Gonzalez, Papachristou) because MA 702a18 ff. makes phantasia the desire-preparer. **PHX-C-011 phantasia as kritikon** is central wherever MA 700b17 is in play. **PHX-C-012 phantasia distinct from doxa (lacks pistis)** is the lynchpin of Gonzalez's "phantasia-with-pistis = rhetorical goal" thesis and of Nussbaum's animal-phantasia argument. **PHX-C-013 phantasia accuracy/fallibility** organizes Caston's whole functional account ("the problem of error") and grounds O'Gorman's "ambivalence about lexis."

**PHX-C-014 phantasma as form-without-matter** is White's storehouse-of-forms thesis in another idiom; it bridges to the noetic substrate doctrine. **PHX-C-016 kata metaphoran clause (DA 428a1–4)** is exegetically central to the Nussbaum-Frede-Gonzalez dispute over whether 428a1–4 brackets ordinary phainetai-uses or only the "mere show" sense. **PHX-C-017 phantasia as 'weak perception'** is the Rhetoric's working definition (1370a28–29) and accordingly organizes the rhetorical wing (O'Gorman, Gonzalez, Hawhee, Papachristou).

**PHX-C-020 emotions as phantasiai** is constitutive for the three rhetorical units (O'Gorman, Gonzalez, Hawhee), all of whom anchor on Rhet. 1382a, 1383a, 1384a's definitions of fear/courage/shame as phantasiai. **PHX-C-021 energeia** and **PHX-C-022 megethos/auxesis/hyperoche** are the rhetorical-actuality and amplification clusters that Hawhee and O'Gorman foreground; in Hawhee they fuel her **PHX-C-023 rhetorical vision** umbrella. **PHX-C-024 phantasia as 'seeing-as'** is Nussbaum's signature gloss, extended by Hawhee's "grafted interpretation" and contested by White and the analytic wing.

**PHX-C-025 image-view of phantasia (target of critique)** is the polemical foil all units must position against. Nussbaum names it (Ross, Hicks, Hamlyn, Rees, Freudenthal, Sorabji); Caston refines it; Hawhee distinguishes herself from it; Frede partially defends a chastened version. **PHX-C-034 lexis as phantasia-medium** is the rhetorical-wing thesis (against the Cope/Freese/Kennedy translation tradition) that absorbs Gonzalez's many fine philological sub-arguments (the adverbial *kai* at 1404a11, the *pros doxan*/reputation-management reading, the *didaskalia* as demonstrative practice, the Freudenthal-Nussbaum genealogy of "mere show").

## Local concepts (Tier-3)

Twenty concepts are one- or two-unit local but indispensable for the authors who deploy them: White's **PHX-C-026 picture-vs-encounter distinction** and **PHX-C-039 phantasia as preserving/storehouse over time**; Caston's **PHX-C-027 causal-powers (not causal-ancestry) account**, **PHX-C-028 non-propositional intentionality**, and **PHX-C-037 infallibility of basic operations**; Frede's **PHX-C-029 synthesis of the manifold** and **PHX-C-030 phantasia-and-epagoge**; Papachristou's **PHX-C-031 indefinite phantasia (phantasia aoristos)** and **PHX-C-040 propositional/pictorial dual register**; Gonzalez's and Papachristou's **PHX-C-032 hypolepsis**; O'Gorman's **PHX-C-033 epideictic as phantasmatic function**; Hawhee's **PHX-C-035 metaphora/asteia**; Nussbaum's **PHX-C-018 phantasia in our power**, **PHX-C-019 phainomenon agathon**, **PHX-C-036 phone as semantikos**, and **PHX-C-038 token-physiology**; White's **PHX-C-041 ousia/genesis polarity**.

These local concepts often signal the disciplinary signature of the author. Nussbaum's Wittgensteinian seeing-as and Goodman-inflected representation-as-interpretation declare her late-twentieth-century analytic-cum-ordinary-language affiliations. White's storehouse-of-forms, geometer's portable slate, and ousia/genesis polarity declare a Thomistic-humanist orientation that surfaces explicitly in Papachristou's much later Aquinas-anchored argument. Caston's causal-powers formalization and subjunctive content-fixing declare contemporary philosophy-of-mind machinery (David Lewis-style analysis of content). Hawhee's neural-imagery bridge and "communicative synesthesia" declare the rhetorical-studies turn to cognitive science. Papachristou's Kosslyn-vs-Pylyshyn import declares her own prior 2006 work.

## Readings (interpretive theses)

The 22 reading nodes group naturally by claimant. Nussbaum (R001–R006) defends phantasia-as-interpretation against the image-view (R001), isolates the decaying-sense passages as physiological hypotheses (R002), rereads kata metaphoran as targeting "mere show" (R003), unifies phantasia-aisthesis on the arete/dikaiosune model (R004), reads deliberative phantasia as inferential rather than collage-of-pictures (R005), and proposes the symbolic-vehicle thesis for "the soul never thinks without phantasma" (R006). White (R007–R009) reads phantasia in DA III.3–8 as preparation for noein (R007), separates phantasia from judgment by the picture-vs-encounter test (R008), and constructs the temporal-mediator/interior-landscape thesis (R009).

Frede (R010–R013) advances the supervenience thesis (R010), the two-functions account (synthesis + bridging; R011), the strict aisthetikon/noeton dualism against Modrak (R012), and the unity-of-phantasia defense against Schofield (R013). Caston (R014–R016) argues that phantasia is introduced to solve the problem of error (R014), constructs the causal-powers content account (R015), and reads phantasia as non-propositional intentionality (R016).

O'Gorman (R017–R018) locates phantasia's rhetorical home in the epideictic function and amplification of megethos (R017), and proposes that the rhetorical order parallels Aristotle's psychic order with epideictic primary (R018). Gonzalez (R019–R020) defends the psychological-phantasia reading of Rhet. III.1 1404a11 against the Cope/Kennedy translation tradition (R019), and constructs the phantasia-with-pistis account of the rhetorical goal (R020). Hawhee (R021) advances the rhetorical-vision thesis that words form direct sensory perception, not only metaphor. Papachristou (R022) argues for three kinds/grades of phantasia against the binary reading.

## The four debate axes

**Axis 1 — Interpretive vs. presentational/image-residue.** Nussbaum and (qualifiedly) Hawhee read phantasia as interpretive seeing-as. White, Frede, Caston, and Papachristou — for different reasons — preserve a presentational or image-residue dimension. White's reproductive-faculty metaphor, Frede's after-image-persistence, Caston's causal-powers analogue of "what s would be about," and Papachristou's mnemonic visual imagery all preserve a residue Nussbaum minimizes. The axis is not simply pro/anti image-theory; even the most image-friendly readers (White, Papachristou) refuse the naive "internal picture inspected by the agent" Nussbaum critiques.

**Axis 2 — Central vs. peripheral/supervenient.** Caston and Nussbaum treat phantasia as central — for Caston as the explanatory load-bearer of Aristotle's solution to the problem of error, for Nussbaum as constitutive of object-as-F awareness. O'Gorman treats it as foundational to public discourse. Frede, by contrast, makes phantasia supervenient on aisthesis, not a separate faculty at all. White is intermediate: phantasia is preparatory to noein, not autonomously central, but indispensable as temporal mediator.

**Axis 3 — Cognitive (philosophy-of-mind) vs. rhetorical.** Five units (Nussbaum, White, Frede, Caston, Papachristou) work primarily within philosophy-of-mind reading the DA. Three units (O'Gorman, Gonzalez, Hawhee) work primarily within rhetoric reading the Rhetoric. The bridge concepts are PHX-C-008 (pro ommaton poiein), PHX-C-017 (weak perception), PHX-C-020 (emotions as phantasiai), and PHX-C-034 (lexis as phantasia-medium); the rhetorical wing's polemic against the Cope/Freese/Kennedy "mere show" tradition (Gonzalez R019) is, in effect, an argument for reading the Rhetoric *through* the DA — i.e., for collapsing this axis.

**Axis 4 — Unitary vs. tripartite.** Seven units read phantasia as unitary (with the standard binary logistike/aisthetike subdivision of DA 433b29 as the only internal differentiation). Papachristou alone, with Aquinas's *Sentencia libri De anima* as the privileged interpretive frame, argues for three kinds — adding an indefinite/sub-sensitive tier (PHX-C-031) for imperfect animals (zoophytes, molluscs) whose phantasma does not persist after the sense object. The tripartite reading turns on a textual emendation (Torstrik's at DA 428a8–11, separating ants/bees from scolex) and a retention-of-phantasmata criterion derived from Aquinas.

## Merge logic notes

Several merges deserve explicit defense. *Decaying-sense*, *motus phantasiae*, *secondary motion*, *echo of sensation*, and *kinesis hypo tes aistheseos* were collapsed into a single canonical concept (PHX-C-004) because every author who uses them treats them as glosses on the same causal-physiological claim; preserving them as distinct would have multiplied the ontology without adding analytic purchase. By contrast, *interpretive seeing-as* (PHX-C-024) and *image-view* (PHX-C-025) are kept as separate concepts despite mutual exclusivity, because the cluster's first debate axis demands them as opposed poles. *Indefinite phantasia* (PHX-C-031) is kept separate from *perceptual/sensitive* (PHX-C-007) because Papachristou's whole reading hinges on the distinction; absorbing it would erase the tripartite thesis from the ontology.

Gonzalez's many fine philological sub-arguments (the adverbial *kai* at 1404a11, the *pros doxan* gloss, the *didaskalia* as demonstrative practice, the *pros auton* vs. *pros akroaten* contrast, the Freudenthal-Nussbaum genealogy, the geometer/orator analogy) were folded into PHX-C-034 (lexis as phantasia-medium) because they all argue *for* the same canonical thesis — the psychological reading of Rhet. III.1 phantasia — and exist as a defended position only at that aggregated level. Similarly, the Rhet. 1.9 amplification triad of *megethos*, *auxesis*, and *hyperoche* was unified as PHX-C-022 because Aristotle coordinates them as one rhetorical operation. *Energeia* (PHX-C-021), however, was kept separate from Hawhee's umbrella *rhetorical vision* (PHX-C-023) because the former is a textual Aristotelian term with its own DA and Metaph. background, while the latter is Hawhee's twentieth-first-century construct. The same reasoning preserves *pro ommaton poiein* (PHX-C-008) as a Tier-1 node distinct from *rhetorical vision*: Aristotle's stylistic strategy is the textual phenomenon; Hawhee's umbrella is the modern interpretive construct.

This ontology accordingly registers both the cluster's lexical-textual substrate (Aristotle's own Greek terms, kept as concept nodes whenever multiple units anchor on them) and its interpretive superstructure (the 22 readings that organize how authors take up that substrate). The four debate axes intersect: Papachristou's tripartite reading (Axis 4) presupposes the presentational pole on Axis 1 and the cognitive pole on Axis 3; Nussbaum's interpretivism (Axis 1) entails her central-faculty view (Axis 2); the rhetorical wing's appropriation (Axis 3) typically conscripts Nussbaum's interpretivism (Axis 1) without endorsing her resistance to image-residue language.
