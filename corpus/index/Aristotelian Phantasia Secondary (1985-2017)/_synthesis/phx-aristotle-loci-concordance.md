*Synthesis of every canonicalized Bekker citation across the eight phantasia-cluster units (PHX-01 … PHX-08). Generated 2026-05-11.*

**Coverage**: 288 canonical loci aggregated from 628 raw locus entries across the eight unit JSONs. Tier-A = cited by ≥3 of 8 units (n=43). Tier-B = 1–2 units (n=245).

**Canonicalization rule**: loci are bucketed by `(work, chapter, Bekker-page, column)` — so e.g. *DA* III.3 428a1, 428a3, and 428a8–11 collapse into a single canonical entry `DA III.3 428a1–24` whose line-range is the min–max of all units' attested line citations. Chapter inference uses standard OCT page ranges for *De Anima*, *Rhetoric*, *Metaphysics*, *NE*, *EE*, and *Politics*.

## 1. Density narrative: which Aristotle works carry the debate

The cluster's center of gravity is unambiguous: ***De Anima*** III.3 is the **single most-fought-over passage in the secondary literature on phantasia from 1985 to 2017**. Of 79 canonical *De Anima* loci, 24 are Tier-A — i.e., nearly **one in three *DA* citations is shared by three or more cluster articles**. The chapter that anchors the whole cluster is *De Anima* III.3 (the *phantasia*-dilemma chapter, 427b14–429a9): the three pages 428a, 428b, and 429a together draw **eight, seven, and seven citing-units** respectively. Each of these three pages is touched by every single article in the cluster except one or two. *DA* III.3 is **the cluster's primary battlefront**.

The runner-up is the much smaller but disproportionately important locus ***De Memoria*** **450a4–27** — the *phantasma-as-pathēma* passage, where memory is parasitic on the imagination-image left behind by an earlier perception. This is cited by **all eight units**. It is the only non-*De-Anima* passage attested by literally everyone. Within the cluster, *De Memoria* functions as the *empirical-physiological supplement* to the abstract psychology of *DA* III.3: when an article wants to ground its phantasia-reading in a more concrete physiology, it cites *De Mem.* 450a.

The third center of gravity is ***De Insomniis*** (the treatise on dreams), with six Tier-A loci concentrated at 458b, 459a, 460a, 460b, 461b, and 462a. *Insomn.* is the *naturalistic-residue* text: it shows phantasmata persisting in the perceptual organs even when the sense-object is absent (the bedrock of the Frede-type 'supervenience' reading). The fact that *Insomn.* has more Tier-A loci than any other minor work is the strongest single piece of evidence that the cluster reads phantasia *primarily as a physiological-perceptual residue capacity*, not as a Romantic-imagination productive faculty.

The ***Rhetoric*** subset is structurally different. Only the rhetoricians (O'Gorman, Gonzalez, Hawhee, with Nussbaum as a frequent visitor) cite it densely, but the loci they share are dense and tight: **Rhet. I.11 1370a18–29 + 1370b32–33 + 1371a9–19** form a compact triplet on *phantasia-as-pleasure-anticipation* — five, four, and four units respectively. The single most disputed locus in the whole rhetoric subset is the **'mere show' crux at *Rhet.* III.1 1404a1–25**, where *phantasia* is glossed (or so traditional translations have it) as theatrical showiness in delivery (*hypokrisis*). Gonzalez 2006 builds his entire essay on rebutting that reading; O'Gorman and Hawhee enlist it for opposite ends.

The ***Nicomachean Ethics*** contributes one Tier-A locus — **NE VII 1147b4–24**, the *akrasia* discussion — cited by Nussbaum, Caston, and Gonzalez. *NE* VI on *phronēsis* is widely cited but each citation tends to be unit-idiosyncratic (cluster-Tier-B).

The ***De Motu Animalium*** is essential to one specific sub-debate: how phantasia drives **animal motion**. *De Motu* 700b (Tier-A, four units: Nussbaum, Caston, O'Gorman, Gonzalez) is the locus classicus for *phantasia-orexis-praxis* causal chains.

The ***Metaphysics*** contributes thirty loci but none reach Tier-A: each citation is too article-specific (Caston cites Met. IV on Protagoras and antirealism; Frede cites Met. IX on energeia; etc.). Met. is *background*, not *battlefront*.

The ***Sophistical Refutations*** functions as Gonzalez's signature side-channel: he uses **SE 164a20–24**, where Aristotle gestures at *phainomenon*-arguments running in parallel with apparent-good arguments, to argue that *phantasia* in the *Rhetoric* III.1 1404a11 reference must be the *same* technical-psychological capacity, not stage-show ornament. Six of the seven *SE* loci are Gonzalez's; one is Hawhee's. The presence of *SE* in the cluster at all is therefore a Gonzalez-effect.

The ***Poetics*** contributes seven loci, all of them rhetorical-vision territory (Hawhee, O'Gorman) — three of them concentrated at **Poet. 17 1455a22–34**, the famous "*pro ommatōn tithesthai*" passage where the dramatist must "set the scene before his eyes." This is the locus that anchors Hawhee's "rhetorical vision" thesis and connects the cluster to *Rhet.* III.10–11 on *energeia*-bringing-before-the-eyes. *Poet.* is the *rhetorical-vision auxiliary*.

The ***Physics*** contributes only eleven loci, almost all in IV (on time and the *now*) — clustered around the Bowin-adjacent question of whether time-perception requires phantasia. These loci are bridges to the parallel "Aristotelian Motion and Time Secondary" pipeline (which contains Bowin 2017 alone) rather than internal phantasia-cluster battlefronts.

Three observations follow. First, the **three rhetoric pieces** (O'Gorman, Gonzalez, Hawhee) genuinely read *Aristotle's* phantasia — they do not stay inside the *Rhetoric*. Gonzalez especially treats *DA* III.3 as the primary reference and *Rhet.* III.1 as the application case. Second, **Frede 1992 is the most *DA*-saturated** of any unit (65 *DA* loci in 9 pages of text), confirming her status as the cluster's *DA*-purist. Third, **Hawhee 2011** has the smallest Aristotle footprint (42 loci total) but the highest *cross-pipeline* density (Greek lemmas, modern rhetoric theorists). She uses Aristotle as a relay-point, not a quarry.

Anyone writing on Aristotle's phantasia must engage the III.3 dilemma-and-resolution sequence; everything else is technique.

## 3. Cross-cluster citation patterns

Three signature patterns emerge from the per-locus citing-unit lists:

**Pattern A — the 'whole-cluster lemma' (8 units)**: only two loci recruit every unit in the cluster: ***DA* III.3 428a** and ***De Mem.* 450a**. Together these define the *minimum mandatory citation list* for any future phantasia paper.

**Pattern B — the 'philosophy vs. rhetoric' fault-line**: every Tier-A *De Anima*, *De Memoria*, *De Insomniis*, *De Motu* locus draws all four philosophy-of-mind authors (PHX-01, 02, 03, 04, 08); every Tier-A *Rhetoric* locus draws all three rhetoricians (PHX-05, 06, 07). The two clusters overlap on **Rhet. I.11 1370a, *DA* III.3 428a, and *De Mem.* 450a** — these three loci are the cluster's *crossover canon*.

**Pattern C — the 'rhetorician's *DA* mediator'**: when a rhetoric paper cites *De Anima*, it almost always cites III.3 428a, III.7 431a–b, or III.10 434a — i.e. the **psychology-of-action portions of *DA* III**, not the methodological *DA* I or the matter-form *DA* II. The rhetoricians treat phantasia as a *practical-cognitive* faculty, not as a metaphysical-soul-faculty. Gonzalez 2006 is the exception that proves the rule: he cites *DA* I.1 explicitly in order to argue that the *Rhetoric* III.1 1404a11 reference is the *same* faculty as the one introduced at *DA* I.1 403a8–10.

## 5. Methodological notes

**Bucketing rule**: each locus key is `(work, chapter, Bekker-page-number, column-letter)`. Within a bucket, all line-range citations (e.g. 428a1, 428a3–5, 428a8–11) are merged into a single canonical entry whose displayed line-range is the min–max of attestations. This deliberately under-discriminates within a column-of-text (so e.g. 'DA III.3 428a1–24' may aggregate three substantively distinct sub-arguments). For fine-grained line-level tracking, consult the per-unit JSONs directly.

**Chapter inference**: for entries that gave only a Bekker number without an explicit chapter, the chapter was inferred from standard OCT page-ranges. The boundary cases handled explicitly are *Rhet.* II.26/III.1 (where 1403b/1404a straddles books II and III; passages from 1404a onward are assigned to III.1, the 'mere show' chapter).

**Coverage gaps**: a small number of Tier-B entries lack Bekker numbers entirely (e.g. *Categories*, *De Interpretatione* tagged at the work level without specific reference). These are retained as `(no-bekker)` placeholders since they nonetheless record that the unit invoked the work.

**Theme summary**: the `theme_summary` column gives the *most informative* per-unit theme available — when units disagree about why they cite a locus, the longest gloss is preserved with the unit-attribution prefix.
