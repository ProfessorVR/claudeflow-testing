# bor-sec-03 — Elpidorou & Freeman, "Affectivity in Heidegger II: Temporality, Boredom, and Beyond" (2015)

**Type:** journal survey/synthesis essay (*Philosophy Compass*, companion piece II of II) · **Discipline:** philosophy (Heideggerian phenomenology of affectivity and temporality) · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** Strand A — the cluster's most direct temporal bridge between *Being and Time* (BT) Division II and FCM's three-form boredom analysis (fcm-04–fcm-07); second half of the two-part study whose companion (bor-sec-02) supplies the general BT-Division-I architecture of Befindlichkeit/Stimmung this essay presupposes throughout.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Elpidorou and Freeman (FE) pick up exactly where bor-sec-02 left off: having restricted that essay to Division I of BT, they now re-describe Befindlichkeit in terms of Heidegger's Division II claim that temporality (*Zeitlichkeit*) is the sense of Dasein's being (FE, p.672, citing BT 38/17). The essay states a threefold aim (FE, p.672, paraphrased): revisit Befindlichkeit in light of Division II's temporality; discuss the fundamental mood (*Grundstimmung*) of boredom (*Langeweile*) and its ontological significance; and close with a brief account of how Heidegger's notion of mood changes in his later thinking. The essay's governing wager, stated explicitly, is that any explication of Heidegger's affectivity that leaves the relation between temporality and Befindlichkeit unspecified "will be importantly incomplete" (FE, p.673). FE cash this wager out twice: first for fear, anxiety, and (briefly) hope via BT §68, and then, at greater length, for Heidegger's three forms of boredom via FCM — before closing with a short account of the post-1930 shift to historically oriented fundamental moods and a concluding appraisal of what Heidegger's affectivity account is worth overall.

## 2. Structure

The essay runs across roughly thirteen journal pages (pp.672–684) in four numbered parts plus an abstract, short biographies, and unusually extensive endnotes:

1. **§1 Befindlichkeit and Temporality** (pp.673–679 approx.), in two subsections:
   - **1.1 Dasein's Temporality** (pp.673–675 approx.) — care (*Sorge*) as the unity of existence/facticity/falling, itself grounded in originary temporality; anticipatory resoluteness (*vorlaufende Entschlossenheit*) as the site where this ecstatic-horizonal unity becomes transparent to Dasein; the ontological priority of the future.
   - **1.2 The Temporality of Befindlichkeit: Having Been** (pp.675–679 approx.) — moods grounded primarily in the originary past; a sub-section **1.2.1 Fear, anxiety, and a bit of hope** individuating fear (grounded in forgetting) from anxiety (grounded in repetition/retrieval) and closing with Heidegger's idiosyncratic, past-grounded reading of hope.
2. **§2 Boredom** (pp.679–682 approx.) — a compressed exposition of FCM's three forms of *Langeweile*: becoming-bored-by (*Gelangweiltwerden von etwas*), being-bored-with (*Sichlangweilen bei etwas*), and profound boredom (*tiefe Langweile*), each individuated by its distinctive relation to "how time passes" and ordered by increasing existential depth.
3. **§3 Moods in Heidegger's Later Thinking** (p.682 approx.) — the post-1930 shift from a transcendental, Dasein-centric conception of mood to an historically oriented one, illustrated by wonder (*Erstaunen*, the mood of the first, Presocratic beginning) and reserve (*Verhaltenheit*, the mood of a possible new beginning).
4. **§4 Conclusion** (pp.682–684 approx.) — a valuation of the overall worth of Heidegger's (especially pre-1930) account of affectivity, followed by short author biographies and fifty-plus lines of endnotes and works cited.

## 3. Constructs

As a synoptic exposition rather than an empirical or debate-staging piece, this essay treats a narrow band of the cluster's construct axis, but treats the temporal dimension of that band with unusual precision.

- **Profound/deep boredom (FCM 3rd form)** is treated at greatest length (pp.681–682 approx.): an "extreme, all-encompassing, and overwhelming experience in which everything bores us" (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.681, approx.) and, unlike the first form, one there is "no point in fighting" (≤12-word anchor, FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.681 approx.). FE stress its depersonalizing character — it is "boring for one," rendering Dasein "an undifferentiated no one" (≤12 words, FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.681–682 approx.) — and its paradoxical productivity: the total withdrawal of significance (a "telling refusal") simultaneously discloses Dasein's own unexploited possibilities and its freedom.
- **Being-bored-with (2nd form)** is treated at moderate length (pp.679–680 approx.) via the dinner-party example: a pleasant evening attended voluntarily, in which nothing is "at all... boring" (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.680 approx.) and yet which retrospective reflection reveals to have bored us all along. FE emphasize that this form "arises from out of Dasein itself" (≤12 words, FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.680 approx.) rather than from an external boring object — the feature that makes it, for FE, more existentially profound than the first form.
- **Being-bored-by (1st form)** receives the briefest treatment (p.679 approx.), via FE's own substituted illustration — waiting at a departure gate for a flight already delayed for hours — rather than Heidegger's train-station scene, though the underlying analysis (left empty + held in limbo; fulfillment postponed; time dragging) is a faithful paraphrase of FCM.
- **Temporality of boredom (time-drag)** is this essay's true organizing construct, not a single row among others but the axis along which FE differentiate all three forms: dragging time (form 1), time standing still (form 2), and an entrancement in which past, present, and future "meld together" into an "unarticulated unity" (form 3) (≤12-word anchor, FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.681–682 approx.).
- **Monotony/meaninglessness** is present but subordinate — most explicit in profound boredom, where "beings as a whole withdraw" and lose "all significance" (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.681 approx.), and echoed structurally (though not terminologically) in FE's earlier treatment of anxiety, where "the world 'has sunk into insignificance'" (≤10 words, FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.677 approx.).
- Not treated at all: boredom proneness, situational-vs-trait/state framing, mind-wandering/task-unrelated thought, attentional disengagement/meta-awareness, boredom-as-regulatory-signal (functional account), and flow/engagement as an explicit contrast pole. These belong to the psychological literature this purely exegetical essay does not engage; several are the express subject of later cluster entries by the same authors (bor-sec-04, bor-sec-05, bor-sec-25, bor-sec-26 — see §7 below).

## 4. Reading of FCM

FE's treatment of §2 is a compressed, largely faithful paraphrase of Heidegger's own argument in Part One of FCM (1929/30) rather than an independent scholarly intervention against it — its distinctive contribution is to weld that argument explicitly onto the BT-Division-II temporality apparatus developed in the essay's own §1, an integration that FCM's own text leaves comparatively implicit.

**fcm-04 (first form).** FE's account of becoming-bored-by tracks fcm-04's core vocabulary directly — "left empty" and "held in limbo" as the two structural moments, with time dragging as the temporal ground of the second (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.679 approx.; cf. fcm-04 §§21, 23c–d). FE substitute an airport-gate scenario for Heidegger's train-station scene but leave the analysis itself unchanged.

**fcm-05 (second form).** FE track fcm-05's dinner-party example and its central claims closely: no determinate object is boring; boredom instead arises from Dasein's own comportment toward the evening; the temporal modification here is not dragging but standing still, because absorption in the party's present has cut Dasein off from its own having-been and future (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.679–680 approx.; cf. fcm-05). FE explicitly rank this form "more profound" than the first because it originates in Dasein itself and constitutes "an inauthentic modification of our temporal existence" (≤12 words, FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.680 approx.).

**fcm-06 (third form).** This is where FE's temporal synthesis is most substantial, and the essay's real center of gravity for FCM. Profound boredom's total withdrawal of significance is temporal "if understood properly" (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.681 approx.): it means not only that surrounding entities are drained of meaning but that nothing carries future prospects and nothing relates to having-been. FE gloss Heidegger's claim that Dasein's originary temporality has itself been "modified" in profound boredom as a melding of all three ecstases — future, present, and having-been — into the single "unarticulated unity" that "entrances" Dasein (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.681–682 approx.; cf. fcm-06 §§29–36). FE also carry forward fcm-06's closing turn: entrancement issues not in despair but in an "exceptional understanding" of Dasein's own being, a call to resolute self-disclosure enacted in the *moment of vision* (*Augenblick*) — explicitly recycling the BT vocabulary of anticipatory resoluteness developed in the essay's own §1.1 (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.682 approx.; cf. fcm-06).

**fcm-07 (particular profound boredom of contemporary Dasein).** FE do not engage fcm-07's specific diagnostic question — whether profound boredom is *the* fundamental attunement of a specific historical Dasein — at all; their FCM exposition stops at the general structure of the third form. FE's own §3, however, on the post-1930 turn toward historically oriented Grundstimmungen (wonder, reserve) that "found" entire epochs of Being, is thematically continuous with fcm-07's question of an epoch-specific fundamental mood, even though FE never draw this connection explicitly (P/anticipatory-application — our own bridge, not a claim FE make themselves).

## 5. Findings/Claims

1. Originary temporality (*ursprüngliche Zeitlichkeit*) is the sense of Dasein's being and grounds care's threefold structure (existence, facticity, falling); it is ecstatic-horizonal and dynamically, not sequentially, unified — the future has ontological "primacy" even though, on Heidegger's own paradoxical formulation, no ecstasis is strictly "later" or "earlier" than another (FE, pp.673–675 approx.).
2. Anticipatory resoluteness is the site where this ecstatic unity becomes transparent: Dasein's authentic comportment toward its own death (*Vorlaufen*) discloses the "primordial phenomenon of future" and requires a correlative retrieval of thrownness, i.e., having-been (FE, pp.674–675 approx.).
3. Moods (Befindlichkeit's ontic manifestations) are grounded primarily, though not exclusively, in the originary past: they "bring Dasein back to something," disclosing the inherited burden of thrownness Dasein did not choose and cannot fully control (FE, pp.675–676 approx.).
4. Fear and anxiety are individuated by their differing mode of having-been: fear is grounded in *forgetting* (an inauthentic having-been), releasing an inauthentic awaiting and a bewildered "jumble of hovering" present; anxiety is grounded in *repetition/retrieval* (*Wiederholung*), releasing authentic anticipation and the authentic *moment of vision* (FE, pp.676–678 approx.).
5. Hope, though it looks futural, is on FE's reading of Heidegger actually grounded in the past: to hope is to "hope for something for oneself," which presupposes a prior relation to oneself as already thrown and burdened (FE, p.678–679 approx.) — the only positively valenced mood BT treats at any length.
6. FCM's three forms of boredom are individuated by their distinct relation to "how time passes": dragging (form 1), standing-still (form 2), and an entrancing melding of all three ecstases (form 3); each form is progressively more fundamental in what it discloses (FE, pp.679–682 approx.).
7. Profound boredom's total withdrawal of significance is not merely privative: the "telling refusal" of beings as a whole is itself "a making manifest" that discloses Dasein's own unexploited possibilities and its freedom, issuing in a call to resolute self-appropriation rather than despair (FE, p.682 approx.).
8. After 1930, Heidegger's notion of mood shifts from a Dasein-centric transcendental structure to an historically oriented one: fundamental moods (wonder for the first, Presocratic beginning; reserve for a possible new beginning, characterized by terror and awe) "found" entire epochs of Being rather than merely disclosing Dasein's own existence (FE, p.682 approx.).
9. FE's concluding valuation: the value of Heidegger's account of affectivity (especially prior to 1930) lies chiefly in showing that affectivity is an irrevocable, constitutive dimension of human existence, inseparable from understanding, falling, and discourse — affectivity shows us "we are here," discloses how we are faring, and "pushes" us toward becoming who we want to be (FE, pp.683–684 approx.).

## 7. Analytical role

*(P/anticipatory-application) — the following are our interpretive links to Part III, not claims FE make themselves.*

FE's temporal individuation of the three boredom forms (drag / stasis / entrancement) offers Part III a ready-made phenomenological vocabulary for distinguishing *qualitatively different* experiences of monotony across the desktop-3D and VR-deployment strata, prior to and independent of any physiological instrumentation *(P/anticipatory-application)*.

FE's insistence that moods are "for the most part" neither caused nor chosen by Dasein but "assail us" out of thrownness cautions against treating survey items that ask participants to explain "why" they were bored as transparent windows onto boredom's actual, largely pre-reflective genesis — a methodological caveat relevant to any future self-report channel in the (not-yet-built) VR Attention Study dataset *(P/anticipatory-application)*.

FE's own exposition — the first form caused "from outside," the second and third "aris[ing] from out of Dasein itself" — anticipates, without naming it, the cluster's own debate axis "monotony/environment-caused ↔ meaning/agent-caused." Part III's survey-analysis distinction between deployment-side design conditions and student-side activation (cf. the VLE proximity-voice correction) is structurally continuous with this same environment-caused/self-caused split, and this essay is the cluster's clearest early statement of why the split matters temporally, not just causally *(P/anticipatory-application)*.

FE's closing claim that profound boredom's entrancement issues not in despair but in a disclosure of freedom and a call to resolute self-appropriation is the direct phenomenological seed later literalized, in functional-psychology terms, as Elpidorou's "regulatory" account of boredom (bor-sec-25, bor-sec-26): what FE describe here as an ontological call to action is what the later solo papers redescribe as boredom's adaptive, action-guiding function. Likewise, this essay's own individuation of fear/anxiety/boredom by their differing temporal grounding (§1.2.1 + §2) is the direct precursor to the dedicated triangulation of exactly these three moods in bor-sec-05 ("Fear, Anxiety, and Boredom," 2020), and its faithful, non-adjudicated exposition of profound boredom's structure is the precise problematic that bor-sec-04 ("Is Profound Boredom Boredom?," 2019) later interrogates against the state/trait psychological literature *(P/anticipatory-application)*.
