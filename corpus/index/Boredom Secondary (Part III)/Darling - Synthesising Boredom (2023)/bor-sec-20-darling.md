# Darling (2023) — *Synthesising Boredom: A Predictive Processing Approach*

**Type:** original research article (*Synthese*, Springer, 202:157, 27pp., DOI 10.1007/s11229-023-04380-3) · **Discipline:** psychology / cognitive science — predictive-processing (computational) theory of affect, in explicit dialogue with Heideggerian phenomenology · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus-role:** Strand-C anchor entry — the cluster's principal predictive-processing/active-inference THEORY-MODEL source. Author Tom Darling (Monash Centre for Consciousness & Contemplative Studies) sets out to *reconcile* rather than adjudicate between the dominant psychological construal of boredom (a self-regulating emotion) and the Heideggerian phenomenological construal (an existentially revelatory mood), using the predictive-processing/active-inference framework as the common formal idiom. Page anchors below follow the article's own running "Page N of 27" pagination (p.1–p.27); marked approximate only where a claim spans a page break.

## Thesis / Aim

Darling identifies a tension between two conceptions of boredom that rarely speak to one another: the psychological literature's dominant view of boredom as an adaptive, self-regulatory emotion (p.1), and the phenomenological tradition's view — following Heidegger — of boredom as a *Grundstimmung*, a fundamental mood that discloses "the predicament of human existence" (Slaby 2010, paraphrased, p.2) (FAITHFUL-Darling, p.2). The paper's stated aim is to use predictive processing (broadly construed, including active inference and the Free Energy Principle) to offer "an integrative account of boredom" that resolves this tension (abstract, p.1) (FE). Three payoffs are claimed at the outset: predictive processing (1) accommodates and extends the functional psychological account by formally explaining the "Goldilocks zone" of optimal engagement and boredom's "call to action" (Danckert & Elpidorou, 2023, p.494) (FE); (2) naturalises the phenomenological tradition without reducing it away, addressing Elpidorou's own worry that no existing account does justice to both outlooks (2018c, p.177, paraphrased) (FE); and (3) dissolves the "unitary vs. plural" debate about boredom's conceptual status by locating emotion and mood at different levels of one predictive hierarchy (pp.2–3) (FE).

## Structure

1. **§1 Introduction: the problem of boredom** (pp.1–4) — stakes the "problem of boredom" (Svendsen), surveys the psychological self-regulation literature, introduces the Heideggerian mood tradition, and previews the predictive-processing synthesis.
2. **§2 Boredom as a self-regulating emotion** (pp.4–6) — the psychological literature: definition, attention, meaning, exploit/explore switching, the Goldilocks zone, functional theories (incl. Westgate & Wilson's MAC model).
3. **§3 Heidegger's phenomenology of mood** (pp.6–9) — *Grundstimmung*, *Stimmung*/*Befindlichkeit*, mood as pre-intentional and world-disclosive, mood vs. emotion.
4. **§4 The Predictive Processing Approach**, incl. **§4.1 Affective inference** (pp.8–13) — predictive processing/active inference/FEP fundamentals; interoceptive, information-theoretic (valence), and computational (Hesp et al.) accounts of affect; mood as hyperprior (Clark et al.; Kiverstein et al.'s "feeling of grip").
5. **§5 Synthesising boredom**, with **§5.1 Adaptive boredom**, **§5.2 Maladaptive boredom**, **§5.3 The existential problem of boredom** (pp.12–19) — the paper's constructive core: boredom-as-emotion tracking error-dynamics, boredom-as-mood as hyperprior, existential escapes/addiction, and the "sticky hyperprior" account of maladaptive/existential boredom, explicitly analogised to depression.
6. **§6 Concluding remarks** (pp.19–20) — distinguishes interoceptive/emotional/affective inference; restates the synthesis.
7. **References** (pp.20–27).

## Constructs

- **Boredom-as-regulatory-signal (functional account)** — the paper's starting frame and, after the predictive-processing turn, its own reconstructed conclusion: boredom as the "aversive experience of wanting, but being unable, to engage in satisfying activity" (Eastwood et al. 2012, p.483, quoted at p.4) (FAITHFUL-Darling, p.4), re-derived in §5.1 as a mechanism tracking the *rate* of prediction-error reduction relative to an agent's expected rate, catalysing exploit/explore domain-switches (pp.2, 4–6, 12–14) (FE).
- **Attentional disengagement / meta-awareness** — attention is framed as boredom's central regulatory mechanism: "the mind may wander" when bored, with agents struggling to sustain attention on the present task (p.4, citing Danckert & Merrifield 2018; Yakobi et al. 2021) (FAITHFUL-Darling, p.4); later reframed computationally as a candidate mechanism for *precision*-estimation, since attention is "often suggested as a mechanism for controlling precision-estimation" (p.17, footnote10) (FE).
- **Mind-wandering / task-unrelated thought** — named explicitly, though briefly, via the "mind may wander" phrase above (p.4); not elaborated as a distinct construct beyond this single mention (FE).
- **Monotony / meaninglessness** — a recurring thread: perceived meaninglessness drives boredom's self-regulatory search for engagement in §2 (pp.4–6, citing Van Tilburg & Igou; Chan et al. 2018) (FE), and resurfaces in §5.3 as the affective core of *existential* boredom, likened to Frankl's "existential vacuum," where "air and life is drained away through meaninglessness" (p.19, paraphrasing Frankl 1967) (FAITHFUL-Darling, p.19).
- **Flow / engagement (contrast pole)** — never named as such (no citation of Csikszentmihalyi or "flow" as a term, though "flow" appears twice in the reference list only), but functions throughout as boredom's implicit contrast: satisfying activity, "meaningful engagement," and the world's "affective allure" (Krueger & Colombetti, p.18) recur as what boredom's absence signals the lack of (pp.4, 6, 13–14, 18–19) (FE).
- **Situational vs. trait/state boredom** — engaged mainly via the "unitary vs. construct" debate (is boredom "one or many"?, citing Elpidorou 2021; Tempelaar & Niculescu 2022, p.5) (FE); predictive processing's own resolution — that emotion and mood are simply different hierarchical levels and timescales of one system — is offered explicitly as dissolving rather than adjudicating this state/trait-adjacent question (pp.3, 20) (FE).
- **Boredom proneness** — a light touch: the term itself is not used in Darling's own prose, but the maladaptive-escape discussion in §5.2 leans on a cluster of boredom-proneness studies (Biolcati et al. 2016, 2018; Blaszczynski et al. 1990; Chou et al. 2018; Watt & Vodanovich 1992) cited for links between boredom and addictive/impulsive behaviour (p.15) (FE).
- **Profound/deep boredom (FCM 3rd form)** — see *Reading of FCM* below; engaged thematically (via Grundstimmung and the "sticky hyperprior"/depression analysis) rather than via FCM's own tripartite vocabulary (pp.6–9, 15, 18–19) (FE).

*Not substantively engaged*: **temporality of boredom (time-drag)** appears only obliquely, via mood-as-"momentum" (Eldar et al. 2016, p.13) rather than the phenomenology of subjective time dragging; **being-bored-by (1st form)** and **being-bored-with (2nd form)** are not discussed at all — Darling's Heidegger section stays at the level of *Grundstimmung* generally and never differentiates FCM's three forms (see below).

## Reading of FCM

Darling's engagement with Heidegger is substantial (an entire section, §3, plus continued integration through §§4–6) but operates almost entirely at the level of Heidegger's *general* phenomenology of mood — drawing on *Being and Time* (1962) for the bulk of its vocabulary (*Befindlichkeit*, *Stimmung*, primordial disclosure, "we are always in a mood") — rather than on *The Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics*' (FCM) specific tripartite analysis of boredom. Direct citations to FCM (Heidegger 1995) are limited to exactly two moments: first, a scene-setting note that Heidegger devoted "some 130 (originally German) pages" to boredom in FCM, there identifying it as a *Grundstimmung* (p.6) (FAITHFUL-Darling, p.6); second, two short quotations from FCM p.67 — moods "colour our entire existence like an essential background atmosphere," and moods are a "fundamental manner and fundamental way of Being" (pp.7–8, quoting Heidegger 1995, p.67) (FAITHFUL-Darling, pp.7-8). Beyond these, Darling's Heideggerian citations (primordial disclosure, p.134; "inner condition which then reaches forth," p.176; "always in a mood," p.183) are all to *Being and Time*, not FCM.

Consequently, Darling's paper contains **no discussion of FCM's tripartite typology** — no being-bored-by (first form), no being-bored-with (second form), no dinner-party or train-station examples, and no section-level (§19–38) citations to FCM's own architecture. This is a materially different engagement strategy from other units in this cluster (e.g., bor-sec-06) that walk through the three forms individually. Where Darling's own analysis does resonate strongly with FCM's *third* form is thematically, not citationally: §5.3's account of a maladaptive "sticky hyperprior" — in which precision-estimation becomes rigid, "no action policy is appealing" (p.17), and "the world itself is boring" (p.18) (FAITHFUL-Darling, p.18) — closely parallels FCM's profound boredom (the impersonal "it is boring for one," the total withdrawal of significance), but Darling reaches this territory via Clark, Watson & Friston's (2018) computational model of mood-in-depression and Ratcliffe's phenomenology of depression, not via any direct citation to FCM's own profound-boredom sections (pp.132–159). Given this, a bridge to **fcm-06-third-form** is warranted only as our own thematic alignment (P/anticipatory-application), explicitly *not* a claim that Darling himself makes about FCM's specific material; no bridge to fcm-04-first-form or fcm-05-second-form is warranted, since neither is engaged in any form.

## Findings / Claims

1. **The central reconciliation**: boredom-as-emotion (psychology's self-regulating adaptive state) and boredom-as-mood (Heidegger's *Grundstimmung*) are not different *kinds* of thing but different *levels* of one predictive hierarchy — short-term precision-weighted emotion vs. longer-term hyperprior mood — dissolving rather than adjudicating the functional/existential tension (pp.2–3, 19–20) (FE).
2. **Adaptive boredom is formally reconstructed**: boredom-as-emotion tracks the *rate* of prediction-error reduction relative to an agent's *expected* rate of error-reduction; negative valence signals a worse-than-expected learning gradient, and this formally cashes out the psychological "Goldilocks zone" as a feature of approximately-optimal Bayesian prediction-error minimisation (pp.12–14) (FE).
3. **Mood is a hyperprior**: boredom-as-mood sits higher in the predictive hierarchy, more time-invariant, averaging error-dynamics over longer timescales and constituting a "feeling of grip" on the world (after Kiverstein et al. 2019, 2020) — offered as the direct formal analogue of Heideggerian attunement (pp.12–13) (FE).
4. **Maladaptive/existential boredom is aberrant precision-estimation**: an overly rigid, "sticky hyperprior" produces globally pervasive prediction errors, so that no course of action resolves expected uncertainty and "the world itself is boring" — a mechanism explicitly likened to Clark et al.'s (2018) and Ramstead et al.'s (2020) active-inference account of major depression, where all policies are, subjectively, "things that could be done, but not things I can do" (2020, p.27, quoted at p.17) (FAITHFUL-Darling, p.17).
5. **Existential-escape behaviours are locally adaptive, globally maladaptive**: addictive coping strategies (binge eating, drinking, gambling, gaming, pornography, social media) reduce local uncertainty and "feel great" in the moment, but degrade an agent's long-term capacity for error-reduction; Darling stresses this need not indicate pathology — "the system is functioning largely how it is meant to (biologically)" (p.15, paraphrased) (FAITHFUL-Darling, p.15).
6. **Affective inference is differentiated into three species** — interoceptive, emotional, and affective inference — clarifying that mood (hyperpriors over precision) and emotion (precision of policy-beliefs) are related but formally distinct constructs within one active-inference scheme (pp.19–20) (FE).
7. **The "dark room problem" is explicitly bracketed**: Darling notes, but declines to resolve, the objection that a pure error-minimiser should seek maximal boredom/inactivity (p.3, footnote 1) (FE).

## Analytical role (anticipatory-application)

*(P/anticipatory-application) — the following are our interpretive links to Part III, not claims Darling makes himself.*

First, Darling's dual-register synthesis gives Part III a principled, non-EEG-dependent vocabulary for interpreting disengagement in VLE/2D/VR contexts as *either* a Goldilocks-zone mismatch (a task too easy or too complex — addressable through redesign) *or* a deeper, mood-level "loss of grip" (harder to remediate through task redesign alone). This computational distinction echoes, without collapsing into, the FCM first/second-form vs. third-form distinction that Strand A's units draw out directly from Heidegger — Part III can cite Darling for the *mechanism* (error-dynamics, precision) and the FCM units for the *phenomenology*, without implying Darling himself performs that FCM exegesis.

Second, because predictive processing formalises precision-weighting as a mechanism that tracks attentional allocation, this source is currently the most plausible theoretical bridge (flagged, not asserted) for interpreting eye-tracking, gaze-variance, or pupillometry channels in the not-yet-built *Boredom Experiment (VR Attention Study)* dataset entry: precision-weighting predicts where, and how rigidly, attention is held, which is exactly what such channels would measure. **`****** UNVERIFIED:`** no `grounds-measure` bridge is asserted as established — the dataset entry remains unbuilt as of 2026-07-14, and Darling's own paper contains no first-party measurement of any kind.

Third, Darling's explicit citation of Elpidorou's regulatory theory of boredom (2018a, "The Bored Mind is a Guiding Mind," cited among the self-regulation literature at p.4) situates this source as *closely related to but not identical with* the cluster's regulatory-theory units (bor-sec-25/bor-sec-26, per this batch's build plan — not yet confirmed on disk). Darling's predictive-processing account can be read as supplying the formal error-dynamics mechanism that Elpidorou's regulatory theory gestures toward without itself formalising; Part III should cite the two as complementary rather than interchangeable — Elpidorou's account motivates *why* boredom regulates, Darling's supplies a candidate mechanism for *how*.

Fourth, the existential-escape/addiction analysis (§5.2) offers Part III a ready framework for discussing avoidant technology use (phone-checking, gaming, social media) as a boredom-management strategy, potentially relevant if Part III's survey or deployment-context material touches disengagement-driven device use during 2D-pole sessions — again pairing rather than substituting for the cluster's dedicated addiction/escape literature.
