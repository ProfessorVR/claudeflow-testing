# bor-sec-28 — Kim, Joochan, Jungryul Seo, and Teemu H. Laine, "Detecting Boredom from Eye Gaze and EEG" (2018)

Its eye-gaze method detail is captured here in full for possible future Strand E cross-reference (e.g., bor-sec-38, Scharinger et al.'s pupil-dilation/EEG-alpha study), though it is classified under its assigned Strand D.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Kim, Seo, and Laine (*Biomedical Signal Processing and Control* 46, 2018, pp.302-313) identify a specific empirical gap: prior physiological boredom-detection studies had examined EEG or eye-gaze signals in isolation, but none had investigated whether the two correlate with one another when a person becomes bored (FE, p.302-303). To fill this gap the authors design and execute a short, single-session experiment (minimum protocol duration 120 seconds) in which participants view a deliberately monotonous, content-neutral video stimulus while wearing a four-electrode consumer EEG headband (Muse) and a screen-based eye tracker (Eye Tribe), bracketed by a neutral baseline image and a post-stimulus self-report boredom rating (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.307). Three hypotheses organize the study: (1) losing attention to the task at hand signals boredom; (2) EEG and gaze data will each show a distinctive pattern, relative to an "expected" engaged state, when bored; and (3) a correlation should therefore exist between the EEG and gaze data during boredom (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.303). A non-machine-learning, threshold-based pipeline — Signal Magnitude Area (SMA) thresholding for EEG and an Area-of-Interest (AOI) hitbox/fixation-count algorithm for gaze — identifies temporally coincident "Potential Boredom Regions" (PBRs) across all 13 analyzed participants, while a post-hoc 1-9 self-report rating independently confirms boredom was elicited but does not itself correlate with any extracted physiological feature (FE, p.309-312).

## 3. Constructs

Mapped onto the controlled construct axis, the source treats:

- **situational vs. trait/state boredom** — explicitly adopted and operationalized as a state: the authors distinguish trait boredom ("the tendency of an individual to become bored," citing Fahlman et al. [16]) from state boredom ("a transitory emotion with a relatively short timespan," citing Mikulas & Vodanovich [39]) and declare that since the video stimulus is an external factor, "we treat the boredom as a state rather than a trait" (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.304).
- **boredom proneness** — background only (trait boredom, Boredom Proneness Scale, Farmer & Sundberg [38], p.304); explicitly flagged as an unaddressed limitation ("our experiment assessed only state boredom while disregarding trait boredom; the latter may have an effect on threshold calculations for individual participants," FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.312), with trait measurement proposed as a future parameter (FE, p.312-313).
- **attentional disengagement / meta-awareness** — the paper's conceptual and experimental core. Eastwood et al.'s attention-based definition grounds the study (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.304), and Fisher/Hamilton's conclusion that "boredom is caused by a failure of attentional processes resulting in an inability to focus or engage attention" (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.304-305) directly motivates Hypothesis 1 and the whole gaze/EEG pipeline, operationalizing disengagement as gaze deviation from a defined AOI plus co-occurring EEG amplitude instability.
- **flow / engagement (contrast pole)** — engagement is the master term for what emotion-aware systems should preserve: "engagement... is a prerequisite for reaching the flow state," citing Csikszentmihalyi's *Flow* (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.302-303, citing [4]); Chanel et al.'s game-adaptation system is cited for its boredom/anxiety/engagement 3-class scheme (FE, p.302-303).
- **monotony / meaninglessness** — monotony is built directly into the induction method: the video stimulus (a small circle slowly tracing a larger circle's outline for 90 seconds) is designed to be generically boring "without relying on any specific genre of content" (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.307), and the Discussion credits "the monotonous video stimulus" with eliciting boredom in all participants (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.312). Meaninglessness in the cognitive-appraisal sense is not separately theorized.
- **temporality of boredom (time-drag)** — cited only, as one of Fahlman et al.'s five manifestations of boredom ("experiencing a slow passage of time," FE, p.304), not independently developed or measured.
- **profound/deep boredom / being-bored-by / being-bored-with (FCM forms)** — not treated; a purely empirical biomedical-signal-processing/HCI paper, Heidegger never cited, no phenomenological vocabulary present.
- **boredom-as-regulatory-signal / mind-wandering / task-unrelated thought** — not substantively treated. The paper frames boredom instrumentally, as a state to detect and mitigate ("boredom may have a negative effect on motivation," FE, p.303), rather than theorizing its adaptive function; "mind-wandering" is never named, the operative vocabulary being "losing attention/focus."

## 4. Measures & methods

The experiment (Section 3) runs on a custom, centrally synchronized physiological data acquisition system with a protocol editor and dual displays (participant-facing and monitor-facing), scheduling stimuli/instructions/questionnaires in sync with sensor streams (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.307). Of the system's nine supported sensor/data-type pairings (Table 4: web camera/facial expression, microphone/voice, Eye Tribe/gaze+pupil size, Microsoft Band/heart rate+skin temperature, Muse/EEG, Myo armband ×2/EMG, combined Eye Tribe+Muse/head movement, Microsoft Band/hand movement, Grove GSR/galvanic skin response), only two were used here: the **Muse headband** (4 scalp sites — FP1, FP2, TP9, TP10 — at 220 Hz, 0.0-1682.815 µV range) and the **Eye Tribe eye tracker** (30 fps binocular gaze position with timestamps) (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.307-308).

The protocol (Fig.2): self-paced instructions/consent → 30-second neutral baseline image (cloud image approximating IAPS #5870, Lang et al. [47]) → 90-second video stimulus (the moving-circle animation) → post-stimulus 1-9 boredom questionnaire ("not boring at all" to "extremely boring"); minimum total protocol duration 120 seconds (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.307).

Seventeen participants were recruited (9 male, 8 female; ages 22-33, mean 27.4; 11 Korean students/staff and 6 international students; all right-handed, healthy, none wearing glasses, since the Eye Tribe tracks glasses-wearers poorly — contextualized against South Korean myopia/glasses-wearing prevalence [48,49]), benchmarked against comparable multimodal studies (Zheng et al. [46], N=5; Soleymani et al. [5]/Kapoor et al. [10], N=24 each) (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.308). Participants attended individually, with one researcher assisting/interviewing and a second monitoring acquisition; the true purpose was withheld to avoid demand effects. Four participants' data were excluded for sensor-connection instability, leaving **N=13** (7 male, 6 female) for analysis (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.308-309).

Two analysis tracks were then cross-correlated. **Gaze**: heat maps in Ogama (Open Gaze And Mouse Analyzer) v5's fixations module (1920×1080 px screen; 30 Hz; max 20-px fixation-point deviation; min 2 samples/fixation; 31-px detection-ring buffer; consecutive fixations merged; diameter ratio 2 — Table 5), visually inspected against the circle's trajectory for "lost focus" regions, plus a programmatic hitbox technique (1-s windows, adapted from Eisenbarth & Alpers [13]) counting fixations within a 0.5 cm buffer (the tracker's default error range) around the moving-circle AOI (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.308-309). **EEG**: raw amplitude visually inspected in MATLAB (`plot()`), then summarized via a modified Signal Magnitude Area (SMA — sum of absolute differences between adjacent amplitude values per 1-second, 220 Hz window) and an individualized threshold, EEGThreshold(x) = AVG(SMAs(x)) + STD(SMAs(x)), computed per participant to classify each window "stable"/"non-stable" (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.309-310). **Cross-modal integration**: Algorithm 1 takes the EEG non-stable start/end window pairs plus the gaze fixation data, counts hitbox fixations per window within each non-stable region, averages per region, and returns the lowest average as the final fixation-count threshold — windows meeting this joint criterion are labeled "Potential Boredom Regions" (PBRs) (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.309-311).

## 5. Findings/Claims

- Visual heat-map analysis (via Ogama) found that 11 of the 13 analyzed participants showed at least one region of lost gaze focus relative to the moving-circle AOI during video playback, an initial confirmation of Hypothesis 1 (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.309-310).
- The EEG SMA-plus-individualized-threshold method classified "stable" vs. "non-stable" amplitude epochs per participant; cross-referencing non-stable epochs against gaze fixation counts via Algorithm 1 identified temporally coincident Potential Boredom Regions (PBRs) — windows where non-stable EEG amplitude coincides with reduced gaze fixation — in **all 13** analyzed participants, confirming Hypotheses 2 and 3 (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.310-311).
- Every participant's average fixation count within their own PBRs fell below 17 (range roughly 1-16.33), fixing 17 as a cross-participant gaze-based boredom-classification threshold for this video stimulus (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.310-311).
- The post-stimulus questionnaire independently confirmed boredom was elicited: mean rating 6.38/9 across 13 participants, with the authors characterizing 11 of 13 (84.62%) as having "answered over 6" and only two answering 3 or below (score frequencies: 2→1, 3→1, 6→3, 7→4, 8→4) (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.311).
- No statistically significant correlation was found between questionnaire scores and any extracted physiological feature (SMA values; mean/SD of fixation counts in PBRs and windows) (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.311).
- Three participants (7, 8, 10) had overall-session average fixation counts already below the derived threshold of 17 — a limitation of the fixation-*count* algorithm, which does not weight fixation *duration* and under-counts participants who stared fixedly at a few off-target locations; despite this, all three still showed EEG-gaze PBR correlations, and their mean questionnaire rating (7.7) was the sample's highest subgroup value (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.312).
- The fixation-threshold algorithm is presented as generalizable to other single, well-defined-AOI stimuli, but the authors caution it does not straightforwardly extend to dynamic, multi-region stimuli where attention may legitimately shift among several valid targets (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.312).
- Four named limitations: sample size (17, reduced to 13); state-only design (trait boredom not measured/controlled); post-hoc-only questionnaire administration (no continuous time-stamped boredom indicator, precluding fine-grained PBR verification); and consumer-grade rather than research-grade sensor hardware (Eye Tribe, Muse) (FAITHFUL-Kim et al., p.312).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* This source is the strongest available candidate in this cluster for a `grounds-measure` bridge to *both* channels of the not-yet-built Boredom Experiment (VR Attention Study) dataset entry, being one of very few corpus sources instrumenting EEG and eye-gaze simultaneously in one boredom-induction paradigm. Its EEG apparatus (4-electrode headband, FP1/FP2/TP9/TP10, 220 Hz, SMA-plus-threshold classification) and gaze apparatus (screen-based binocular tracker, AOI/hitbox fixation-counting, Ogama heat-mapping) each offer a plausible methodological template for the corresponding dataset channel — unconfirmed until that entry specifies its own instrumentation, especially since a VR headset's built-in tracking would differ from this study's screen-based, external-sensor setup. For the same reason, it is well positioned for cross-reference by Strand E units, most plausibly bor-sec-38 (Scharinger et al., pupil dilation and EEG alpha power) — a comparable dual-measure design, though addressing a different task (reading/executive load vs. passive video-induced boredom) and not mutually cited with this source *(anticipatory-application)*.

*(anticipatory-application)* The paper's null result — no significant correlation between the 1-9 self-report boredom score and any extracted EEG/gaze feature, despite both physiological channels independently indicating boredom in nearly all participants — is a direct methodological caution for Part III's own self-report boredom/engagement measures: convergence between subjective and physiological indices of boredom should not be assumed even when both separately register the same state *(anticipatory-application)*.

*(anticipatory-application)* Kim et al.'s operationalization of monotony directly *as* the induction stimulus (a slow, generic, content-neutral circular motion, deliberately built to avoid confounding boredom with any particular genre) offers Part III a precedent for isolating monotony as an independent variable, distinct from the meaninglessness/low-value appraisals dominant in this cluster's phenomenological and psychological literature (Strands A-C) — Part III might ask whether its own "boring" VLE segments conflate monotony (repetitive form) with meaninglessness (perceived value) *(anticipatory-application)*.
