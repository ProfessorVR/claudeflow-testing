# Withy (2019) — *Finding Oneself, Called*

**Chapter 7 of 12 in Hadjioannou (ed.), *Heidegger on Affect*** (Palgrave Macmillan, "Philosophers in Depth" series). **Type:** book chapter, individually authored — Christos Hadjioannou is the volume's editor only; this chapter's content belongs to Katherine Withy. **Discipline:** philosophy — Heideggerian phenomenology of mind, close exegesis of *Befindlichkeit*. **Depth:** deep (~23pp, book pp.153–176). **Corpus-role:** Strand-G methodological unit; the cluster's most sustained treatment of "finding oneself" (*Befindlichkeit*, *sich befinden*) as a disclosive structure broader than mood, with one narrow but load-bearing bridge to Heidegger's boredom analysis in FCM.

## Thesis / Aim

Withy asks what Heidegger's *Befindlichkeit* — which she renders as "finding" (*Befinden*), rejecting "affectivity," Macquarrie and Robinson's "state-of-mind," and the common "attunement" — actually names, and argues that scholarship's habit of approaching it almost exclusively through the lens of mood (*Stimmung*) is a mistake. Heidegger himself states that mood is the ontic expression of finding (SZ 134), but he also names sensing as another ontic mode (SZ 137), and Withy takes this seriously: if finding can express itself ontically in more than one way, then a theory built solely from mood's features risks either missing finding's full scope or importing mood-specific features illegitimately into finding as such. Her positive thesis is that finding is a dimension of disclosing — equiprimordial with understanding (*Verstehen*) and discoursing (*Reden*) — that operates on three levels at once: it discloses the self, discloses the world as a space of possibility, and discovers entities. Her governing formula, argued across the chapter's five sections, is that to be finding is "to find ourselves called by both vocations and… solicitings" (p.153), and that these two kinds of call are related to one another through understanding's own disclosing activity.

## Structure

1. **Existentiell Self-Disclosing: Finding Myself Called to Be Me** (§1, pp.154–157) — identities as vocations (*Ruf*, from L. *vocāre*) that we are called to rather than choose; the call to a vocation as delivered by conscience; facticity (*Fakten* vs. *Tatsachen*, SZ 56) as what we find ourselves already stuck with.
2. **Existential Self-Disclosing: Finding Oneself Called to Be Dasein** (§2, pp.157–162) — Heidegger's official claim that conscience's finding-dimension is angst (SZ 277); Dasein's uncanniness as its inability ever to get behind the ground of its own "that it is"; angst as *Grundbefindlichkeit*; the extension, in a single paragraph (p.160), to profound boredom as an equivalent ground-finding.
3. **Discovering: Finding Myself Called by Solicitings** (§3, pp.161–162) — solicitations as a special, "charged" class of Gibsonian affordances; finding as being open to entities' summons.
4. **World-Disclosing: Finding the World as a Possibility Space** (§4, pp.162–168) — the chapter's technical core: understanding opens a space of possible affordings, and finding brings these back to Dasein's having-been-thrown so that they become possibilities of soliciting; Table 1 (p.168) summarizes the resulting division of labor between understanding and finding.
5. **Modes of Finding** (§5, pp.168–174) — Tables 2–4 formalize vocational and solicitous finding; an extended case study of fear; the folk-psychological mood/emotion distinction is set aside as irrelevant; the body's inessential role in finding-as-such is argued; further modes (normative responsiveness, practical usefulness, being-with as loving/dominating, sensing) are derived from the method itself. The chapter closes (p.174) by returning to the opening idiom, "*Wie befinden Sie sich?*"

## Constructs

Checked honestly against the cluster's boredom-specific construct axis, this chapter's engagement is narrow — as expected of a piece whose target text is *Being and Time*, not *The Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics* — but one hit is genuine and analytically consequential, and two further items admit only an analogical, non-boredom-specific reading.

- **Profound/deep boredom (FCM 3rd form)** is engaged in a single, dense paragraph (p.160): Withy proposes that boredom, no less than angst, can serve as the *Grundbefindlichkeit* through which Dasein is called to the vocation of being Dasein as such, citing FCM 143 and FCM 165 directly and deferring the full argument to her own (2013) essay. In her own Table 2 (p.169) this identification is flagged with a hedging "(?)" — she does not treat it as fully secured.
- **Flow/engagement (contrast pole)** appears only analogically: her account of practical-usefulness as a mode of solicitous finding contrasts feeling "capable and confident" when things present themselves as "the right tool for the job" (p.172) against extreme anxiety's "overwhelming, general sense of impossibility and incapacity" (p.172) — a structural cousin of the flow/disengagement contrast, though Withy never uses the term "flow" and is not discussing boredom.
- **Monotony/meaninglessness** likewise appears only obliquely, in her brief remark that "unaffording" entities can be distracting, impeding, or irrelevant, leaving us "distracted, frustrated, or indifferent" (p.172) — again a structural analogue, not a boredom-specific claim.
- **Temporality of boredom (time-drag)** is essentially absent. Withy's one generic remark that finding is "associated with… the past" (p.154) characterizes *Befindlichkeit* as such, not boredom's phenomenology of dragging time, which she never discusses.
- **Being-bored-by (1st form), being-bored-with (2nd form), situational vs. trait/state boredom, boredom proneness, mind-wandering/task-unrelated thought, attentional disengagement/meta-awareness, and boredom-as-regulatory-signal** do not appear anywhere in this chapter. Withy's project is a BT-internal analysis of conscience, vocation, and solicitation; she has no occasion to engage FCM's tripartite typology beyond the single move described above, and no occasion at all to engage the empirical or psychological boredom literature.

## Reading of FCM

This chapter's relationship to Heidegger's boredom analysis is real but deliberately minimal, and it is important to state precisely what it does and does not do. Withy is reconstructing *Being and Time*'s account of conscience (Division II, §§54–60), and her governing question in §2 is what conscience's finding-dimension is. Heidegger's own answer is angst (SZ 277): the call of conscience speaks from Dasein's uncanniness, and "being uncanny reveals itself authentically in the basic finding… of angst" (SZ 276, quoted p.158). Having reconstructed why angst occupies this role — because its three structural moments (that in the face of which, that about which, and the anxious disclosing itself) all coincide as being-in-the-world (SZ 188) — Withy adds, almost as an aside at the end of §2 (p.160), that this functional role need not require dread or sweaty palms: "we could just as easily call it 'boredom'" — as Heidegger does in FCM. She then cites two specific FCM passages in support: FCM 143, where Heidegger names the telling announcement of profound boredom's being-held-in-limbo a "calling" (*Anrufen*) — material belonging to the third form's core analysis (fcm-06) — and FCM 165, where this calling is cashed out as "the most extreme demand," namely that Dasein as such is demanded of, and given to, the human being to be there — material belonging to FCM's separate "contemporary boredom" chapter (fcm-07). On this basis she concludes only that, structurally, boredom "is the call to the vocation of being Dasein" (p.160), just as angst is. She footnotes her own (2013) piece, "The Strategic Unity of Heidegger's *The Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics*," as the place where this identification is argued in full; here it is asserted and left standing, with the qualifying "(?)" in Table 2 (p.169) signaling that she herself is not claiming to have settled it. She does not discuss the first or second forms of boredom, the two structural moments (*Leergelassenheit*/*Hingehaltenheit*) in their own right, FCM's *Zeitvertreib* (passing-the-time) analysis, or the "contemporary Dasein" diagnosis (the "has man become boring to himself?" material) beyond the single extreme-demand quotation deployed for her own BT-internal purpose. In short: this chapter treats profound boredom as one instance — alongside angst — of a general phenomenon (ground-finding as the call to Dasein's own being) that is her actual object of study; it is not an interpretation of Heidegger's boredom analysis for its own sake, and readers seeking FCM exegesis proper should look to the cluster's dedicated FCM units or to bor-sec-04.

## Findings / Claims

1. **Finding is not coextensive with mood.** Mood is one ontic expression of the ontological structure *Befindlichkeit*; Heidegger names sensing as another (SZ 137, p.154), and Withy argues in principle there may be further modes — which she goes on to derive in §5. Treating finding as if it just were mood risks both underdescribing finding and smuggling mood-specific desiderata (bodily grounding, the folk-psychological emotion/mood split) into a theory of finding as such (p.154).
2. **Finding, like understanding, discloses on three levels at once** — the self, the world (as a possibility space), and entities (as mattering) — rather than each discourse-partner in Heidegger's account being assigned a single disclosive job (p.155, pp.161–162, Table 1 p.168).
3. **Vocational self-disclosing (§1).** Identities (entrepreneur, grandparent, coffee drinker) are vocations in the etymological sense (L. *vocāre*, to call) — they must "speak" to us, not merely be socially available — and taking one up presupposes finding oneself called to it (pp.155–156). What we resolve upon is given, not chosen, "experienced as a moment of being chosen" (p.156, SZ 275), and facticity names the class of identity-relevant Facts (*Fakten* — gender, race, standing, aptitude, body, history) we find ourselves already stuck with and must take up, as distinct from mere present-at-hand facts (*Tatsachen*) (p.157, SZ 56). Being self-finding, in the broadest sense, just is finding oneself called to a vocation (p.157).
4. **Existential self-disclosing (§2).** At the deeper, existential level, conscience's finding-dimension is angst, which discloses Dasein's uncanniness: not felt estrangement, but the plain fact that Dasein can never "come back behind" the ground of its own thrown being (p.158, SZ 284). Conscience calls Dasein to take over this obscure, burdensome Fact as its own — Heidegger's "guilt" (pp.158–159) — and this finding is a *Grundbefinden*, a ground-finding, because it is the foundational opening that makes there be Dasein at all (p.160).
5. **The boredom extension (p.160).** Withy proposes that this same structural office — ground-finding as the call to the vocation of being Dasein as such — can equally be occupied by profound boredom, citing FCM 143 ("the calling," *Anrufen*) and FCM 165 (Dasein's being "demanded of man… to be there," paraphrased). She marks this claim as asserted rather than fully argued here (full argument reserved for Withy 2013), and hedges it with a question mark in her own Table 2 (p.169).
6. **Discovering entities via solicitation (§3).** Solicitations are a "charged" subclass of Gibsonian affordances (Gibson 1986): a coffee cup affords drinking-from generally but *solicits* it when one is undercaffeinated (p.161). Crucially, solicitations are not discovered as pre-existing independently of finding — finding and solicited entities are "attuned together," not tuned into like a radio finding an independent frequency (p.162).
7. **World-disclosing as the technical center of the chapter (§4).** Understanding opens a network of possible ways entities can be coordinated with a projected identity (possibilities of *affording*); finding "brings these possibilities back" to Dasein's having-been-thrown, thereby implicating one's vocation and producing possibilities of *soliciting* (pp.163–166). This is why the detrimental (an affording) and the fearsome (a soliciting) come apart — one can fear the non-detrimental and fail to fear the genuinely detrimental (p.166, citing SZ 140, 341) — and why this difference is neither the fact/value nor the objective/subjective distinction (p.165–166).
8. **Fear as extended case study.** "Fearfulness" (world-disclosing) is a "slumbering possibility" of being solicited by the threatening (p.168, SZ 141), distinct from fear-as-discovering (fearing this or that). Withy sets aside the folk-psychological mood/emotion distinction (duration, intentional-object-lessness) as inapplicable to Heidegger's own account (pp.169–170) and glosses Aristotle's point that "what is frightening is not the same for everyone" (NE 1115b) as a claim about differential vocations and skills, not differential physical strength (p.170).
9. **The body is not essential to finding as such.** Embodiment plays no demonstrated role in the tripartite self/world/entity-disclosing structure itself, though it may be an essential ontic condition of particular modes of solicitous finding, such as sensing (pp.171–172). Contra critics (cited via Freeman 2016 and Ratcliffe 2013, p.154 n.3) who fault Heidegger's account of mood for neglecting the body, Withy argues this is "no neglect at all" so far as the account of finding proper is concerned (p.172).
10. **Further modes of solicitous finding, derived rather than merely catalogued.** Withy's method — deducing modes of finding from the existentiell and existential projects Dasein must or can take up — yields: normative responsiveness (being bound/compelled/repelled by requirements and prohibitions, pp.171–172); practical usefulness (feeling capable versus incapacitated, p.172); being-with others as loving or dominating, correlating with Heidegger's leaping-in-for/leaping-ahead-of (SZ 122, p.173); and sensing, illustrated through a worked example of taste and appetizingness (pp.173–174).
11. **Ground-moods versus existentiell-vocational modes.** Table 2 (p.169) distinguishes the modes of existential vocational finding — ground-moods (*Grundstimmungen*/*Grundbefinden*) "such as angst, boredom, etc.(?)" — from the modes of existentiell vocational finding (particular projects), which Withy explicitly leaves uninvestigated (p.169).
12. **Open questions, stated as such.** Withy closes by flagging three matters as unresolved: further modes of solicitous finding remain to be identified; the modes of existentiell-vocational finding have not been explored; and whether vocational and solicitous finding are two aspects of one phenomenon or genuinely distinct kinds of being-open is left an open question (pp.173–174).
13. **Closing return to the idiom.** The chapter ends where it began: "how we are faring" (*wie es einem geht*) just is how the carrying-out of a called vocation, amid entities that put that vocation at stake, is going (p.174) — a formula that ties together vocational and solicitous finding without formally resolving whether they are one phenomenon or two.

## Analytical role (anticipatory-application)

*(P/anticipatory-application) — the following are our interpretive links to Part III and to the cluster's mood-methodology, not claims Withy makes herself.*

First, and most directly responsive to this chapter's own emphasis: Withy's central methodological argument — that mood is only one ontic expression of a broader disclosive structure, and that sensing, normative responsiveness, practical-usefulness, and vocational call are structurally parallel modes — cautions any Part III or dataset design that treats "boredom" as exhausted by mood-report or affect-labeling instruments. If finding can register ontically in channels other than felt mood, then a VR-attention protocol relying solely on self-reported mood or a single physiological correlate risks missing modes of disengagement that manifest as, say, degraded practical-usefulness engagement (a participant no longer finding the task environment as "the right tool for the job," p.172) rather than as an explicitly moody affect. This reinforces, from an independent angle, the multi-instrument caution already flagged in bor-sec-04's own analytical-role section regarding the (not-yet-built) VR Attention Study corpus entry.

Second, Withy's account of world-disclosing — that finding brings possibilities of affording back to a participant's own thrown vocation to produce possibilities of soliciting (pp.163–166) — offers a conceptual resource for reframing why nominally identical VR or classroom stimuli might solicit engagement for one participant and merely afford (without soliciting) disengagement for another: the variance may lie less in a fixed trait (as boredom-proneness scales assume) and more in which vocational or identity-relevant projects a given task implicates for that participant. **`****** UNVERIFIED:`** whether this framing can be operationalized for the VR Attention Study corpus entry remains to be seen once that entry is built; no measure exists yet to test it against.

Third, and narrowly: the p.160 identification of boredom (alongside angst) as a *Grundbefindlichkeit* calling Dasein to the vocation of being Dasein corroborates, from BT's conscience-analysis rather than from FCM exegesis, the debate axis bor-sec-02 explicitly flagged but left open — "anxiety vs. profound boredom as candidate *Grundstimmung*." Withy takes a position bor-sec-02 only gestured toward, though she hedges it herself (Table 2's "(?)", p.169) and defers full argument to her own 2013 essay. Part III or any future FCM-secondary synthesis should read this chapter alongside bor-sec-02 and bor-sec-04 as three independent, non-identical routes to the same structural claim, rather than treating any one of them as dispositive on its own.

Finally, this chapter contains no operationalizable measure, self-report instrument, or empirical claim whatsoever; its contribution to Part III is exclusively conceptual and methodological, and any downstream use should be framed as philosophical clarification of what "being called" by a task-environment could mean, never as evidence bearing directly on a measured outcome.
