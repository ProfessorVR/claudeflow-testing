# bor-sec-55 — Slaby & Thonhauser, "Heidegger and the Affective (Un)Grounding of Politics" (2019)

Chapter 12 of 12 (final chapter) in Christos Hadjioannou (ed.), *Heidegger on Affect*, Palgrave "Philosophers in Depth" series, 2019, book pp.265–290. Hadjioannou edits the volume only; this chapter's argument and prose belong to its two authors, Jan Slaby and Gerhard Thonhauser, jointly.

**Type:** book chapter (edited-volume; political phenomenology) · **Discipline:** philosophy (Heideggerian phenomenology; postfoundational political theory) · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** Strand G closing unit — the cluster's third Slaby-authored/co-authored piece (with bor-sec-12, bor-sec-13), the only one written jointly, and the only one whose announced target is not boredom scholarship but a general theory of "the political" grounded in Heideggerian affectivity. Its Section 5, on Heidegger's Rektoratszeit, is reproduced — per the chapter's own footnote 5 — from Slaby's 2017 paper "Living in the Moment," i.e. bor-sec-12.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Slaby and Thonhauser argue that Heidegger's account of *Grundstimmungen* (basic attunements) — worked out across *Being and Time*'s analysis of angst and *The Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics* (FCM)'s analysis of boredom — discloses the radical ungroundedness and contingency of human existence, and that this disclosure is not merely psychological or existential but constitutes "the founding dimension of the political" (FE, p.265–266). They distinguish "the political" (an ontological register: the ungrounding grounds of politics) from "politics" as an ontic sub-system of society governed by institutions and rules (FAITHFUL-Slaby & Thonhauser, p.266). Their explicit aim is to trace the "post-foundationalist" line of twentieth-century political thought back to its Heideggerian origins in the theory of affectivity, in order to assess both the "potentials and pitfalls" of a Heideggerian politics (FE, p.266). The chapter's thesis has a sharp critical edge: the same affective structure that discloses contingency and thus opens onto freedom does not itself dictate any particular political response to that opening — a "radically democratic" reading (invoking Lefort) is available, but Heidegger's own historical response, culminating in his 1933/34 Rektoratszeit collaboration with the NSDAP, took the opposite, authoritarian path (FE, p.266–267). The authors' final diagnosis is that an "inevitable ambivalence" inhabits any ontology of political affectivity that discloses ungroundedness without itself determining how that ungroundedness ought to be lived (FE, p.286).

Six numbered/titled divisions structure the body (an initial, unlabeled "Introduction" plus five numbered sections):

1. **[Introduction, unlabeled]** (p.265[missing]–267) — states the political-ontological thesis; previews a chapter roadmap (Befindlichkeit → angst → boredom → Rektoratszeit interlude → postfoundational outlook); invokes Lefort's "radically democratic situation" and Arendt's dictum that freedom is the *raison d'être* of politics.
2. **"The Gist of Befindlichkeit"** (p.267–270) — reconstructs Heidegger's general theory of mood/attunement from both FCM §17 and *Being and Time* §29–30: findingness as disclosing facticity and thrownness, and as constitutively prone to falling (*Verfallen*).
3. **"Angst—Breakdown of Significance"** (p.270–274) — reconstructs BT §40's phenomenology of angst via its contrast with fear; develops a two-plane model of existence (ungrounded depth vs. stabilized surface) and reads this model onto ordinary politics vs. "the political" as such; closes with an Arendtian gloss on angst's disclosure of freedom.
4. **"Profound Boredom"** (p.274–281) — reconstructs the FCM lecture's tripartite typology of boredom, concentrating on the second and third forms; argues that, unlike angst, Heidegger explicitly frames boredom's *Grundstimmung* as an attunement of a *Volk* (people) rather than an individual dasein, and traces how this communal reading licenses Heidegger's demand that "we" awaken to a collective, activist self-determination.
5. **"The Collapse of Dasein and Work in the Rektoratszeit"** (p.281–283) — via Werner Hamacher's critique, shows how "work" (*Arbeit*) comes to occupy, in Heidegger's 1933 "Rede an Arbeitslose" and 1934 Logic Lecture, exactly the structural place reserved for the *Augenblick* in BT and FCM. Footnote 5 states explicitly that "the text of this section is reproduced with permission from Slaby (2017b)" — the References list confirms Slaby 2017b is "Living in the Moment: Boredom and the Meaning of Existence in Heidegger and Pessoa," i.e. this cluster's bor-sec-12, whose own third section, "Interlude: The collapse of dasein and work in the Rektoratszeit" (bor-sec-12, p.244–246), covers identical ground (Hamacher, the 1933 speech, the 1934 Logic Lecture citation, the postwar-German-continuity point) in what is, on the evidence of both units' own structural summaries, essentially the same text.
6. **"The Affective (Un)Grounding of Politics"** (p.283–286) — the chapter's synthesis, sharing its title with the chapter itself: recaps angst's and boredom's shared disclosure of ungroundedness; develops Lefort's "empty place of power" and "People-as-One" as the two poles of a democracy/totalitarianism antagonism; surveys republican (Arendt, Lefort) and agonistic (Rancière, Mouffe) postfoundational responses; concludes that this same antagonism is latent within Heidegger's own Befindlichkeit-analysis, since Heidegger's texts stage the most acute postfoundational insight available while Heidegger the political actor answered it autocratically.

Eight endnotes (p.286–288, funding acknowledgments, translation conventions, a further-reading list, and the Slaby-2017b attribution) and a References list (p.288–290) close the chapter.

## 3. Constructs

Checked honestly against the controlled boredom-construct vocabulary: this chapter's own boredom-specific content is concentrated in Section 4 (roughly seven of the chapter's twenty-five pages) and is markedly compressed relative to bor-sec-12/bor-sec-13's chapter-length treatments, because boredom here is instrumental to the chapter's political argument rather than its own topic.

- **profound/deep boredom (FCM 3rd form)** — treated at moderate depth (p.276–280): the "undifferentiated no one," total indifference of entities as a whole, the "telling refusal" as itself "a telling," and the call to the *Augenblick* (FAITHFUL-Slaby & Thonhauser, p.277–279). Distinctively, the authors foreground a dimension bor-sec-12/13 do not develop: Heidegger's insistence that this *Grundstimmung* is not detectable via individual introspection and that its proper "subject" is a *Volk*, not a single dasein (FAITHFUL-Slaby & Thonhauser, p.278–279, citing FCM 60/GA 29/30: 90f).
- **being-bored-with (2nd form)** — the dinner-party example is retold compactly (p.275–276): the self is "abandoned" in an "odd suspension," and a freely chosen span of time becomes a "standing now" (*stehendes Jetzt*), an existential futility (FAITHFUL-Slaby & Thonhauser, p.275–276).
- **being-bored-by (1st form)** — named only via Heidegger's shabby-train-station example, not developed (FE, p.275), mirroring bor-sec-12's identical bracketing of this form.
- **temporality of boredom (time-drag)** — a connective construct throughout Section 4: *Langeweile* as lived time literally "becoming long," glossed via Bergson's *durée*; the "standing now" of the second form and the fused, indefinitely stretched threefold horizon of the third (FAITHFUL-Slaby & Thonhauser, p.276–278).
- **monotony / meaninglessness** — central to both the angst section (the "collapse" of everyday significance, SZ 186) and the boredom section (entities become "indifferent as a whole," FCM 138/GA 208) (FAITHFUL-Slaby & Thonhauser, p.271, 277).
- **boredom-as-regulatory-signal (functional account)** — reconstructed faithfully via the "telling refusal is itself a telling" formula (FCM 140/GA 211f), but here the signal's addressee is explicitly politicized: what profound boredom's message "demands" is not (as in bor-sec-12/13's more individual-authenticity register) merely resolute self-disclosure, but the founding of a *Volk* as a political community (FAITHFUL-Slaby & Thonhauser, p.278–280).
- **flow / engagement (contrast pole)** — present only in the dinner party's surface/depth divergence: outward lively participation masking an underlying boredom (FAITHFUL-Slaby & Thonhauser, p.275–276).
- **attentional disengagement / meta-awareness** — present only diffusely, via the general claim that findingness's ontic concretions "for the most part" take the form of an "evasive turning away" (SZ 136), and that angst and boredom are especially prone to remaining latent behind distraction (FAITHFUL-Slaby & Thonhauser, p.269–270); this is Heidegger's general theory of falling rather than an empirical claim about attention, so any mapping onto the cognitive-science meta-awareness literature is this cluster's own bridge (P/anticipatory-application).
- **situational vs. trait/state boredom; boredom proneness; mind-wandering/task-unrelated thought** — not addressed in the psychological idiom. Notably, this chapter goes further than merely omitting these constructs: it *actively contests* the individual-state/trait framing as a misreading of Heidegger's own intention for *Grundstimmungen*, which Heidegger explicitly denies can be settled by any individual's introspective report (FAITHFUL-Slaby & Thonhauser, p.278–279, citing FCM 60/GA 90f) — a genuine textual basis for treating this axis as *contested* rather than merely silent (P/anticipatory-application for the framing as a "contest," since the psychological vocabulary itself is this cluster's addition).

Beyond the boredom axis, the chapter's own primary constructs are political-theoretical: "the political" as ontological ungrounding-ground of "politics" (THEME); *Grundstimmung* as a communal rather than individual phenomenon (THEME); the postfoundationalist antagonism of democracy (power as an "empty place," Lefort) versus totalitarianism ("People-as-One," Lefort) (THEORY-MODEL); and the republican (Arendt/Lefort) versus agonistic (Rancière/Mouffe) wings of postfoundational political thought (DEBATE-AXIS). These carry the chapter's actual argumentative weight and are documented fully in the Findings section below.

## 4. Reading of FCM

Section 4's citations track fcm-05-second-form, fcm-06-third-form, and fcm-07-particular-profound at high fidelity, in a sequence that closely parallels — and in several loci is textually identical to — bor-sec-12's own FCM citations, which is unsurprising given the two chapters' shared authorship and the explicit reproduction of Section 5 from the same author's earlier paper.

The second-form material (p.275–276) paraphrases the dinner-party scene and the "standing now" at FCM 119/GA 180 and FCM 125/GA 189 — the identical loci fcm-05 itself isolates (bridges-to-fcm, fcm-05-second-form). The third-form material (p.276–280) works through FCM 134f/GA 203 (the "undifferentiated no one" formula: "It is boring for one"), FCM 138/GA 208 (indifference of entities as a whole), FCM 140/GA 211f (the telling-refusal-as-telling formula), FCM 143/GA 214 (the self's "nakedness" before itself), and FCM 148f–149/GA 223–224 (the disclosure of "the freedom of Dasein as such" and the *Augenblick*) — the same core sequence fcm-06 documents as its own load-bearing passages (bridges-to-fcm, fcm-06-third-form). The chapter's treatment of fcm-04-first-form is, as in bor-sec-12, only a passing mention of the train-station example, with no German locus cited; the omission is registered here as deliberate rather than developed (bridges-to-fcm, fcm-04-first-form).

Where this chapter's reading of FCM diverges from bor-sec-12/13 is not in which passages it cites but in what it extracts from them politically. Both prior Slaby units read the third form's "telling refusal" as issuing a demand addressed to an individual dasein, which Heidegger (on Slaby's view) then over-hastily equates with authentic, resolute self-assertion. This chapter adds a textual observation neither predecessor develops: that Heidegger explicitly frames the *addressee* of this demand as a *Volk*, and that Grundstimmungen in FCM are said to be historically and culturally variable rather than universal structures of dasein as such — a marked departure from BT's treatment of angst as an invariant existential possibility (FAITHFUL-Slaby & Thonhauser, p.278–279, citing FCM 59–60, 69/GA 89–90f, 103). The chapter locates the same passage fcm-07-particular-profound isolates (FCM 165/GA 246–47 — the diagnosis that contemporary Dasein has forgotten how to "throw Dasein upon his shoulders" and has slipped out of the "danger-zone of Dasein") as the textual hinge where this communal, Volk-directed reading crystallizes into an explicit political demand (bridges-to-fcm, fcm-07-particular-profound; FAITHFUL-Slaby & Thonhauser, p.279–280) — matching exactly the locus bor-sec-12 flags as the point where the lecture "crashes down" into proto-Nazi rhetoric, but read here through the lens of collective rather than individual address.

## 5. Findings/Claims

- Angst (BT §40) is objectless and thus, unlike fear, potentially limitless in scope: what oppresses in angst is "nothing and nowhere" (SZ 186), a collapse of everyday significance that discloses a baseline ungroundedness of "all and everything" (FAITHFUL-Slaby & Thonhauser, p.271).
- Existence is modeled as two intertwined but unstable planes: an ungrounded, insecure depth, and a stabilizing "surface" of routine, familiar social practice — "the warp and weft of all our days" (Haugeland 2013, 54) — which is itself fragile and can be broken open by angst "at any time, for no particular reason" (FAITHFUL-Slaby & Thonhauser, p.271–272).
- This two-plane structure is read as directly analogous to the intertwining of ordinary politics (tedious institutional administration) and "the political" (rare moments when the contingency of the whole social order becomes apparent) (FE, p.272).
- Angst individualizes dasein, pulling it out of public entanglement (SZ 191), yet in the same act discloses dasein's own active role in constituting a meaningful world — which is, in Arendtian terms, the essence of the political: freedom as the task of plural self-determination under conditions of indeterminacy (FAITHFUL-Slaby & Thonhauser, p.273–274, quoting Arendt 1961, 146).
- Profound boredom parallels angst structurally but, distinctively, Heidegger frames its *Grundstimmung* as belonging to a *Volk*: "our" attunement, not "the" attunement of any individual (FAITHFUL-Slaby & Thonhauser, p.278–279, FCM 59/GA 89).
- The demand profound boredom is said to place on "us" — that Dasein "is given to him — to be there" (FCM 165/GA 246) — is, in the authors' reading, transformed by Heidegger into "a mobilizing appeal, a call to arms" addressed to a political community rather than a private self (FAITHFUL-Slaby & Thonhauser, p.280).
- From roughly §38 of the FCM lecture onward, the text's register shifts from existential ontology toward what the authors describe as material anticipating Heidegger's Rektoratszeit rhetoric (FE, p.280).
- In the 1933 "Rede an Arbeitslose" and a 1934 Logic Lecture (GA 38: 128), Heidegger installs *Arbeit* (work) at precisely the structural position — the place of the *Augenblick* — that BT and FCM reserve for resolute, authentic temporality; this section (Section 5) is, per footnote 5, reproduced from Slaby's own 2017b paper (FAITHFUL-Slaby, p.281–282, citing Hamacher 2002).
- The prizing of *Arbeit* did not end with denazification; Hamacher (2002) traces a "worrisome continuity" in postwar German culture's valuation of work, and Heidegger himself later reversed course, coming to see work (via his notes on Ernst Jünger's *Der Arbeiter*, GA 90) as the metaphysical epitome of modern subjectivity rather than existential salvation (FAITHFUL-Slaby & Thonhauser, p.282–283).
- The chapter's closing synthesis (Section 6) holds that Heidegger's ontological analyses of angst and boredom are among the most acute available expositions of "postfoundational political affectivity," yet Heidegger himself could not bear the very openness and indeterminacy his own analyses disclosed, responding instead with a demand for decisive authority that forecloses, rather than sustains, the space of democratic contestation (FAITHFUL-Slaby & Thonhauser, p.285–286).
- Following Lefort, democracy is defined by power occupying an "empty place" that no group can permanently claim, while totalitarianism reunifies society around a single "People-as-One" (Lefort 1986, 303–304); the authors locate this same antagonism — democracy vs. totalitarianism as two possible responses to an affectively disclosed ungroundedness — as latently operative within Heidegger's own itinerary, with his 1933/34 turn serving as "a case in point" of the totalitarian resolution (FAITHFUL-Slaby & Thonhauser, p.284–286).
- The chapter surveys, without adjudicating between, two postfoundational lineages: a republican/consensual one (Arendt, Lefort — freedom and contingency organized through plural self-determination) and an agonistic/conflictual one (Rancière's "part of those without part"; Mouffe's arena of "substantive conflict") (FE, p.285).
- The chapter's final sentence names the stakes directly: an "inevitable ambivalence" inhabits any ontology of political affectivity that discloses ungroundedness without thereby determining how that ungroundedness is to be answered (FAITHFUL-Slaby & Thonhauser, p.286).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* This chapter supplies Part III with a structurally distinct caution from bor-sec-12's: where bor-sec-12 warns against reading an individual's boredom-signal as automatically demanding corrective self-transformation, this chapter warns against reading a *collective* disengagement-signal (a cohort's, a classroom's, a community's shared low-arousal or "flatness") as automatically licensing a mobilizing, corrective intervention at the level of the group. The chapter's own cautionary example — Heidegger's slide from "boredom discloses that nothing matters" to "therefore this Volk must resolutely reassert itself" — is a philosophically serious warning against over-reading any disengagement signal, individual or collective, as self-interpreting evidence for a predetermined remedial program *(anticipatory-application)*. Because the VLE's proximity-voice co-watching and universal group-assignment already put boredom in this cluster's corpus in a partly collective register (per this cluster's corrected reading of VLE design), this chapter's Volk-level reframing of Grundstimmung is a genuinely apt, rather than merely imported, bridge for thinking about shared or classroom-level affective states in Part III, more so than either of Slaby's individual-focused 2010/2017 papers *(anticipatory-application)*.

On the Slaby self-citation: footnote 5 of this chapter states that its Section 5 ("The Collapse of Dasein and Work in the Rektoratszeit") is "reproduced with permission from Slaby (2017b)." The chapter's own References list identifies Slaby (2017b) as "Living in the Moment: Boredom and the Meaning of Existence in Heidegger and Pessoa" — the exact title of bor-sec-12 in this cluster.
