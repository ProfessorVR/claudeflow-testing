## 1. Thesis/Aim

Vallega-Neu's chapter pursues, across three explicit movements framed by an unheaded introduction and an unheaded closing reflection, the relation between truth, errancy, and the lived, "bodying" body. Her governing claim, announced in the introduction (pp.205–207): although Heidegger held, from his 1930 essay "On the Essence of Truth" onward, that there is no event of truth without errancy, he never posed the further question of how attunement (*Stimmung*/*Befindlichkeit*) relates to errancy, nor how bodily disposition mediates that relation. Her essay supplies both omissions. First, she argues that grounding attunements (*Grundstimmungen*) — the disclosive moods through which being, not merely beings, comes to presence — are inseparable from a "double concealment" of mystery and errancy, such that even the most authentic, truth-disclosive attunement remains shot through with an ineliminable susceptibility to being led astray. Second, she argues this susceptibility can be understood only once the lived body is given a constitutive, not incidental, role: attunement, on her reconstruction, just *is* the temporal texture of the body's spacing — a "dense pluriform connective tissue" (her own phrase, p.220) binding Dasein to things, others, and world prior to any subject/object distinction. Her most consequential and most unsettling conclusion, reached only in the essay's final unheaded pages (pp.223–224), is that this bodily/attunement entanglement dissolves the tidy Heideggerian distinction between grounding attunements that hold errancy "at bay" and non-grounding, thing-directed attunements vulnerable to distortion: all attunement, including Heidegger's own, remains ineliminably vulnerable to errancy. She closes by explicitly situating "Heidegger's errancies together with everybody else's" (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, p.224) — a self-critical gesture folding her philosophical argument back onto Heidegger's own political failure without treating that failure as either exceptional or fully explained by it.

## 2. Structure

1. **Introduction** (unheaded, pp.205–207) — situates *Stimmung*/*Befindlichkeit* as the guiding, if under-thematized, concern of Heidegger's later work; lists *Grundstimmungen* (Angst, deep boredom, restraint, presentiment, wonder) with an FCM citation (GA 29/30: 238); distinguishes grounding from non-grounding attunements; poses the unasked question of attunement's relation to errancy; introduces *Befindlichkeit* ("disposition") as attunement's bodily register; cites Stolorow (2014) approvingly; announces the essay's three-part structure.
2. **§1 "Truth, Errancy, and Attunements"** (pp.207–216) — a sustained reading of "On the Essence of Truth" (propositional truth/correspondence → openness → freedom as letting-beings-be → *Gestimmtheit* as prior to *Erlebnis*/*Gefühl*) into the double concealment of mystery and errancy; extends into *Contributions to Philosophy*'s complex grounding attunement (*Erschrecken*/*Verhaltenheit*/*Scheu*) and the machination/lived-experience diagnosis; traces errancy's further degeneration into distortion (*Verkehrung*) in *Besinnung*; closes with a critical, self-reflexive digression questioning this truth/distortion differentiation in light of Heidegger's Nazism and its reinstatement of a body/spirit hierarchy, pivoting into non-fundamental, thing-directed attunements (fear, curiosity, the "boring") as bodily dispositions.
3. **§2 "Attunement and the Body"** (pp.216–220) — turns to the *Zollikon Seminars*' treatment of *Leib* (lived body) versus *Körper* (objective body); *Befindlichkeit* as the attunedness determining Dasein's relation to world/others/self; the unity of thrownness-projection-language (saying/*Rede*); "being addressed" (*Angesprochensein*); *Leiben* ("bodying forth") as a mode of Da-sein, necessary but not sufficient for relation to things; the train-station example of bodying forth exceeding corporeal limits; Heidegger's own claim that the understanding of being outstrips bodying forth; Vallega-Neu's extension of attunement as the "temporal texture" of bodily spacing.
4. **§3 "Bodying Forth and Truth"** (pp.220–224) — draws on "The Origin of the Work of Art" (world/earth strife) and her own earlier monograph (Vallega-Neu 2005) to argue bodies are beings that "shelter" truth's double concealment via their own earthly, self-secluding quality; discusses the GA 70 §108 "Da-sein and Humans" schema as the one late passage bearing on the event/body relation; stages her central critical move, questioning Heidegger's own separation of attuning being from bodily being.
5. **Closing reflection** (unheaded, pp.223–224) — draws out the consequence for errancy/distortion: the boundary blurs; attunements are always multiple, entangled with other humans, animals, spaces, weather, and technology (cell phones, wireless devices); meditation/"clearing" can at best let attunements be, never control them; closes on the self-implicating note about Heidegger's own errancy.
6. **Notes** (41 endnotes, pp.224–226) and **References** (p.226).

## 5. Findings/Claims

**Introduction — attunement, historicity, and the unasked question (pp.205–207).**
- F1. *Stimmung* — which Heidegger treats interchangeably with *Befindlichkeit* in *Being and Time* — has guided his thinking and writing especially since the 1930s, even where it is not his explicit topic (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, p.205).
- F2. Grounding attunements (*Grundstimmungen*) — Angst, deep boredom, restraint, presentiment, wonder — unsettle Dasein from concern with particular beings into an experience of the occurrence of being as such; and, at least since FCM, such attunements are historical, determining how being discloses "in certain epochs of Western philosophy for a people" (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, pp.205–206, citing GA 29/30: 238).
- F3. Heidegger never posed the question of how attunements relate to errancy, despite maintaining, from "On the Essence of Truth" (1930) onward, that there is no event of truth without errancy (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, p.206).
- F4. Answering that question requires attention to the lived body (*Leib*), since it is through the body that Dasein is concretely situated in relation to both being and beings; she credits Stolorow (2014) with the observation that Heidegger's own privileging of attunement implicitly places the body at the core of his thought (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, pp.206–207, citing Stolorow 2014).
- F5. She introduces a terminological differentiation not native to Heidegger's own usage: *Befindlichkeit* will name attunement's bodily register ("disposition"), *Stimmung* its non-bodily/linguistic register ("attunement") — a split she will ultimately destabilize in §3 (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, p.207).

**§1 — Truth, errancy, and the double concealment (pp.207–216).**
- F6. Reading "On the Essence of Truth," she argues that even paradigmatically "rational" truth-assertions are grounded in an open comportment that is always already attuning and attuned — attunement precedes and conditions rational judgment, not the reverse (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, pp.208–209, citing GA 9: 185–186/PM 142).
- F7. Freedom, for Heidegger, is "letting beings be" — not an activity of a subject but a way Da-sein ("there-being") occurs, granting the relatedness of beings as a whole to humans rather than being exercised by humans over beings (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, pp.208–209, citing GA 9: 188, 190/PM 144–145).
- F8. In §5 of the essay, *Gestimmtheit* (being-attuned) is explicitly differentiated from *Erlebnis* (lived experience) and *Gefühl* (feeling): all historical human comportment toward beings as a whole is attuned, whether or not this is explicitly felt or grasped (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, pp.209–210, citing GA 9: 192/PM 147).
- F9. Truth's disclosure is constituted by a double concealment: the "mystery" (an originary concealment "older than any disclosedness of this or that being," FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, p.210) and "errancy" (*Irre*) — non-truth is not truth's opposite but its condition (p.210, citing GA 9: 193f/PM 148).
- F10. In *Contributions to Philosophy*, Heidegger thinks a complex grounding attunement proper to the current epoch of being's withdrawal (machination): shock (*Erschrecken*), unsettling thinking from customary familiarity; restraint (*Verhaltenheit*), a hesitant self-withholding; and diffidence (*Scheu*), a drawing-near to what remains most remote (the divine) (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, p.211, citing GA 65: 15–16/CP 14–15).
- F11. Errancy names the way historical human beings are driven from the mystery toward what is readily available, from one thing to the next; it belongs to the "inner constitution" of Da-sein and cannot be removed, though experiencing it as errancy opens the possibility of not being led further astray (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, pp.212–213, citing GA 9: 196/PM 149–150).
- F12. She illustrates errancy phenomenologically: the way anxiety, arising from nowhere, drives us to localize its cause somewhere in the body (a suspected illness) is itself a form of errancy — a back-and-forth between abyssal being and a determinate, locatable threat (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, p.213).
- F13. The forgetting of the mystery, entrenched across the history of Western being since the Greeks, produces what Heidegger calls "the plight of the lack of plight" (*die Not der Notlosigkeit*): people feel close to life and no longer feel the need to question being; "lived experience" (*Erlebnis*) — in which only what can be incorporated into subjective life counts as "being" — is, for Heidegger, more dangerous than machination, since it forecloses the need for questioning altogether (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, pp.213–214, citing CP §63).
- F14. In *Besinnung* (1938), Heidegger further differentiates errancy from distortion (*Verkehrung*, "being turned in the wrong direction"): errancy belongs to truth's own clearing, even carrying a certain "dignity," while distortion "computes" causes for everything (drives, inclinations, pleasures) and reduces all things to the merely present-at-hand; awareness of the danger of distortion allows one to ward it off (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, p.214, citing GA 66: 11–12).
- F15. Vallega-Neu raises a pointed critical objection to this differentiation, on two fronts. Biographically/politically: Heidegger's claim to ward off distortion through steadfastness in truth sits uneasily beside his actual involvement in National Socialism — including a 1933-era address to beneficiaries of the National Socialist "labor service" program in which Heidegger equates "knowing" with "being master of the situation into which we are placed" (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, p.215, quoting Wolin 1993, 58). Philosophically: the truth/distortion differentiation reinstates a metaphysical hierarchy of spirit over body — distortion glossed via bodily drives and appetites, truth via the "announced god" — a hierarchy she judges "quite problematic" and continuous with a tradition denigrating the body (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, p.215). She presses the resulting, genuinely open question — how one distinguishes steadfastness in truth from blind fundamentalism — as one of the essay's unresolved difficulties (p.215).
- F16. She reframes Heidegger's own drives/pleasures/inclinations vocabulary (the register of "distortion") as itself a kind of attunement — non-fundamental, thing-directed attunement, exemplified by something appearing as "frightening" or "boring" — arguing these should be understood as bodily dispositions rather than segregated from the attunement-vocabulary altogether (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, pp.215–216).

**§2 — Attunement and the body (pp.216–220).**
- F17. In the *Zollikon Seminars*, Heidegger approaches the body (*Leib*, against *Körper*, the objective body) through spatiality rather than through attunement — a juxtaposition Vallega-Neu finds puzzling given his own emphasis on attunement elsewhere (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, p.217).
- F18. *Befindlichkeit* is "the attunedness that attunes and determines Dasein" in its relation to world, other Dasein, and itself; it is founded in thrownness and belongs together with understanding (*Verstehen*), itself reconceived as the opening of possibilities of being rather than an explicit cognitive act (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, p.217, citing Heidegger 1987, 182).
- F19. The unity of thrownness and projection is determined, per the *Zollikon Seminars*, by language understood as "saying" (*Sagen*) — Heidegger's late recasting of *Being and Time*'s *Rede* (discourse); on this basis humans can be "addressed" (*Angesprochensein*) by beings, a notion Heidegger applies directly to attunement in conversation with Medard Boss: "Being attuned and being related are one" (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, pp.217–218, quoting Heidegger 1987, 251; cf. 263).
- F20. The body, thought out of Da-sein, occurs as *Leiben* ("bodying forth"), a mode of being rather than an object; the body is "the necessary but not sufficient condition" for relation to things, and bodying forth extends beyond the corporeal limit of the skin to "the horizon of being in which I dwell" — illustrated by Heidegger's claim that one could not go pick up a friend at a distant train station without one's bodily being already there with the station (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, p.218, citing Heidegger 1987, 110, 113, 232).
- F21. Yet Heidegger insists the understanding of being reaches further than bodying forth: "no bodying forth occurs" in the mere horizon of the understanding of being as such (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, p.219, quoting Heidegger 1987, 244). Bodily orientation (front/behind, above/below, right/left) is, on this account, itself derivative of being "directed" by what addresses us out of the openness of world — not the reverse (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, p.219, citing Heidegger 1987, 293f).
- F22. Vallega-Neu's own extension: if the body articulates the temporal spacing of our relation to what addresses us, then attunements name "the temporal texture" of that bodily spacing — "a dense pluriform connective tissue" through which we are bodily connected to beings, drawn out toward, withdrawing from, or suspended in relation to what appears fearful, desirable, or indifferent; our "attuned bodying forth" thus precedes conscious perception (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, p.220).

**§3 — Bodying forth and truth (pp.220–224).**
- F23. Drawing on "The Origin of the Work of Art" and her own earlier monograph (Vallega-Neu 2005), she argues bodies are beings that, in their own "earthly," self-secluding quality, delimit and configure "concrete sites" of Da-sein — of the there-being of truth — much as a work of art holds open the strife between world and earth (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, pp.220–221, citing GA 5: 27–36/BW 167–174).
- F24. Our "attuned, ecstatically spatializing and temporalizing" bodies are constitutive of truth's own appearing and non-appearing — and, by the same token, they shelter truth's double concealment (mystery and errancy) via our bodily relation to death and to our tendency toward beings (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, pp.221–222).
- F25. She cites, as the one late passage where Heidegger gestures toward the event's relation to human bodiliness, a schematic outline from *Über den Anfang* (GA 70, §108, "Da-sein and Humans," 1941): a sequence running Being → Appropriation (of Da-sein) → Voice (*Stimme*) → Attunement (*Stimmung*) → Demand of courage (*Zumutung*) → Gathered mood (*Gemüt*) → Indwelling (*Inständigkeit*) → Disposition (*Befindlichkeit*, glossed there as "bodily being," explicitly distinguished from its *Being and Time* sense) (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, p.222, citing GA 70: 131).
- F26. Her central critical move: she "begins calling into question" precisely the distinction this GA 70 schema draws — between attuning being (*Stimmung*, arising prior to and beyond the human) and bodily being (*Befindlichkeit*) — proposing instead to rethink attunement as the very texture of "human" bodying itself, one that both discloses and recedes with respect to what appears (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, pp.222–223).
- F27. This move destabilizes the boundary of "the body" itself: she puts "our" and "human" in scare quotes throughout this closing stretch, arguing the boundary between what attunes/addresses us and our own bodying cannot be clearly marked — an "anonymous quality" of embodiment she credits to Merleau-Ponty and, in more Heideggerian terms, attributes to the earthly, self-secluding character of bodies; she further credits Nietzsche with having identified this as unconscious drives and affects constitutive of what we come to call an "I" (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, p.223, citing Merleau-Ponty 2012).

**Closing, unheaded reflection (pp.223–224) — the consequence for errancy and vigilance.**
- F28. The blurred boundary between bodying and attunement has direct consequences for the errancy/distortion distinction of §1: since attunements determining our relational being are "always multiple and highly complex" — woven from other humans, animals, configured spaces (buildings, cities), air, weather, light, machines, and the invisible traffic of cell-phone and wireless communication constantly permeating our bodily spaces — the clean separation between truth-revealing attunements that hold errancy "at bay" and attunements that relate us to (and thus risk distorting us via) other beings cannot be sustained (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, p.223).
- F29. Concentration or meditation can create a certain "spacing" with respect to attunements — related to what Heidegger calls "clearing" — but even at its most rigorous, this spacing only lets attunements be; it cannot control or choose which attunements become revelatory, and even in moments of clearing we remain blind to most of the attunements disposing our actions and thoughts (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, p.224).
- F30. The essay's closing sentence generalizes this ineliminable blindness to Heidegger himself: it is here, she writes, that she would situate "Heidegger's errancies together with everybody else's" (FAITHFUL-Vallega-Neu, p.224) — refusing either to exempt Heidegger's political failure from the analysis or to treat it as its sole or sufficient explanation.

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* This chapter's primary dissertation relevance runs to the broader affect-theory apparatus behind Parts II and III rather than to Part III's boredom construct specifically, which the chapter barely touches.

First, and most directly usable for Part III: Vallega-Neu's grounding/non-grounding attunement distinction offers an independent, non-FCM-specific philosophical vocabulary for insisting that profound/deep boredom and situational/trait boredom differ in *kind*, not merely degree — a second, philosophically distinct route to the same caution E&F's chapter (bor-sec-04) draws from within Heidegger scholarship proper, this time grounded in the truth-disclosive, historically-epochal status of *Grundstimmungen* generally rather than in FCM's boredom analysis specifically (P/anticipatory-application). Because her route to this caution does not depend on FCM's tripartite structure at all, it can be cited as converging, independent support without duplicating E&F's argument.

Second, her sustained argument that the lived body ("bodying forth") is constitutive of, rather than incidental to, attunement offers Part III's embodiment-adjacent material (EEG, gaze, pupillometry, and any VR-immersion physiological channel) a philosophical vocabulary for treating bodily/physiological signal not as a mere correlate of an inner mental state ("boredom") but as itself partly constitutive of the attunement measured — a caution against a crude signal-to-mental-state mapping in the not-yet-built VR Attention Study corpus entry (P/anticipatory-application). No `grounds-measure` bridge is warranted, however: this is a philosophical essay with no instrumentation of any kind, and manufacturing a dataset-channel pointer here would fabricate a connection the source gives no material for.

Third, and more speculatively: her closing observation that attunement is woven together with configured spaces, machines, and "the invisible traffic of cell-phone and wireless communication" (p.223) anticipates, in a general philosophical register, the technologically-mediated embodiment questions Part II's VR/G&G material and Part III's VR-deployment context both raise — though she writes about ambient communication technology, not head-mounted VR, so this should be flagged as a loose, generalizable resonance rather than a source-specific claim about virtual environments (P/anticipatory-application).

Fourth, her critique of Heidegger's own truth/distortion differentiation — and her insistence that even the most disclosive, "authentic" attunements remain ineliminably vulnerable to errancy — offers the broader affect-theory framework (Part II especially) a caution against treating any single mood-state, including flow, immersion, or absorbed engagement, as an unmixed or error-free register of authentic disclosure; any argument leaning on "authenticity" as a clean, achieved state should register her point that vigilance can, at best, let attunements be and never secure itself against them (P/anticipatory-application).

Finally, and negatively: because this chapter does not engage FCM's boredom analysis and cites Heidegger's own boredom material only once, in passing, it should **not** be cited as a source for claims about profound boredom specifically; its citation value lies in the truth/errancy/embodiment argument. Where Part III needs a philosophical anchor for embodiment-and-attunement more broadly, this chapter, not the E&F chapter, is the more directly relevant in-cluster source.
