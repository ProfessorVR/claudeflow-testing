# bor-sec-50 — McManus, "Affect and Authenticity: Three Heideggerian Models of Owned Emotion" (2019) — Chapter 6 of 12 in Hadjioannou (ed.), *Heidegger on Affect*

**Type:** chapter in a 12-chapter, 13-author edited collection (Palgrave, "Philosophers in Depth" series), book pp.127–152 · **Discipline:** philosophy (Heideggerian phenomenology in dialogue with analytic philosophy of action, ethics, and moral psychology) · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** Strand G. This chapter is authored by **Denis McManus** (University of Southampton); Christos Hadjioannou is the volume's *editor* only (he separately authored chapter 4, bor-sec-48) and is never the source of this chapter's claims. McManus's chapter never mentions boredom (*Langeweile*) anywhere in its twenty-five pages, and its citations of Heidegger's *Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics* (FCM 64, 66, 67–68, 283) are drawn exclusively from FCM's general, prefatory discussion of *Stimmung*/attunement-as-such — material that precedes and is presupposed by, but is textually distinct from, Heidegger's specific three-form typology of boredom that this cluster's fcm-04 through fcm-07 bridge units track. Accordingly this unit claims **no fcm-0N bridge**. Its substantive contribution lies squarely in this dissertation's "affect" concept independent of boredom: a sustained analytic reconstruction of what "owned" or "authentic" *emotion* (as opposed to owned *understanding*, which dominates the secondary literature) could mean for Heidegger, staged as a contest between three rival models.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

McManus sets out to determine what authenticity (*Eigentlichkeit*) in one's *affective* life might be, a question he judges under-addressed because, as he notes via Kate Withy, "[m]ost interpretations of [Heidegger's] account of authenticity … follow Heidegger in focusing on owned understanding" (FAITHFUL-McManus, p.128, quoting Withy 2015, 21) rather than owned emotion. The chapter's own thesis is comparative and adjudicative rather than a single positive claim: it stages three rival "models" of what owning one's affective life could mean — the **decisionist model** (authenticity as a radical, groundless choice of one's values), the **standpoint model** (authenticity as the consistent owning of a vocation, project, norm-set, or narrative), and McManus's own preferred **All-Things-Considered Judgment Model (ATCJM)** (authenticity as a non-evasive responsiveness to the full plurality of demands one's concrete situation makes) — and argues that only the third survives the objections the first two face (FE, pp.127–128). McManus is explicit that Eigentlichkeit itself resists straightforward translation: "authenticity" captures the ordinary-German sense of "real"/"genuine," while "own-ness"/"ownedness" better captures the literal *eigen* ("own," contrasted with *fremd*, "alien"); he uses the standard translation throughout while asking the reader to hear both resonances (FAITHFUL-McManus, pp.127–128).

## 2. Structure

The chapter (book pp.127–152) comprises an unheaded introduction, eight numbered sections, forty-three endnotes, and a reference list.

1. **Introduction** (pp.127–128) — the elusiveness of affective authenticity; the *Eigentlichkeit*/"authenticity"-vs-"ownedness" translation dispute; the three models previewed.
2. **§1 Heidegger on Affect — I: *Befindlichkeit* and *Stimmung*** (pp.128–131) — Withy's observation that owned-emotion is under-discussed; *Befindlichkeit* (disposedness/findingness) and *Stimmung* (mood/attunement/"tuning"); the 1924 *Basic Concepts of Aristotelian Philosophy* (BCAP) gardener example; the "Promethean" worry (via David Cooper) that authenticity's "individualizing" seems to require standing apart from Dasein's inescapable *Gestimmtsein*.
3. **§2 The Decisionist Model** (pp.131–133) — Michael Friedman's reading of authenticity as a "thoroughgoing" choice resting on "no taken for granted background framework at all"; applied to affect, this yields: we own our emotions only by freely choosing the values/concerns that determine how we are moved; textual support from SZ 169–170 ("das Man prescribes one's *Befindlichkeit*").
4. **§3 Heidegger on Affect — II: Disclosive Submission to a World That Matters** (pp.131–134) — Heidegger's own positive account of affect as "a primordial kind of Being for Dasein" disclosing prior to cognition and volition; Heidegger located (via Susan Bordo) within a "recessive" anti-denigration tradition alongside Aristotle, Hume, and Nietzsche; affect as precondition of intentionality itself.
5. **§4 Criticism of the Decisionist Model Reframed** (pp.134–135) — Iris Murdoch's and Ernst Tugendhat's objection that a choice with no grounding reasons is one over which "it was not I who chose"; McManus's original addition: read through Heidegger's account of mood, decisionist "liberation" is not a Promethean freedom but a condition of affective *stasis* — the will "shuts down."
6. **§5 The Standpoint Model** (pp.135–139) — authenticity as owning a vocation/project (Varga), a set of norms/commitments (Crowell, Kukla, Haugeland), or a life-narrative (Guignon); applied to affect via Varga's righteous-indignation example and Anthony Rudd's "serious ethical agent" who cannot "turn off" generosity and compassion; McManus's student-essay-mark counterexample against unconditional affective constancy.
7. **§6 Normative and Emotional Pluralism** (pp.138–140) — real human lives are subject to many competing norms/roles at once (son, father, colleague, teammate, etc.); the standpoint model's "subtler … Prometheanism"; Varga's "openness to change" clause shown not to resolve the pluralist intuition.
8. **§7 The All-Things-Considered Judgment Model** (pp.140–141) — authenticity as a responsiveness to *all* aspects of one's situation at once (an "all-things-considered judgment," ATCJ) rather than to a single role/goal/norm; the Moore's-paradox-style argument distinguishing partial from ATC judgments; grounding in Heidegger's readings of Aristotelian *phronesis* and of "guilt" (*Schuld*).
9. **§8 Owning Emotions and All Things Considered** (pp.141–145) — answers the objection (via Bill Blattner) that ATCJM over-intellectualizes Dasein; argues authenticity requires *emotional openness* to the full range of what matters, and that inauthenticity is an "evasive turning-away" (SZ 136) from that range, illustrated by a rugby-practice/friend's-worries example.
10. **Notes 1–43** (pp.145–150) — extended textual apparatus, including the chapter's own flagged lacuna regarding anxiety (n.35) and its engagement with Frankfurt (n.39) and Withy (n.42).
11. **References** (pp.150–152).

## 3. Constructs

This chapter is not about boredom in any register, and none of the cluster's eleven controlled boredom constructs is directly treated — the word "boredom" does not occur once in its twenty-five pages. The one place an honest analogical link is available — flagged explicitly as *our* extension, not McManus's claim (P/anticipatory-application) — concerns **monotony/meaninglessness**: McManus's critique of the decisionist model (§4) describes the condition a will would have to occupy to be "totally free" in Murdoch's sense — one "devoid of normative characteristics," stripped of any sense of "things mattering" (FAITHFUL-McManus, p.133–134) — as a condition of affective *stasis*, in which, rather than achieving Promethean liberation, Dasein simply "shuts down" (FE, p.134). Structurally, a world drained of all mattering-significance is exactly the state that the boredom literature (and Heidegger's own FCM analysis, which this chapter does not cite for this purpose) associates with profound boredom's world-emptying and with monotony/meaninglessness generally. McManus draws no such connection himself; the parallel is worth flagging for this cluster only because it shows the same phenomenological shape (significance draining away) doing radically different argumentative work in two different corners of the Heidegger-affect literature — here, as a *reductio* of a rival model of choice; in the boredom literature, as the very structure boredom itself exhibits.

All other ten constructs (profound/deep boredom, being-bored-by, being-bored-with, situational vs. trait/state boredom, boredom proneness, mind-wandering/task-unrelated thought, attentional disengagement/meta-awareness, boredom-as-regulatory-signal, flow/engagement, and temporality of boredom) receive no treatment whatsoever. What the chapter *does* treat, richly, are its own native constructs: *Eigentlichkeit*/authenticity as ownedness (pp.127–128); *Befindlichkeit* and *Stimmung*/*Gestimmtsein* (pp.128–131); the decisionist, standpoint, and All-Things-Considered Judgment models of authenticity (pp.130–145); normative and emotional pluralism (pp.138–140); Heideggerian "guilt" (*Schuld*) as the finite creature's inescapable foreclosure of unchosen possibilities (pp.141–143); and "evasion" (*Ausweichen*)/"evasive turning-away" as this chapter's own preferred marker of inauthentic affective life (pp.143–146).

## 4. Reading of FCM

This chapter does **not** engage Heidegger's boredom analysis in *Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics* (GA 29/30) at all. There is no mention anywhere in the main text or the forty-three endnotes of *Langeweile*, the three forms of boredom, the station scene, the dinner-party scene, *Zeitvertreib*, *Leergelassenheit*/*Hingehaltenheit*, the "telling refusal" (*Versagen*), or the *Augenblick*-as-disclosed-through-boredom. McManus's four FCM citations — "*Attunement [Stimmung] is … the fundamental way in which Dasein is as Dasein*" (FCM 67, quoted p.129); attunement as "the presupposition" and "'medium' within which" thinking, doing, and acting happen (FCM 64, 67–68, quoted p.134); and a note-level cross-reference (FCM 283, n.12) — are drawn entirely from FCM's general, introductory account of *Stimmung* as an existential structure prior to cognition and volition. In this cluster's own page-mapping (established by bor-sec-04's readings), that material precedes even the first form of boredom (which begins around FCM p.78); McManus is drawing on the doctrinal scaffolding the boredom analysis presupposes, not on the boredom analysis itself.

The one place the chapter comes closest to the cluster's central concerns is a single footnote. Note 35 (p.148) explicitly flags, as "[a]nother obvious lacuna," that the chapter does not work out "how the different models discussed would understand *anxiety*, a *Grundstimmung* that obviously has a crucial part to play in Heidegger's discussions of authenticity," deferring the decisionist and standpoint models' treatment of anxiety to McManus's own earlier essay (2015a) and explicitly postponing "a full account of the third model's understanding of anxiety" as "a topic for another day" (FAITHFUL-McManus, p.148 n.35). Boredom is not named even as a candidate Grundstimmung requiring this treatment. Given that this very volume's chapter 8 (Elpidorou and Freeman, "Is Profound Boredom Boredom?," already built in this cluster as bor-sec-04) argues at length that profound boredom is, on grounds directly analogous to anxiety's, an authenticity-disclosing and motivating mood, McManus's silence — treating anxiety as the sole Grundstimmung meriting extension of his three models, without so much as raising boredom as an alternative or additional candidate — is itself a notable, honestly-reportable absence rather than a null result. See §7 (Analytical role) below for why this matters for the cluster.

## 5. Findings/Claims

**§1 (pp.128–131).**
1. Owned *emotion* is comparatively neglected in the Heidegger-authenticity literature, which follows Heidegger's own emphasis on owned *understanding* (FAITHFUL-McManus, p.128, quoting Withy 2015, 21) — despite emotion being central to *Befindlichkeit* and *Stimmung*, the two concepts through which Heidegger stresses that Dasein always already finds itself exposed to, and "tuned" to, a mattering world (FE, pp.128–129). The gardener who "takes pleasure in a good harvest, worries over the first signs of blight, and despairs over a drought" illustrates this affective "tuning" (FE, p.129); the 1924 BCAP lectures add that such occurrences "happen to," "befall," and "strike" Dasein, so that emotion is "a 'being-taken' of Dasein … from without" — properly understood not as passive victimhood but as constitutive of Dasein's very being-in-the-world (FAITHFUL-McManus, p.129, quoting BCAP 131–133).
2. This inescapable "being-attuned" (*Gestimmtsein*) generates a standing interpretive problem: what could authenticity's "radical individualizing" of Dasein be, other than — as David Cooper puts it — a "Promethean" "standing apart from or above" conditions (absorption-in-the-world, dispersal in *das Man*) that Heidegger elsewhere treats as essential, structural features of Dasein (FAITHFUL-McManus, p.130, quoting Cooper 1997, 110, 112)?

**§2 (pp.131–133).**
3. On Michael Friedman's influential reading, Heideggerian authenticity is Dasein's recognition that its normal, unquestioningly-inherited "practical context is simply one possibility among others," subject to a "thoroughgoing decision" resting "on no taken for granted background framework at all" (FAITHFUL-McManus, p.131, quoting Friedman 2000, 51–52). Applied to affect: since "das Man prescribes one's *Befindlichkeit*" (SZ 169–170, quoted p.132), our emotions have "not been our own" — we have simply "learned to feel as *das Man* feels" — and can be "remedied" only by freely choosing, for ourselves, the concerns and values by which we will be moved (FE, p.132).

**§3 (pp.131–134).**
4. Against a philosophical tradition (Descartes onward) that treats affect as a "third class," merely accompanying "psychical phenomenon" that "obfuscates and inhibits" thought (FAITHFUL-McManus, pp.131–132, quoting SZ 139, 340 and FCM 64), Heidegger — standing, per Susan Bordo, in a "recessive" counter-tradition with Aristotle, Hume, and Nietzsche (FAITHFUL-McManus, p.131, citing Bordo 1987, 114–118) — argues affect discloses "a primordial kind of Being for Dasein," prior to all cognition and volition, through which Dasein has "already submitted itself to having entities within-the-world 'matter' to it" (FAITHFUL-McManus, p.132, quoting SZ 136–137, 341).
5. Heidegger concedes Dasein can, through knowledge and will, "become master of its moods" — but insists this mastery is achieved only "by way of a counter-mood," so that "we are never free of moods" (FAITHFUL-McManus, p.133, quoting SZ 136). More radically, affect is not merely revelatory of some features of the world but a *precondition of intentionality as such* — "the 'medium' within which" thinking, doing, and acting "happen" (FAITHFUL-McManus, p.134, quoting FCM 64, 67–68) — such that "even the purest *theoria* has not left all moods behind it" (FAITHFUL-McManus, p.134, quoting SZ 138).

**§4 (pp.134–135).**
6. Iris Murdoch and Ernst Tugendhat's independent objection to decisionist "liberation": a choice made "not … in the light of reasons" leaves "how I choose to accident," so that "it was not I who chose" (FAITHFUL-McManus, p.133, quoting Tugendhat 1986, 216, and Murdoch 1970, 8, 30, 91).
7. McManus's own extension: since being free to choose *how to feel* requires (per Heidegger's account of affect) being stripped of any sense of things mattering, decisionist "liberation" — read through the lens of *Gestimmtsein* — is not a thwarted will "wildly leaping" but a condition of *stasis*: "when Dasein becomes free in this sense, one might say it shuts down" (FE, p.134). Because there is, for Heidegger, no possible "subject which merely beholds" apart from all mattering, the decisionist's presupposed pure chooser cannot exist even in principle (FAITHFUL-McManus, pp.134–135, quoting SZ 341, 317).

**§5 (pp.135–139).**
8. The standpoint model (instanced, with differences McManus sets aside, by Varga's "wholeheartedly endorsed" projects, Crowell's and Kukla's "owning of norms," Haugeland's self-consistency, and Guignon's narrative "continuity and constancy") holds that authenticity is the consistent owning of a vocation, commitment, or life-story across contexts, so that the inauthentic "fall into line" with whatever context they are in while the authentic remain answerable to a standpoint recognizably their own (FAITHFUL-McManus, pp.135–137).
9. Applied to affect: the authentic person is moved by the world in a consistent manner tracking her owned commitments — Varga's example of readiness for righteous indignation "outlin[ing] in advance" that social injustice matters to her (FAITHFUL-McManus, p.136, citing Varga 2011, 72); Rudd's "serious ethical agent," who cannot simply "turn … off" private-life "generosity and compassion" on "entering the office," lest she be "hollow at the core, or a mere chameleon" (FAITHFUL-McManus, pp.136–137, quoting Rudd 2009, 69).
10. McManus's counterexample: a student comes to McManus upset about a low essay mark; were McManus, "no mere chameleon," to act from his own "well-known generosity and compassion" and simply award extra marks, "this doesn't seem right" — he "ought to be moved by a few other things here, including professionalism and a sense of fairness" (FE, p.137). Unlike the decisionist model, the standpoint model has no Promethean standing-apart-from-*Gestimmtsein*; but McManus argues a "subtler form of Prometheanism" remains (FE, p.137).

**§6 (pp.138–140).**
11. Real human lives are normatively multi-dimensional: McManus lists being "a son, a father, a husband, a brother, a friend, a neighbor, a colleague, a teacher, a philosopher, a member of a team…" (FE, p.139) — a plurality no single wholehearted commitment can capture without fancifully "renouncing all the rest" (FE, p.139). The standpoint model's subtler Prometheanism is thus a standing-apart from this essential plurality of commitments in favor of one or two of them.
12. Varga's own concession that authenticity requires "openness to eventual changes" in one's wholehearted projects (citing Varga 2011, 72) does not resolve the pluralist worry, McManus argues, because it only licenses *replacing* one wholehearted project with another ("reemplotment" of one's "life as a whole"), not holding multiple, simultaneously competing commitments open at once (FAITHFUL-McManus, p.138, quoting Varga 2013, 117–118).

**§7 (pp.140–141).**
13. McManus's own **All-Things-Considered Judgment Model (ATCJM)**: authenticity is a responsiveness not to the partial view of a single office, goal, norm, or project, but to *all* the relevant aspects of one's concrete situation at once, and to the need to adjudicate between them — an "all-things-considered judgment" (ATCJ) (FE, p.140). McManus's Moore's-paradox-style argument: a judgment of the form "(1) From the point of view of goal *w* or role *x*, what ought to be done is *y*" invites the further question "yes, but what do *you* think ought to be done?", while "(2) All things considered, what ought to be done here is *y*" does not — asserting (2) while adding "though I think what ought to be done is *z*" would be self-undermining (FAITHFUL-McManus, pp.139–140).
14. Two textual grounds offered for reading Heidegger's authenticity via ATCJ (McManus explicitly declines to argue for these at length here, deferring to his own forthcoming work): Heidegger's extended reading of Aristotelian *phronesis*, where the person who "bear[s] witness" to himself must "say what is best" rather than merely "recommend something as profitable" for a given goal or role (FAITHFUL-McManus, p.140, quoting BCAP 112); and Heidegger's account of "guilt" (*Schuld*), on which the finite Dasein "constantly is not" and has "waived" other possibilities, so that "[e]very action is … marked by guilt," since "the possibilities of action are limited in comparison with the demands of conscience" (FAITHFUL-McManus, p.141, quoting SZ 285 and WDR 169).

**§8 (pp.141–145).**
15. Anticipated objection (via Bill Blattner): doesn't ATCJM make authenticity "too deliberative, too intellectualized," when "[d]eliberation is a decidedly secondary phenomenon for Heidegger … rather than a basic form of Dasein's activity" (FAITHFUL-McManus, p.142, quoting Blattner 2013, 332)? McManus agrees with Blattner's premise but argues ATCJ is compatible with it: what is basic is not explicit propositional deliberation but "the breadth of one's horizon," one's affective "range of disclosure" (FE, p.142).
16. The chapter's central move: ATCJ requires an *emotional* openness — acknowledging "the full range of emotions that my situation elicits from me," since these correspond to "the many ways in which it matters" (FE, p.143). Inauthenticity, for this third model, is illustrated by McManus's own example: heading home to drive his son to rugby practice rather than listen to a worried friend, on the (true, but disburdening) general-social ground that "I've got to take care of my family" — a move that "disguises" rather than resolves the fact that society also generally accepts that one ought to take care of one's friends, so that invoking the family-norm merely evades, rather than performs, the needed ATCJ (FE, pp.144–145).
17. Conclusion: authenticity, on this model, requires resisting das Man's "prescri[ption]" of "the possibilities of attunement" and remaining open to the full range of ways one's situation "summons" one; inauthenticity is "precisely 'an evasive turning-away'" (FAITHFUL-McManus, p.145, quoting SZ 136) from the fullness of one's affective life and the guilt-laden normative pluralism it alone discloses.
18. Two footnote-level clarifications matter for how "owning" affect works on this model. Against Harry Frankfurt's view that we "own" only the emotion corresponding to the demand we decide to act on, "dissociat[ing]" ourselves from the rest as "external to [the] will" (FAITHFUL-McManus, p.149 n.39, quoting Frankfurt 1988, 170, 174), McManus judges this "an odd, forced conclusion": emotions not acted upon remain "very much one's own"; what one dissociates from through evasion is *not making an ATCJ at all* (FE, p.149 n.39). And McManus aligns his account with Katherine Withy's (2015) independent characterization — drawn from the same 1924 Aristotle lectures — of "owning affectivity" as a condition in which Dasein "resist[s] averageness" and "hold[s] itself open," letting itself "be moved," rather than closing itself off to "the customary ways of being so" (FAITHFUL-McManus, p.149 n.42, quoting Withy 2015, 29–30).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* This chapter's primary dissertation relevance runs to Part II (Heideggerian affect/metaphysics), with one genuinely substantive, if indirect, point of contact for Part III.

First, and most valuably for this cluster: this chapter's own silence about boredom is itself worth recording as a finding. McManus explicitly flags anxiety — not boredom — as the one *Grundstimmung* whose bearing on authenticity his three models still owe an account of (n.35, p.148), deferring even that to future work, without so much as raising boredom as an alternative candidate mood *(anticipatory-application)*. This replicates, in miniature and without either side being aware of the other, the exact question this same volume's chapter 8 (Elpidorou and Freeman, bor-sec-04) interrogates directly: whether profound boredom is entitled to the same authenticity-disclosing status conventionally reserved for anxiety. Read alongside bor-sec-04's conciliatory finding that profound boredom shares anxiety-like, authenticity-revealing force without being reducible to ordinary boredom, McManus's chapter supplies a data point on the *other* side of that question — a rigorous three-model taxonomy of owned affect that simply never considers boredom a candidate, tacitly reproducing the conventional anxiety-privileging McManus's own field (per his footnote) has not yet corrected. Part II's discussion of profound boredom's authenticity-status can cite this chapter precisely as an instance of that unexamined privileging, not as a source that argues for it.

Second, McManus's third-model vocabulary of "evasion" (*Ausweichen*)/"evasive turning-away" (SZ 136, quoted p.145) as the mark of inauthentic affective life is a striking, purely structural echo — not a citation, and not McManus's own intention — of the boredom-secondary-literature's own vocabulary for inauthentic responses to boredom: fleeing into distraction/pastime (*Zeitvertreib*) rather than "letting" the mood tell (cf. bor-sec-04's *Augenblick* discussion; Slaby's and Aho's accounts elsewhere in this cluster). Both literatures independently converge on "openness versus flight" as the marker of authentic affective life; flagging this convergence, purely as our own observation (P/anticipatory-application), is useful for Part II's synthesis of what "owned" affect means across this volume's chapters, but it licenses no claim that McManus himself is discussing boredom.

Third, for Part III specifically: McManus's normative-pluralism argument (§6) — that real students inhabit many simultaneous, competing roles (learner, teammate, player, deadline-holder, friend) rather than one dominant, wholehearted commitment — offers a modest methodological caution for interpreting any single self-report boredom/engagement item collected in a VR or desktop-2D session: an apparent dip in engagement may reflect the pull of a *different*, momentarily weightier normative demand (a looming assignment, a social distraction) rather than the task itself lacking meaning or monotony in the disengagement-literature's sense *(anticipatory-application)*. This is a caution about interpretation, not a measure or a bridge; no `grounds-measure` edge is warranted, since the not-yet-built VR Attention Study corpus entry has no natural docking point in this chapter's purely conceptual argument.

Finally, a cluster-navigation note: this chapter is analytically isolated within this cluster's Heideggerian-boredom secondary literature. It cites no author already built as a unit here (not Elpidorou, Freeman, Slaby, Aho, Gibbs, Mihačević, Quaranta, or Zabalo), drawing instead on a distinct citation network in analytic ethics and philosophy of action (Murdoch, Tugendhat, Frankfurt, Korsgaard, Guignon, Rudd, Varga, Crowell, Haugeland, Velleman, Blattner, Mele). No `cites-cluster-author` edge is warranted for this unit.
