# bor-sec-49 — Dahlstrom, "Missing in Action: Affectivity in *Being and Time*" (2019)

**Type:** book chapter — Chapter 5 of 12 in Christos Hadjioannou (ed.), *Heidegger on Affect*, Philosophers in Depth series (Palgrave Macmillan, 2019), pp.105–126 · **Discipline:** philosophy (Heideggerian phenomenology; internal critique of *Being and Time*) · **Depth:** deep · **Strand:** G · **Corpus role:** first Strand-G unit built from *Heidegger on Affect*. The chapter's author is Daniel O. Dahlstrom (Boston University); Hadjioannou is the volume's editor only and has no authorial claim on this chapter's argument.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Dahlstrom's chapter poses a single governing question against the grain of a broadly admiring first half: Heidegger "talks the talk" about the foundational, equiprimordial status of affectivity (disposedness/*Befindlichkeit*, mood/*Stimmung*, feeling, emotion) in *Being and Time* (BT) — but does he "walk the walk" (FE, p.111)? The chapter's title supplies its verdict: affectivity, despite Heidegger's own explicit commitments, is repeatedly "missing in action" from analyses where, on Heidegger's own terms, it should be present. Dahlstrom's method is textually granular rather than programmatic: he first reconstructs, sympathetically and in detail, Heidegger's positive theory of disposedness (§1), then audits BT section by section for places where affectivity is asserted as equiprimordial with understanding but is, in practice, lexically subordinated, omitted, or gestured at without elaboration (§2), and finally presses the same test on the highest-stakes case — Heidegger's account of authentic existence, guilt, conscience, and anticipatory resoluteness — where the stakes of the omission are greatest because affectivity there is explicitly invoked but left "underdetermined to a fault" (FE, p.119). The chapter closes without resolving the tension it has diagnosed, ending instead on a further, unanswered question: what mood, if any, corresponds to doing fundamental ontology itself (§4)? This is not a piece about boredom. Heidegger's boredom analysis (*Die Grundbegriffe der Metaphysik* [FCM]) is never engaged; the word "boredom" occurs exactly once in the entire chapter, and only inside a citation of a companion essay's title (n.16, p.123, citing Elpidorou and Freeman 2015b, "Affectivity in Heidegger II: Temporality, Boredom, and Beyond"). The chapter's proper object is BT's general architecture of affectivity and Heidegger's fidelity (or infidelity) to his own commitments regarding it.

## 2. Structure

1. **§1 The Essentialness of Affectivity** (pp.105–111) — a sympathetic, detailed reconstruction of Heidegger's positive account: disposedness's "three essential characters" (thrownness-disclosure; the equiprimordial disclosure of world, others, and existence; world-openness/mattering); the existential/existentiel distinction; structural analyses of fear and angst, including their differing temporal characters.
2. **§2 Missing Modes of Affectivity** (pp.111–118) — the chapter's critical core, in two numbered subsections:
   - **2.1 *Gleichursprünglichkeit* (Equiprimordiality)** (pp.111–113) — Heidegger's explicit doctrine that disposedness and understanding are co-equal existentials, set against the lexical asymmetry of his own prose (*befindliches Verstehen* vs. *verstehende Befindlichkeit*) and the absence of any affective parallel to *Seinsverständnis*.
   - **2.2 *Begleitphänomenen* (Accompanying Phenomena)** (pp.113–118) — a survey of BT passages on concern, tool-use, significance, spatiality, being-with, das Man, idle talk, ambiguity, curiosity, and fallenness, tracking where affectivity is present, absent, or merely gestured at.
3. **§3 *Angst-Bereit* (Angst-Preparedness)** (pp.118–120) — the chapter's sharpest test case: Heidegger's account of authentic conscience, guilt, and anticipatory resoluteness, where affective terms (uncanniness, "sober angst," "armed joy") are named but left largely unexplicated.
4. **§4 Concluding Remark** (p.120) — restates the thesis and closes on the further, unresolved question of fundamental ontology's own corresponding mood.
5. **Notes 1–29** (pp.120–125) — including an extended defense of "disposedness" as the translation of *Befindlichkeit* (n.1) that cross-references Elpidorou and Freeman (2015a) for a fuller translation survey, and a citation of Elpidorou and Freeman (2015b) for the temporal dimensions of affectivity that this chapter itself declines to re-argue (n.16).
6. **References** (p.125).

## 3. Constructs

This chapter's controlled-vocabulary treatment is genuinely thin on boredom-specific constructs, since its object is BT's general theory of affectivity rather than boredom — a fact the chapter itself makes plain by never using the word "boredom" except inside a citation title. Honest treatment, construct by construct:

- **monotony / meaninglessness** — treated substantively, though as an object of *angst* rather than of boredom. Dahlstrom's account of angst's structure holds that an experience of "the meaninglessness of anything" encountered within the world brings with it a parallel sense of "the meaninglessness of our individual being-in-the-world" itself (FAITHFUL-Dahlstrom, p.109–110, glossing SZ 186–188). This is the general disclosure-of-meaninglessness machinery that FCM's profound-boredom analysis and the cluster's other boredom units treat as boredom's own signature; here it belongs to angst instead.
- **flow / engagement (contrast pole)** — treated obliquely via curiosity and fallenness. Dahlstrom highlights Heidegger's account of curiosity's "restlessness and excitement, the distracted and uprooted character" of chasing "the next new thing" (FAITHFUL-Dahlstrom, p.115–116, glossing SZ), and of fallenness's "sedating tranquility" of "mindless absorption in the world" (FAITHFUL-Dahlstrom, p.117, glossing SZ), captured by Heidegger's *Wirbel* (whirlwind) metaphor for "this dizzying plunge into nothingness" (p.117–118). Both are inauthentic modes of worldly absorption — the negative, self-concealing analogue of flow's positive engagement — treated here as evidence that Heidegger *can* attend to affect when curiosity or fallenness are at stake, sharpening rather than resolving Dahlstrom's critique.
- **temporality of boredom (time-drag)** — not treated as such; boredom's temporality is explicitly outsourced by this chapter's own footnote to the companion essay (n.16, p.123, "Elpidorou and Freeman [2015b, 7–12]"). What *is* treated at length is the general temporal architecture of fear and angst (*Gewesenheit*, awaiting vs. anticipatory "running-ahead") that underlies any such analysis (p.110–111) — the same architecture bor-sec-03 (and FCM) extend specifically to boredom's time-drag (P/anticipatory-application).
- **attentional disengagement / meta-awareness** — not treated as a named construct, but structurally anticipated: Dahlstrom observes that "moods can be transparent," so that one "can be oblivious to the disposedness underlying" a given mood (FAITHFUL-Dahlstrom, p.109, glossing SZ 35). This opacity-of-one's-own-affective-state thesis is a general precursor to the boredom literature's meta-awareness/attentional-disengagement problem, though the chapter never makes that application itself (P/anticipatory-application).
- **profound/deep boredom (FCM 3rd form), being-bored-by (1st form), being-bored-with (2nd form), situational vs. trait/state boredom, boredom proneness, mind-wandering/task-unrelated thought, boredom-as-regulatory-signal** — not treated at all. None of the FCM three-form vocabulary, nor any empirical boredom construct, appears anywhere in the chapter's own argument.

## 4. Reading of FCM

This chapter does not engage Heidegger's boredom analysis. It is squarely a BT chapter: every citation is to *Sein und Zeit* (by the standard German pagination) or to the 1925 lecture course *Prolegomena zur Geschichte des Zeitbegriffs* (GA 20), never to *Die Grundbegriffe der Metaphysik* (FCM, 1929/30 lectures). Boredom surfaces exactly once, inside a citation of Elpidorou and Freeman's companion-essay title (n.16, p.123). Per this cluster's own bridging rule — bridge only where a chapter genuinely engages Heidegger's boredom analysis specifically — no `bridges-to-fcm` edge is claimed for this unit. Section 3 above records the honest degree of overlap: the chapter's meaninglessness-disclosure, absorption/tranquility, and mood-opacity analyses are the same general BT machinery that FCM's boredom chapters (and this cluster's FCM bridge units) draw on for boredom specifically, but Dahlstrom himself draws no such connection, and the cluster records this only as our own analogical extension (P/anticipatory-application), not as the chapter's content.

**Comparison to bor-sec-02 (Elpidorou and Freeman, "Affectivity in Heidegger I," 2015).** The two units are thematically close enough to require direct comparison, and the citation direction is unambiguous: Dahlstrom (2019) cites Elpidorou and Freeman (2015a, 2015b) — bor-sec-02 and its companion bor-sec-03 — twice, by name, in his own notes. Note 1 (p.120) sends the reader to "Elpidorou and Freeman (2015a), 4 note 4 and 5 note 5" for a fuller survey of *Befindlichkeit*/*Stimmung* translation options than Dahlstrom offers himself; note 16 (p.123) sends the reader to "Elpidorou and Freeman (2015b, 7–12)" for "a clear exposition of Heidegger's account of the temporal dimensions of affectivity" — precisely the topic (and, in bor-sec-03's case, the boredom application) this chapter declines to re-argue. There is no citation running the other direction, since bor-sec-02/03 (2015) predate this chapter (2019); bor-sec-02 does, however, already list "Daniel Dahlstrom" among the translators it surveys for rendering *Befindlichkeit* as "disposition" (bor-sec-02, n.4, p.663), so the two pieces were already in implicit conversation about translation before this chapter made the citation explicit and reciprocal.

Substantively, the two pieces converge closely and then diverge sharply. They converge on the reconstruction itself: both works derive from BT §29 (and GA 20) essentially the same three-part disclosive structure of disposedness — bor-sec-02's "(a) thrownness, (b) being-in-the-world as a whole, (c) what matters" (bor-sec-02, p.664–666) maps almost one-to-one onto this chapter's "first," "second," and "third essential character" (p.106–107). Both also treat the existential/existentiel distinction, moods as prior to any subject/object split, and angst's disclosure of meaninglessness and "potentiality-for-Being," in substantially compatible terms. They diverge in critical orientation and in scope. Bor-sec-02 is a sympathetic, synthesizing exegesis that flags only one internal tension (whether fear is better classified as mood or emotion) and otherwise builds toward its own constructive proposal (moods as the necessary background for emotions); it explicitly defers BT's temporal architecture and any boredom analysis to its own companion piece (bor-sec-03) rather than adjudicating Heidegger's textual practice. This chapter does the opposite: it takes the same positive reconstruction as its starting point and then subjects Heidegger's entire text to a granular audit of whether the equiprimordiality this reconstruction presupposes is actually honored in BT's execution — concluding that it typically is not, especially at the point (authentic resoluteness) where the stakes are highest. The relationship is best described as **extension with an added critical register**, not a straightforward contest: Dahlstrom does not dispute bor-sec-02's account of what Befindlichkeit discloses; he extends it into a diagnostic of Heidegger's own inconsistency in delivering on that disclosure across the text as a whole (P/anticipatory-application for the characterization of the relationship; the citations themselves are FAITHFUL-Dahlstrom, n.1 p.120 and n.16 p.123).

One further point of convergence is worth flagging for the cluster's debate-map. Bor-sec-02 already noted, in its own footnote, that "the word 'Grundstimmung' appears only once in BT (358/310)" and becomes prominent only after 1929 — i.e., in FCM itself (bor-sec-02, n.2, p.662) — framing a cluster debate-axis (DA3: anxiety vs. profound boredom as candidate *Grundstimmung*). This chapter, working independently and for its own unrelated purpose (distinguishing mood from emotion via the shame/guilt analogy), notes that Heidegger names Angst "both a *Grundbefindlichkeit* and a *Grundstimmung*" (FAITHFUL-Dahlstrom, n.6, p.121–122, citing SZ 188–190, 310) — locating one of BT's rare "Grundstimmung" occurrences at exactly SZ 310, inside the very anticipatory-resoluteness passage this chapter's own §3 analyzes at length. The corroboration is incidental (Dahlstrom is not tracking the cluster's boredom debate) but genuine, and it sharpens bor-sec-02's point: BT's sole clear naming of a *Grundstimmung* sits inside the passage this chapter independently flags as the site of its "missing in action" critique — i.e., the one place BT calls something a ground-mood by name is also the place Dahlstrom finds Heidegger's affective elaboration thinnest.

## 5. Findings/Claims

- Disposedness has three essential characters, established through close reading of BT §29 and GA 20: it discloses (1) thrownness — the passivity of moods, akin to Greek *pathē*/*pathos* against *praxis* — into which Dasein is "swept up" rather than the moods' author (FE, p.105–106, glossing SZ 134–138); (2) "an existentially basic sort" of the "equiprimordial disclosedness of world, others who are here" (FAITHFUL-Dahlstrom, p.106–107, quoting SZ 137); and (3) Dasein's "openness to the world" and its mattering — that things can "touch" us, threaten or reassure us (FE, p.107, glossing SZ 137).
- Disposedness (existential/ontological) is not identical with mood, feeling, or emotion (existentiel/ontic); a mood is "an exponent of disposedness" (FAITHFUL-Dahlstrom, p.108, quoting GA 20:353), and — in an explicitly Aristotelian idiom — "moods are first for us" while "disposedness is first by nature," so the existential-ontological analysis is ultimately rooted in the existentiel-ontic one (FAITHFUL-Dahlstrom, p.109, glossing SZ 13, 35).
- Moods and the disposedness underlying them are often occluded from the subject who has them: "we can be oblivious to the disposedness underlying and co-constituting the mood," which is precisely why the existential phenomenologist's task is to make explicit what "moods and emotions" do not themselves display (FAITHFUL-Dahlstrom, p.109, glossing SZ 35).
- Fear and angst receive genuinely masterful structural analyses, including their contrasting temporal characters: fear, disclosed via "awaiting" an impending worldly threat, assimilates Dasein inauthentically to something merely handy; angst, disclosed via no awaiting at all, brings Dasein back to itself through "resolute anticipation" of "the meaninglessness of anything we might encounter within the world" and of "our individual being-in-the-world as such" (FE, p.109–111, glossing SZ 185–188, 341–345).
- **Central critical thesis**: despite Heidegger's explicit doctrine that disposedness and understanding are "equiprimordial" (FAITHFUL-Dahlstrom, p.111–112, quoting SZ 160, 220), his own prose privileges understanding — "disposed understanding" (*befindliches Verstehen*) "surfaces at least fourteen times in SZ," against only four occurrences of the reverse order, "understanding disposedness" (*verstehende Befindlichkeit*) (FAITHFUL-Dahlstrom, p.112, glossing SZ). Similarly, "*Seinsverständnis*" (understanding of being) is "a go-to expression for practically every analysis," while "there is no comparable talk of a disposedness or attunement to being" (FAITHFUL-Dahlstrom, p.112–113, glossing SZ 12, 85).
- A section-by-section survey (concern, tool-use, circumspection, significance, trust, "captivation," spatiality, being-with, das Man's modes of solicitude) finds affectivity largely absent from analyses that, on Heidegger's own equiprimordiality thesis, "should" contain it — most "egregiously," in Dahlstrom's own word, from the account of being-with-others, where Heidegger "exclusively invokes understanding" and affect is "conspicuously... absent" (FAITHFUL-Dahlstrom, p.113–115, glossing SZ 76, 83, 87, 109, 119–125).
- Curiosity and fallenness are the chapter's counter-examples: Heidegger *is* explicit about curiosity's "restlessness and excitement" and about fallenness's "sedating tranquility," rendered vivid through the *Wirbel* (whirlwind) metaphor for "this dizzying plunge into nothingness" (FE, p.115–118, glossing SZ). Far from softening the critique, these passages show Heidegger *can* attend fully to affect — he simply does not do so consistently where his own theory says he should.
- The authenticity case sharpens the stakes: Heidegger explicitly asks what mood corresponds to "wanting-to-have-a-conscience" (the uncanniness of conscience, disclosed through "the disposedness of angst") and later names "the sober angst that brings up the individualized potential-to-be" together with "the armed joy at this possibility" as the mood of anticipatory resoluteness (FAITHFUL-Dahlstrom, p.118–119, quoting SZ 296, 310). But these allusions are "meager to a fault" given how much of the analysis of temporality and authenticity rests on them (FE, p.119).
- Heidegger's own scope-disclaimer — that elaborating "basic moods" like joy oversteps a fundamental-ontological (as opposed to philosophical-anthropological) inquiry (SZ 17, 49f., 131, 183, 194, 200, 301) — is judged "a dodge or diversion," since Heidegger freely "enlists moods" (curiosity, tranquility, uncanniness) elsewhere "when it suits his purposes" (FE, p.119–120).
- The chapter ends on an open, unresolved question rather than a verdict: the "missing in action" pattern may be "of a piece" with a further, deeper absence — BT nowhere names the mood that corresponds to doing fundamental ontology itself (FE, p.120).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* This unit's chief service to Part III is methodological caution rather than substantive boredom content. First, Dahlstrom's opacity-of-affect thesis — that disposedness and the moods that manifest it can be "transparent" to the very Dasein having them (p.109) — is a philosophical precedent for treating any single-timepoint self-report of "boredom" in the dissertation's survey instrument as, at best, a partial and possibly retrospectively-reconstructed trace of an affective state whose primary mode of givenness need not be reflectively available to the participant at all *(anticipatory-application)*. Second, the chapter's central diagnostic method — checking whether a theory's stated commitments about affectivity are actually honored in its executed analysis, section by section, rather than taking the theory's self-description at face value — is itself a transferable auditing procedure Part III can apply to its own instruments: a survey item that names "engagement" or "boredom" in its label does not thereby guarantee that its item-level content actually operationalizes the construct with the granularity the label promises *(anticipatory-application)*. Third, the chapter's curiosity/fallenness material (restless distraction vs. sedating absorption) offers a ready-made phenomenological contrast pair for characterizing the *texture* of disengagement Part III's desktop-3D and video-only strata might separately elicit — a restless, curiosity-like drift toward novelty versus a flatter, tranquilized absorption — prior to and independent of any physiological measurement *(anticipatory-application)*. Finally, because this chapter and bor-sec-02/bor-sec-03 converge on the same BT-level reconstruction of disposedness while diverging on critical method, the pairing gives Part III's literature review a clean illustration of how the same primary text supports both a sympathetic architecture-building reading and an internal-consistency audit — a distinction worth preserving explicitly whenever the dissertation cites "Heidegger on affectivity" as though the secondary literature spoke with one voice *(anticipatory-application)*.
