# bor-sec-38 — Scharinger, Kammerer & Gerjets, "Pupil Dilation and EEG Alpha Frequency Band Power Reveal Load on Executive Functions for Link-Selection Processes during Text Reading" (2015)

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Scharinger, Kammerer, and Gerjets (Knowledge Media Research Center, Tuebingen) target a narrow gap in hypertext-reading research: prior work established that hyperlinks impose load on executive functions (EFs) somewhere in the reading-browsing process, but no study had isolated the load contributed by the *initial link-selection decision itself* — distinct from navigating/integrating subsequent pages — nor directly compared two physiological load-measures, pupil dilation and EEG alpha frequency band power, in one reading task (FE, p.1-4). EFs are defined as attention-related top-down control processes comprising updating, shifting, and inhibition (FAITHFUL-Scharinger et al., p.1, citing Miyake et al.); a link-selection decision is reasoned to recruit shifting (reading-to-deciding) and inhibition (ignoring distractors) (FE, p.1-2). Across three experiments they embed a simulated hyperlink-selection task inside a standardized German reading-comprehension text (the LGVT), comparing "test" epochs (clicking the one context-matching word among three bracketed options) against "baseline" epochs (pure reading, later matched for motor/perceptual demands) while recording pupil diameter and 27-channel EEG (FAITHFUL-Scharinger et al., p.4-5). Experiment 1 establishes the core effect; Experiments 2-3 rule out perceptual, motor, and structural confounds (FE, p.4, 15-17). Both measures show the predicted load increase during selection, yet — the paper's most striking finding — do not correlate with one another (FE, p.9-10, 18-20).

## 2. Structure

The article runs pp.1-24 of the extracted PDF (references/supporting information occupy roughly the final third); page numbers below are the article's own embedded "n / 24" running-head markers and are close approximations, not the journal's own e-locator pagination.

1. **Abstract** (~p.1) — states the gap, the dual-measure methodology, and the surprising non-correlation between the two load-measures.
2. **Introduction** (~p.1-4) — defines EFs (updating/shifting/inhibition, citing Miyake et al.); reviews Kintsch's construction-integration model, distinguishing a construction step (affected by link-selection) from an integration step (affected by subsequent pages); reviews the one directly comparable prior study (Fitzsimmons, Weal & Drieghe) finding no overall disruption effect; reviews pupil-dilation load literature (Kahneman & Beatty; Laeng et al.'s locus-coeruleus/arousal link; Di Stasi et al.'s hypermedia null-difficulty-sensitivity finding) and EEG-alpha load literature (event-related desynchronization, Pfurtscheller & Lopes da Silva; Klimesch; Antonenko & Niederhauser's hypertext-leads alpha study); states three hypotheses (1a: pupil dilation increases in the test condition; 1b: alpha power decreases; 2: the two difference-scores correlate negatively).
3. **Experiment 1** (~p.4-10) — Methods (N=23; LGVT text with 23 bracketed 3-word decision points; SMI 250 Hz eye-tracker; 27-electrode EEG); Results (task-condition main effects on pupil size and Pz alpha power; a 3-way hemisphere×site×condition ANOVA; correlations among the physiological and behavioral scores); Discussion (confirms 1a/1b, disconfirms 2, flags perceptual/motor confounds).
4. **Experiment 2** (~p.10-14) — modifies baseline to require a matched click and removes the color cue, controlling perceptual/motor confounds; N=20 recruited/19 analyzed; replicates Experiment 1's pattern and non-correlation.
5. **Experiment 3** (~p.14-16) — equalizes test/baseline task structure to rule out a difficulty confound; N=24; pupil-only design shows no condition difference, as predicted.
6. **General Discussion** (~p.16-20) — synthesizes across experiments; offers two non-adjudicated accounts of the pupil/alpha non-correlation; discusses ecological-validity limitations of the artificial, non-functional hyperlink paradigm; situates the work within "neuro-ergonomics"; proposes future differential-EF-loading and more naturalistic hypertext studies.
7. **Supporting information / references** (~p.20-24) — three supplementary data tables; 85 references, drawn from cognitive-psychology, psychophysiology, and hypertext/HCI literatures.

## 3. Constructs

A full-text search of the extracted source confirms **zero occurrences** of "bore," "boredom," or any cognate anywhere in its roughly 13,500-word body. This is a purely cognitive-load/executive-function paper; none of the cluster's eleven controlled constructs is treated in the source's own vocabulary. The three entries below are included only because the paper's executive-function apparatus supplies the same explanatory currency (attention, inhibition, cognitive control) that boredom researchers elsewhere in this cluster use for attentional disengagement, mind-wandering, and engagement — each is flagged explicitly as an **analogical, not textual, link** (P/anticipatory-application):

- **attentional disengagement / meta-awareness** (analogical) — the paper's "inhibition" EF, defined as "general processes of interference control or executive attention" (FAITHFUL-Scharinger et al., p.1), names the very control-failure mechanism that boredom researchers (e.g., Fisher/Hamilton-style attentional-failure accounts, cited in this cluster's Strand D units) invoke for attentional disengagement — but Scharinger et al. study intact, effortfully deployed inhibition, not its breakdown (P/anticipatory-application).
- **flow / engagement (contrast pole)** (analogical) — the authors deliberately calibrate their task so participants are "neither over- nor under-loaded" (FAITHFUL-Scharinger et al., p.5) and report comprehension scores in the "upper third of the common LGVT outcomes" (FAITHFUL-Scharinger et al., p.7) — an empirical portrait of bounded, effortful task engagement that could serve analogically as the engaged/non-bored contrast pole, though the authors never use "engagement" or "flow" vocabulary (P/anticipatory-application).
- **mind-wandering / task-unrelated thought** (analogical) — the same EF-inhibition mechanism (ignoring task-irrelevant distractor words during selection, FAITHFUL-Scharinger et al., p.1-2) is the executive-control substrate that resource-control models of mind-wandering invoke for failures to suppress task-unrelated thought, though this paper never engages that literature or names mind-wandering (P/anticipatory-application).

Not treated at all, with no textual basis for even an analogical reading: profound/deep boredom (FCM 3rd form), being-bored-by (1st form), being-bored-with (2nd form), situational vs. trait/state boredom, boredom proneness, boredom-as-regulatory-signal (functional account), monotony/meaninglessness, and temporality of boredom (time-drag). The LGVT text is a standardized-difficulty comprehension passage, not a monotony-inducing stimulus, and no self-report affective scale of any kind is administered.

## 4. Measures & methods

**Apparatus (shared across experiments).** Eye-tracking used a 250 Hz SMI infrared remote eye-tracker below a 22-inch Dell monitor (1680×1050 px), chin rest fixing eye-to-screen distance at ~70 cm, 9-point SMI Experiment Center calibration (FAITHFUL-Scharinger et al., p.5-6). EEG used 27 scalp electrodes (10/20 system), right-mastoid reference, AFz ground, three EOG electrodes, recorded via PyCorder/ActiCHamp at 500 Hz with active ActiCap electrodes, impedances <5 kΩ (FAITHFUL-Scharinger et al., p.6). Eye-tracking data were upsampled to 500 Hz and synchronized with EEG via EEGLAB/EYE-EEG; EEG was filtered (0.5-40 Hz FIR), EOG-corrected via ICA, re-referenced to the average, and epoched into 2-second windows (±100 µV artifact rejection) (FAITHFUL-Scharinger et al., p.6-7).

**Task/protocol.** Participants read a standardized 1,727-word German reading-comprehension text (the LGVT, a validated high-school-level reading instrument) as a single web page. At 23 embedded positions, three bracketed words appeared (one target, two distractors); participants clicked the matching word, simulating hyperlink selection (FAITHFUL-Scharinger et al., p.5). Test epochs ended 500 ms before the click (excluding motor artifacts); baseline epochs came from intervening pure-reading lines (FAITHFUL-Scharinger et al., p.6-7). LGVT reading-comprehension score (+2/−1 per correct/incorrect selection) and total reading time were also recorded as convergent, non-physiological indices (FAITHFUL-Scharinger et al., p.7).

**Sample and modifications.** Experiment 1 (N=23; mean age 24.83; 13 female) used color-coded blue-to-red links, introducing perceptual/motor confounds addressed in Experiment 2 (N=20 recruited, 19 analyzed; mean age 24.90; 9 female), which equalized click-demands and removed color-coding; both measures were recorded in both experiments. Experiment 3 (N=24; mean age 23.38; 16 female) reduced the test condition to a single non-distractor word and recorded pupil dilation only, for efficiency, since Experiments 1-2 already showed comparable cross-channel sensitivity (FAITHFUL-Scharinger et al., p.10-15).

## 5. Findings/Claims

- **Pupil dilation increased in the test (link-selection) condition versus baseline in both Experiment 1** (3.58 mm vs. 3.51 mm, F(1,22)=21.85, p<.001, η²p=.50) **and Experiment 2** (3.64 mm vs. 3.57 mm, F(1,18)=32.61, p<.001, η²p=.64), confirming Hypothesis 1a (FAITHFUL-Scharinger et al., p.7-8, 11-12).
- **EEG alpha frequency band power (8-13 Hz) at parietal electrode Pz decreased** in the test condition in both Experiment 1 (6.58 vs. 7.96 µV²/Hz, F(1,22)=16.05, p=.001, η²p=.42) and Experiment 2 (8.05 vs. 8.87 µV²/Hz, F(1,18)=15.00, p=.001, η²p=.45), confirming Hypothesis 1b (FAITHFUL-Scharinger et al., p.8, 12).
- A 3-way ANOVA (hemisphere × electrode site × condition) in both experiments showed the alpha decrease was **left-lateralized and largest at parietal-occipital sites**, consistent with prior reports of left-lateralized alpha effects for linguistic material (FAITHFUL-Scharinger et al., p.8-9, 12-13).
- **Experiment 3 found no pupil-dilation difference** between conditions once selection difficulty was equalized (3.67 mm vs. 3.66 mm, F(1,23)=.04, p=.85), ruling out a structural/difficulty confound for the Experiment 1-2 effects (FAITHFUL-Scharinger et al., p.15-16).
- **Contrary to Hypothesis 2, pupil dilation and EEG alpha power changes did not correlate** (Exp.1 r=−.16, ns; Exp.2 r=−.14, ns) despite both independently registering the load effect (FAITHFUL-Scharinger et al., p.9, 12-13, 18-20).
- **Alpha power change correlated negatively with LGVT reading-comprehension score** (Experiment 1: r=−.41, marginal; Experiment 2: r=−.50, p<.05), while pupil-dilation change showed no such relationship in either experiment — read by the authors as evidence that alpha power indexes a narrower, more purely cognitive process than pupil dilation, which they characterize as a broader measure also sensitive to motivational/emotional/arousal factors (FAITHFUL-Scharinger et al., p.9, 19-20).
- Two non-adjudicated explanations are offered for the persistent non-correlation: (a) differential sensitivity to distinct load aspects (cognitive vs. motivational/emotional/arousal), or (b) individual differences making one measure "better" for some participants, with implications for neuro-ergonomics research design (FAITHFUL-Scharinger et al., p.19-20).
- Named limitations: ecological validity limited by non-functional (dead-end) links, forced linear reading, and repeated same-position link sequences unlike naturalistic browsing; downstream comprehension effects of link-selection are not tested separately from text-base construction (FE, p.17-18).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* This source is a strong `grounds-measure` candidate for **both** channels of the not-yet-built Boredom Experiment (VR Attention Study) dataset entry, offering research-grade methodological detail absent from lighter consumer-hardware studies elsewhere in this cluster: a 250 Hz remote infrared eye-tracker protocol (chin-rest-stabilized, 9-point calibration) for the pupillometry channel, and a 27-electrode, 500 Hz, ICA-artifact-corrected EEG protocol with a defined alpha-band (8-13 Hz) power-extraction procedure for the EEG channel — though a VR headset's built-in eye- and (if present) brain-sensing would differ substantially in geometry, sampling, and form factor from this screen-based, external-sensor laboratory setup, so linkage remains unconfirmed until the dataset entry specifies its own instrumentation *(anticipatory-application)*.

*(anticipatory-application)* The paper's central empirical surprise — pupil dilation and EEG alpha power both independently track an experimentally manipulated load increase, yet do not correlate with one another — is a direct methodological caution for Part III's own use of multiple attention/engagement indices (physiological, behavioral, self-report): convergence between measures of ostensibly the same underlying state should not be assumed, and a null cross-measure correlation does not itself invalidate either measure *(anticipatory-application)*. This closely parallels, and independently reinforces, the analogous convergent-validity caution already drawn from Strand D's Kim et al. (bor-sec-28), whose self-report boredom rating likewise failed to correlate with either of that study's physiological channels.

*(anticipatory-application)* Regarding Strand D's Kim et al. (bor-sec-28) — also a dual EEG-plus-gaze physiological study — this source's 85-item reference list shows **no citation overlap** with Kim et al.'s boredom-specific literature (Eastwood, Fahlman, Farmer & Sundberg, Russell 1980, Csikszentmihalyi, Chanel et al.): the two are not mutually cited and share no author. The relationship is a **thematic parallel only** — both pair EEG with an eye-based physiological measure to track a state under experimentally induced conditions, and both report an unexpected non-correlation between candidate indices — not a citational or theoretical one; no citation link is fabricated where the text supplies none *(anticipatory-application)*.

*(anticipatory-application)* Because Scharinger et al.'s participants are, on the paper's own account, effortfully engaged and appropriately loaded (comprehension scores in the "upper third" of norms), the study offers Part III a contrast case for separating *cognitive load under a structured, meaningful task* from *monotony/boredom under repetitive, low-value conditions* — states this cluster's phenomenological and educational sources (Strands A-C) are careful not to conflate, and which this source's silence on boredom vocabulary throws into relief by omission *(anticipatory-application)*.
