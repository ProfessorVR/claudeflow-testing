# bor-sec-18 — Mansikka, "Can Boredom Educate Us? Tracing a Mood in Heidegger's Fundamental Ontology from an Educational Point of View" (2008/2009)

**Type:** journal article (philosophy of education / Heideggerian phenomenology of mood), *Studies in Philosophy and Education* 28:255–268 · **Discipline:** philosophy of education (Heidegger studies; phenomenology of *Stimmung*/boredom); no empirical, affective-science, or attention-science content, though the source's own abstract and opening paragraph explicitly invoke Csikszentmihalyi's "flow" as a named contrast pole · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** this cluster's single most FCM-central Strand-B source. Mansikka gives a compressed but faithful exposition of all three of Heidegger's forms of boredom from *The Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics* (1929/30) and fits them to an original three-part pedagogical typology — meaningless/technical instruction, engaged-but-inauthentic performance-learning, and profound boredom as a site of possible pedagogical rupture — before reaching, in its epilogue, directly into FCM's Chapter Five material on contemporary Dasein's "absence of distress." Independently confirmed as a citation target of bor-sec-07 (Gibbs, Strand A, cited at pp.602 and 611) and very probably the same essay Mansikka himself footnotes as "Thomson 2003" — almost certainly a reprint of this cluster's bor-sec-19 (Thomson 2001) in M. A. Peters's 2003 edited volume — for its "essence of paideia" quotation.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Mansikka reads Heidegger's phenomenology of boredom from *The Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics — World, Finitude, Solitude* (1929/30), with supporting reference to *Being and Time*'s treatment of *Befindlichkeit* and *What is Metaphysics?*'s treatment of anxiety and nothingness, as a resource for education (FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.255). His governing wager is that boredom's phenomenology contains "transformative potentialities" that can "illuminate our cultural situation as a whole," and that turning away from boredom is, in his own phrase, always also a "turning away from ourselves" (FE, p.255) — a formula stated in the abstract and cashed out across the paper. The paper opens by setting Heidegger's boredom-analysis against the era's favored contrast term, Csikszentmihalyi's "flow" — "a feeling of wholeness and full involvement" (FE, p.255) — before asking whether classroom boredom is best read as a lack of student interest, a teachers' failure to awaken interest, or an existential matter of self-relation (FE, p.255). The paper's organizing move, developed across its central section, is to read Heidegger's three phenomenologically distinguished forms of boredom (situative/first, self-forming-emptiness/second, profound/third) as correlating with three modes of (un)meaningful learning, before turning, in a final movement, to profound boredom's connection to authenticity, waiting, and a non-Platonic "Socratic Method" of teaching (FE, pp.262–266).

## 2. Structure

1. **Introduction** (p.255–256) — Opens on the flow/boredom contrast (Csikszentmihalyi 1990) and the everyday, evaluative sense in which boredom and interest function as a *coincidentia oppositorum* registering how situations "matter to us" (FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.255–256); raises the pedagogical and existential questions (lack-of-interest vs. teacher failure vs. self-responsibility) that the rest of the paper answers via Heidegger.
2. **The Concept of Boredom** (p.256–258) — Traces a conceptual history: the medieval *acedia* as a spiritual disorder of "too little love" (Aquinas via Raposa, FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.256–257); the romantic "expressive subject" for whom boredom signals a distorted relation to the infinite (Svendsen, FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.257); the modern reduction of boredom to an individual psychological/physiological disorder, which Mansikka rejects as unable to explain boredom's intimate relation to *world*-experience (FE, p.257); and the reframing of boredom as a strictly relational concept — nothing is intrinsically boring — tied to the experience of (lack of) meaningfulness (FAITHFUL-Mansikka citing Raposa, p.257–258).
3. **Moods as a Fundamental Way of Being-in-the-World** (p.258–259) — Establishes *Stimmung* (mood/attunement) and *Befindlichkeit* (state-of-mind) as existentials that disclose Dasein's Being-in-the-world "as a whole" prior to any subject/object split, quoting Heidegger's definition of mood as a "way" that "sets the tone" for being (FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.258); notes that anxiety (*Being and Time*), nothingness (*What is Metaphysics?*), and boredom (FCM) are Heidegger's three sustained analyses of *fundamental* moods, each disclosing Being-in-the-world as a whole (FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.258–259, footnote 3); observes that "philosophy in each case happens in a fundamental mood [*Grundstimmung*]" (FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.258–259).
4. **Heidegger and the Experience of Boredom** (p.259–262) — A compressed, three-part exposition, each part closed by Mansikka's own two-row Being/Time diagram reproducing Heidegger's structural moments: (a) *situative* (first-form) boredom — the railway-station example, being held in delay by dragging time and left empty by things that offer nothing (FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.259–260); (b) *self-forming-emptiness* (second-form) boredom — the evening-invitation example, in which boredom "becomes more and more concentrated on us," time is spent and lost simultaneously, and the "now" stretches without a past or future (FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.260–261); (c) *profound* (third-form) boredom — indifference that "makes everything of equally great and equally little worth," the "telling refusal" of beings as a whole, and the rupturing *Augenblick* Mansikka explicitly ties to Kierkegaard (FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.261–262).
5. **Three Ways of (not) Learning and Being Bored** (p.262–266) — Mansikka's own extension, in three named subsections: **(a) A Learning that is Not Meaningful** (p.262–263) maps first-form boredom onto technical, "objectivistic" instruction that brackets lived experience (drawing on *Being and Time*'s account of theoretical looking, and on Egan's account of enthusiasm and wonder, FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.263); **(b) A Learning that is Meaningful but Leaves Us Empty** (p.263–264) maps second-form boredom onto engaged-yet-inauthentic learning — the student who is fully occupied, even high-achieving, yet governed by *das Man* (the "they-self") rather than an authentic self-relation (FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.264); **(c) Beyond Learning and Waiting** (p.265–266) reaches for profound boredom's educational potential via the recovered "essence of paideia" (quoted from Heidegger via Thomson 2003, p.265), Biesta's account of the educator's "responsibility for what is to come," Dasein's *waiting* as a keeping-to-itself, and a "modern version of the Socratic Method... without platonic metaphysical assumptions" (FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.265–266), closing with Fromm's account of Calvinist activity as escape from anxiety (FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.266).
6. **Epilogue** (p.266–268) — Surveys a shared Christian/Buddhist ambivalence about indifference and emptiness (Raposa, FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.266–267); reads profound boredom, via Agamben, as Heidegger's account of human "anthropogenesis," and via Svendsen as boredom's function as "an initiation to metaphysics" (FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.267); connects Heidegger's implicit "counter-education" (Gur-Ze'ev) to Foucauldian accounts of circulating power and to critical pedagogy (Giroux & McLaren) and Maxine Greene's "opening the world" (FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.267); closes with a cultural critique of the entertainment industry's continuous "driving away" of boredom, quoting Heidegger's diagnosis of contemporary Dasein's "absence of distress" and "smug contentment in not being endangered" (FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.267–268), and insists the resulting emancipation is effortful and non-linear, requiring both waiting and openness to interruption (FE, p.268).

## 3. Constructs

- **profound/deep boredom (FCM 3rd form)** — genuinely and substantially **treated**: exposited at p.261–262 (indifference, telling refusal, *Augenblick*) and returned to as the pedagogical center of gravity at p.265–268 (paideia, waiting, the entertainment-industry critique, the closing "smug contentment" passage).
- **being-bored-by (1st form)** — genuinely **treated**: the railway-station exposition (p.259–260) and its educational transposition onto meaningless/technical instruction (p.262–263).
- **being-bored-with (2nd form)** — genuinely **treated**: the evening-invitation exposition (p.260–261) and its educational transposition onto engaged-but-inauthentic, "they-self"-governed learning (p.263–264).
- **situational vs. trait/state boredom** — a **false friend**, flagged rather than counted: Mansikka's own label "situative boredom" for the first form invites confusion with this psychometric state/trait distinction, but his contrast is strictly phenomenological (bound to a determinate situation vs. arising from Dasein itself), never an individual-differences or state-vs-trait claim; no boredom-proneness measure or population comparison appears anywhere in the source.
- **boredom proneness** — **not treated**; no psychometric or individual-differences claim of any kind appears.
- **mind-wandering / task-unrelated thought** — a **false friend**: "slipping away from ourselves toward whatever is happening" (p.260–261, second form) superficially resembles attentional drift, but Mansikka's own point (following Heidegger) is that this is a mode of self-abandonment to *das Man*, an ontological-existential claim about authenticity, not a claim about task-unrelated cognitive content.
- **attentional disengagement / meta-awareness** — **not treated**; the source's register is ontological/existential throughout, never attentional-cognitive.
- **boredom-as-regulatory-signal (functional account)** — genuinely, if loosely, **treated as a philosophical analog**: Mansikka repeatedly frames boredom — especially its profound form — as something that "wishes to tell us something... not something arbitrary or contingent" (FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.265) and that has "transformative potentialities" (FE, p.255); this functions rhetorically as a signal/disclosure account, but it is an existential-hermeneutic claim about Dasein's self-relation, not an empirical adaptive- or regulatory-function claim from affective science — flagged here so the register difference is not lost in future concordance work.
- **flow / engagement (contrast pole)** — genuinely and **explicitly treated**: Csikszentmihalyi's *Flow: The Psychology of Optimal Experience* (1990) is cited by name in the paper's own opening paragraph as boredom's named contrast (FE, p.255) — the source's own abstract signals this contrast, per this cluster's task brief.
- **monotony / meaninglessness** — genuinely and substantially **treated**: dull, technical, "objectivistic" educational situations that reduce phenomena to abstractions (p.262–263); Egan's "desiccated and devoid of energy and life" (quoted, FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.263); the entertainment-industry critique of constant, meaning-flattening amusement (p.267–268).
- **temporality of boredom (time-drag)** — genuinely and substantially **treated**: the *Langeweile* ("long while") etymology (p.259); dragging time and being held in delay (p.259–260); the "stretched now" without past or future (p.260–261); entrancement and the *Augenblick* (p.261–262).

## 4. Reading of FCM

Mansikka's relation to FCM is **primarily expository and faithful, with one genuine extending move**. Across "Heidegger and the Experience of Boredom" (p.259–262) he compresses Part One, Chapters Two through Four of the 1929/30 lecture course (fcm-04, fcm-05, fcm-06) into roughly three journal pages, reproducing — with his own two-row Being/Time diagrams — the same two structural moments (*Leergelassenheit*/being-left-empty and *Hingehaltenheit*/being-held-in-delay) that fcm-04 identifies as the "structural primitives" inherited and transformed by fcm-05 and fcm-06. The railway-station example (p.259–260) is Heidegger's own example as treated in fcm-04 §23a–d; the evening-invitation example and its "self-forming emptiness" (p.260–261) is Heidegger's own example as treated in fcm-05 §24b–§27; the "telling refusal," total indifference, and rupturing *Augenblick* (p.261–262) is Heidegger's own material as treated in fcm-06 §29–§34, with Mansikka's own gloss ("Kierkegaard's conception of *Augenblick*," FE, p.261–262) matching fcm-06 §32b's explicit crediting of Kierkegaard. None of this is contested by Mansikka; it is a compressed, accurate paraphrase (FAITHFUL-Mansikka throughout).

The paper's second, deeper reach into FCM is easy to miss because it is not signaled as a return to the same lecture course. To frame his central pedagogical section, Mansikka quotes Heidegger's complaint that education concerns itself only with "learned competencies that can be instilled," which can never substitute for genuine "strength and power" (FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.264–265, quoting Heidegger 1995, p.164). To close the Epilogue, he quotes Heidegger's diagnosis of a contemporary "absence of distress," attested by a "smug contentment in not being endangered" pervading our busy self-organizing (FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.267–268, quoting Heidegger 1995, pp.164–166). Both passages are drawn verbatim from the same page range (GA pp.244–46) that fcm-07 identifies as Chapter Five §38a's keystone diagnosis of contemporary Dasein's absence of essential oppressiveness. Mansikka thus reaches past the three-forms typology (fcm-04–06) into fcm-07's material on the *particular* profound boredom of *our* Dasein — without naming Chapter Five as such — to underwrite his own claims that the entertainment/amusement industry and a competency-accumulation model of education both function as forms of busy activity that suppress profound boredom's disclosive potential (P/anticipatory-application: this identification of the exact fcm-07 passage is our own cross-reference, not a claim Mansikka makes about "Chapter Five" by name).

Mansikka's one genuine **extension** of the FCM material, rather than paraphrase of it, is the three-part isomorphism he proposes between Heidegger's boredom-typology and three modes of (un)meaningful learning (§5 above) — a THEORY-MODEL that is his own pedagogical contribution, not present in Heidegger's text, though it is presented as a natural application ("I think it is possible to discern an interesting correlation here," FE, p.262).

## 5. Findings/Claims

- Boredom and interest are evaluative registers of how situations, events, and activities "matter to us," applicable to educational situations no less than any other (FE, p.255–256).
- Boredom is a strictly relational, not intrinsic, property — nothing is boring "in itself" — a claim Mansikka draws from Raposa to set up Heidegger's Dasein-analytic (FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.257–258).
- Fundamental moods are existentials that disclose Being-in-the-world "as a whole" prior to any subject/object split; they are not "added" to experience after the fact but make experience possible in the first place (FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.258).
- Heidegger's three forms of boredom progressively deepen the same two structural moments (being-left-empty, being-held-in-delay) toward pure temporality, culminating in profound boredom's "telling refusal" of beings as a whole (FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.259–262).
- [Mansikka's own extension] There is a workable "correlation" between the three forms of boredom and three modes of (un)meaningful learning: meaningless/technical rote instruction (form 1), engaged-but-inauthentic performance-learning under *das Man* (form 2), and profound boredom as a possible site of pedagogical rupture toward authenticity (form 3) (FE, p.262–266).
- An objectivistic, purely theoretical educational stance that brackets lived experience can *increase* rather than dissolve meaninglessness and boredom (FE drawing on *Being and Time*, p.262–263).
- A student can be outwardly exemplary — fully engaged, high-achieving — while remaining inauthentic in Heidegger's technical sense, because *das Man* rather than the student's own self is making the decisions that matter (FE, p.264).
- Mansikka explicitly disclaims prescribing induced profound boredom as an educational method; his claim is only that education's over-arching goal should cultivate a "responsive hermeneutic receptivity" leading students back to themselves (FE, p.265).
- Genuine education, on Heidegger's gloss of Plato's *paideia* (quoted via Thomson 2003), "lays hold of the soul itself" rather than depositing information into an empty vessel (FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.265).
- The contemporary entertainment/amusement industry functions as a structural mechanism for continuously "driving away" boredom, foreclosing the very possibility of confronting one's "innermost possibilities" (FE, p.267–268).
- Heidegger's own diagnosis of contemporary Dasein's "absence of distress," masked by "smug contentment in not being endangered" (FCM Ch.5/§38a; cf. fcm-07), directly underwrites Mansikka's educational-cultural critique of busyness and entertainment (FAITHFUL-Mansikka, p.267–268).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* Mansikka's three-part typology — meaningless/technical instruction, engaged-but-inauthentic performance-learning, and profound boredom as rupture — is the most directly usable diagnostic frame Strand B offers Part III's student-boredom material: it gives the dissertation vocabulary for distinguishing a VLE/classroom boredom rooted in dull, competency-accumulation content from a *different*, harder-to-detect boredom in which a student is fully "engaged" (submitting work, participating, even excelling) while that engagement is governed by *das Man* — performing for the LMS or the grade rather than out of any authentic self-relation to the material *(anticipatory-application)*. This second form is the more analytically valuable of the two for Part III, since it names exactly the kind of boredom that behavioral-engagement metrics (time-on-task, submission rates, forum posts) would systematically fail to detect, precisely because engagement and inauthentic hollow-boredom are, on Mansikka's reading, compatible rather than opposed *(anticipatory-application)*.

Second, Mansikka's closing critique of the entertainment/amusement industry as a mechanism for continuously "driving away" boredom bears directly, though by extension rather than by the source's own argument, on any VLE or courseware design that leans on gamification or engagement-maximizing features: such features could, on this reading, function exactly like Heidegger's "smug contentment" busyness — closing off, rather than opening, the rupture profound boredom might otherwise afford *(anticipatory-application)*. This is our own extension of Mansikka's cultural critique to instructional-design contexts; Mansikka himself never discusses VLEs, gamification, or any specific technology *(anticipatory-application)*.
