# bor-sec-13 — Slaby, "The Other Side of Existence: Heidegger on Boredom" (2010)

**Type:** book-chapter-length secondary reading (philosophy of emotion) · **Discipline:** philosophy (phenomenology, philosophy of emotion/affectivity) · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** Strand A's heaviest single-source FCM bridge — a sustained, chapter-length reconstruction of all three Heideggerian forms of boredom (unlike bor-sec-12, Slaby's later and more general "Living in the Moment"), plus one explicit, source-authored critical departure from Heidegger's own conclusion.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Slaby's chapter proposes moods and emotions as "the other side of cognition" — indispensable, still under-theorized ingredients of thought and evaluation rather than reason's antagonists — and argues that a single, carefully chosen case (boredom) can carry this larger claim about human affectivity better than a survey of many (FE, p.101). His guiding thesis is that Heidegger's 1929/30 lecture course *The Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics* (FCM) offers "a remarkable existential interpretation" of three varieties of boredom that reveals an inextricable bond between the temporality of Dasein and "existential significance" — such that the draining-away of meaning in boredom just *is* the strange slowing or halting of time (FE, p.101–102). Slaby's own methodological aim is to reconstruct this analysis as a paradigm case of "affective intentionality" — feelings as a passive-yet-proto-conceptual "sense of possibility" that discloses the world as a space of possibilities we are oriented toward or away from (FE, p.102–103). A second, more limited aim, executed only in the closing section, is to register a friendly dissent from the final stage of Heidegger's interpretation, on the grounds that it too aggressively imposes a specific, activist conception of existence onto profound boredom (FE, p.103).

## 2. Structure

The chapter has four sections, unlabeled by number in the running text but internally announced.

1. **Unheaded introduction** (pp.101–104) — states the "other side of cognition" thesis; previews Heidegger's tripartite boredom analysis and its temporality/significance payoff; introduces "affective intentionality" as the chapter's own theoretical lens (self-citing Slaby, *Gefühl und Weltbezug*, 2008, and Slaby & Stephan, "Affective Intentionality," 2008).
2. **"From states of consciousness to existential orientations"** (pp.103–107) — develops the general theoretical vocabulary (existential orientations, following Ratcliffe's "feelings of being") through which the boredom analysis will be read; glosses Heidegger's *Befindlichkeit* (attunement) as this same middle-ground structure, joined inseparably to understanding (*Verstehen*); introduces the Aristotelian *hexis*/German *Haltung* analogy for actively "holding oneself" in an orientation.
3. **"Heidegger on Boredom"** (pp.106–117) — the chapter's core, in three numbered subsections:
   - **3.1 "Becoming bored by something"** (pp.107–108) — the first form; the railway-station and boring-book examples; being held in limbo and left empty by a determinate boring item.
   - **3.2 "Being bored with something"** (pp.108–112) — the second form; the evening-invitation example; the self-forming emptiness of casual, freely chosen engagement; the "standing Now" (*stehendes Jetzt*) and the collapse of past/future into an isolated present.
   - **3.3 "Profound Boredom"** (pp.112–117) — the third form; the "undifferentiated no one"; the total, situation-independent indifference of world and self; the "telling refusal" (*Versagen*) of beings as itself a "telling" (*Sagen*) that announces Dasein's withheld possibilities; the entrancement by a fused temporal horizon; the call to resolute self-disclosure in the "moment of vision" (*Augenblick*) and to freedom.
4. **"Concluding remarks"** (pp.117–120) — recapitulates the three forms' escalating depth; then pivots to critique: Heidegger's demand that profound boredom's "message" be answered by resolute, meaning-creating action is judged an unargued imposition of an activist ideal, illustrated by counter-passages from Fernando Pessoa's *Book of Disquiet* depicting an affirmed, undemanding dwelling within depersonalized detachment.

## 3. Constructs

- **profound/deep boredom (FCM 3rd form)** — the chapter's culminating and most extensively treated construct (pp.112–117): total, impersonal, situation-independent; the self as "undifferentiated no one," world as wholesale meaninglessness, their relation as a mode of "unrelatedness" (FAITHFUL-Slaby, p.113).
- **being-bored-by (1st form)** — treated at full length in §3.1 (pp.107–108) via the railway-station/boring-book examples, defined as being held in limbo and left empty by a determinate external item that withholds an expected fulfilment (FAITHFUL-Slaby, p.107–108).
- **being-bored-with (2nd form)** — treated at full length in §3.2 (pp.108–112) via the evening-invitation example; distinguished from the first form by the emptiness being "of our own making" and by the transformation of time into a "standing Now" (FAITHFUL-Slaby, p.109–111).
- **situational vs. trait/state boredom** — approached analogically rather than in the psychological literature's own terms: Slaby's "existential orientation" is explicitly positioned as a middle ground between dispositional character traits and momentary foreground intentional states (pp.103–104) (FE). This three-way scheme does not map one-to-one onto the psychological situational-state/trait-proneness binary; treating it as illustrative rather than equivalent (P/anticipatory-application).
- **attentional disengagement / meta-awareness** — Dasein's default mode is "being-away" (*Weg-sein*): non-attentive, absent-minded absorption, only rarely interrupted by lucid "being-there"; boredom's default tendency toward being-away is said to be especially powerful, provoking active flight into distraction (pp.106–107) (FAITHFUL-Slaby, p.106–107).
- **boredom-as-regulatory-signal (functional account)** — present only in an attenuated, non-Elpidorou form: Heidegger's claim that beings' "telling refusal" is itself a "telling" that discloses Dasein's own possibilities functions as a proto-signal account (boredom as bearing information), which Slaby reconstructs faithfully but ultimately judges over-extended when it licenses a demand for resolute action (pp.114–118) (FAITHFUL-Slaby, qualified by Slaby's own critique). This is a productive point of contact with, not an endorsement of, the functional-account literature (P/anticipatory-application, see debate-axis edge to bor-sec-25/26).
- **monotony / meaninglessness** — central throughout: "the significance of the world and the self drain away" in boredom generally (p.101–102); intensified across the three forms from a locally boring item, to a freely chosen but existentially irrelevant evening, to the "wholesale meaninglessness of world" in the third form (FAITHFUL-Slaby, p.101, 113).
- **temporality of boredom (time-drag)** — the chapter's connective thread: boredom as *Langeweile*, literally a "long while," points toward time as what the fundamental questions of metaphysics are tied to (p.102); each form is read via its own temporal signature — dragging time (1st), the standing Now (2nd), and the fused, entranced threefold horizon (3rd) (FAITHFUL-Slaby, p.102, 111, 115–116).

Not treated: boredom proneness (self-report trait scale), mind-wandering/task-unrelated thought, and flow/engagement — none of these cognitive-science constructs appear in this phenomenological reconstruction, which stays within Heidegger's own idiom throughout its expository sections.

## 4. Reading of FCM

Slaby's §3.1 (pp.107–108) reconstructs fcm-04-first-form's own material at high fidelity: the railway-station example, the twin structural moments (rendered as "held in limbo" and "left empty"), and the definition of the boring as what postpones an expected fulfilment. This is compressed restatement of FCM §§19–23 (fcm-04 §§3–8), not an extension (FAITHFUL-Slaby, p.107–108).

Slaby's §3.2 (pp.108–112) restates fcm-05-second-form's §§24–28 apparatus at comparable fidelity, contributing one clarifying gloss: the second form's emptiness is explicitly named "of our own making" because it originates in a freely chosen engagement (the dinner party) rather than an externally imposed situation (the train delay) (FAITHFUL-Slaby, p.109–110). This sharpens, in explicit free-choice/externally-imposed terms, what fcm-05 itself states more abstractly — that the boring "does not come from outside" but "arises from out of Dasein itself" (fcm-05 §8, C12, p.128/GA pp.192-94/pdf 76) — a minor interpretive sharpening rather than a departure.

Slaby's §3.3 (pp.112–117) restates fcm-06-third-form's core sequence — the undifferentiated no one, total indifference of world and self, the "telling refusal is itself a telling," entrancement by the threefold temporal horizon, and freedom announced in refusal — at high fidelity, then draws out its practical payoff into the explicit vocabulary of a "call" and a "message… forced upon us" (p.115–116), closely tracking fcm-06's own "telling announcement (*Ansagen*) of possibilities left unexploited" (fcm-06 §4, p.141/GA p.212/pdf 82).

Slaby's concluding critique (§4, pp.117–119) is where the chapter stops restating and starts contesting — and what it contests belongs to fcm-07-particular-profound's territory rather than fcm-06's. Heidegger's insistence that failing to heed profound boredom's "message" is a falling-short, and that Dasein is thereby called to resolute self-disclosure, sits in the same activist-diagnostic register as fcm-07's own question — "has man in the end become boring to himself?" — and its demand that Dasein "resolutely open himself again" to the "moment of vision" (fcm-07 §5, p.165/GA pp.246-47/pdf 94). Slaby's own page citations never reach FCM's chapter five (his citations top out at p.224/149, within fcm-06's range), so the fcm-07 connection is this unit's own extrapolation, not Slaby's explicit citation: Slaby's objection to Heidegger's "activist ideal" functions as an implicit rejection of the very demand fcm-07 formalizes at the level of "contemporary Dasein," even though Slaby's argument is textually pitched against fcm-06's closing pages (P/anticipatory-application).

## 5. Findings/Claims

- Boredom is best understood not as a subjective feeling-state but as an "existential orientation" — a background structure encompassing experience, thought, volition, and action, distinct from both character traits and momentary foreground intentional states (FE, p.103–104).
- Heidegger's *Befindlichkeit* (attunement) is not a generic label for affective states but names precisely this existential-orientation structure, inseparable from understanding (*Verstehen*): "each attunement is understanding and each understanding has its attunement" (FAITHFUL-Slaby, p.105–106).
- The three forms of boredom differ along both situation-relatedness and temporal structure: the first is bound to a determinate boring item and dragging clock-time; the second's emptiness is self-formed through a freely chosen engagement and collapses time into a "standing Now"; the third is total, impersonal, and decoupled from any situation, entrancing the whole threefold temporal horizon (FE, p.107–116).
- Heidegger's culminating claim is that beings' "telling refusal" in profound boredom is itself "a telling, i.e. a making manifest" — it announces the very possibilities of Dasein that it withholds, and ultimately "the freedom of Dasein as such" (FAITHFUL-Slaby, p.114–116, quoting Heidegger's own "all withholding is a telling… making manifest").
- This disclosure culminates in a call to resolute self-disclosure in the "moment of vision" (*Augenblick*), through which Dasein is said to be the free, responsible creator of whatever meaning its life has (FAITHFUL-Slaby, p.116).
- Slaby's own critical departure: Heidegger's final move too aggressively forces a specific, activist understanding of existence onto profound boredom, implicitly construing any failure to answer its "call" as inauthentic without adequately arguing for that verdict (FE, p.117–118) — this is Slaby's own contestation of Heidegger, not this unit's extrapolation.
- Pessoa's *Book of Disquiet* is mobilized as a counter-exemplar: a literary stance that dwells affirmatively within depersonalized, meaningless detachment — "to be no more, have no more, want no more" — without answering any call back to resolute, meaning-creating action (FAITHFUL-Slaby, p.118–119).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* As the cluster's most heavily FCM-bridged single source, this unit is Part III's best available compact restatement of all three forms in one place, useful whenever the dissertation needs a citable, faithful gloss of "held in limbo," "left empty," the "standing Now," or the "undifferentiated no one" without re-deriving them from FCM directly *(anticipatory-application)*. Slaby's "existential orientation" vocabulary — a background structure distinct from both trait and foreground state — offers Part III a way to describe default modes of student attentional withdrawal (the VLE's baseline absorption/non-attentiveness) without forcing that description into the psychological state/trait-proneness binary that Strand C's instruments use; the two vocabularies should be kept explicitly distinct rather than silently merged when both appear in the same paragraph *(anticipatory-application)*. Slaby's account of Dasein's default "being-away" and its active flight into distraction (p.106–107) supplies phenomenological vocabulary for describing why passing-time behaviors (fidgeting, glancing, side-conversation) might co-occur with — rather than contradict — an underlying attunement of boredom, a caution directly relevant to Part III's own reconciliation of self-report boredom with observed on-task behavior *(anticipatory-application)*. Most consequentially, Slaby's own critical distancing from Heidegger's activist reading of profound boredom's "message" is a philosophically independent version of the caution Elpidorou's regulatory-account units (bor-sec-25/bor-sec-26, not yet built in this cluster) will supply from cognitive science: that treating boredom as necessarily *demanding* corrective action risks over-reading a signal whose disclosive content may be more modest, or differently valenced, than a call to resolute self-transformation — a debate-axis worth carrying forward into Strand A/C synthesis rather than resolving in this unit *(anticipatory-application)*. Finally, because this chapter (2010) predates Slaby's own "Living in the Moment" (2017, bor-sec-12) by seven years, any claim that this unit's temporality analysis "anticipates" or is "extended by" the later paper is this cluster's own forward-linking construction, not a citation Slaby himself could have made *(anticipatory-application)*.
