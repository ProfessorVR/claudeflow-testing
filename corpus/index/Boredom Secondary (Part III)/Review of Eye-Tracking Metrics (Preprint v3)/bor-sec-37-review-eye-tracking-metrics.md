# bor-sec-37 — Skaramagkas, Giannakakis, Ktistakis, Manousos, Karatzanis, Tachos, Tripoliti, Marias, Fotiadis & Tsiknakis, "Review of Eye Tracking Metrics Involved in Emotional and Cognitive Processes" (preprint v3, n.d.)

**Type:** STEM literature review (biomedical signal processing / affective computing / eye-tracking methodology) — surveys eye- and pupil-behaviour metrics across the published literature, without conducting an original experiment · **Discipline:** biomedical engineering, HCI · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** Strand E — an instrument atlas rather than a boredom source. The review's own target constructs are visual attention, emotional arousal/stress, and cognitive workload; the word "boredom" never appears in the text, nor do "mind-wandering," "monotony," "meaninglessness," or any Heideggerian vocabulary. Its cluster value is almost entirely as a `grounds-measure` bridge to the not-yet-built Boredom Experiment (VR Attention Study) dataset entry's eye-tracking channel(s), and as a citation-network node connecting to two other Strand E units (bor-sec-34, bor-sec-36) via shared authorship.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

The authors — a FORTH (Heraklion)/University of Ioannina biomedical-engineering group — identify a gap parallel to, but broader than, the boredom-specific literature elsewhere in this cluster: eye and pupil behaviour is mediated by the autonomic nervous system just as biosignals (EEG, EDA, ECG) are, yet it "has remained in the research background" relative to those modalities (FE, ~p.1). The review's stated aim is threefold: (1) identify which eye/pupil metrics show a significant relation to visual attention, emotional arousal, and cognitive workload; (2) determine efficient computational recognition methods based on those metrics; and (3) specify the most robust combined feature set for multimodal approaches (FAITHFUL-Skaramagkas et al., ~p.2). The authors are explicit that no single eye feature discriminates cleanly among the three processes, which is precisely why a comprehensive collection-and-comparison exercise is needed (FE, ~p.2). The review restricts its scope to studies of "urban environments and daily life activities" (driving, reading, working on a PC), rather than laboratory-only paradigms (FAITHFUL-Skaramagkas et al., ~p.2).

## 3. Constructs

The controlled construct axis maps onto this source only through interpretive bridging, since the review itself never uses boredom vocabulary. Treated honestly:

- **profound/deep boredom, being-bored-by, being-bored-with (FCM forms), boredom proneness, boredom-as-regulatory-signal, temporality of boredom** — **not treated.** No Heidegger, no trait-boredom scale, no functional/regulatory framing of any affective state, no discussion of time-drag. These constructs simply do not appear in this source *(P/anticipatory-application: their absence is itself worth recording, since it distinguishes this unit's contribution as purely instrumental rather than conceptual)*.
- **attentional disengagement / meta-awareness** — the closest genuine kinship in the source. The review's entire "visual attention" construct (Section II-A, elaborated across Section V-A) is operationalized via exactly the eye metrics that boredom researchers elsewhere in this cluster use to index disengagement: fixation count/duration, gaze dispersion, scan-path/saliency deviation, and blink rate tied to "attentional engagement" (Maffei & Angrilli, cited at ~p.6, ~p.9) *(P/anticipatory-application — the source's own object is general visual attention, not boredom-specific disengagement, but the metric vocabulary is identical)*.
- **situational vs. trait/state boredom** — not treated by name, but every process the review surveys (arousal, workload, attention) is measured as a momentary, stimulus-or-task-bound condition within single sessions, never as a stable trait; this is a structural analogue to state-boredom framing found elsewhere in the cluster (e.g., bor-sec-28) *(P/anticipatory-application, thin)*.
- **mind-wandering / task-unrelated thought** — not named, but the review's scan-path, saliency, and "center bias" literature (Section V-A) implicitly defines what on-task visual engagement with a stimulus looks like, which is the baseline against which mind-wandering-driven gaze deviation would register *(P/anticipatory-application, thin — inferred baseline, not a direct treatment)*.
- **flow / engagement (contrast pole)** — appears once, incidentally: blink-rate decrease is linked to "attentional engagement and mental workload" (Maffei & Angrilli 2019, [39], ~p.4). No theorization of flow or engagement as a state in its own right *(P/anticipatory-application, thin)*.
- **monotony / meaninglessness** — not named as such, but the review does report a "habituation effect" during repeated in-vehicle-information-system (IVIS) task exposure, where average pupil size rose specifically on first exposure to a repeated condition (~p.9) — a physiological signature adjacent to, though not equivalent to, monotony-driven habituation *(P/anticipatory-application, thin)*.

## 4. Measures & methods

This section is the review's real center of gravity, and the reason it belongs to Strand E rather than any construct-driven strand. **Fixation metrics** (Section III-A, V throughout): number of fixations, fixations per area of interest (AOI), total fixation count, fixation duration and total fixation duration, time-to-first-fixation, fixation density, repeat fixations, and Krejtz et al.'s coefficient K (ratio of fixation duration to subsequent saccade amplitude, distinguishing focal from ambient scanning, ~p.6). **Saccadic metrics** (Section III-B-D): number of saccades, saccadic velocity/amplitude, saccade rate/duration; microsaccade rate and amplitude (a fixational eye movement studied for covert-attention tracking); smooth pursuit gain (maintained gaze on a moving target, disrupted by fatigue, alcohol, or distraction). **Pupil metrics** (Section III-E): pupil diameter/area variation, governed by sympathetic/parasympathetic antagonism and reflecting "autonomic involuntary activity" tied to emotional, cognitive, or sexual arousal (~p.3) — but the review devotes unusual care to pupil-metric reliability confounds: illumination sensitivity, ambient humidity/temperature, age-related baseline reduction, and camera position/angle (~p.3-4), recommending centered stimuli and normal-state baselining as best practice. **Blink metrics** (Section III-F): blink rate (blinks/min), blink duration (0.1-0.4 sec average), and — introduced later, in the multimodal Table IV — Percentage of Eyelid Closure (PERCLOS) and Average Eye Closure Speed (AECS).

Apparatus-wise, Section IV surveys eye-tracking hardware generations: electrooculography (EOG, contact-electrode, historically first); photo/videooculography; scleral contact lens/search coil; and video-based tracking (now dominant), itself split into 2D regression (pupil-centre/corneal-glint mapping, accuracy 0.8-8°), 3D model-based (single- or multi-camera corneal/optical-axis reconstruction), and cross-ratio methods (NIR-LED projection geometry). Head-mounted, desktop, and mobile form factors are all documented, with head-mounted (glasses-form) flagged as the emerging popular option. Pupil-area estimation methods reviewed range from classical thresholding and dual-state open/closed eye models (Tian et al.) to a CNN-based segmentation approach (~p.5).

Section VI turns from metrics to **recognition/classification methods**: the review tabulates roughly fourteen studies (Table IV) that classify emotional-arousal or cognitive-workload states from eye features alone or eye features plus biosignals (EEG, EDA, GSR, HR/HRV, skin temperature). Classifiers surveyed include SVM (dominant), Random Forest, Naive Bayes, Decision Tree, AdaBoost, Bagging Trees, Gaussian Mixture Models, k-NN, ANN, and Deep Belief Networks. Section VII then surveys roughly a dozen public datasets (Table V) — EMOd, ETMD, EGTEA Gaze+/GTEA Gaze+, MAMEM Phase I, DOVES, MIT CSAIL, MIT CVCL, USC CRCNS, SFU ETDB, UT Multi-view, EyeC3D, ErrP — comparing subject counts, stimuli, and which eye features (2D gaze, fixations, saccades, blinks, pupil diameter) each one actually provides.

## 5. Findings/Claims

- **Visual attention (Table I):** fixation number, fixation duration, and total fixation time are the most robust and consistently significant (p<.01) indicators of increased visual attention across cited studies; saccade amplitude and microsaccade rate are also useful; blink rate decreases with attentional focus; pupil size shows only scattered, less consistent involvement (FAITHFUL-Skaramagkas et al., ~p.6-7).
- **Emotional arousal/stress (Table II):** pupil size increase is "the most common indicator for detecting emotional arousal," appearing in nearly every cited study; blink duration also increases with arousal; most other metrics (fixation duration, gaze distribution, dwell time) show inconsistent (non-significant) patterns across studies, and the authors explicitly caution that detecting — let alone quantifying — arousal from pupil diameter alone is "questionable" (FAITHFUL-Skaramagkas et al., ~p.7-8).
- **Cognitive workload (Table III):** unlike arousal, a wider variety of metrics show reliable involvement — fixation number increases and fixation duration decreases with workload; saccadic velocity, saccade rate, and microsaccade amplitude increase; pupil size increases and its standard deviation also increases; blink rate, blink duration, and microsaccade rate tend to decrease (FAITHFUL-Skaramagkas et al., ~p.9-10).
- **Classification accuracy (Table IV):** eye-feature-only classifiers (5 of ~14 studies for emotion; 5 of ~14 for workload) average 71.5% accuracy for emotional-arousal discrimination (best 93%, a depression-detection binary task using blink metrics alone) and 78.64% for cognitive-workload discrimination (best 99.5%); adding biosignals (chiefly EEG) raises average accuracy to 81.76% (emotion) and 87.55% (workload) — roughly a ten-point gain — with SVM the most frequent best-performing classifier in both domains (FAITHFUL-Skaramagkas et al., ~p.11-12).
- **Datasets (Table V):** the vast majority of public eye-behaviour datasets provide only 2D gaze coordinates; only two of the surveyed datasets include pupil diameter (ETMD, EGTEA Gaze+), and only one includes blink-related features (EGTEA Gaze+); no dataset spans all three target processes simultaneously, a gap the authors flag as an open call for future dataset construction (FAITHFUL-Skaramagkas et al., ~p.12-14).
- **Discussion synthesis:** pupil size and blink rate are the two features most implicated in emotional-arousal detection; fixation-related metrics dominate visual-attention detection; pupil size, fixation number, and saccadic velocity are the most reliable positive indicators of cognitive workload. The authors explicitly name a research gap: none of the surveyed studies attempts joint discrimination *between* emotional and cognitive processes (rather than levels within one process), and result generalizability is limited by small, heterogeneous samples and tasks (FAITHFUL-Skaramagkas et al., ~p.14-15).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* This unit's principal cluster function is as a `grounds-measure` bridge candidate for the not-yet-built Boredom Experiment (VR Attention Study) dataset entry's eye-tracking channel(s), given its unusually comprehensive treatment of pupillometry, gaze/fixation dispersion, and blink metrics as a triad — precisely the three eye-based channels a VR attention study would need to instrument. However, none of its apparatus discussion is VR-specific: the video-based, 2D-regression/3D-model/cross-ratio gaze-estimation methods it surveys (Section IV) are screen- or head-mounted-glasses systems, not integrated VR-headset eye trackers, so the specific hardware fit remains unconfirmed until the dataset entry specifies its own instrumentation *(P/anticipatory-application)*.

*(anticipatory-application)* The review's explicit pupil-reliability caveats — illumination sensitivity, ambient temperature/humidity, age-related baseline shift, and camera-angle dependency (~p.3-4) — are a direct design caution for any future VR-based boredom study using headset-integrated pupillometry: a VR headset's internal IR illumination and enclosed optical path may substitute for, rather than eliminate, the ambient-lighting confound this review flags for screen-based setups, meaning the confound is likely to persist in altered form rather than disappear *(P/anticipatory-application)*.

*(anticipatory-application)* The review's quantitative eye-only-versus-multimodal contrast (71.5-78.64% eye-only vs. 81.76-87.55% eye-plus-biosignal average accuracy) is a concrete design datum for Part III: if a future VR attention study aims only at binary boredom/non-boredom discrimination, eye-tracking alone may suffice at a similar accuracy ceiling to what this review reports for binary emotional/workload classification; but multiclass or graded boredom-intensity discrimination would likely require pairing eye-tracking with an additional biosignal channel (EEG or HR), echoing this cluster's Strand D EEG units' own methodological choices *(P/anticipatory-application)*.

*(anticipatory-application)* Unlike its Strand D dual-modality sibling bor-sec-28 (Kim et al.), which found no significant correlation between self-report boredom ratings and any extracted physiological feature, this review does not itself test self-report-versus-physiology convergence — but its own categorical framing of "subjective," "performance," and "physiological" measurement as three distinct, only loosely coordinated workload-assessment types (~p.2-3) is consistent with, and lends independent STEM-methodological support to, that caution against assuming convergence *(P/anticipatory-application)*.
