# Scholarly Evolution — Boredom Secondary (Part III), 2001–2025

A **thematic** arc, not a generational one: the four movements below overlap in time, and the point of the document is
that they **do not succeed one another**. The later movements did not absorb the earlier ones. They accumulated
alongside them, without contact.

Span: 2001 (Thomson) → 2025 (Yuvaraj). The date distribution is bimodal by strand, and that is itself a finding.

---

## Movement 1 · Ontological education (2001–2015)

**Units:** bor-sec-19 (Thomson 2001) · bor-sec-18 (Mansikka 2008/09) · bor-sec-07 (Gibbs 2011) · bor-sec-13 (Slaby 2010) ·
bor-sec-02, bor-sec-03 (Elpidorou & Freeman 2015)

Boredom enters this literature through **education**, not through psychology. Thomson's Heideggerian critique of the
university (2001) contains no boredom analysis at all; Mansikka (2008/09) is the first unit to put *Langeweile* and
pedagogy in the same frame, and Gibbs (2011) follows with the *Augenblick* as a pedagogical opportunity.

The characteristic move is **refusal**: Gibbs explicitly rejects the mainstream framing that boredom is a flaw solvable
by more entertaining teaching (against Breidenstein; Mann & Robinson). The movement's question is not *how do we
eliminate boredom?* but *what does boredom disclose?*

Slaby (2010) supplies the movement's most durable contribution and its self-critique simultaneously: a high-fidelity
reconstruction of all three forms, followed by an argument that Heidegger's activist conclusion is **not required by the
phenomenon** (CR-03).

Elpidorou & Freeman's two-part *Affectivity in Heidegger* (2015) closes the movement by systematizing the *Being and
Time* affectivity architecture — and, in a footnote, flagging that *Grundstimmung* appears only once in BT and becomes
central only after 1929 (DA-03). The flag is not pursued for four years.

## Movement 2 · The functional turn (2018)

**Units:** bor-sec-25, bor-sec-26 (Elpidorou 2018a/b) · bor-sec-21 (Raffaelli et al. 2018) · bor-sec-24 (Clark & Hassert 2013)

**2018 is the cluster's hinge year**, and it is essentially one author's.

Elpidorou's paired papers reframe boredom as informative and regulatory — and, crucially, **partition** the question
rather than winning it: state boredom is functional; trait boredom is a dysfunction of the same mechanism (DA-01). The
move is what lets the functional account absorb the deficit literature instead of fighting it.

Raffaelli et al.'s review lands the same year and does the opposite of resolving: it documents field-wide inconsistency
(arousal direction, fMRI regions) and declines to say whether boredom is one thing or many, recommending a taxonomy
instead (DA-06). Its **directional fractionation** hypothesis — subjective low arousal coexisting with autonomic high
arousal — is the movement's most useful gift to Part III, because it is the mechanism that makes the self-report problem
tractable rather than merely embarrassing (DA-04).

*(P/anticipatory-application)* Note what does **not** happen in 2018: the functional turn does not reach back into
Movement 1. Elpidorou cites his own phenomenological work, and Hughes (2023) will later object that the whole turn is a
*departure* from Heidegger. The bridge is built by one person, from one side, and it is contested from the other.

## Movement 3 · Neuro/eye operationalization (2007, 2018–2025)

**Units:** bor-sec-27 (Barry et al. 2007 — an outlier, and no boredom in it) · bor-sec-28, bor-sec-29 (2018) ·
bor-sec-30, bor-sec-31 (2019) · bor-sec-22 (Tam et al. 2021) · bor-sec-32 (Yakobi et al. 2021) · bor-sec-20 (Darling 2023) ·
bor-sec-33 (Yuvaraj et al. 2025) · most of Strand E

The measurement literature arrives **late and separately**. Its own internal development is real: from Kim et al.'s
threshold-based EEG+gaze detection (2018) to Seo et al.'s validated 86.73% k-NN classifier (2019) — which reuses Kim's
stimulus and is the same research programme, not an independent confirmation (CR-11) — to Yuvaraj et al.'s functional
connectivity in an educational context (2025), the cluster's most Part-III-relevant empirical study.

Darling (2023) supplies the movement's theoretical apparatus, predictive processing, and does something none of the
instrument papers do: it **dissolves** the unified/plural debate by making emotion and mood levels of one hierarchy
(DA-06).

But the movement's defining feature is **what it never cites**. Strand D has one outgoing citation in the whole cluster;
Strand E has none, in either direction. Barry et al. (2007) — the paper that establishes alpha as a *global arousal*
marker, and thereby undermines the frontal-asymmetry approach the movement's own bor-sec-30 relies on — sits inside the
cluster **unread by it** (DA-05).

## Movement 4 · VR/education application (2008, then 2019–2025)

**Units:** bor-sec-44 (Nacke & Lindley 2008) · bor-sec-41 (2019) · bor-sec-42 (Haj-Bolouri 2023) ·
bor-sec-43 (Lin et al. 2024) · bor-sec-33 (2025) · bor-sec-15, bor-sec-17 (2020–22)

The application layer, and the thinnest. It also has a **pre-history that then goes quiet for eleven years**. Nacke &
Lindley (2008) built purpose-designed boredom, immersion, and flow game levels *as experimental conditions* — the single
most Part-III-shaped methodological gesture anywhere in the cluster, made in 2008 — and the next application unit here
does not arrive until 2019. The gap is not a sampling artifact of this cluster's scope so much as a fact about the
thread: the design-a-boredom-condition move was available from the start and was not taken up.

Lin et al.'s review (2024) — VR and student engagement, the closest single unit to Part III's own setting — was checked
against every other unit in this cluster and shares **zero references** with any of them.

Haj-Bolouri (2023) is the instructive case: explicitly "phenomenology-inspired", genuinely Heideggerian, and drawing on
*Being and Time*'s mood/care architecture — yet with **no engagement with FCM's boredom material at all**. A
phenomenology-of-VR paper written in 2023 does not reach the boredom analysis. That is the wall, holding even where every
condition for crossing it is met.

## The shape of the arc

| | Movement 1 | Movement 2 | Movement 3 | Movement 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Span** | 2001–2015 | 2018 | 2007, 2018–2025 | 2008, 2019–2025 |
| **Asks** | what does boredom disclose? | what does boredom *do*? | what does boredom *look like* in a signal? | how do we design around it? |
| **Cites Heidegger?** | FCM + BT | no (except Elpidorou's own) | no | BT only (bor-sec-42), never FCM |
| **Measures?** | no | surveys measures | yes | self-report only |
| **Reads Movement 1?** | — | only via Elpidorou | **no** | **no** |

**The arc is not a progression.** A conventional evolution story would say: phenomenology posed the question,
psychology operationalized it, neuroscience measured it, application deployed it. That story is **false for this
literature**, and the cluster's data refutes it three ways (`citation-network.md` §2,
`boredom-construct-measure-concordance.md` §2):

- Movement 3 does not cite Movement 1. Not selectively — **at all**.
- Movement 1 does not cite Movement 3. Not selectively — **at all**.
- The only connection is Elpidorou citing himself across his own two programmes, plus one critical footnote (Hughes).

What actually happened is that **two literatures grew in parallel under one word for twenty-four years.**

## Two live frontiers

*(P/anticipatory-application)*

1. **Educational EEG (2025).** Yuvaraj et al. is the newest unit and the only first-party EEG connectivity study set in an
   educational context — the closest anything here comes to Part III. Its methodological gap is instructive: it uses **no
   resting baseline at all**, sidestepping rather than answering Barry's 2007 caution (DA-05).
2. **The un-instrumented third form.** No source in 55 instruments profound boredom with any hard measure. Part III's
   design has **no precedent in this literature**. That is a contribution to claim, not a gap to hide — and the
   concordance is the evidence for the claim.

## Dating caveats

- **bor-sec-38 (Scharinger et al.)** is **2015**, not 2019 — confirmed via the article's own PLoS ONE citation line
  (10(6): e0130608) and DOI. It therefore belongs to the *end of Movement 1's period*, not Movement 3's, though it is
  thematically Movement 3. Corrected in `manifest.json` (CR-12b).
- **bor-sec-18 (Mansikka)** is 2008 online / 2009 print. The plan mis-attributed this paper to "Standish (2015)"; the
  correction moves the first boredom-and-education unit **seven years earlier**, which is why Movement 1 begins where it
  does (CR-12a).
- **bor-sec-27 (Barry et al. 2007)** is the oldest **measurement** unit — not the cluster's oldest overall, which is
  Thomson (bor-sec-19, 2001). Barry contains **no boredom** at all, and is dated into Movement 3 by function, not topic.
  Its being the measurement literature's oldest member is precisely the point of DA-05: the caution was on the record
  *before* the frontal-asymmetry work it undercuts.
- **bor-sec-44 (Nacke & Lindley)** is **2008**, and its authorship runs **Nacke first, then Lindley** — resolved at
  Phase 4 against the plan ("Lindley & Nacke", order reversed), the filename ("Nacke, L. and Craig, L.", given name
  mis-parsed as surname), *and* this cluster's own earlier check, which had endorsed the plan on the strength of a
  non-layout `pdftotext` dump that interleaved the two-column byline. The recovered year is what gives Movement 4 its
  eleven-year silence (CR-12c).
- **6 of 55 units carry no year** in their extracted text and are placed by content, not date: bor-sec-11 (Strand A) and
  bor-sec-34/35/36/37/39 (five of Strand E's eight). Movement 3 is therefore the movement whose date boundaries are most
  **partly inferred** — a caveat that does not touch the disjointness finding, which rests on citation and instrument
  data rather than on dates.
