# Contested Readings — Boredom Secondary (Part III)

Twelve disagreements about **how to read a specific text or passage**, as distinct from the conceptual disputes in
`debate-map.md`. A debate axis asks *what boredom is*; a contested reading asks *what this passage says*.

Several entries are **citation-fidelity findings** rather than interpretive disputes — cases where a source's own
apparatus is internally inconsistent. These are recorded because Part III cites through this cluster and inherits its
errors otherwise. They are marked **[fidelity]**.

Provenance: **FE** = the source's own claim · **P/anticipatory-application** = our observation about the source.

---

## CR-01 · Is the loss of meaning uniform across the three forms?

**The passage.** FCM's account of what is lost as boredom deepens.

- **Uniform reading** — Svendsen, and Elpidorou & Freeman: the three forms differ in depth and scope of the same loss.
- **Differentiated reading** — Mihačević: only the **second** form is a genuine loss of *personal* meaning; the **first**
  merely *suspends* it; the **third** negates the personal but **not meaning-as-such** (bor-sec-09, pp.40–41, FE).

**Why it matters.** On the uniform reading, profound boredom is maximal meaninglessness. On Mihačević's, it is
specifically the loss of *personal* meaning against a retained meaning-as-such — which is what makes it disclosive rather
than merely devastating. The two readings license different Part III interpretations of "my studies feel meaningless."

## CR-02 · Are the three forms a hierarchy or co-present modalities?

**The passage.** FCM's ordering first → second → third, and the `deepens-into` relation.

- **Staged/developmental reading** — the default in most of Strand A, and in FCM's own presentation, where each form
  re-runs the prior structure at greater depth.
- **Horizontal/co-present reading** — Hernández Albarracín, Álvarez González & Pallarès Piquer **explicitly reject** the
  staged reading in their conclusion, insisting the three forms are equally available modalities rather than steps toward
  profundity (bor-sec-11, p.220, FE).

**Why it matters.** *(P/anticipatory-application)* A staged reading implies Part III could observe students "progressing"
toward profound boredom; a co-present reading implies the three forms are three different things a student might be in at
any moment, with no developmental relation. The instrument design differs accordingly.

## CR-03 · Is Heidegger's activist conclusion required by the phenomenon?

**The passage.** FCM's closing move from profound boredom to the demand for resolute self-assertion.

- **Textually necessitated** — the orthodox reading: the *Augenblick* is where the analysis was always going.
- **Ideologically imported** — Slaby: Heidegger's construal is **not required by the phenomenon itself** but reflects
  (and later feeds) his political-existential temperament; Pessoa's affirmed, undemanding dwelling is an equally
  available conclusion from the same phenomenology (bor-sec-12, pp.242–246; bor-sec-13 §4, FE).

**Why it matters.** This is the hinge of DA-11. If Slaby is right, the activist payoff is detachable, and the
boredom analysis survives the political critique. If not, they come as a package.

## CR-04 · What is *Befindlichkeit* in English?

**The passage.** *Being and Time* §29.

**Eight competing renderings**, documented in a single footnote: "state-of-mind" (Macquarrie & Robinson) · "disposition"/
"disposedness" (Carman, Dahlstrom, Kisiel, Wrathall) · "ontological disposition" (Mayr & Askay) · "findingness"
(Haugeland) · "affectedness"/"affectivity" (Dreyfus, Blattner, Crowell) · "situatedness" (Guignon) · "attunement"
(Stambaugh) · left untranslated (Elpidorou & Freeman's own choice) (bor-sec-02, p.663, n.4, FE).

**Why it matters.** This is not pedantry: "state-of-mind" imports exactly the inner-mental-state connotation the concept
is designed to refuse, and it is the rendering a psychologist reading Heidegger in the standard translation will
encounter first. The translation choice **pre-decides** DA-09.

## CR-05 · Does Heidegger practise the equiprimordiality he preaches?

**The passage.** *Being and Time*'s claim that *Befindlichkeit* and understanding are equiprimordial.

- **Heidegger honours it** — the standard reconstruction, including Elpidorou & Freeman's sympathetic exegesis
  (bor-sec-02).
- **Heidegger does not** — Dahlstrom's audit: Heidegger "talks the talk" on equiprimordial affectivity but does not
  "walk the walk" in his actual textual practice, most severely in the account of authentic resoluteness (bor-sec-49, FE).

**The relation is `extends-reading-of`, not `contests-reading-of`.** Dahlstrom cites Elpidorou & Freeman (2015a, n.1
p.120; 2015b, n.16 p.123) and **converges with them** on the positive reconstruction — the same three-part structure of
thrownness / world-as-a-whole / mattering, derived from BT §29 and GA 20. He then converts that agreed reconstruction
into a **critical diagnostic**. The two pieces differ in orientation, not in doctrine.

## CR-06 · Ontic/ontological confusion, or truth/meaning conflation?

**The passage.** Heidegger's "sublimation" of boredom into the metaphysical questions.

- **Ontic/ontological confusion** — Svendsen's diagnosis.
- **Truth/meaning conflation** — Mihačević relocates the cause to a conflation internal to Heidegger's own conception of
  Being (bor-sec-09, pp.29, 40–41, FE). This is the paper's core original claim.

## CR-07 · Is Quaranta's "Temporal Entrancement" Heidegger's *Gebanntheit*?

**No — and the cluster records the distinction rather than smoothing it.** Quaranta's section title "Temporal
Entrancement" is **her own cinematic gloss**, not a citation of *Gebanntheit*. She cites FCM at pp.134, 138–141, 147–150,
158 (all inside fcm-06's range) but never uses Heidegger's structural vocabulary for the third form — not *Gebanntheit*,
not *Augenblick*, not *Leergelassenheit*/*Hingehaltenheit* (bor-sec-10, FE).

**Why it matters.** *(P/anticipatory-application)* A reader could reasonably assume the section is expounding
*Gebanntheit*. It is not, and attributing the fuller structural apparatus to Quaranta would misreport her.

---

## CR-08 · Murphy et al. read a **two**-form Heidegger · [fidelity]

Murphy et al. read pandemic-lockdown boredom through FCM but collapse the threefold typology into a **twofold** one,
citing only the first form (train-station) and profound boredom, and **silently omitting the second form** (bor-sec-23,
FE).

**Why it matters.** The second form is precisely the one Mihačević argues is the genuine loss of personal meaning (CR-01)
and the one whose surface-engagement/depth-emptiness structure is most relevant to a student who *looks* engaged. A
two-form Heidegger is missing the form Part III most needs. *(P/anticipatory-application)*

One further locus discrepancy: Murphy et al.'s FCM citation at p.295 (for "authentic being-in-the-world" / "moment of
vision") falls **outside** the boredom chapters' page range (pp.78–168) established elsewhere in this cluster — flagged,
not reconciled.

## CR-09 · Gibbs's Heidegger reference is mis-keyed · [fidelity]

Gibbs's reference list appears to assign "1995a" to a different Heidegger lecture course than the one his in-text page
citations actually match — which is FCM (bor-sec-07, FE). Two further internal inconsistencies in the same paper:
Simmel dated 1978 in text and 1991 in the list; Rorty 1979 vs. 1999 for the same essay.

**Also recorded:** Gibbs's citation of "Mansikka (2009), *Can Boredom Educate Us?*" is **accurate** — this was the thread
that exposed a mis-attribution in this cluster's own plan (see CR-12).

## CR-10 · Charoenpit & Ohkura's pupil rule contradicts their own tables · [fidelity]

The paper states that "a high pupil diameter ratio indicates that learner is bored." Its own correlation tables report
the opposite sign — pupil diameter ratio correlates **positively with interest** and **negatively with boredom**
(bor-sec-35, FE).

**Why it matters.** This is the single clearest instance of DA-04's problem inside one paper: the stated rule and the
reported data disagree, and a reader taking the rule at face value would build a Part III pupillometry design on an
inverted assumption.

Two further apparatus inconsistencies in the same source: an in-text/reference-list author mismatch (ref [7], cited as
"Ismail and Mohamed", listed as Haddioui & Khaldi), and a numbering mismatch between three named emotion models and their
numbered references.

## CR-11 · Seo et al.'s abstract claims a classifier the paper never runs · [fidelity]

The abstract states SVM, Random Forest, and k-NN were trained. The Methods and Results report only **k-NN, MLP, and
Random Forest** — **SVM never appears** in the actual experiments (bor-sec-31, FE).

**Why it matters.** The cluster's headline classification accuracy (86.73%, k-NN) is sound; the abstract's method list is
not. Cite the Results, not the abstract.

**Related, and more consequential:** Seo et al. **reuse the boredom-inducing video stimulus from Kim et al.** (bor-sec-28
— same co-authors) and explicitly claim methodological improvement over it. *(P/anticipatory-application)* Part III must
treat bor-sec-28 and bor-sec-31 as **one evolving research programme, not two independent convergent findings**. Counting
them as independent replications would overstate the evidence base.

## CR-12 · Two bibliographic corrections made by this cluster · [fidelity]

**(a) The "Standish 2015" paper does not exist.** This cluster's own plan assigned bor-sec-18 to "Standish, P. (2015),
*Can Boredom Educate Us?*". The PDF filed under that name is a **mislabeled duplicate of Mertel (2020)** — confirmed via
embedded PDF metadata and a full-text diff. The real paper is **Mansikka, Jan-Erik (2008/2009)**, *Studies in Philosophy
and Education* 28(3):255–268. The unit was rebuilt from the genuine source after the user supplied it. Gibbs (CR-09) had
cited Mansikka correctly all along; the error was the plan's, not the literature's.

**(b) Scharinger et al. is 2015, not 2019.** The article's own PLoS ONE citation line (10(6): e0130608), running head, and
DOI confirm publication 15 June 2015 (received Dec 2014, accepted May 2015). Corrected in `manifest.json`; the folder
name retains "(2019)".

**(c) Nacke & Lindley (2008) — and this entry previously had it backwards.** The canonical citation is **Nacke, L., &
Lindley, C. A. (2008)**, *Flow and immersion in first-person shooters*, FuturePlay 2008 (Nov 3–5, Toronto), ACM
978-1-60558-218-4. Three independent sources concur: the `pdftotext -layout` title-page byline (**Lennart Nacke** left
column, **Craig A. Lindley** right column); the PDF's embedded Author metadata ("Lennart Nacke and Craig Lindley"); and
decisively, the authors' **own self-citations** in their reference list (refs [22]/[23]: "Nacke, L. and Lindley, C.").

Both prior attributions were wrong, in different ways: the plan ("Lindley & Nacke") reversed the author order, and the
on-disk filename ("Nacke, L. and Craig, L.") got the order right but mis-parsed Lindley's given name *Craig* as his
surname. **This entry's own first version was also wrong** — it read a non-layout `pdftotext` dump in which the
two-column byline was interleaved, saw "Craig A. Lindley" first, and declared the plan correct. The tell it missed:
that same dump printed the two emails in the *reverse* order from the two names, which is the signature of column
interleaving. Re-verified at Phase 4 with column order preserved. The year, absent from the manifest until Phase 4, is
**2008** (conference banner + ACM copyright line + PDF CreationDate).

*Method note:* this is the second citation error in this cluster traceable to reading `pdftotext` output without
`-layout` on a multi-column source. Treat any byline recovered from a non-layout dump of a two-column paper as
unverified.
