# bor-sec-30 — Perone, Sammy, Elizabeth H. Weybright, and Alana J. Anderson, "Over and Over Again: Changes in Frontal EEG Asymmetry Across a Boring Task" (2019)

**Type:** empirical psychophysiology study (EEG, within-subjects repeated-measures) · **Discipline:** developmental/personality psychophysiology — frontal EEG asymmetry (FEA) as a neural correlate of approach/avoidance motivational style, applied to trait and state boredom · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** Strand D — the cluster's flagship frontal-EEG-asymmetry boredom study and the strongest candidate for a `grounds-measure` bridge to the not-yet-built "Boredom Experiment (VR Attention Study)" dataset's EEG frontal alpha asymmetry channel; directly cites cluster-sibling bor-sec-27 (Barry, Clarke, Johnstone, Magee, & Rushby, 2007) for its eyes-open/eyes-closed baseline-interpretation rationale — a genuine in-text citation, not merely an anticipated one.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Perone, Weybright, and Anderson (*Psychophysiology* 56, no. 10, 2019, e13427) test whether trait boredom — its two empirically distinguished dimensions, boredom proneness (avoidance-associated) and boredom susceptibility (approach-associated, novelty-averse) — relates to frontal EEG asymmetry (FEA), the alpha-band (8-13 Hz) power difference between homologous right (F4) and left (F3) frontal sites long treated as a trait-level neural correlate of approach versus avoidance motivational style (FAITHFUL-Perone et al., ~p.1-2). Two hypotheses organize the study (~p.3): first, baseline FEA would differ by trait-boredom subtype — proneness predicting relatively less left baseline activity (avoidance), susceptibility predicting relatively greater left baseline activity (approach); second, irrespective of baseline, people low in trait boredom would show a progressive leftward shift in frontal activity as situational boredom was induced by a repetitive, unchallenging task (computerized peg turning), reflecting active approach motivation to cope with and resolve the boring situation. Fifty-four undergraduates completed trait-boredom self-report scales, resting baseline EEG, and roughly 9.4 minutes of peg turning while EEG was recorded continuously; a post-task self-report scale confirmed the task induced state boredom. The first hypothesis was not supported — no baseline FEA-trait boredom relations were observed. The second was supported: boredom proneness and leisure boredom (though not susceptibility, despite a similar trend) predicted a steeper leftward, more approach-consistent shift in FEA across the four task blocks, specific to frontal (not parietal) sites and robust to reference-montage choice. The authors read this as evidence that FEA is not solely a static trait marker but also a dynamic state-level signature of online emotion-regulatory engagement: "a signature of dynamic online emotion regulatory processes" (FAITHFUL-Perone et al., abstract).

## 3. Constructs

- **profound/deep boredom (FCM 3rd form) / being-bored-by (1st form) / being-bored-with (2nd form)** — not treated; no Heideggerian vocabulary or citation appears anywhere in the article.
- **situational vs. trait/state boredom** — the paper's entire organizing framework: it investigates how trait boredom bears on coping with a state of boredom in real time (FE, ~p.1), operationalizing a trait level (baseline FEA, self-report trait scales) and a state level (FEA during peg turning, post-task self-report).
- **boredom proneness** — the trait-boredom dimension most robustly linked to the key finding: proneness (and its correlate, leisure boredom) inversely predicted the FEA leftward slope during peg turning (FAITHFUL-Perone et al., ~p.6-7). Boredom susceptibility, proneness's approach-oriented dispositional counterpart, yields a directionally similar but non-significant pattern.
- **boredom-as-regulatory-signal (functional account)** — central to the interpretive frame: the FEA leftward shift is read as an active regulatory process that emerges over time as boredom ensues (FAITHFUL-Perone et al., ~p.6), indexing online emotion-regulatory processes of coping with and resolving boredom (FE, ~p.7-8).
- **flow / engagement (contrast pole)** — present only functionally, never named as such: low-trait-boredom individuals are described as engaging an approach motivation to create stimulation (FAITHFUL-Perone et al., ~p.1-2), the paper's closest analogue to an engagement/flow contrast.
- **monotony / meaninglessness** — the peg-turning task is chosen for its redundancy (rated the single most boring task in a prior battery; FE, ~p.4), and the Leisure Experience Battery boredom subscale frames leisure boredom as cognitively unsatisfying, meaningless activity (FAITHFUL-Perone et al., ~p.5).
- **temporality of boredom (time-drag)** — one item on the post-task state-boredom scale, "Time is passing by slower than usual" (FAITHFUL-Perone et al., ~p.5), not developed into an independent mechanism.
- **attentional disengagement / meta-awareness** — mentioned once via a quoted definition of boredom as an aversive state of being unable to attentionally engage (Mercer-Lynn, Bar, & Eastwood, 2014, quoted at ~p.1); background framing, not this paper's own operationalized construct.
- **mind-wandering / task-unrelated thought** — not treated; the paper's apparatus is motivational (approach/avoidance), not attentional.

## 4. Measures & methods

Consistent with this strand's practice, this section substitutes for a "Reading of FCM" with the study's apparatus, sample, and protocol.

**Participants.** N = 54 undergraduates (Mage = 20.87, SD = 1.55; 47 female), self-identified as White (55.55%), Hispanic/Latino (12.96%), African American/Black (11.11%), Asian (9.25%), multiracial (7.41%), and Native/Indigenous (3.7%); recruited for extra course credit. Completion counts (Table 1): 54 trait-boredom scales, 51 baseline EEG, 52 state boredom, 47 peg-turning EEG, with attrition from acquisition failure, artifact contamination, or missed second session (FAITHFUL-Perone et al., ~p.3).

**Design and procedure.** Two lab visits within a 10-day window. Visit 1: baseline demographic/personality survey, then a 128-channel HydroCel geodesic net (Electrical Geodesics, Inc.), impedance <100 kΩ; baseline EEG recorded in a dimly lit room across two blocks of alternating eyes-closed/eyes-open 2-minute trials (both conditions included because baseline-FEA correlates are not always observed for both — a rationale supported by direct citation to cluster-sibling Barry, Clarke, Johnstone, Magee, & Rushby, 2007); sampled at 1,000 Hz, high-pass filtered at .1 Hz, referenced online to Cz. Visit 2: a computerized peg-turning boredom-induction task (originating with Festinger & Carlsmith, 1959; modeled after Markey, Chin, Vanepps, & Loewenstein's 2014 version, in which peg turning was independently rated the most boring of a task battery) — participants clicked an illuminated peg in a 2×4 virtual grid to rotate it one quarter-turn at a time, with a 1,000-1,500 ms jittered interval before each rotation, across 10 blocks of 32 rotations, in E-Prime 2.0 with a Chronos response pad; the task took ~9.44 minutes on average (SD = 0.80). A post-task state-boredom self-report followed immediately; EEG acquisition during peg turning matched baseline (FAITHFUL-Perone et al., ~p.3-4).

**EEG processing.** Custom MATLAB scripts (EEGLAB, ERPLAB, FieldTrip, CSD toolbox). Continuous EEG was downsampled to 500 Hz, high-pass filtered at 1 Hz with a 60 Hz notch; noisy electrodes were interpolated; ICA identified and removed blink components; data were re-referenced to the average; EEG was segmented into 1-s epochs (75% overlap), with peg-turning epochs divided into four equal blocks. Surface potentials were Laplacian-transformed into current source density (CSD toolbox; m = 4, head radius = 10, λ = 0.00001) to reduce distal-source contamination at frontal sites, then FFT-decomposed (Hanning window) on artifact-free epochs. FEA was computed as natural-log alpha (8-13 Hz) power at F4 minus F3, so higher/positive scores index relatively greater left (approach) activity and lower/negative scores index relatively greater right (avoidance) activity, since lower alpha reflects greater cortical activation. FEA was computed for baseline (eyes-open, eyes-closed) and each of the four peg-turning blocks, with an identical asymmetry score computed at parietal sites (P3/P4) as a specificity control. Regression coefficient analysis (RCA) — not previously applied to FEA — regressed each person's four block-level scores onto Blocks 1-4 to yield an FEA slope, with block recentered to also yield Block 1 and Block 4 intercepts (FAITHFUL-Perone et al., ~p.4-5).

**Trait- and state-boredom instruments.** Boredom Proneness Scale (Farmer & Sundberg, 1986; 28 items, 5-point Likert; current α = .84, lit. range .79-.90); Leisure Experience Battery for Adolescents boredom subscale (Caldwell, Smith, & Weissinger, 1992; 4 items; one item dropped for poor performance, final α = .67, lit. range .68-.73); Boredom Susceptibility Scale (Zuckerman, 1994, Sensation Seeking Scale subscale; 10 forced-choice items; current α = .38, low but consistent with lit. range .44-.65, retained for its unique relation to approach-oriented traits); Multidimensional State Boredom Scale (Fahlman, Mercer-Lynn, Flora, & Eastwood, 2013; 7 items, 7-point scale, post-task; current α = .89, M = 5.17, SD = 1.14, closely comparable to Markey et al.'s 2014 M = 5.59, SD = 1.02 — corroborating that peg turning induced genuine state boredom) (FAITHFUL-Perone et al., ~p.5).

## 5. Findings/Claims

- Trait-boredom scales intercorrelated in the expected direction: leisure boredom and boredom proneness were positively associated with each other and with state boredom during peg turning, indicating people high in trait boredom experienced more state boredom in the task; boredom susceptibility showed no significant relations with the other scales (FAITHFUL-Perone et al., ~p.6).
- Hypothesis 1 (baseline FEA differs by trait-boredom subtype) was not supported: no significant bivariate correlations were observed between baseline FEA (eyes-open or eyes-closed) and any trait-boredom scale (FAITHFUL-Perone et al., ~p.6).
- Hypothesis 2 (leftward FEA shift among low-trait-boredom individuals) was supported for two of the three trait scales: boredom proneness and leisure boredom were both inversely related to FEA slope during peg turning — lower trait boredom predicted a more pronounced leftward (approach-consistent) shift in frontal asymmetry over the four blocks; leisure boredom alone was also inversely related to the Block 4 FEA intercept. Tertile-grouped plots showed the same pattern visually: low-scoring individuals shifted leftward, high-scoring individuals shifted rightward, across peg-turning blocks; boredom susceptibility showed a similar directional trend but did not reach significance (FAITHFUL-Perone et al., ~p.6-7).
- The effect was frontally specific (no significant parietal P3/P4 relations at baseline or peg turning, all ps > .30) and robust to reference-montage choice, replicating under an average-reference re-analysis (leisure boredom: r = -0.47, p < .01, d = -1.07; boredom proneness: r = -0.48, p < .01, d = -1.09) (FAITHFUL-Perone et al., ~p.7).
- Exploratory dense-array scalp-topography mapping showed the leisure-boredom effect was robust at F4/F3 and adjacent/central sites, while the boredom-proneness effect was more narrowly localized to F4/F3 and immediately adjacent sites — evidence that FEA's sensitivity to trait boredom varies by which trait scale is used (FAITHFUL-Perone et al., ~p.7).
- No relation was observed between FEA at any point and post-task self-reported state boredom (FE, ~p.8) — a dissociation the authors note directly.
- The authors interpret the pattern through Coan, Allen, and McKnight's (2006) "capability model": neural correlates of emotion-regulatory capacity may emerge only when that capacity is actively challenged by a specific task context (here, peg turning), not at rest (FAITHFUL-Perone et al., ~p.7-8), and situate the finding within Fox's (1994) framework of FEA as a dynamic hemispheric interplay shaped jointly by trait disposition and context — their data show FEA evolving directionally over several minutes as task redundancy persists, rather than remaining a static trait quantity (FAITHFUL-Perone et al., ~p.8).
- The discussion gestures toward two future directions: neurofeedback (citing Zotev et al., 2016, on depression) as a possible means of shifting avoidance-oriented FEA biases toward approach in high-trait-boredom individuals, and a developmental-systems account of how early approach/avoidance biases (present from infancy; malleable per Hane & Fox, 2006) might crystallize into trait boredom over childhood and adolescence (FE, ~p.8-9).
