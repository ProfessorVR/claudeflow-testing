# bor-sec-42 — Haj-Bolouri, A., "The Experience of Immersive Virtual Reality: A Phenomenology Inspired Inquiry" (2023)

**Type:** qualitative/interpretive Information Systems (IS) research article, phenomenological inquiry with an embedded interview-based empirical case · **Discipline:** Information Systems / Heideggerian phenomenology (not cognitive science, not biomedical signal processing) · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** Strand F — the cluster's clearest VR/immersion source explicitly built on Heidegger, but on the general *Being and Time* apparatus of mood, meaning, meaningfulness, and care rather than on boredom (*Langeweile*) or *Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics* (FCM) specifically; see §5 below for the honest verdict on why no FCM bridge is asserted.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Haj-Bolouri (*Communications of the Association for Information Systems* 52, Article 38, 2023, pp.782-814) opens from a gap in IS research on Immersive Virtual Reality (IVR): studies exist on IVR's socio-technical uses (work, education, training) but not on how the IVR experience itself *becomes meaningful* for end-users (FE, p.784). His response is a single research question asking how individuals use the IVR experience "to create meaning in the context of meaningful escapism" (FAITHFUL-Haj-Bolouri, p.784) — pursued through a phenomenological inquiry drawing on Heidegger's (1962) phenomenology, with "a particular focus on meaning, meaningfulness, and the notion of 'world'" (FAITHFUL-Haj-Bolouri, p.790), operationalized as phenomenological interviewing and Interpretive Phenomenological Analysis (IPA) within an empirical case of IVR fire-safety training conducted with SJ, Sweden's largest train operator (FE, pp.790, 793). The inquiry unveils four characteristics of the IVR experience as a "meaningful escapism" — Sense of Content, Sense of Familiarity, Sense of Mood, and Sense of Care — and, on that basis, proposes "Virtual Dasein" (being-in-the-virtual-world) as an extension of Heidegger's Dasein (being-in-the-world) for IS theorizing about IVR (FE, pp.782, 804-805).

## 3. Constructs

Mapped onto the controlled construct axis, this qualitative IS source treats a narrow cluster and is explicit that boredom itself is absent — worth registering precisely rather than assumed by strand position.

- **Flow / engagement (contrast pole)** — the paper's most directly cognate construct. "Sense of Content" is glossed as "Flow through World (creating meaning)" (FAITHFUL-Haj-Bolouri, Table 5, p.795), and interviewees describe the training exercise as having "a flow to it" once representational constraints of the physical world (walls, furniture, injury) are lifted (FAITHFUL-Haj-Bolouri, Train Operator 2, p.796). No Csikszentmihalyi-style flow-state theory is cited; "flow" here names an experiential quality of frictionless world-engagement, not a measured psychological state.
- **Monotony / meaninglessness** — meaninglessness (not monotony) is the operative half of this pair, functioning throughout as meaningfulness's contrast term: an IVR experience can be "filled with meaning" yet fail to become meaningful if it does not "fit my lifeworld" (FAITHFUL-Haj-Bolouri, p.803); a skill acquired in the virtual world can likewise become "meaningless" if it cannot be actualized in the encountered physical situation (FE, p.802). Monotony in the sense of repetitive stimulus design is not discussed.
- **Attentional disengagement / meta-awareness** — present only in the *engaged* rather than disengaged direction: "presence" is defined as feeling "present in the mediated environment, rather than in the immediate physical environment," true "when one's attention is fully directed towards the IVR environment" and not the physical space (FAITHFUL-Haj-Bolouri, quoting Biocca & Levy 2013, p.787). This is immersive absorption, the inverse pole of what the controlled vocabulary's boredom-oriented "disengagement" row targets, cited here only as a contrast case for the cluster's own concordance.
- **Temporality of boredom (time-drag)** — not present as dilation; the paper instead documents temporal *compression*: psychological immersion produces a feeling of "selflessness," including "forgetting about time" (FAITHFUL-Haj-Bolouri, p.787), and Sense of Care is explicitly bound up with "historicity and temporality of meaning" (FAITHFUL-Haj-Bolouri, p.801) — a temporality-of-mattering, not a felt slowing of time. Flagged as a near-miss, not a match.
- **Profound/deep boredom, being-bored-by, being-bored-with, boredom proneness, situational vs. trait/state boredom, mind-wandering/task-unrelated thought, boredom-as-regulatory-signal** — none treated. The word "bore/boredom" occurs in this ~30-page article exactly once outside its reference list, in the general observation that "Dasein is always in some mood" and that "even indifference is considered to be a mood" (FAITHFUL-Haj-Bolouri, p.791) — a citation of Heidegger's general mood doctrine (*Being and Time* §29), not of boredom.

## 4. Reading of FCM

The phenomenological tradition this source draws on is stated explicitly and narrowly: the author surveys three streams of phenomenology — the lived experience as a phenomenon of consciousness (Husserl), as bodily perception (Merleau-Ponty), and as "the primordial mode of Dasein or being-in-the-world" (Heidegger) — before stating plainly, "For this study, the latter view of Heidegger is employed" (FAITHFUL-Haj-Bolouri, p.790). Husserl and Merleau-Ponty are named only to be set aside; Ihde's postphenomenology of technology (multistability, human-technology relations) is never mentioned at all. The framework is thus squarely Heideggerian, and squarely *Being and Time* (1962 Macquarrie & Robinson translation, cited roughly thirty times): Dasein, being-in-the-world, world as a totality of practices/equipment/skills, the meaning/meaningfulness distinction (Table 2), mood (*Befindlichkeit*) as that through which Dasein discloses the world, and care (*Sorge*) as what makes an experience matter to the lifeworld.

Applying this dissertation cluster's standing test — genuine engagement with Heidegger's boredom analysis specifically, not a generic phenomenological "immersion as absorption" framing — the honest verdict is **no bridge is warranted**. The word "boredom" appears in the body text not once; the only occurrence of the root "bore-" anywhere in the article is embedded in a bibliography title, Freeman & Elpidorou's "Affectivity in Heidegger II: Temporality, boredom, and beyond" (2015), itself cited four times in-text (pp.784, 791, 803, 804) but on every occasion for its general claims about mood, care, and meaningfulness — never once for its boredom argument. Elpidorou & Freeman's companion piece, "Affectivity in Heidegger I: Moods and emotions in *Being and Time*" (2015), is cited the same way (p.784). *Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics* itself is never cited; none of FCM's vocabulary appears — no *Langeweile*, no first/second/third form, no *Leergelassenheit*/*Hingehaltenheit*/*Gebanntheit*/*Augenblick*, no "profound boredom." The single passage that comes closest is Heidegger's general claim that "Dasein is always in some mood" (p.791, *Being and Time* §29, including indifference as a mood) — the doctrinal home from which FCM's boredom analysis later specializes — but Haj-Bolouri stops at the general level and never makes that specialization himself.

One coincidental resonance is worth flagging for the cluster's own synthesis without over-reading it: the empirical setting is train operators and train drivers undergoing routine safety drills, faintly echoing Heidegger's own paradigm scene for the *second* form of boredom in FCM (waiting at a train station). The author never invokes this parallel, and it is noted here only as an unexploited resonance (P/anticipatory-application), not as evidence of engagement.

## 5. Findings/Claims

1. Four characteristics of meaningfulness, each paired with a "feature of escapism," answer RQ1: **Sense of Content** ("Flow through World," creating meaning), **Sense of Familiarity** ("Orientation to/from World," recognizing meaning), **Sense of Mood** ("Imagining/Perceiving a World," enacting meaning), and **Sense of Care** ("Agency/Becoming Possibilities in World," actualizing meaning) (FAITHFUL-Haj-Bolouri, Table 5, p.795).
2. Meaning and meaningfulness are conceptually distinct: meaning is "representational," "created," and "attributed" — an intelligibility-conferring property of an experience situated in a world — whereas meaningfulness is the "subjective," "experienced," and "significant" quality of an experience that matters to the lifeworld via mood and care (FAITHFUL-Haj-Bolouri, Table 2, pp.791-792). An IVR experience can be "filled with meaning" without becoming meaningful (FE, p.803).
3. Escapism is reframed as a "dual sword" rather than simply negative: it can supply safety, freedom from real-world consequence, and low-risk skill rehearsal (Sense of Content: one operator felt "much freer to express myself" without real-world consequences, Train Operator 1, p.796), but also disorientation (teleportation-induced dizziness, Train Operator 3, p.798) and risk of addiction or withdrawal from the physical world if pursued to excess (FE, pp.789, 806).
4. "Breakdown situations" — a term borrowed from Dreyfus's (1991, 2002) reading of Heidegger rather than from Heidegger's own vocabulary (explicitly conceded, p.803) — differentiate *obstructive* breakdowns (permanent technology failure, collapsing the IVR experience into a merely present-at-hand object) from *obstinate* breakdowns (temporary malfunction, e.g. a glitching fire-hose replica that shifted the training's mood "from being felt as rather serious, to becoming funny," Train Driver 2, p.799-800), the latter capable of stimulating rather than destroying Sense of Care.
5. "Virtual Dasein" (being-in-the-virtual-world) is proposed as an alternative/extension of Dasein (being-in-the-world) for IVR: end-users become "thrown into" a virtual world that does not fully carry bodily experience from the physical world across, yet still discloses meaning through mood and care (FAITHFUL-Haj-Bolouri, pp.804-805).
6. Methodologically, the study is presented as demonstrating "the viability of phenomenology for IVR research in the field of IS" (FAITHFUL-Haj-Bolouri, p.784), situated within — and extending — an existing lineage of Heideggerian IS scholarship (Table 1) that had not previously been applied to IVR specifically.
7. Limitations are named directly: Heidegger's phenomenology is "complex," simplified here for an IS rather than philosophical audience, and the empirical illustration is confined to one context (IVR fire-safety training), with generalization to other IVR domains (education, entertainment, social IVR) left to future research (FAITHFUL-Haj-Bolouri, p.807-808).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* This is one of the strongest Part III relevance candidates in the cluster on subject-matter grounds — an IVR/HMD empirical case (fire-safety training) sits directly inside Part III's lab=VR pole — but the relevance is thematic/methodological, not boredom-evidential: nothing here can be cited as evidence about boredom, profound boredom, or FCM without misrepresenting the source.

*(anticipatory-application)* The "meaningful escapism" frame, and specifically the Sense of Content/Familiarity/Mood/Care quartet, offers Part III a candidate qualitative vocabulary for interpreting *why* lab-VR participants might report different engagement or affective profiles than deployment-2D-PC participants, independent of any boredom claim — e.g., a VR condition's felt "flow through world" or "orientation to/from world" (disorientation from novel locomotion, echoing Train Operator 3's teleportation-dizziness account, p.798) could plausibly interact with whichever boredom/engagement measures Part III's own instrument uses, without the two constructs being the same thing.

*(anticipatory-application)* The obstinate/obstructive breakdown distinction (Dreyfus, borrowed here for the fire-hose glitch) supplies a ready-made qualitative vocabulary for coding technical hiccups in Part III's own VR sessions (tracking loss, control glitches, latency) and their effect on participant mood — worth distinguishing from genuine equipment failure (obstructive) when interpreting any qualitative debrief notes from lab-VR participants.

*(anticipatory-application)* **Dataset bridge, tagged `****** UNVERIFIED:`** — the not-yet-built Boredom Experiment (VR Attention Study) dataset entry is the natural home for a `grounds-measure` edge from this source, given the direct VR-immersion match; however, honesty requires noting that this source contributes no hard channel (no EEG, gaze, or physiological apparatus) to ground. What it can plausibly inform, if that dataset entry ever specifies a qualitative or mixed-methods supplement, is *item/probe design* for a presence-or-immersion self-report channel — Bevan's (2014) three-stage interview structure is a reusable qualitative-interview template, not a physiological instrument, and the linkage remains unverified until the dataset entry specifies its own instrumentation.

*(anticipatory-application)* Finally, this source is a useful negative control for the cluster's own internal audit: it demonstrates that "phenomenology-inspired" and "Heideggerian" are not proxies for "engages FCM's boredom analysis." Strand F sources should be checked individually on this point rather than assumed bridgeable by genre resemblance to Strand A.
