# bor-sec-01 — Aho, "Heidegger on Melancholia, Deep Boredom, and the Inability-to-Be" (2020)

**Type:** peer commentary/discussion piece (journal symposium note) · **Discipline:** philosophy (phenomenology) / philosophical psychiatry · **Depth:** map · **Corpus role:** Strand A short bridge unit — links Heideggerian melancholia literature to FCM's third-form profound-boredom account and gestures toward an epoch-level "ground mood" reading.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Aho's three-page commentary (Philosophy, Psychiatry, & Psychology 27.3, pp.215–217) responds to Emily Hughes's article on melancholia as ontological "dying" (Sterben) by arguing that Heidegger's own 1929/30 account of "deep" or "profound boredom" (tiefe Langeweile), first worked out in Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics (FCM) just two years after Being and Time (BT), illuminates the same desynchronized, meaning-stripped temporality that Hughes diagnoses in major depression (FE). Aho's further, more ambitious claim — developed only in the piece's closing movement — is that Heidegger's later "turn" (Kehre) toward historical "ground moods" (Grundstimmungen) lets profound boredom be read epochally: depression as the possible ground mood of modernity itself, not a brain disorder (FE).

## 2. Structure

The piece carries a single roman-numeral marker ("I") and no further headers; it unfolds as six argumentative movements rather than formal sections.

1. **Framing Hughes** (p.215) — melancholia as ontological death/"dying," temporal desynchronization, the sufferer "unable to live and unable to die."
2. **BT backdrop** (p.215–216) — temporality (Zeitlichkeit), thrown projection (geworfen Entwurf), the disclosive unity of time; therapy as re-establishing the patient's future-orientation.
3. **The stakes of assisted death** (p.216) — physician-assisted suicide as fraught precisely because it forecloses ontological death's potentially liberating rupture from "the They" (das Man).
4. **Turn to FCM's profound boredom** (p.216) — tiefe Langeweile introduced as a parallel register: bored not by a particular thing but by "everything," an atemporal limbo.
5. **The Kehre and ground moods** (p.216) — Grundstimmungen of historical epochs (Hellenistic "wonder"/Erstaunen vs. modern disenchantment); depression reframed as the ground mood of an epoch, not an inner/chemical state.
6. **Closing ambivalence** (p.217) — the epochal "nihilating space" holds a possible "new beginning" (neuer Anfang), but Heidegger's own doubt (Der Spiegel, "only a God can save us") is left standing.

## 3. Constructs

- **profound/deep boredom (FCM 3rd form)** — the organizing construct. Aho glosses it via Heidegger's own claim that in this mood "we are elevated beyond the particular situation" (p.216, quoting Heidegger 1995, p.137) — i.e., bored with the world as a whole rather than a determinate thing (FAITHFUL-Aho, p.216).
- **being-bored-by (1st form)** — invoked only contrastively, via Heidegger's own stock examples of situational boredom (a boring book, drive, or airport wait) to set off profound boredom's non-situational character (p.216) (FAITHFUL-Aho, p.216); Aho never names the "first form" as such.
- **situational vs. trait/state boredom** — the same contrast operates at the level of "particular situations or events" versus an "affective atmosphere directed at the world as a whole" (p.216) (FAITHFUL-Aho, p.216).
- **monotony / meaninglessness** — the modern world is described as "bleached out" of enchantment and qualitative significance, leaving things "as much or as little value as everything else" (p.216) (FAITHFUL-Aho, p.216).
- **temporality of boredom (time-drag)** — the central connective tissue between melancholia and profound boredom: both desynchronize the unified time-horizon, producing a "meaningless atemporal void" (p.215–216) (FAITHFUL-Aho, p.215).
- **boredom-as-regulatory-signal (functional account)** — present only in a qualified, non-Elpidorou sense: profound boredom's "nihilating space" is read as opening onto authentic self-recovery or (at the epochal level) a "new beginning" (p.216–217) — an existential-disclosive function, not an evolutionary/regulatory one (FE, qualified).

Not treated: mind-wandering/task-unrelated thought, attentional disengagement/meta-awareness, flow/engagement, boredom proneness — none of these cognitive-science constructs appear in this phenomenological commentary.

## 4. Reading of FCM

Aho's engagement with FCM is anchoring rather than exegetical: he cites Heidegger's own formula that profound boredom is a mood in which "we are not bored with particular things; we are bored with everything" (p.216, quoting 1995 p.137) — a passage that sits within fcm-06-third-form's own §3 treatment of being-left-empty (the third form's totalizing indifference to "beings as a whole"). Aho does not walk through fcm-06's structural machinery (Leergelassenheit/Hingehaltenheit, the entrancement of the temporal horizon, the Augenblick); he takes only the third form's headline result — boredom directed at the world as such, not a determinate situation — and uses it to gloss Hughes's depression phenomenon (P/anticipatory-application: this unit's own mapping of Aho's citation onto fcm-06, not a claim Aho makes explicitly).

A second citation (Heidegger 1995, p.79, the "insidious creature" line) falls, by fcm-04-first-form's own pagination (book pp.78–105), within FCM's Chapter Two — i.e., material introducing boredom's general evasiveness before the three forms are formally differentiated, rather than the third-form chapter proper (book pp.132–159). This is worth flagging for the concordance: Aho's page citations range across FCM's earlier framing material and its third-form culmination without distinguishing the two, consistent with a short commentary that borrows FCM's conclusions rather than reconstructing its argument (P/anticipatory-application).

Aho's more consequential move is to leave FCM behind altogether for the piece's final third: the "ground mood" (Grundstimmung) of a historical epoch is a notion FCM itself gestures toward (the lecture course's own task is "awakening" a fundamental attunement of contemporary Dasein — see fcm-07's discussion of a "particular profound boredom" as the attunement of "our contemporary Dasein"), but the epochal content Aho draws on — Hellenistic "wonder" (Erstaunen), the "flight of the gods," the reduction of humanity to "a mass" — comes from Heidegger's post-Kehre texts (Introduction to Metaphysics, Basic Questions of Philosophy, "Messkirch's Seventh Centennial," the Spiegel interview), not from FCM's own body. Aho's bridge from fcm-06's individual-Dasein third form to a civilizational diagnosis is therefore an extension beyond FCM's own textual scope, using FCM only as the originating concept (FAITHFUL-Aho, p.216, with the extension flagged rather than presented as FCM's own claim).

## 5. Findings/Claims

- Melancholia's temporal desynchronization (Hughes, via BT's Sterben) and FCM's profound boredom share a phenomenological structure: both strip the world of qualitative meaning and leave the sufferer in an atemporal, futureless present (FE, p.215–216).
- Profound boredom, like BT's anxiety, opens a "nihilating space" that can rupture tranquilized everydayness ("the They") and return Dasein to itself (FE, p.216).
- Via the Kehre, boredom is no longer only an individual mood but can be the "ground mood" of an entire historical epoch (FE, p.216).
- Modern depression, read as the ground mood of modernity, is not reducible to a "brain disorder" or "chemical imbalances of serotonin or dopamine" — moods are not "inside us" but bound up with the historical situation into which Dasein is thrown (FE, p.216, quoting Heidegger 1962/1973).
- The epochal nihilating space holds a potential collective "new beginning" (neuer Anfang), but Heidegger's own ambivalence about whether such a transformation will occur is left unresolved, closing on "only a God can save us" (FE, p.217).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* This unit's chief value for Part III is as Strand A's connective tissue between the depression/melancholia secondary literature and FCM's own third-form account, giving the cluster a citable instance of "profound boredom" glossed in the exact language ("elevated beyond the particular situation," bored "with everything") that Part III can reuse when distinguishing situational student disengagement from a deeper existential register — but the mapping must stay at the level of vocabulary, not diagnosis: Aho's epochal claim (depression/boredom as modernity's collective ground mood) operates at a civilizational scale entirely unlike the individual-subject measures (survey, EEG, gaze) Part III's own study collects, and importing it directly would overstate what a classroom-level VR/desktop study can show *(anticipatory-application)*. Aho's rejection of the biomedical/chemical-imbalance account of depression is a useful methodological caution for Strand D's EEG-based units: it names, from outside cognitive science entirely, the same worry (that physiological signal alone cannot exhaust "boredom") that motivates this cluster's insistence on phenomenological grounding alongside physiological measurement *(anticipatory-application)*. Finally, Aho's "nihilating space"/new-beginning reading of boredom's positive function sits in productive tension with Elpidorou's regulatory-signal account (bor-sec-25/26): both treat boredom as *doing* something rather than merely being a deficit, but one is existential-disclosive and the other evolutionary-adaptive — a debate-axis worth carrying into the cluster's Strand A/C synthesis rather than resolving here *(anticipatory-application)*.
