# bor-sec-39 — Sharma, Kshitij, Michail Giannakos, and Pierre Dillenbourg, "Eye-Tracking and Artificial Intelligence to Enhance Motivation and Learning" (2020)

**Type:** empirical educational-technology / learning-analytics study (single-modality eye-tracking + machine-learning experiment, N=40 MOOC learners) · **Discipline:** computer science / learning analytics, HCI-adjacent · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** Strand E — the cluster's clearest instance of a rigorously eye-tracking-and-AI-instrumented study of student attention and motivation that never once uses the word "boredom": a direct text search of the full extraction returns zero occurrences of "bore-", "monoton-", "mind-wander-", "disengag-", "flow", or "meaningless." Its constructs (motivation, gaze-based attention/"with-me-ness," learning) and its prediction pipeline (VGG-19 heat-map features + LASSO + Gaussian-Process/SVM/Random-Forest/GAM regression) are read here as adjacent and analogical to this cluster's boredom vocabulary rather than as direct engagement with it. Every construct-mapping and bridge below is flagged as this entry's own interpretive extension, not the source's stated aim.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Sharma, Giannakos, and Dillenbourg (*Smart Learning Environments* 7:13, 2020) address a stated evidentiary gap in Massive Open Online Course (MOOC) learning analytics: click-stream, keystroke, and other interaction-log variables "do not always capture users' learning and behavior (e.g., passive video watching)" (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.1). The paper poses two research questions: (1) how eye-tracking behaviour mediates the relationship between students' motivation and their learning within a MOOC, and (2) how well learning gain and motivation can be predicted from eye-tracking data "in its most basic form" (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.2). To answer the first, the authors define a family of stimulus-derived gaze variables — content coverage, scanpath-based variables (AOI misses, AOI backtracks), and "with-me-ness" (perceptual and conceptual) — and test each as a mediator between a validated motivation questionnaire (predictor) and post-video quiz performance (outcome), using Baron and Kenny's (1986) three-step mediation procedure (FE, p.10). To answer the second, the authors extract deep-learning features from per-slide gaze heat-maps via a pretrained VGG-19 convolutional network, reduce dimensionality with LASSO, and benchmark five regression algorithms for predicting both learning and motivation scores (FE, p.11-12). Boredom is never named in the paper; the operative affective-motivational vocabulary is exclusively "motivation" (deep/surface, from Biggs, Kember, and Leung's Study Process Questionnaire) and "with-me-ness" (a gaze-based teacher-student co-attention construct original to Sharma's own prior work).

## 2. Structure

The article runs pp.1-19 with reliable embedded page markers throughout (page anchors below are exact, not approximate, except where a claim spans a page boundary).

1. **Abstract/keywords** (p.1) — states the MOOC-analytics gap, previews the with-me-ness/gaze-variable framework and the AI prediction method, reports the headline result (learning predicted within ~5% error).
2. **Introduction** (p.1-2) — motivates gaze-based measurement over click-stream analytics; surveys prior academic-performance predictors (prior grades, motivation/effort, socioeconomic status, schooling quality, attention, participation); states the two research questions and the perceptual/conceptual with-me-ness distinction.
3. **Related work — Video based learning; Eye-tracking and education** (p.2-4) — Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning (Mayer & Moreno) and dual-channel/cue-summation theory (Paivio; Severin) frame prior video-modality comparisons (Tobagi; Cennamo, Savenye & Smith; Choi & Johnson); Prieto et al.'s teacher-orchestration-load eye-tracking and individual (Kizilcec et al.)/collaborative (Sharma et al. 2015a/b) online-learning eye-tracking lead into the eye-mind hypothesis (Just & Carpenter, 1980), the paper's foundational warrant for treating gaze as an attention proxy.
4. **Methodology — Participants and procedure** (p.4-5) — N=40 (12 female); SMI RED 250 eye-tracker; two "Functional Programming Principles in Scala" MOOC videos; SPQ administered before viewing, quiz after; sample-size justified against two systematic reviews spanning 85 eye-tracking studies (84.71% used 8-60 participants).
5. **Methodology — Measures** (p.5-10) — four gaze-variable levels of increasing semantic dependency (Table 1): content coverage (low, stimulus-only); scanpath variables (medium, AOI-dependent); perceptual with-me-ness (high, AOI + teacher deictic gestures); conceptual with-me-ness (highest, AOI + teacher dialogue); plus motivation (SPQ) and learning (post-video quiz).
6. **Methodology — Data analysis** (p.10-12) — mediation analysis (Baron & Kenny 1986); feature extraction for prediction (heat-map → VGG-19 → 1,000 features/slide → sliding-window reduction to 100); LASSO feature selection (Tibshirani 1996, 10-fold CV); five regression algorithms benchmarked via 80/20 train-test split (32/8 students) with 5-fold CV, evaluated by NRMSE (Pelánek 2015).
7. **Results** (p.11-15) — separate mediation results for content coverage (full mediation), scanpath variables (partial mediation, both AOI misses and backtracks), and with-me-ness (full mediation, both perceptual and conceptual) (Table 2); prediction results (Fig.11): best learning-prediction error 5.04% and best motivation-prediction error 9.04%, both Gaussian Process with polynomial kernel.
8. **Discussions and conclusions; Abbreviations/acknowledgements/references** (p.16-19) — interprets each mediation result against prior reading-time/rereading literature; situates conceptual with-me-ness within grounding theory (Dillenbourg & Traum 2006); discusses real-time computability of each gaze variable; states future-work plans (pair problem-solving stimuli, novice comparisons, gaze-aware adaptive feedback); Norwegian Research Council funding (FUTURE LEARNING 255129/H20; Xdesign 290994/F20); no participant data shared due to re-identification risk.

## 3. Constructs

Mapped onto the controlled construct axis — with the caveat, stated once and applying throughout, that none of the paper's own vocabulary names "boredom," "monotony," or "mind-wandering." Every mapping below is a same-domain analogy supplied by this entry, not the source's own framing.

- **flow / engagement (contrast pole)** — the paper's nearest functional equivalent is its "motivation" construct (deep vs. surface, via SPQ) paired with "with-me-ness" as an operationalized attention/co-attention measure. Deep motivation is defined as having "intrinsic motivation towards learning," while surface motivation is defined as "fear of failing in the tests" (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.10, citing Biggs, Kember & Leung 2001) — a valence split loosely parallel to engagement-as-flow versus engagement-as-anxiety-avoidance, though the paper itself never frames the distinction this way (P/anticipatory-application for the flow-mapping specifically).
- **attentional disengagement / meta-awareness** — the closest operational analogue is the **AOI miss**, defined as a participant who "did not look at that particular AOI at all during the period the AOI was present on the screen," glossed as "completely ignoring some parts of the slides" (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.6). This is read here as a direct behavioral proxy for attentional disengagement, though the source frames it purely as a scanpath/learning-behavior variable and never invokes an attention-failure or meta-awareness literature (contrast with Kim et al., bor-sec-28, which explicitly cites Eastwood's and Fisher/Hamilton's attentional-failure accounts of boredom).
- **monotony / meaninglessness** — thin, tangential treatment only: the Introduction's opening complaint that click-stream analytics fail to capture "passive video watching" (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.1) gestures at a low-engagement viewing mode adjacent to monotonous consumption, but the paper never develops this as a construct, offers no induction method, and does not measure it directly.
- **situational vs. trait/state boredom** — not treated. The SPQ motivation measure is a validated instrument for approach-to-learning orientation (deep/surface), not for boredom state or trait, and the paper never frames it as either.
- **boredom-as-regulatory-signal (functional account) / mind-wandering / task-unrelated thought / temporality of boredom (time-drag) / profound/deep boredom / being-bored-by / being-bored-with** — not treated. No phenomenological or regulatory-affect vocabulary appears anywhere in this learning-analytics paper.

## 4. Measures & methods

**Apparatus.** An SMI RED 250 remote eye-tracker records gaze at 250 Hz (justified in-text via a "4Vs" big-data framing — volume, variety, velocity, veracity — of continuous gaze streams) (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.4). No EEG and no physiological channel (heart rate, GSR) appears anywhere in the study; the design combines one eye-tracking channel, one paper-based questionnaire, and one behavioral outcome test.

**Sample.** N=40 university students (12 female) from an unnamed "major European university," recruited on a single eligibility criterion: completion of an introductory Java course the previous semester, a prerequisite for the target "Functional Programming Principles in Scala" MOOC (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.4). Sample-size adequacy is defended by benchmarking against two systematic reviews spanning 85 eye-tracking studies, 84.71% of which used between 8 and 60 participants (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.4-5).

**Protocol.** Consent → Study Process Questionnaire (SPQ; Biggs, Kember & Leung 2001; 10 items, 5 deep/5 surface, 5-point Likert) → two MOOC video lectures (gaze recorded throughout) → an instructor-validated, Coursera-style multiple-choice quiz after each video (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.4).

**Gaze-variable construction** (Table 1, four levels of increasing semantic dependency):
- *Content coverage* (low semantics, stimulus-only): 10-second video slices; per-slice heat-maps; an image-subtraction-plus-connected-components algorithm isolates "attention points"; combined attention-point area per window operationalizes content coverage (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.5-6).
- *Scanpath variables* (medium semantics, AOI-dependent): **AOI misses** (an AOI never fixated during its on-screen period — ignored slide content) and **AOI backtracks** (a saccade to an already-visited, non-forward-reading-direction AOI — rereading-adjacent behavior, explicitly distinguished by the authors from literal text rereading in the Millis & King/Dowhower sense) (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.6-7).
- *Perceptual with-me-ness* (high semantics, AOI + teacher deictic gestures via dual-camera capture of face and hand/pen movement): computed from entry time, first-fixation duration, and revisit count relative to the referred site, combined arithmetically and normalized by total deictic-reference duration (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.7-9).
- *Conceptual with-me-ness* (highest semantics, AOI + teacher dialogue transcription): proportion of time spent looking at objects the teacher verbally references, normalized by slide duration (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.9-10).

**AI/prediction approach.** Per-slide gaze heat-maps (15 per participant, one per lecture slide) are passed through a pretrained VGG-19 convolutional network (Simonyan & Zisserman 2014) to extract 1,000 features per image; a non-overlapping sliding window (size 10) reduces this to 100 features; LASSO (Tibshirani 1996; 10-fold cross-validation for the shrinkage parameter) performs final feature selection (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.11-12). Five regression algorithms are benchmarked — Gaussian Process (linear/polynomial kernels), Support Vector Machine (linear/polynomial kernels), Random Forest, and Generalized Additive Model — selected for suitability to high-frequency, few-example datasets; an 80/20 train-test split (32/8 students) with 5-fold cross-validation on the training set controls selection bias; NRMSE (Pelánek 2015) is the evaluation metric (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.12).

## 5. Findings/Claims

- Content coverage fully mediates the motivation→learning relationship: motivation significantly predicts both content coverage and learning, but the direct motivation→learning path becomes non-significant once content coverage is added as a covariate (Table 2, row 1) (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.11, 13).
- AOI misses and AOI backtracks each only *partially* mediate motivation→learning: both scanpath variables are significantly predicted by motivation and significantly predict learning, but the direct motivation→learning path remains significant with the mediator included (Table 2, rows 2-3) (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.13).
- Perceptual and conceptual with-me-ness each fully mediate motivation→learning, matching the content-coverage pattern (Table 2, rows 4-5); conceptual with-me-ness shows a larger percentage-mediation effect than perceptual (39% vs. 33%) (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.13, 17).
- Good performers with high motivation show significantly fewer AOI misses, significantly more AOI backtracks, and greater content coverage/reading time than poor performers with low motivation — directly contradicting Byrne, Freebody, and Gates's (1992) finding that the best readers are the fastest, and instead aligning with Just and Carpenter's (1980) caution that "there is no single mode of reading" (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.15-16).
- Conceptual with-me-ness correlates with test score at r=0.36 (p<0.05), read as support for treating it as a gaze-based measure of a student's effort to sustain common ground with the teacher, drawing on Dillenbourg and Traum's (2006) grounding theory (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.16).
- Best learning-score prediction: 5.04% NRMSE (SD=0.52%), Gaussian Process with polynomial kernel; on the 10-question quiz scale this translates to "less than one question" of error (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.14-15, 17).
- Best motivation-score prediction: 9.04% NRMSE (SD=0.56%), same algorithm/kernel; second-best for both outcomes was SVM with polynomial kernel (8.07% learning, 10.98% motivation); worst was GAM (11.18% learning, 16.11% motivation) (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.14-15).
- Content coverage and scanpath variables are argued to be real-time/low-preprocessing computable; perceptual with-me-ness would need computer-vision gesture detection to be automated; conceptual with-me-ness requires manual dialogue transcription, making it the least ready of the four variables for real-time adaptive feedback (FAITHFUL-Sharma et al., p.17).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* This source's single most useful methodological parallel for Part III is structural rather than terminological: the Introduction's own framing problem — that click-stream/keystream analytics "do not always capture users' learning and behavior (e.g., passive video watching)" — is the same evidentiary gap Part III faces with self-report and behavioral-log measures of student boredom in VLE settings. Sharma et al.'s response (adding a stimulus-derived, multi-level gaze-variable framework rather than relying solely on interaction logs) offers Part III a template for triangulating a self-report boredom/motivation measure against independent behavioral or physiological channels, distinct from but complementary to the EEG-gaze triangulation modeled in Kim et al. (bor-sec-28) *(anticipatory-application)*.

*(anticipatory-application)* The "with-me-ness" construct — gaze-based teacher-student co-attention, split into perceptual (following deictic gesture) and conceptual (following verbal reference) components — is a plausible proxy instrument for measuring joint or shared attention in VLE co-watching contexts. Given the corrected Part III reading that the VLE's spatial-proximity-plus-voice co-watching design entails structural, group-assigned co-attention (rather than an individually chosen "WE"), with-me-ness-style measures could in principle test whether that structurally mandated co-attention is actually achieved at the level of gaze — a question this source's method could address empirically even though the source itself never discusses group-assignment or the WE/unmet-want framing *(anticipatory-application)*.

*(anticipatory-application)* The paper's own silence on "boredom" is itself an instructive methodological caution: a well-instrumented, AI-assisted motivation/attention study can proceed productively for nineteen pages without ever invoking boredom as a construct, which means motivation-deficit or low-engagement findings drawn from adjacent educational-technology literature should not be treated as automatically equivalent to a boredom finding — Part III should keep the vocabulary boundary explicit when citing STEM eye-tracking/AI sources that, like this one, target motivation and attention rather than boredom directly *(anticipatory-application)*.

*(anticipatory-application)* The VGG-19-plus-LASSO-plus-regression prediction pipeline, and its explicit real-time-computability gradient across gaze variables (content coverage and scanpath variables real-time-ready; perceptual with-me-ness automatable via computer vision; conceptual with-me-ness manual-transcription-bottlenecked), together offer a concrete engineering template for a future automated engagement/boredom-scoring channel in the not-yet-built Boredom Experiment (VR Attention Study) dataset entry — unconfirmed until that entry specifies its own instrumentation and whether it would even attempt an analogous AI-prediction layer *(anticipatory-application)*.
