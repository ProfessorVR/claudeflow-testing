# Miyauchi & Kawasaki (2018) — *Electroencephalogram Oscillations Support the Involvement of Task-Unrelated Thoughts in the Mechanism of Boredom: A Pilot Study*

**Type:** empirical pilot study (*Neuroscience Letters* 677:72–77, DOI 10.1016/j.neulet.2018.04.034) · **Discipline:** cognitive neuroscience / psychophysiology (EEG) · **Depth:** map (6pp) · **Corpus-role:** Strand-D EEG grounding unit; `cites-cluster-author` link to Raffaelli, Mills & Christoff (bor-sec-21, this cluster); prospective `grounds-measure` bridge to the not-yet-built *Boredom Experiment (VR Attention Study)* dataset's EEG channel.

## Thesis / Aim

Miyauchi and Kawasaki (M&K) test, at the level of oscillatory brain activity, a two-stage cognitive-process model of boredom — task-unrelated thoughts (TUTs) followed by an appraisal of those thoughts (Fig. 1, p.72 approx.) — credited to Raffaelli et al.'s proposal that boredom is a product of appraisals made about task-unrelated thoughts (paraphrase, p.72 approx.), the same review anchoring this cluster's Strand C (bor-sec-21). Their gap is temporal: prior EEG-boredom studies recorded activity *during* an already-established boring state, not at the moment boredom occurs, leaving the oscillatory signature of becoming-bored (versus being-bored) unknown (p.72–73 approx.). The design isolates this moment via a self-paced key-press marking the instant a participant first feels bored, contrasted against a "dislike" control that shares appraisal but not the TUT component (Fig. 1).

## Structure

1. **Introduction** (p.72 approx.) — definitional variability; boredom-as-attentional-failure; Raffaelli et al.'s TUT-appraisal model; DMN literature; prior EEG-alpha/beta boredom findings (Oswald; Tabatabaie et al.); the during-vs-onset gap; study aim.
2. **Materials and methods** (pp.73–74 approx.) — 2.1 Participants; 2.2 Task protocol (melody-switching paradigm); 2.3 Assessments (ASRS); 2.4 EEG recording and analyses (electrodes, artifact correction, wavelet time-frequency method, statistics).
3. **Results** (pp.74–75 approx.) — 3.1 Behaviour (response times); 3.2 Attention deficits (ASRS distribution); 3.3 EEG (alpha/theta/beta amplitude dynamics, condition contrasts, ASRS/RT correlations).
4. **Discussion** (pp.75–76 approx.) — interpretation of theta-as-TUT vs. alpha-as-appraisal; the ASRS/boredom-proneness discrepancy; limitations; conclusion.

## Constructs

- **Mind-wandering / task-unrelated thought** is the paper's organizing construct, named in its title and operationalized as the theorized first stage of boredom's cognitive process (p.72 approx., Fig. 1), adopting Raffaelli et al.'s TUT terminology over "mind-wandering," consistent with this cluster's Strand C entry for that source.
- **Attentional disengagement / meta-awareness** frames the study's motivating claim that boredom is related to attentional failure (paraphrase, p.72 approx.) and is operationalized indirectly through the ASRS attentional-deficit self-report and its correlation with the EEG and behavioral measures (pp.74–76 approx.).
- **Boredom proneness** surfaces only in the Discussion, where M&K flag their own ASRS–response-time correlation as apparently discrepant with prior literature linking boredom proneness to attention-deficit symptoms (pp.75–76 approx.) — see Findings below.
- **Situational vs. trait/state boredom** is an implicit but consequential framing: M&K distinguish their design, which measures the actual occurrence of boredom, from most prior studies, which use subjective trait-like boredom-proneness self-report (paraphrase, p.76 approx.). Flow/engagement, monotony/meaninglessness, and the FCM profound-boredom vocabulary are absent from this source; per Strand-D convention, no `bridges-to-fcm` edges are asserted.

## Measures & methods

Seventeen healthy Japanese volunteers were recruited (6F/11M, mean age 21.1±2.2, range 18–25); two were excluded (task misunderstanding; noisy EEG), leaving **N=15** (4F/11M, mean age 21.3±2.2) (p.73 approx.). Across three 5-minute sessions, seated with eyes closed in a sound-shielded room, participants heard novel, lyric-free melodies (~10 s each, mixed genres) and pressed one key when bored, a second when they disliked the melody; the melody then changed (p.73 approx.). Attentional-deficit tendency was assessed via the 18-item Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale v1.1 (ASRS; α=0.78) (p.73 approx.). EEG was recorded from 27 active scalp electrodes (actiCAP/BrainAmp DC, 1000 Hz, mastoid reference), with ocular artifacts removed via ICA components correlated with EOG (p<0.01); Morlet-wavelet time-frequency analysis covered 2–15 Hz (theta 6–8 Hz, alpha 10–12 Hz, beta 14–20 Hz), amplitude baseline-corrected against inter-melody intervals, over the 3.0 s window immediately preceding each key press (p.73–74 approx.). Statistics were Pearson correlations (EEG amplitude × ASRS score / response time) and Mann-Whitney U tests (boredom vs. dislike), both Bonferroni-corrected (p.74 approx.).

## Findings / Claims

1. Mean response time (RT) to feel bored was 35.33±3.01 s vs. 8.81±1.62 s for dislike, and the two RTs were uncorrelated (r=.43, p=.11), suggesting independent processes; boredom RT correlated positively with ASRS total score (r=.52, p<.05), dislike RT did not (r=.47, p=.07), and no sex or age effects emerged (p.74 approx., Fig. 2).
2. Transient alpha (10–12 Hz) enhancement appeared in right temporal electrodes (peak T8), and tonic theta (6–8 Hz) enhancement in frontal/temporal electrodes (peak FC2, T8), both peaking ~2.4 s before the key press, then dissipating 1.0–0.5 s before it (p.74–75 approx., Figs. 3–4). Only the tonic theta increase differentiated boredom from dislike (p<.01); phasic alpha and the theta/alpha decrements did not differ by condition (p.75 approx.).
3. Frontal/temporal theta amplitude correlated negatively with ASRS scores and with boredom RT at several pre-press latencies; alpha showed no ASRS correlation but a positive correlation with RT (p.75 approx., Fig. 5). M&K interpret tonic theta as the boredom-specific TUT signal, and phasic alpha as shared appraisal/internal-processing activity common to both conditions (pp.75–76 approx.).
4. The positive ASRS–boredom-RT correlation (higher attention-deficit tendency, slower to feel bored) is presented as apparently contradicting prior work linking boredom proneness to attention-deficit symptoms; M&K reconcile this via the negative theta–ASRS correlation and by arguing their design measures real-time boredom occurrence rather than trait boredom-proneness (p.76 approx.).
5. Authors' own limitations: no emotional-preference control; ASRS as an unverified proxy for neural attentional deficit; small, gender-imbalanced sample; alternative (e.g., memory-based) mechanisms not excluded; causal confirmation (e.g., via TMS) deferred to future work (p.76 approx.).

## Analytical role

*(P/anticipatory-application) — the following are our interpretive links to Part III and the future dataset entry, not claims M&K make themselves.*

Most directly, this source is the strongest available candidate for `grounds-measure` bridging into the not-yet-built *Boredom Experiment (VR Attention Study)* corpus entry's EEG channel: tonic frontal/temporal theta (6–8 Hz) as a boredom-specific marker, distinct from phasic alpha shared with a negative-appraisal control, offers a concrete, if pilot-scale (N=15), hypothesis for what an EEG channel in that future dataset might look for. **`****** UNVERIFIED:`** no such dataset entry exists as of 2026-07-14, so this mapping is a plausible future grounding, not a confirmed one.

Second, the theta/alpha dissociation offers a candidate discriminating signature between being genuinely bored and merely disliking a stimulus, relevant wherever a future physiological channel needs to distinguish TUT-driven boredom from generic negative affect rather than treating "bored" and "displeased" survey responses as interchangeable.

Third, the ASRS–boredom-proneness discrepancy this paper reports against prior trait literature is a small-scale instance of the operationalization inconsistency Raffaelli et al. (bor-sec-21) diagnose field-wide: a real-time, attentional-deficit-linked latency-to-feel-bored measure and a trait boredom-proneness self-report scale are not interchangeable and may correlate in opposite directions, a distinction Part III's survey should keep explicit.

Finally, given the pilot-scale sample (N=15) and single-paradigm design, Part III should treat these electrode/frequency findings as hypothesis-generating, not a validated biomarker for direct reuse.
