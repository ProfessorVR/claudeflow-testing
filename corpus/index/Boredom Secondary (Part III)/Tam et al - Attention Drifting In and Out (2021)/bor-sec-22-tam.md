# bor-sec-22 — Tam, Katy Y. Y., Wijnand A. P. van Tilburg, Christian S. Chan, Eric R. Igou, and Hakwan Lau, "Attention Drifting In and Out: The Boredom Feedback Model" (2021)

**Type:** theoretical review / model-building article (empirical-behavioral psychology, non-EEG) · **Discipline:** personality and social psychology, integrating attentional, functional, and cognitive-appraisal accounts of boredom · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** Strand C — the cluster's central psychological-model unit; proposes the Boredom Feedback Model (BFM), a componential feedback-loop account built on "inadequate attentional engagement" (IAE), and explicitly positions itself against the Meaning and Attentional Components (MAC) model and in line with Elpidorou's regulatory/functional account (bor-sec-25/26 in this cluster).

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Tam, van Tilburg, Chan, Igou, and Lau (*Personality and Social Psychology Review* 25.3, 2021, pp.251-272) synthesize established and emerging research on boredom to propose the Boredom Feedback Model (BFM): a psychological feedback loop centered on attention shifts instigated by inadequate attentional engagement (IAE), defined as the discrepancy between one's actual (objectively measurable) and subjectively desired levels of attentional engagement (FE, p.251-252). The authors argue that this integrative, process-oriented account resolves five long-standing unresolved issues in the boredom literature — coping and regulation, the relation between boredom and self-control, how attention and meaning jointly produce boredom, why boredom co-occurs with both high- and low-arousal emotions, and what constitutes chronic boredom (boredom proneness) — by treating antecedents, experiences, and consequences of boredom as stages within a single recursive attentional process rather than as separately theorized phenomena (FE, p.251-252, 256). The model is explicitly not offered as a new definition of boredom but as an integrative synthesis of existing empirical knowledge that generates novel, testable hypotheses (FAITHFUL-Tam et al., p.256).

## 3. Constructs

Mapped onto the controlled construct axis, the source treats:

- **profound/deep boredom (FCM 3rd form)** — not treated. No citation of Heidegger, *Being and Time*, or *The Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics* appears anywhere in the article; the paper's vocabulary is drawn entirely from empirical-behavioral and cognitive psychology.
- **being-bored-by (1st form) / being-bored-with (2nd form)** — not treated, for the same reason; the tripartite Heideggerian structure of boredom is simply absent from this literature.
- **situational vs. trait/state boredom** — explicitly adopted as the paper's organizing distinction: "This review focuses on boredom as a transient affective state" (FAITHFUL-Tam et al., p.251), with trait boredom (boredom proneness) reserved for the fifth unresolved issue and treated as a distinct, chronic phenomenon (FE, p.256, 264-265).
- **boredom proneness** — given sustained, independent treatment in "Implications for Chronic Boredom": BFM proposes it reflects either trait-like attentional weakness or malleable, non-trait "long-term factors" (e.g., lacking a compelling life goal), a novel bifurcation not previously drawn in the literature (FAITHFUL-Tam et al., p.264-265).
- **mind-wandering / task-unrelated thought** — one of BFM's three named attention-shift routes ("turn inward, e.g., mind-wandering, self-reflection"), and independently linked to boredom proneness ("positively with mind-wandering... and negatively with flow proneness"; FAITHFUL-Tam et al., p.256, 264).
- **attentional disengagement / meta-awareness** — the conceptual core of the model: IAE is defined and operationalized via DAN/DMN network balance or dual-task performance cost (FAITHFUL-Tam et al., p.256).
- **boredom-as-regulatory-signal (functional account)** — BFM is explicitly positioned as consistent with, and complementary to, functional accounts (Bench & Lench, 2013; Elpidorou, 2018a; Van Tilburg & Igou, 2012): "boredom serves a vital function of prompting individuals to direct their attention to... something that is of value" (FAITHFUL-Tam et al., p.263, 265-266).
- **flow / engagement (contrast pole)** — engagement (not flow by name) is the master term throughout; "humans desire to be optimally engaged" is the paper's foundational premise (FAITHFUL-Tam et al., p.256), and flow proneness appears once as a boredom-proneness correlate (FE, p.264, citing Harris, 2000).
- **monotony / meaninglessness** — both are treated at length as cognitive-appraisal antecedents of IAE: repetition/perceived monotony (O'Hanlon, 1981; Daschmann et al., 2011) and meaninglessness (Fahlman et al., 2009; Van Tilburg & Igou, 2012, 2017a) each independently lower one's "intention to attend" (FAITHFUL-Tam et al., p.258-259).
- **temporality of boredom (time-drag)** — listed among boredom's characteristic experiential features ("time passing slowly"; FAITHFUL-Tam et al., p.259-260, citing London & Monello, 1974 and Martin et al., 2006) but not developed into an independent theoretical mechanism the way Husserlian retention/protention is in Feldges (bor-sec-16).

## 5. Findings/Claims

- IAE — the discrepancy between actual (objectively measurable) and subjectively desired levels of attentional engagement — is proposed as "a key condition for boredom" that "instigates attention shifts" forming the feedback process underlying the emotion (FAITHFUL-Tam et al., p.256).
- Robust convergent evidence (correlational, psychophysiological, neuropsychological) supports the IAE-boredom link: attentional errors on the Sustained Attention to Response Task correlate with self-reported boredom before and after task blocks (Hunter & Eastwood, 2016); boredom co-occurs with rising heart rate and falling skin conductance, the inverse of attentionally engaged states (Merrifield & Danckert, 2014); and an fMRI study found the same anticorrelated DMN/anterior-insula activation pattern in both boredom-induction and sustained-attention conditions, distinct from the interest-mood condition (Danckert & Merrifield, 2018) (FAITHFUL-Tam et al., p.255-257).
- Even "objectively" engaging, high-demand activities (video games, piloting drones) can still produce boredom if the desired level of engagement exceeds the actual level — IAE, not low engagement per se, is the operative condition (FAITHFUL-Tam et al., p.256-257).
- Boredom antecedents bifurcate into two scenarios: IAE "with something to do" (governed by the interaction of attentional resource, intention to attend, task characteristics, cognitive appraisals, and other internal factors — only the high-resource/high-intention quadrant avoids boredom) and IAE "with nothing in particular to do" (an absence of both environmental constraint and a desired target of engagement) (FAITHFUL-Tam et al., p.257-260).
- When bored, attention shifts along three non-mutually-exclusive routes — outward (to unrelated stimuli, novelty-seeking, snacking, risk-taking, prosocial/symbolic responses), inward (mind-wandering, daydreaming, nostalgic reverie), or back to the source (cognitive reappraisal, goal reminders, task restructuring) — and if the resulting attentional target is inadequate, the loop recurs (FAITHFUL-Tam et al., p.259-261).
- The feedback loop amplifies over time through operant and classical conditioning: avoidance strategies (e.g., reaching for a smartphone) that successfully reduce boredom are negatively reinforced, and eventually a mere drop in attentional engagement becomes a sufficient conditioned stimulus to trigger avoidance, independent of actual boredom level (FAITHFUL-Tam et al., p.261).
- Contra Westgate and Wilson's (2018) MAC model, which treats attention and meaning as orthogonal predictors of boredom (allowing "meaningless boredom" under full engagement), BFM holds it is "impossible for people to feel bored while... adequately engaged in something" — meaning operates as a precursor to IAE rather than an independent axis (FAITHFUL-Tam et al., p.263).
- The self-control/boredom relationship is explained via three integrated mechanisms: effortful redirection of attention back to a task depletes self-regulatory resources (strength model, Baumeister & Vohs, 2016); the feedback loop shifts priorities from "have-to" to "want-to" goals (process model, Inzlicht & Schmeichel, 2012); and low-level (vs. high-level) construals of the task increase both boredom and self-control failure (construal-level account, Fujita, 2008) (FAITHFUL-Tam et al., p.261-263). The paper further proposes that some findings attributed to "ego-depletion" may actually reflect failure to attain IAE rather than prior self-control effort (FE, p.263).
- Whether boredom manifests as high-arousal (frustration, anger, anxiety) or low-arousal (apathy, sadness, loneliness) depends on where repeated failed attentional engagement directs attention — e.g., rumination on negative thoughts yields low-arousal states, reaching out to others yields loneliness upon realizing relational discrepancies (FAITHFUL-Tam et al., p.263-264).
- Chronic boredom (boredom proneness) is proposed to result from sustained dysfunction of the feedback loop, attributable to two distinguishable factors: trait-like attentional weakness (chronic hypo-/hyper-sensitivity to stimulation) and non-trait "long-term factors" (not knowing what one wants to engage with in life, or being unable to pursue it) — a distinction the authors say prior research has not drawn, with divergent intervention implications (attention training vs. life-goal/meaning-focused intervention) (FAITHFUL-Tam et al., p.264-265).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* BFM's core IAE construct — a *discrepancy* between desired and actual engagement, rather than low engagement per se — offers Part III a conceptual caution for interpreting any attention or engagement self-report item: two respondents reporting identical "low engagement" scores could be in qualitatively different states (a low-desire respondent experiencing no discrepancy and thus no boredom, versus a high-desire respondent experiencing a large discrepancy and thus acute boredom), and Part III's survey analysis should consider whether desired-engagement level was ever assessed as a moderating variable rather than assuming raw engagement scores map directly onto boredom *(anticipatory-application)*.

*(anticipatory-application)* The model's account of chronic boredom as bifurcating into trait-like attentional weakness versus malleable "long-term factors" (not knowing what one wants to engage with, or being unable to pursue it) offers Part III a useful interpretive frame for individual-difference variation in its own strata: elevated boredom in the deployment/2D-PC pole might, per BFM's logic, reflect either a stable individual disposition or a context-specific absence of a compelling desired target of engagement, and these two readings carry different implications for whether the design (versus the individual) is the appropriate locus of intervention *(anticipatory-application)*.

*(anticipatory-application)* BFM's feedback-loop account of boredom amplifying through operant/classical conditioning over repeated failed attentional-engagement attempts is a plausible mechanism for any observed relationship in Part III's data between longer or repeated exposure (e.g., extended VLE sessions, multi-session designs) and increasing self-reported boredom or disengagement — the model would predict that early inadequate engagement, if uncorrected, should compound rather than plateau *(anticipatory-application)*.

*(anticipatory-application)* This source is the strongest candidate in the currently-built portion of Strand C for a `grounds-measure` bridge to the not-yet-built Boredom Experiment (VR Attention Study) dataset, since its two behavioral operationalizations of attentional engagement — dual-task performance cost (RT/error) and DAN/DMN network balance — map plausibly onto attention-based measures a VR attention study would likely collect. *(anticipatory-application)* Finally, BFM's explicit self-positioning as consistent with, rather than opposed to, functional/regulatory accounts of boredom (Bench & Lench, 2013; Elpidorou, 2018a, 2018b) — the same theoretical lineage this cluster builds as bor-sec-25/26 — suggests Part III can cite Tam et al. and Elpidorou's regulatory-theory papers together as a converging, non-competing empirical-psychological warrant for treating boredom as functionally meaningful (a "guiding" or self-regulatory signal) rather than as a mere deficit state, complementing rather than substituting for this cluster's phenomenological (Strand A/B) treatment of boredom's meaning-disclosive role *(anticipatory-application)*.
