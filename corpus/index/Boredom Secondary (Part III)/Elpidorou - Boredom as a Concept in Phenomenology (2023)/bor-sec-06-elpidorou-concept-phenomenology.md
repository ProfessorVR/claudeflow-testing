# Elpidorou (2023) — *Boredom, as a Concept in Phenomenology*

**Type:** encyclopedia entry (*Encyclopedia of Phenomenology*, ed. de Warren & Toadvine, Springer) · **Discipline:** philosophy — phenomenology of boredom, tri-partite survey · **Depth:** map (~8pp) · **Corpus-role:** Strand-A compact survey anchor; the cluster-hub author's own overview of the field, positioning Heidegger's FCM as boredom's "most sustained and detailed" phenomenological treatment against two other candidates (Husserl, Sartre). Filename note: the source PDF is misspelled "Boredome"; the in-text title and running head both read "Boredom, as a Concept in Phenomenology," confirmed directly against the byline page.

## Thesis / Aim

The entry's stated aim is to present three different phenomenological perspectives on boredom so as to show not just what boredom is, according to phenomenology, but what a phenomenology of boredom can reveal about human existence (p.1) (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou, p.1). It opens by staking out boredom's stakes for phenomenology generally: boredom gives rise to perceptions of meaninglessness, difficulties in effective agency, lapses of attention, an altered perception of the passage of time, and a diverse array of behavioral outcomes, and it demarcates the engaging and meaningful from what is not (p.1) (FE). Despite Heidegger's landmark 1929–30 treatment in *The Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics* (FCM), the entry frames boredom as historically under-attended within phenomenology relative to how well-suited the method is to explicate it (p.1) (FE).

## Structure

1. **Definition / Introduction** (p.1) — boredom's phenomenological stakes and the three-perspective plan.
2. **Boredom and Husserl** (pp.1–3) — an original construction, not Husserl's own topic, of boredom via inner time-consciousness (retention, primal impression, protention, the "living present").
3. **Boredom and Heidegger** (pp.3–6) — FCM's three forms (being-bored-by, being-bored-with, profound boredom), keyed to the *Befindlichkeit*/*Grundstimmung* background of *Being and Time* (BT).
4. **Boredom and Sartre** (pp.6–8) — *Nausea*'s Roquentin and the *Sketch for a Theory of Emotions* (STE, which never itself discusses boredom) extended into a functional/purposive account, including the apathetic/agitated distinction.
5. **References** (pp.8–9).

## Constructs

- **Temporality of boredom (time-drag)** — the entry's connective thread across all three thinkers: Husserl's account grounds the "stalling" of time in a blurred living present (pp.1–3); Heidegger's three forms are each defined by a distinctive mode of time's passing, from a bounded dragging interval (first form) to originary temporality's total collapse (third form) (pp.4–6) (FE).
- **Being-bored-by (1st form)** — glossed as the "most familiar," ordinary-experience form (waiting for an appointment, an empty room, a repetitive task), leaving us empty and holding us in limbo through a slower passage of time and deferred goals (p.4) (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou, p.4).
- **Being-bored-with (2nd form)** — the dinner-party example: an activity that is pleasant in the moment yet recognized retrospectively as boring because it was not "our own," an inauthentic following of a social ideal, which transforms our relation to time rather than merely dragging it (pp.4–5) (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou, p.4).
- **Profound/deep boredom (FCM 3rd form)** — treated at greatest length: the impersonal "it is boring for one," the "undifferentiated no one," and the total withdrawal of significance from past, present, and future alike, disclosed as a revelatory *Grundstimmung* rather than a pathology to be evaded (pp.5–6) (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou, p.5).
- **Monotony / meaninglessness** — recurs across all three registers: Husserl's empty/unmemorable "now," Heidegger's banal dinner party and the total withdrawal of significance, and Sartre's "insignificant, banal, or meaningless" lecture material (pp.3, 5, 7) (FE).
- **Attentional disengagement / meta-awareness** — central to the Husserlian construction: boredom is glossed as consciousness "not greatly affected by" or failing to attend to its object, weakening primal impression, retention, and protention together (pp.2–3) (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou, p.2).
- **Mind-wandering / task-unrelated thought** — appears explicitly, though only in the Sartre section's bored-student vignette: boredom motivates the bored subject "to mind wander, to doodle" (p.7), among other escape behaviors (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou, p.7).
- **Boredom-as-regulatory-signal (functional account)** — the entry's Sartrean payoff: boredom is read as "an embodied and unreflective attempt to resolve some pressing difficulty" by transforming the agent's appraisal of a situation, an "active attempt to re-establish a proper cognitive relationship to the world" rather than passive deficit (pp.6–8) (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou, p.8), explicitly continuous with the entry's own earlier regulatory-theory paper (cited at p.3 and p.8; **cites-cluster-author** → bor-sec-25).
- **Situational vs. trait/state boredom** — touched only in passing, via the citation of individuals with a "high propensity to boredom" who show characteristic duration-estimation errors — a trait-adjacent aside subordinated to the entry's dominantly situational/state analyses (p.1) (FE).
- **Flow / engagement (contrast pole)** — never named as such (no citation of Csikszentmihalyi or "flow"), but functions throughout as boredom's implicit negative-defining contrast: boring objects are repeatedly those that fail to "engage," "engross," or "absorb" us (pp.2, 7) (FE).

## Reading of FCM

The Heidegger section is compressed exposition, not extension or contestation: it restates FCM's own architecture (three forms, keyed to *Leergelassenheit*/being-left-empty and *Hingehaltenheit*/being-held-in-limbo) without disputing it or pressing it toward a novel thesis (pp.3–6) (FE). Its treatment of the first form (**fcm-04-first-form**, FCM §§19–23, book pp.78–105) paraphrases Heidegger's own diagnosis that this is boredom's "most familiar" register, tied to a determinate boring thing and a bounded, dragging interval of waiting (p.4) — a faithful compression of fcm-04's structural apparatus without engaging its methodological detail (§22's anti-consciousness directive, the train-station scene) (P/anticipatory-application: this unit's own pointer from Elpidorou's brief gloss to fcm-04's fuller treatment, not a claim Elpidorou makes about that unit's specific sections). Its treatment of the second form (**fcm-05-second-form**, FCM §§24–28, book pp.106–131) stays close to Heidegger's own dinner-party example and the retrospective, "arises from Dasein itself" character of this form (p.4) (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou, p.4), again without engaging FCM's own "standing present" (*stehendes Jetzt*) terminology.

Its treatment of the third form (**fcm-06-third-form**, FCM §§29–36, book pp.132–159) is the entry's center of gravity: the impersonal "es ist einem langweilig" (p.5, quoting Heidegger 1995, p.134), the "undifferentiated no one" (p.5, quoting p.135), the merging of the three temporal dimensions into an "unarticulated unity" (p.5, quoting p.148), and the "telling refusal" (*Versagen*) that becomes a perspicacious "telling" (*Sagen*) disclosing Dasein's freedom or ability-to-be (p.6, citing pp.140, 148–52) (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou, pp.5–6). The entry does not, however, take up FCM's further and historically specific move — the "particular profound boredom" of *contemporary* Dasein discussed in FCM §§37–38 (**fcm-07-particular-profound**, book pp.160–168) — and no bridge to that unit is warranted here; Elpidorou's own citations top out within fcm-06's chapter range even where he labels a passage "§38" (p.6), an apparent internal inconsistency in the source's own citation, not a citation of fcm-07's distinct material (P/anticipatory-application: our own cross-check against the FCM unit boundaries, not a claim the source makes about its own citation accuracy).

## Findings / Claims

1. **A Husserlian account of boredom is constructible though Husserl never offers one.** Boredom's hallmark — an object or situation failing to "engage us in a satisfactory manner" — weakens the primal impression and, with it, retention and protention together, blurring the tripartite living present; this blurring *is* the phenomenon of time "dragging" (*Langeweile*, "long while") (pp.2–3) (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou, p.3). Elpidorou treats this as consistent with, though not proof of, empirical findings linking boredom to both attentional difficulty and perceived meaninglessness (p.3) (FE).
2. **Heidegger's account is unique for its ontological and historical ambition.** Of the three phenomenologists surveyed, only Heidegger treats boredom (specifically its profound form) as a fundamental, revelatory mood to be *cultivated* rather than evaded — evasion is framed as antithetical to "philosophizing proper" (p.6) (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou, p.6).
3. **Sartre's STE, though silent on boredom by name, straightforwardly extends to it.** Given Sartre's functional, "magical" account of emotion — an embodied transformation of how a difficulty is appraised, not a change in the world itself (p.6) (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou, p.6) — boredom becomes a purposive strategy that relieves an agent of an unmeetable demand for cognitive engagement, illustrated by a bored student who reappraises difficult lecture material as meaningless rather than continuing to struggle with it (p.7) (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou, p.7).
4. **A finer-grained typology follows from the Sartrean extension.** Citing Greenson (1953), the entry distinguishes *apathetic* boredom (withdrawal, feels like apathy) from *agitated* boredom (restless, an unsuccessful attempt at renewed engagement), while holding that both share the same underlying problem: a situation that fails to meet the subject's demand for satisfactory cognitive engagement (p.7) (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou, p.7).
5. **A unifying, synthetic thesis emerges across the three readings**, though the entry never states it as such in one place: each phenomenological register cashes out the same core failure — an object or situation's failure to engage/absorb the subject — differently (as a breakdown of time-consciousness for Husserl, an ontologically revelatory mood for Heidegger, an embodied purposive response for Sartre) (pp.1–8) (P/anticipatory-application: this is our own synthetic reading across the entry's three sections, offered as a compact summary, not a sentence the source itself states).
