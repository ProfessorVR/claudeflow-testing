# bor-sec-27 — Barry, Robert J., Adam R. Clarke, Stuart J. Johnstone, Christopher A. Magee, and Jacqueline A. Rushby, "EEG Differences Between Eyes-Closed and Eyes-Open Resting Conditions" (2007)

**Type:** empirical psychophysiology / EEG-methodology article (STEM; not a boredom study) · **Discipline:** clinical neurophysiology / psychophysiology (Brain & Behaviour Research Institute, University of Wollongong) · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** Strand D — a foundational EEG-baseline-methodology paper. ## 1. Thesis/Aim

Barry, Clarke, Johnstone, Magee, and Rushby (*Clinical Neurophysiology* 118, 2007, pp.2765-2773) ask whether the well-documented reduction in EEG alpha activity from eyes-closed to eyes-open rest reflects one undifferentiated process ("cortical activation" broadly construed) or two separable processes: a global change in **arousal** (the organism's current energetic level) and a topographically-focused change in **activation** (task/stimulus-specific mobilization of energy for processing), following a distinction the same laboratory had argued for previously (FAITHFUL-Barry et al., ~p.2765-2766). Using simultaneous EEG (four bands, nine scalp regions) and skin conductance level (SCL) across alternating eyes-closed/eyes-open blocks, they aim to identify which EEG measure(s) index arousal versus activation, and to recommend a baseline-choice practice for EEG researchers (FE, ~p.2765, 2771-2772).

## 4. Measures & methods

The paper's contribution to this cluster is methodological, so this section substitutes for a "Reading of FCM" with the study's apparatus and design.

**Participants.** N = 28 University of Wollongong undergraduates (18 female), mean age 19.7 (range 17-46), 26 right-handed; screened for neurological/psychiatric conditions; abstained from caffeine and psychoactive substances ≥4 hours pre-test (FAITHFUL-Barry et al., ~p.2767).

**Recording.** SCL recorded continuously at 64 Hz from Ag/AgCl electrodes on digits II/III of the non-dominant hand (0.5 V constant voltage). EEG recorded from 19 scalp sites per the International 10-20 system, linked-ears reference, with EOG for eye-movement monitoring; bandpass 0-30 Hz, 16-bit A/D at 512 Hz, impedance <5 kΩ (FAITHFUL-Barry et al., ~p.2767).

**Procedure.** 32 minutes of alternating 2-minute eyes-closed/eyes-open blocks, tone-cued, with eyes-open blocks fixating a screen cross. Analysis used the first three and last three blocks as tone-bracketed "triplets," allowing an order/sequence-controlled average of each condition at an early and a late point in the session (FAITHFUL-Barry et al., ~p.2767-2768).

**Extraction and analysis.** 2-second epochs (artifact- and cue-response-contaminated epochs discarded); absolute power via FFT (Welch window) in delta (1.5-3.5 Hz), theta (4-7.5 Hz), alpha (8-13 Hz), beta (13.5-25 Hz). SCL and each band were examined via 2×2 (condition × time) repeated-measures ANOVA; electrodes were pooled into 9 topographic regions and submitted to a 3-way condition × sagittal × lateral ANOVA with planned contrasts, with vector scaling (McCarthy and Wood, 1985) applied to confirm any condition-by-topography interaction was genuine rather than a scaling artifact (FAITHFUL-Barry et al., ~p.2768).

## 5. Findings/Claims

- Eyes-closed SCL correlated negatively with mean alpha at the first (r=-.37, p=.032) and second (r=-.36, p=.035) periods, but not significantly by the third (r=-.11), replicating the authors' prior SCL-alpha arousal linkage between subjects (FAITHFUL-Barry et al., ~p.2769).
- SCL increased significantly eyes-closed→eyes-open (F=5.53, p<.05); all four bands showed significant across-scalp power reductions (delta F=46.47; theta F=23.42; alpha F=15.12; beta F=8.71; all p<.01-.001), with no time or interaction effects (FAITHFUL-Barry et al., ~p.2769).
- Delta and theta reductions were significantly *larger* in specific regions (delta: frontal/midline-right-central; theta: posterior) rather than uniform — focal, activation-consistent changes superimposed on the global reduction (FAITHFUL-Barry et al., ~p.2769-2770).
- Alpha was the outlier: its ~40% reduction was uniform at every site, with no condition-by-topography interaction surviving vector scaling — consistent with a purely global, non-regional (arousal) process (FAITHFUL-Barry et al., ~p.2770).
- Beta showed the most complex pattern: significant posterior/right-hemisphere reduction accompanied by a small but reliable left-frontal *increase* (Fp1, Fp2, F7, F8, T3), so the net omnibus reduction masks opposite-signed focal effects (FAITHFUL-Barry et al., ~p.2770).
- The alpha change score correlated negatively with the SCL change score (r=-.32, p=.047), closing the loop on alpha as the confirmed arousal index (FAITHFUL-Barry et al., ~p.2771).
- Conclusion: alpha indexes global arousal; topographically-focal delta/theta/beta changes index activation. Eyes-closed and eyes-open are therefore non-equivalent baselines — the authors recommend eyes-closed as the default arousal baseline and eyes-open as the default activation baseline for tasks involving visual processing or fixation (including ERP paradigms), since treating the two as equivalent risks misattributing activation-driven topographic change to whatever process is under study (FAITHFUL-Barry et al., ~p.2771-2772).
