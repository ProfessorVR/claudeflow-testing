## 1. Thesis/Aim

The article presents Heidegger's three forms of boredom (*Langeweile*) — "bored *by* …," "bored *with* …," and the impersonal "one is bored" — together with the two structural modes he isolates for each, *Hingehaltenheit* ("darnos largas," being-held-in-limbo) and *Leergelassenheit* ("dejarnos vacíos," being-left-empty), as developed in Heidegger's 1929–30 Freiburg lectures (i.e., FCM). Its distinctive contribution is methodological: it interweaves these phenomenological structures with five narrative films, "transcending the sign-bound limits of writing" (FE, p.204) so that cinema itself becomes an interpretive vehicle for reading modernity's entertainment culture. The authors take boredom's essence — *aburribilidad*/*Langweiligkeit* — to be the *Grundstimmung* (fundamental attunement) of the present era, and read cultural entertainment and technological "overflow" as symptomatic of a modern disease that estranges Dasein from encounter, questioning, and self-care (*cuidado de sí*) (FE, p.194). A framing commitment runs through the whole piece: moods are not internal states produced by an external stimulus and awaiting clinical measurement, but structures that disclose a world; the authors explicitly reject the "scientistic" impulse to "name and calculate" a psychological cause behind an attunement (FE, pp.195–196).

## 2. Structure

The article has no numbered sections; it unfolds as seven movements.

1. **Introduction** (pp.195–198) — grounds *Langeweile* in *Being and Time*'s *Befindlichkeit* (affective disposedness); rejects causal-psychological readings of mood via Wittgenstein's "world of the happy/unhappy" and Heidegger's "mood is not a mere accompanying tint of experience" (p.196).
2. **Historical brief** (*Breviario histórico-conceptual del aburrimiento*, pp.198–203) — a genealogy of boredom's marginalization in Western thought: monastic *acedia*, Robert Burton's melancholy, Enlightenment duty/Kant, Huysmans's decadence, Pascal, Kierkegaard (via Svendsen), Romantic-era literary *ennui* (Baudelaire, Hugo, Lamartine), Durkheim.
3. **First form** — "*aburrimiento por…*" (pp.203–207), read through Gus Van Sant's *My Own Private Idaho* (1991) and Andrei Tarkovsky's short *The Killers* (*Ubiytsy*, 1956, after Hemingway).
4. **Second form** — "*aburrimiento en…*" (pp.207–211), read through John Huston's *The Dead* (1987, after Joyce) and Ingmar Bergman's *Hour of the Wolf* (*Vargtimmen*, 1968).
5. **Third form** — "*uno se aburre*" / profound boredom (pp.211–214), read through Luchino Visconti's *White Nights* (*Le notti bianche*, 1957, after Dostoevsky), followed by an excursus on kairological time and the *Augenblick* (moment of vision) via *Being and Time* §45 (pp.214–216).
6. **Technology/modernity coda** (*Ocupación de sí y relaciones tecnológicas*, pp.216–219) — Heidegger's technology critique, the "technological overflow" (*desbordamiento tecnológico*) thesis, brief gestures at science-fiction cinema, and Méndez's "metaontology."
7. **Conclusion** (pp.219–220) — the three forms as horizontally co-present rather than hierarchically staged; boredom as opening onto the question of Being.

## 3. Constructs

- **being-bored-by (1st form)** — the article's most "everyday and recognizable" form (FE, p.203), staged through waiting-for-an-event: the traveler on the empty road in *My Own Private Idaho*, the hitmen waiting on a clock in *The Killers* (FAITHFUL-HERNÁNDEZ ALBARRACÍN, pp.204–207).
- **being-bored-with (2nd form)** — the form that "gives itself time" rather than fighting it; the pastime becomes indistinguishable from the situation itself (the Dublin dinner party, the vampiric castle soirée) (FAITHFUL-HERNÁNDEZ ALBARRACÍN, pp.207–211).
- **profound/deep boredom (FCM 3rd form)** — "*es ist einem langweilig*," staged through the anonymous stroller of *White Nights* on a silent Sunday, for whom no pastime is even available (FAITHFUL-HERNÁNDEZ ALBARRACÍN, pp.211–214).
- **situational vs. trait/state boredom** — the article does not use this vocabulary but structurally pre-empts it: it insists *aburribilidad* (boringness) is not a "state" of the subject caused by an external stimulus and diagnosable "psychologically" (as with neurosis), but an ontological structure of Dasein's being-in-the-world (FE, pp.195–196).
- **temporality of boredom (time-drag)** — the connective construct across all three forms: *Zeitvertreib* (pastime) as "driving time along" (p.205); clock-checking as a "helpless" symptom of failed pastime (p.206); the kairological instant (*Augenblick*) contrasted with chronological/clock time in the post-third-form excursus (pp.214–216) (FAITHFUL-HERNÁNDEZ ALBARRACÍN, pp.204–206, 214–216).
- **monotony/meaninglessness** — modern technological and entertainment culture diagnosed as producing a meaninglessness it simultaneously markets as escape from boredom (FE, pp.216–219).
- **flow/engagement (contrast pole)** — appears only negatively: the Bergman dinner-party "pastime" is itself shown relapsing into boredom rather than escaping it, undercutting any simple engagement/boredom binary (FE, pp.209–211).
- **boredom-as-regulatory-signal (functional account)** — explicitly contested. The authors reject reducing mood to an internal event with a specific cause "to be analyzed psychologically in order to determine and measure the factors that forged [a] neurosis" (FE, p.195).
- **mind-wandering/task-unrelated thought** and **attentional disengagement/meta-awareness** — glancingly present as phenomenal description (looking away "toward nothing in particular," p.206; the *White Nights* stroller's diffuse observation, p.213) but never thematized in cognitive-science terms.

Not treated: **boredom proneness** — a psychometric-trait construct entirely absent from this phenomenological register.

## 4. Reading of FCM

The article does not reconstruct FCM's structural machinery; it takes each form's headline result as an interpretive key and supplies a cinematic instance, consistent with a piece whose stated method is hermeneutic-phenomenological rather than exegetical (FE, p.204). Its first-form reading (*My Own Private Idaho*, *The Killers*) corresponds to fcm-04-first-form's *Gelangweiltwerden von etwas* — the train-station apparatus of *Hingehaltenheit*/*Leergelassenheit* bound to a determinate situation and thing (FCM book pp.78–105) — though the source substitutes a lonely highway and a bar for Heidegger's own station scene without altering the structural claim (P/anticipatory-application: this unit's own mapping, not a citation the source makes explicit at the level of section numbers). Its second-form reading (*The Dead*, *Hour of the Wolf*) corresponds to fcm-05-second-form's *Sichlangweilen bei etwas* (FCM book pp.106–131), where the pastime dissolves into "giving oneself time" for the occasion itself, producing an extended, self-enclosed present cut off from a determinate past and future (FE, p.211, echoing Heidegger's own §26–27 result). Its third-form reading (*White Nights*) corresponds to fcm-06-third-form's *es ist einem langweilig* (FCM book pp.132–159), where no pastime is even available and beings-as-a-whole refuse themselves — the article's own gloss that the stroller's Sunday affords "no possibility of doing nor any possibility of omitting" (FAITHFUL-HERNÁNDEZ ALBARRACÍN, p.213) directly echoes fcm-06's totalizing refusal.

Most consequentially, the article's own framing thesis — that *aburribilidad*/*Langweiligkeit* names "the fundamental mood (*Grundstimmung*) of our era" (FE, p.194) — is not an extension the authors invent but a direct restatement of fcm-07-particular-profound's own subject matter: the question of "a *particular* profound boredom" as "the fundamental attunement of our contemporary Dasein" (FCM book pp.160–168). The source does not cite this material by FCM section number, but its diagnostic register (technological/entertainment "overflow" as the symptom calling for the epoch's boredom to be *awakened* rather than fled) reproduces fcm-07's own diagnostic ambition almost exactly (P/anticipatory-application).

One methodological point is worth flagging for the concordance: the source's rejection of causal and "transference" (metaphor) models of mood-attribution (pp.195, 199–200) tracks Heidegger's own critique, in FCM §21, of the Aristotelian *metaphorá* schema for explaining how boredom seems to belong to things (fcm-04 §3c). The authors do not cite §21 directly, but their insistence that cinematic images *disclose* rather than merely *illustrate* boredom's structure is functionally the same anti-transference commitment applied to film rather than to language (P/anticipatory-application).

## 5. Findings/Claims

- Boredom (*Langeweile*) is a *Grundstimmung*, not a psychological state produced by an external cause and awaiting clinical diagnosis; moods "regulate" not only the one who has them but all who come into contact with them (FE, pp.195–196).
- The first form's *Hingehaltenheit*/*Leergelassenheit* is legible in narrative cinema as the repeated clock-check and failed pastime (waiting for a car, tossing peas on a bar while waiting for six o'clock); clock-watching is "the helpless proclamation of the failure of the pastime" (FAITHFUL-HERNÁNDEZ ALBARRACÍN, p.206).
- The second form's pastime does not repel time but is coextensive with the situation itself — "the pastime is the gathering: that itself ends up boring us" (FAITHFUL-HERNÁNDEZ ALBARRACÍN, p.211) — producing an *Unheimlichkeit* (not-being-at-home) beneath an outwardly pleasant occasion.
- The third form's *es ist einem langweilig* opens not onto clock/chronological time but onto kairological, ecstatic time — the *Augenblick* of *Being and Time* §45 — in which Dasein "must shoulder its existence" rather than disperse it into *das Man* (FE, pp.214–216).
- Modern technological and entertainment culture is diagnosed as having "overflowed" (*desbordamiento tecnológico*) the entire horizon of Dasein's possibilities, foreclosing authentic care (*Sorge*) even as technological possibilities multiply within that horizon (FE, pp.216–219).
- The three forms are not hierarchically or developmentally ordered from shallow to deep; they are horizontally co-present modalities of Dasein's relation to world, each available "according to the factical nature of the horizontal relation between Dasein and world" (FAITHFUL-HERNÁNDEZ ALBARRACÍN, p.220).
- The essay closes on a speculative, gestural note (not developed as an argument): that the very boredom produced by technological overflow might itself be the route back to authentic self-relation (FE, pp.218–219).

The article's firm rejection of causal-psychological, "diagnose and measure" accounts of mood (pp.195–196) is a needed philosophical counterweight for Part III's own empirical strata: when Strand D (EEG) and Strand E (eye-tracking/gaze) units operationalize boredom via frontal alpha asymmetry, blink rate, or fixation dispersion, this source insists such physiological indices track the *ontic* management of an attunement (clock-checking, gaze-drift as failed *Zeitvertreib*) rather than the *Grundstimmung* itself — a caution against conflating a measured correlate with the phenomenon *(anticipatory-application)*. This puts the source in productive tension with the functional/regulatory account (Elpidorou, bor-sec-25/bor-sec-26): both treat boredom as *doing* something rather than being mere deficit, but this source's "doing" is world-disclosive, not evolutionarily adaptive — a debate-axis worth carrying into the cluster's Strand A/C synthesis rather than resolving here *(anticipatory-application)*.
