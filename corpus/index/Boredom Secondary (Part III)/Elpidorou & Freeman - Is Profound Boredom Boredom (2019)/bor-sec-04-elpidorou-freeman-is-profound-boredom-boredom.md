# Elpidorou & Freeman (2019) — *Is Profound Boredom Boredom?*

**Type:** book chapter (in Hadjioannou, ed., *Heidegger on Affect*, Palgrave) · **Discipline:** philosophy — Heidegger scholarship in dialogue with the psychology of boredom · **Depth:** deep (~27pp) · **Corpus-role:** Strand-A keystone; primary in-cluster grounding for the debate axis *profound boredom is boredom ↔ profound boredom is a distinct existential mood*.

## Thesis / Aim

The chapter asks its title question directly and answers it with a qualification rather than a yes or no. Elpidorou and Freeman (E&F) set out "to investigate profound boredom's place within contemporary psychological and philosophical research on boredom" (p.2), testing whether Heidegger's *tiefe Langeweile* can be identified with any construct psychology already has a name for. Their governing claim, stated at the outset, is that although profound boredom "shares some features" with state and trait boredom, "it cannot be seamlessly assimilated to any known category of boredom" (p.2). The consequence they draw is interpretive discipline in both directions: one may not treat Heidegger's profound boredom as simply identical to ordinary or scientific boredom, nor may one help oneself to Heidegger's account to make general claims about boredom as such (p.2). The chapter's own resolution, reached only in its final section, is conciliatory: profound boredom is not sui generis (entirely other than boredom) but a rare, hybrid mode of existence, combining the totalizing severity of trait boredom with the motivational, authenticity-disclosing benefit of state boredom (pp.20–23).

## Structure

1. **Boredom: A Primer** (§1, pp.3–4) — surveys the psychology of boredom via the state/trait distinction, introduces the Boredom Proneness Scale (BPS; Farmer and Sundberg 1986) and the Boredom Susceptibility Scale (ZBS; Zuckerman 1979), and catalogues the correlates of trait boredom.
2. **Heidegger's Boredom** (§2, pp.5–8) — expository pass through FCM's three forms: 2.1 becoming-bored-by (first form), 2.2 being-bored-with (second form), 2.3 profound boredom (third form), keyed to the two structural moments (*Leergelassenheit*/being-left-empty, *Hingehaltenheit*/being-held-in-limbo).
3. **Understanding Profound Boredom** (§3, pp.9–20) — the argumentative core, testing three candidate identifications in sequence: 3.1 state boredom is not profound enough (pp.9–12); 3.2 trait boredom is too negative, too personal (pp.12–15); 3.3 profound boredom as sui generis (pp.15–20).
4. **Locating Profound Boredom** (§4, pp.20–23) — the conciliatory synthesis and Table 1 comparison of profound, trait, and state boredom.

## Constructs

E&F's own vocabulary maps cleanly onto the cluster's construct axis, though the mapping is itself one of the chapter's arguments rather than a neutral translation.

- **Profound/deep boredom (FCM 3rd form)** is the chapter's whole subject; treated as *es ist einem langweilig* ("it is boring for one," pp.7–8), the impersonal, totalizing, rare attunement that renders Dasein an "undifferentiated no one" (p.8, quoting FCM 135).
- **Being-bored-by (1st form)** and **being-bored-with (2nd form)** are each given brief exposition (pp.6–7) and then redeployed in §3 as the comparison class against which profound boredom is tested — first form aligned (with qualification) to *state* boredom (p.9), second form read as a retroactive "cognitive attitude" rather than a self-luminous affective episode (pp.9–10).
- **Situational vs. trait/state boredom** is the chapter's imported psychological scaffold (p.3), used twice over: once to organize the psychology-of-boredom literature review, and again as the two-pronged test that profound boredom must fail before the sui generis question can even arise (§§3.1–3.2).
- **Boredom proneness** is treated narrowly, as what the BPS actually operationalizes (p.4) and later as the closest scientific proxy for Bargdill's (2000) "life boredom" testimonies (pp.13–14).
- **Boredom-as-regulatory-signal (functional account)** appears only in §4, where state boredom's motivational, meaning-restorative function (citing Elpidorou 2014, 2018a, 2018b and Van Tilburg and Igou 2011, 2012) is borrowed to explain why profound boredom, too, can be motivating without being trait-like (pp.21–22).
- **Monotony/meaninglessness** recurs as a feature shared across all three levels — state boredom's "perception of lack of meaning" (p.3), the second form's retroactive recognition of a meaningless evening (pp.9–10), and profound boredom's world-draining of significance (p.8).
- **Temporality of boredom (time-drag)** is the chapter's connective tissue: state boredom's "altered perception of time" (p.3) is set beside Heidegger's *Zeitvertreib* analysis and the claim that in profound boredom "the passing of time is altogether missing" (p.8).
- **Attentional disengagement/meta-awareness** and **mind-wandering/task-unrelated thought** are only lightly touched — the former via state boredom's "attentional difficulties" (p.3), the latter not discussed by name at all; this is expected of a source that stays inside philosophy of mind/Heidegger scholarship rather than cognitive psychology proper.
- **Flow/engagement** as an explicit contrast pole is absent; E&F's contrast class throughout is *not* flow but *psychological boredom* (state and trait), which sharpens rather than dilutes the chapter's comparative work.

## Reading of FCM

E&F's reading is expository and comparative rather than revisionist; they do not contest Heidegger's account so much as test its portability. Their treatment of the first form (fcm-04) para­phrases Heidegger's own examples — a delayed flight, a late doctor, an unwanted phone call (p.6) — and imports the two structural moments largely intact. Their treatment of the second form (fcm-05) stays close to the dinner-party example and its "standing present" (*stehendes Jetzt*) analysis, citing FCM 109, 111–112, 114, and 124 directly (pp.6–7); their gloss that "boring" here denotes "casualness" and "the inauthentic following of a social ideal" (p.7) is a compact paraphrase of Heidegger's own point rather than an extension of it. Their treatment of the third form (fcm-06) is the chapter's center of gravity: they track the impersonal "es ist einem langweilig," the collapse of the three temporal dimensions, and the passage from being-left-empty/being-held-in-limbo through the "telling refusal" (*Versagen*, FCM 140, quoted p.8) to the *Augenblick* as Dasein's authentic self-disclosure (p.8, citing FCM 149). Where E&F go beyond straightforward exposition is in §2's closing observation, developed at length in §3, that the first form "appears to be akin to state boredom, although Heidegger would not call it a 'state'" (p.9) — an explicitly *comparative*, not textually-internal, move that FCM itself never makes, since Heidegger has no interest in cross-mapping his three forms onto empirical psychological categories. The chapter's footnote 6 (pp.23–24) also gestures toward FCM's brief and separate discussion of "contemporary boredom" (FCM §18c, §§37–38 — fcm-07), but explicitly declines to reconstruct it, deferring instead to de Beistegui (2003, 63, 68–80) for "a comprehensive and compelling reconstruction and interpretation" — a citation rather than an independent reading.

## Findings / Claims

1. **State boredom is not profound enough** (§3.1, pp.9–12). Profound boredom is a *Grundstimmung* (fundamental, grounding mood), not an emotion; state boredom, by contrast, is "a relatively short-lived, flexible, multi-dimensional response" (p.10) that fails to be totalizing and carries no philosophical or ontological weight — "it does not shake us up" (p.12).
2. **Trait boredom is too negative, too personal** (§3.2, pp.12–15). Trait boredom is totalizing in the sense that it can render one's *whole world* boring, and is thus superficially closer to profound boredom than state boredom is. But it fails on three counts: it is conceptually and causally *dependent* on frequent state boredom, whereas profound boredom is not (p.14); it is grounded in personality/biology rather than in the ontological structure of care shared by every Dasein (p.14); and, crucially, it is pathological and incapacitating rather than authenticity-disclosing — testimonies of "life boredom" (Bargdill 2000; Maltsberger 2000, pp.13–15) show trait boredom immobilizing rather than motivating the sufferer, "the opposite result of profound boredom" (p.15).
3. **Profound boredom is not sui generis** (§3.3, pp.15–20). E&F construct and then reject the reading on which profound boredom is entirely other than boredom — a reading with textual purchase in Heidegger's own use of "formal indication" (*formale Anzeige*) to treat ordinary words ("care," "guilt," and by extension "boredom") as bearing meanings "importantly different from our ordinary understanding" (p.16). Against this, E&F give four counter-considerations: (a) it would make the chapter's own sequential treatment of the first two forms methodologically pointless (p.18–19); (b) FCM 156 itself states an ontological dependency — "the first is itself presumably still rooted in the possibility of the third" (quoted p.19) — which a sui generis reading cannot accommodate; (c) elements of profound boredom (meaninglessness, disengagement, altered time-perception) recur, in lesser degree, in both trait and state boredom (p.19); (d) a fully sui generis profound boredom would be metaphilosophically self-undermining, severing Heidegger's account from any phenomenon we could recognize or learn from (p.20).
4. **The conciliatory finding** (§4, pp.20–23): profound boredom is neither state nor trait boredom, and is not sui generis either. It is best understood as sharing state boredom's *motivational* and authenticity-promoting character and trait boredom's *totalizing*, existentially revealing severity — "as if one experiences trait boredom but only for a moment" (p.22) — a combination the authors, following Spinoza's *Ethics* (Vp42s, quoted p.23), concede is "as difficult as [it is] rare."

## Analytical role (anticipatory-application)

*(P/anticipatory-application) — the following are our interpretive links to Part III, not claims E&F make themselves.*

E&F's tripartite refutation (state-too-shallow / trait-too-pathological / sui-generis-too-disconnected) supplies Part III with a ready-made caution against a specific measurement slippage: any survey instrument that scores "boredom proneness" via BPS-style items (as Part III's own survey does) is, on E&F's own account, measuring *trait* boredom — a construct they argue is definitionally incapable of reaching the ontological register Heidegger's third form occupies (p.14, p.20). Part III's survey findings about student boredom should therefore be framed as evidence about situational/trait boredom, never glossed as evidence about "profound boredom" in the Heideggerian sense, however tempting that rhetorical move might be when the phenomenology chapters discuss VR/deployment-context disengagement.

Second, E&F's own comparative method — testing a philosophical construct against the *specific* psychological categories it might be confused with, rather than treating "boredom" as a single univocal target — models the discipline Part III needs when triangulating self-report, EEG, and gaze data from the (not-yet-built) VR Attention Study corpus entry. Their insistence that depth or profundity "can[not] be read off" from any single feature (duration, totalizing scope, or affective intensity alone) anticipates the same caution the FCM secondary literature raises for the King–Salvo bridge's surface/depth EEG-gaze split (fcm-05 §10, fcm-06 §10): a single instrument cannot certify which "form" of boredom a subject is in.

Third, the chapter's own resolution — that profound boredom, when it occurs, borrows state boredom's motivational function and trait boredom's totalizing scope simultaneously (p.22, Table 1) — offers Part III a nameable middle category for VR-immersion contexts, where a participant might exhibit high-arousal, motivating disengagement (state-like) *and* a totalizing scope of disinterest (trait-like) without either construct alone capturing the datum. **`****** UNVERIFIED:`** any direct empirical instantiation of this hybrid category in the VR Attention Study dataset remains to be confirmed once that corpus entry is built; no grounds for a `grounds-measure` bridge exist yet.

Finally, and most directly: this chapter is the primary in-cluster combatant on the "profound boredom is boredom ↔ distinct existential mood" axis, and it does not sit at either pole. Where a strict, formal-indication-driven Heideggerian reading would hold that "boredom" as Heidegger uses it for the third form names something "entirely other" and methodologically insulated from psychology's boredom research (the sui generis position E&F construct from Heidegger's own 1920s remarks on formal indication, p.16, and which the formal-indication scholarship of Dahlstrom 1994, Crowell 2001, Kisiel 1993, MacAvoy 2010, and Streeter 1997 supplies the resources for, p.24–25 n.10), E&F explicitly reject that pole (pp.17–20) and argue instead that profound boredom *is* boredom — continuous with, conditioning, and partially recognizable within our ordinary and scientific understandings of the phenomenon (p.19, citing FCM 156's own claim that the first form is "rooted in the possibility of" the third). Part III should cite this chapter, not a generic "Heideggerian" source, whenever it needs philosophical warrant for treating profound/deep boredom as a form of boredom continuous with (if irreducible to) the survey- and physiology-measurable forms — while flagging that E&F's own position is a qualified, hybrid "yes," not an unqualified identification.
