# Gibbs (2011) — *The Concept of Profound Boredom: Learning from Moments of Vision*

**Type:** journal article (*Studies in Philosophy and Education* 30, pp.601–613, DOI 10.1007/s11217-011-9256-5) · **Discipline:** philosophy of education / Heideggerian phenomenology · **Depth:** deep (13pp) · **Corpus-role:** Strand-A unit reading FCM's three forms and the *Augenblick* toward workplace/classroom pedagogy; education-adjacent, cross-noted toward Strand B (boredom & education) per its own subtitle and focus.

## Thesis / Aim

Gibbs argues that profound boredom, Heidegger's third and deepest form (FCM), is not a pathology to be chased away by busyness but a disclosive attunement that — via the *Augenblick* (moment of vision) — opens authentic, edifying possibilities for learning in the workplace, classroom, and home (FE, Abstract, p.601). His organizing move is temporal: modern "datable time" (semester, credit hour, examination date) conceals the "originary" or primordial temporality in which profound boredom does its disclosive work, so that consumerist busyness is not boredom's opposite but the mechanism of its concealment (FE, p.601–602). The paper's pedagogical payoff, reached only in its final movements, is that formal education should cultivate "edifying conversation" (borrowed from Rorty) rather than instrumental, metrics-driven instruction, moving toward *Bildung* as its telos (FE, p.612–613).

## Structure

1. **Abstract + epigraph** (p.601) — Benjamin's "dream bird" image of boredom frames the whole piece.
2. **Introduction** (pp.601–604) — a compressed history of boredom-and-modernity scholarship (acedia, Kierkegaard's "root of all evil," McDonald, Simmel, Benjamin, Svendsen, Goodstein, Pezze & Salzani, Stafford & Gregory, Mansikka), then a direct rejection of the mainstream educational-literature framing (Breidenstein 2007; Mann & Robinson 2009; Belton & Priyadharshini 2007) that treats boredom as a "flaw" correctable by more entertaining, technologically enhanced teaching (pp.603–604).
3. **"Heidegger and the Experience of Boredom"** (pp.604–607) — an expository pass through FCM's three forms, culminating in Table 1's seven-point comparison (p.607, reproducing FCM's own §28 table).
4. **"The Moments of Vision as the Basis of Learning..."** (pp.607–609) — the *Augenblick*'s ecstatic, non-instantaneous character (via McNeill, Blattner, Dreyfus); the *kairos*/*phronēsis* analogy; *dunamis*/*energeia* (via Witt, Brogan).
5. **"Listening to the Telling Refusal of Profound Boredom not Pushing it Away..."** (pp.609–610) — pedagogical uptake: learning must not be reduced to instilled "competencies," quoting Heidegger's own FCM §38a passage on competencies vs. strength (p.610).
6. **"The Moment of Vision as a Moment of Edification"** (pp.610–612) — Rorty's edification/ironist distinction imported to frame profound boredom's educational payoff against credentialed, market-driven schooling.
7. **Concluding Comments** (p.612) — a call for formal education to embrace edifying conversation and *Bildung* against the "homogenised markets" of consumerist higher education.

## Constructs

- **Profound/deep boredom (FCM 3rd form)** is the paper's center of gravity from p.606 onward: the impersonal "one is bored," indifference "enveloping beings as a whole," and the "telling refusal" that discloses unexploited possibilities (FAITHFUL-Gibbs, p.606, closely paraphrasing FCM's own third-form vocabulary).
- **Being-bored-by (1st form)** is given brief, faithful exposition via the "confrontation with time" and "driving away of boredom that drives time on" formulas (FAITHFUL-Gibbs, p.605), matching FCM's dragging-time/*Hingehaltenheit* analysis almost verbatim.
- **Being-bored-with (2nd form)** is glossed through Boss's (2009) "I bore myself" gloss and the uneventful-dinner-party example, with "standing of time" and the "prolonged now" as its temporal signature (FAITHFUL-Gibbs, pp.605–606).
- **Temporality of boredom (time-drag)** is the connective construct across the whole paper: datable/world time (in which "we tend to function in our everyday existence") is opposed to "originary temporality," the threefold unity the *Augenblick* makes accessible (FE, p.602; p.607).
- **Monotony/meaninglessness** appears via Simmel's "loss of identity and meaningfulness" and "definitive, valuable goals are lacking" (FAITHFUL-Gibbs, p.601–602), and again as "a life going nowhere" describing the second form's everyday register (FAITHFUL-Gibbs, p.606).
- **Flow/engagement (contrast pole)** operates negatively throughout: "busyness" and consumerist distraction are the inauthentic alternative that *forecloses* profound boredom rather than a positive engaged state in its own right (FE, p.601–603) — this source has no genuine flow construct, only busyness-as-evasion.
- **Boredom-as-regulatory-signal (functional account)** is present only in a philosophical, edifying register: boredom has "specific value in awakening our ability to experience meaningfulness" and is "the positive attunement it is" (FAITHFUL-Gibbs, p.602; p.606) — this is an existential-disclosive claim, not an empirical/evolutionary regulatory theory, and should not be conflated with Elpidorou's account (bor-sec-25/26) (P/anticipatory-application, flagging the distinction).
- **Situational vs. trait/state boredom** has no explicit psychological-science analogue here, but Gibbs's own first/second-form-vs-third-form contrast (situation-bound vs. "one is bored" as a general disposition) loosely tracks the situational/trait distinction at the level of ordinary-language description; this is *our* structural mapping, not Gibbs's own vocabulary (P/anticipatory-application).
- Not treated: boredom proneness, mind-wandering/task-unrelated thought, attentional disengagement/meta-awareness — none of the cognitive-science constructs appear; this is a purely phenomenological-pedagogical reading with zero empirical instrumentation.

## Reading of FCM

Gibbs's engagement with the three forms is close paraphrase rather than contestation, and each form maps cleanly onto its dedicated bridge unit. His first-form treatment (p.605) — the train-station "confrontation with time," "time drags and thus paralyzes us," "becoming bored is a being held in limbo by time as it drags over an interval of time" — directly tracks **fcm-04-first-form**'s own §23 material (FCM pp.93–100, book pp.78–105), reproducing *Hingehaltenheit*'s core formula almost word for word (FAITHFUL-Gibbs, p.605). His second-form treatment (pp.605–606) — the evening invitation, "the evening is that with which we are bored… what we are bored with is passing the time," the "standing of time" that "sets us in place" — tracks **fcm-05-second-form** (FCM pp.112–126, book pp.106–131) equally closely (FAITHFUL-Gibbs, pp.605–606). His third-form treatment (pp.606–609) is the paper's most sustained engagement: das Man's "entrainment of the inauthentic," indifference "enveloping beings as a whole," the "telling refusal" of possibilities, and the *Augenblick* as "resolute self-disclosure" all track **fcm-06-third-form** (FCM pp.134–151, book pp.132–159) (FAITHFUL-Gibbs, pp.606–609).

One passage constitutes a precise, previously unflagged citation match to **fcm-07-particular-profound**: Gibbs quotes Heidegger's remark (p.610) that a culture preoccupied solely with learned, instillable competencies forecloses genuine strength and power — the state Heidegger names "the suffocation of all such things" (FAITHFUL-Gibbs, p.610). This reproduces, almost verbatim, FCM's own Chapter Five §38a passage on the absence of oppressiveness in contemporary Dasein (FCM p.164, book pp.160–168) — the chapter that names the "particular profound boredom" of *our* Dasein specifically, rather than the formal three-form analysis. Gibbs does not identify this as a distinct fourth movement of the argument, but the locus match is exact and gives this unit its strongest, most specific FCM anchor.

Gibbs's genuine *extension* beyond FCM's own text is pedagogical: FCM's argument stops at naming "world" as the horizon disclosed by profound boredom (fcm-07 §7) and does not itself discuss classrooms, workplaces, or curricula. Gibbs's application of the *Augenblick* to an "ontological pedagogy" and to workplace/educational "edification" (pp.607–612) is his own addition, built by importing Rorty's edification/ironist vocabulary and the *kairos*/*phronēsis* analogy (via Dreyfus 2004 and Brogan 2005) — material external to FCM's own text, used to carry the Augenblick into domains Heidegger's 1929/30 course never addresses (FE, extending beyond FCM's own scope, pp.608–612).

A citation-fidelity note worth flagging for the cluster's Phase-3 concordance work: Gibbs's own reference list appears to swap citation keys. His in-text "(Heidegger 1995a, pp.93–150)" citations for the entire three-forms discussion verifiably match FCM's own pagination (confirmed against fcm-04 through fcm-07's own loci above), yet his reference list assigns "1995a" to a *different* work — Heidegger's *Aristotle's Metaphysics, Vols. 1–3* lectures (trans. Brogan & Warnek) — while FCM itself is listed, correctly but without the "a," as plain "Heidegger (1995)." This looks like a reference-list key error internal to Gibbs's own paper rather than any error on our part; it does not affect the accuracy of the bridge edges above, which are anchored to FCM's actual pagination rather than to Gibbs's citation key.

## Findings / Claims

- Modern consumerist busyness does not eliminate profound boredom but actively *drives it from* our "integrated temporal experience," concealing rather than resolving it (FE, p.602).
- The *Augenblick* is not an instant but a duration-bearing "unfolding of the presencing of the situation," combining past, present, and future rather than holding them apart (FAITHFUL-Gibbs, p.608, citing McNeill 1999 and Heidegger 1962, p.387).
- Resolute self-disclosure in the moment of vision is structurally analogous to *phronēsis* (practical wisdom): both require "capability and vision to act" together with situational judgment, not a general rule mechanically applied (FE, p.608, citing Heidegger's own Plato's *Sophist* lectures).
- Learning cannot be reduced to "learned competencies that can be instilled"; authentic strength and power are not equivalent to, and can be actively suffocated by, competency-accumulation (FAITHFUL-Gibbs, p.610, quoting FCM/fcm-07 directly).
- Rorty's edification — discourse that is "abnormal," that displaces us from our "old selves" — supplies the pedagogical form appropriate to profound boredom's disclosive function; formal education's proper task is twofold: opening a space for profound boredom to surface, and supplying the "technical, moral and intellectual skills" to make sense of what it discloses (FE, pp.610–611, quoting Rorty 1979, p.360).
- Contemporary market-driven higher education (Bologna-process accreditation, self-sustaining U.S. market pressures) produces only "commonsensical iterative dialogues," not genuinely edifying, Rortyan-ironist conversation (FE, p.611).
- The paper's own conclusion recommends *Bildung*-as-flourishing over an "instrumental way of being… enframed in the metrics of quality" as education's proper telos (FE, p.612).

## Analytical role (anticipatory-application)

*(P/anticipatory-application) — the following are our interpretive links to Part III, not claims Gibbs makes himself.*

Gibbs's central move — treating profound boredom as pedagogically generative rather than a deficit to be designed away — is a direct philosophical counterweight to any purely deficit-framed reading of student boredom in Part III's VLE/lecture-hall material, and complements the cluster's already-logged correction that VLE disengagement readings must distinguish design/grouping failure from student-side activation failure: Gibbs supplies the further distinction between boredom-as-flaw-to-be-entertained-away (the position he explicitly rejects in Breidenstein and Mann & Robinson, pp.603–604) and boredom-as-disclosive-opportunity. Any Part III passage that cites "student boredom" as straightforwardly bad should at minimum acknowledge this alternative Heideggerian-pedagogical framing exists in the secondary literature, even where Part III's own empirical argument does not adopt it.

Gibbs's *Augenblick*-as-pedagogy reading also gives Part III a precise vocabulary for a specific rhetorical risk: because his "edifying" register is explicitly *not* reducible to measurable competency or engagement metrics, any dissertation passage that tries to operationalize "moments of vision" via survey, EEG, or gaze data would misapply this source — Gibbs's own argument (pp.610–611) is that authentic disclosure exceeds exactly this kind of instrumental accounting. This is a caution, not a citation to avoid: the source is valuable precisely as the register Part III's empirical chapters are *not* operating in, and should be cited to mark that boundary rather than to ground a measurement claim.

Finally, though filed under Strand A for its Heideggerian orientation, Gibbs's subtitle ("Learning from Moments of Vision") and its sustained workplace/classroom/home focus make it a natural cross-note to Strand B's education cluster (bor-sec-16 Feldges, bor-sec-18, bor-sec-19 Thomson) — flagged here as a `bridges-to-part-iii`-style cross-reference per the task brief's own instruction, even though the unit remains filed under Strand A.
