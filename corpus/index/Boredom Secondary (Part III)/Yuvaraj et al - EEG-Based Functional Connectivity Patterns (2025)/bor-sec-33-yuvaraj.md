# bor-sec-33 — Yuvaraj, Rajamanickam, N. P. Guhan Seshadri, Sampathraman Samyuktha, Jack S. Fogarty, Jun Song Huang, Samuel Tan, and Teck Kiong Wong, "EEG-Based Functional Connectivity Patterns During Boredom in an Educational Context" (2025)

**Type:** empirical EEG study (network neuroscience / affective computing) · **Discipline:** cognitive neuroscience / educational psychology (STEM) · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** Strand D — EEG/neurophysiology of boredom; the cluster's most recent (2025) source and its strongest measure-match to Part III's own EEG channel and to the not-yet-built Boredom Experiment (VR Attention Study) dataset entry's functional-connectivity channel. Its explicitly educational framing also makes it a genuine thematic (non-citational) parallel to Strand B's Feldges (bor-sec-16), though the two works never cite one another.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Yuvaraj et al. (*Scientific Reports* 15:35439, 2025) present what they claim is the first study to compare whole-scalp EEG functional-connectivity network organization between a boredom state and a comparison ("excitement") state in an educational setting (FE, p.3). Eighty-four university students (final analytic N=73 after exclusion) watched a brief nature-documentary video, then a 10-minute boring lecture video on input/output energy concepts, while 64-channel EEG was recorded; spectral coherence built 61×61 functional-connectivity matrices per 2-second epoch across five frequency bands, and five graph-theoretic network measures (clustering coefficient, characteristic path length, global and local efficiency, node strength) were compared between conditions (FE, p.5-9). The central claim: boredom is not a passive, network-idle state. Gamma-band connectivity shows significantly higher clustering/efficiency and shorter path length during boredom than the comparison condition, a pattern the authors read as "more locally integrated yet globally less efficient network organization" reflecting internally-directed processing rather than mere disengagement (FAITHFUL-Yuvaraj et al., p.10). The paper closes by proposing boredom may function as a signal prompting cognitive reorganization that could, under the right conditions, support rather than only hinder learning (FE, p.12-13).

## 2. Structure

The article runs its full 15 published pages.

## 3. Constructs

- **situational vs. trait/state boredom** — explicitly operationalized as an induced, transient state rather than trait boredom: a piloted boring video induces state-level boredom "passively" within a single ~60-minute session, and standardized trait measures (Multidimensional State Boredom Scale, Boredom Proneness Scale) are named and explicitly set aside as unsuited to this transient, single-exposure design (FAITHFUL-Yuvaraj et al., p.4).
- **boredom proneness** — named only as an individual-level antecedent factor ("high boredom proneness" raising susceptibility to boredom) in the introduction's causal framing, and again via the Boredom Proneness Scale, which is cited but not administered (FE, p.1, p.4).
- **mind-wandering / task-unrelated thought** — central to the interpretive frame: gamma/beta connectivity increases are read, via the prior mind-wandering/Default Mode Network literature, as reflecting a shift toward "internally directed attention or mind-wandering" during low-stimulation states (FAITHFUL-Yuvaraj et al., p.11).
- **attentional disengagement / meta-awareness** — treated via the Executive Control Network/Default Mode Network contrast borrowed from the mind-wandering literature: boredom is framed as disengagement from the external environment coupled with a "distinct neural signature" the study aims to isolate from ordinary resting-state DMN dominance (FAITHFUL-Yuvaraj et al., p.2).
- **boredom-as-regulatory-signal (functional account)** — the Discussion and Conclusion explicitly reframe boredom as a state that may prompt the brain to seek new sources of stimulation rather than simply signal deficit, closing on the claim that brief boredom could be "a potential catalyst for cognitive growth" (FAITHFUL-Yuvaraj et al., p.12).
- **flow / engagement (contrast pole)** — operationalized directly as the study's comparison condition (the "excitement" video), and the introduction cites flow-theory-adjacent engagement literature (Shernoff et al., 2003) to frame boredom's opposite pole (FE, p.2, p.4).
- **monotony / meaninglessness** — the boredom-inducing stimulus is explicitly engineered around monotony: the lecture video was piloted and selected for slow, monotonous delivery and low-value content in standard lecture format, with minimal cognitive challenge (FAITHFUL-Yuvaraj et al., p.4).
- **profound/deep boredom (FCM 3rd form)**, **being-bored-by / being-bored-with**, **temporality of boredom (time-drag)** — not treated. The paper's vocabulary is drawn entirely from cognitive-affective neuroscience and educational psychology, with no contact with Heideggerian categories or the phenomenology of time-experience; per the Strand D brief, no `bridges-to-fcm` edge is forced.

## 4. Measures & methods

This STEM unit replaces "Reading of FCM" with the study's apparatus, sample, and protocol.

**Participants.** N=84 university students recruited at Nanyang Technological University, Singapore (38 male, 46 female; mean age 26.90±5.29, range 21-45); right-handed, normal/corrected vision, no neurological history; NTU IRB-2022-1048; compensated S$10 (FAITHFUL-Yuvaraj et al., p.3). Eleven participants were excluded post hoc for SAQ responses inconsistent with the intended condition (not bored during the boredom video, or bored during the comparison video), leaving a final analytic N=73 (FAITHFUL-Yuvaraj et al., p.6).

**Stimuli and protocol.** Two videos via E-Prime 3.0: (i) a 4.33-minute BBC *Planet Earth* marine-life clip, previously validated as a low-arousal "neutral" stimulus (Merrifield & Danckert, 2014) but rated by this sample as significantly exciting instead, prompting the authors to relabel it "excitement"; and (ii) a 10-minute lecture video (a science teacher explaining input/output energy concepts), piloted to reliably induce boredom via low cognitive demand and monotonous delivery. Participants could stop the boredom video after 6 minutes minimum (mean actual play time 8.50±1.10 min; 46.43% stopped early); video order (excitement before boredom) was fixed, without counterbalancing, to avoid carry-over (FAITHFUL-Yuvaraj et al., p.3-4). After each video, a 90-second window allowed completion of a 5-item State Affect Questionnaire (SAQ; adapted from PANAS, rating excitement/disgust/neutral/boredom/distress 0-8), used both as EEG-labeling ground truth and as a manipulation check via two-way repeated-measures ANOVA (FAITHFUL-Yuvaraj et al., p.4).

**Apparatus.** Continuous 64-channel scalp EEG (plus electrocardiogram and eye-gaze, collected simultaneously but not analyzed in this study) at 1000 Hz via an AntNeuro eegoTM sports amplifier; 10-10 electrode system; CPz active reference; AFz ground; impedance <10 kΩ; hardware event-marker synchronization (Chronos, via E-Prime) tagging video onset/offset (FAITHFUL-Yuvaraj et al., p.4-5).

**Preprocessing and connectivity construction.** EEG down-sampled to 256 Hz, common-average re-referenced, notch-filtered at 50 Hz; EOG artifact removed via wavelet (Daubechies db9, level 6) SURE thresholding; the excitement-video EEG and the final 4.2 minutes of boredom-video EEG were retained (matching segment length, isolating peak-boredom timing) and segmented into 2-second non-overlapping epochs; wavelet-packet decomposition into delta (0-4 Hz), theta (4-8 Hz), alpha (8-16 Hz), beta (16-32 Hz), gamma (32-64 Hz); spectral coherence computed between all pairs of 61 channels (mastoids and EOG excluded) per epoch per band, retained as a full unthresholded 61×61 weighted matrix — not binarized for analysis, only thresholded at 0.6 for display (FAITHFUL-Yuvaraj et al., p.6-7).

**Network measures.** Five measures computed per matrix via the Brain Connectivity Toolbox in MATLAB 2023b: clustering coefficient (Ceff), characteristic path length (Cpl), global efficiency (Eglo), local efficiency (Eloc), and node strength (Nstr); condition differences tested via Wilcoxon signed-rank (non-normal per Shapiro-Wilk/Kolmogorov-Smirnov), Bonferroni-corrected (α=.002 for Ceff/Cpl/Eglo/Eloc; α=.0001 for the 61-channel Nstr comparisons) (FAITHFUL-Yuvaraj et al., p.7-8).

*Baseline-design note*: unlike bor-sec-27 (Barry et al.)'s resting eyes-closed/eyes-open paradigm, this study uses no resting-state baseline — both conditions are active, eyes-open video-viewing, differing only in induced affect. This sidesteps Barry et al.'s eyes-open/eyes-closed equivalence caution but raises its own baseline question (is "excitement" video-viewing itself a clean comparison state?), one the authors partly concede when their "neutral" stimulus instead elicited excitement in this sample *(P/anticipatory-application)*.

## 5. Findings/Claims

- The SAQ manipulation check confirmed successful boredom induction: boredom-video ratings for the "boredom" item (mean 5.29±2.67) were significantly higher than all other reported emotions on that video (p<.001), while the excitement video predominantly elicited excitement and neutral ratings (FAITHFUL-Yuvaraj et al., p.9).
- Gamma-band coherence showed the most consistent, largest-magnitude differences across all four global/local network measures: significantly higher clustering coefficient (z=-14.79, p<.001) and local efficiency (z=-21.94, p<.001), significantly lower characteristic path length (z=-25.36, p<.001), and significantly higher global efficiency (z=-24.17, p<.001) during boredom relative to excitement (FAITHFUL-Yuvaraj et al., p.10).
- Alpha and beta bands showed smaller but still significant differences in characteristic path length and global efficiency (both shorter/higher during boredom), and alpha additionally showed lower local efficiency during boredom; delta showed no significant differences on any measure (FAITHFUL-Yuvaraj et al., p.10).
- Node-strength topography during boredom showed the largest z-value differences in the gamma band (broadly distributed across frontal, parietal, temporal, and occipital regions) and the beta band (concentrated frontally/centrally), interpreted as a compensatory, effortful attempt to sustain cognitive engagement under low external stimulation (FAITHFUL-Yuvaraj et al., p.9, p.11).
- The authors interpret the joint pattern — higher clustering/efficiency, shorter path length, specifically in gamma — as "more locally integrated yet globally less efficient network organization," consistent with elevated gamma activity during low-attention or disengaged states reported elsewhere (Laufs et al., 2003) (FAITHFUL-Yuvaraj et al., p.10).
- Discussion explicitly denies a passive-idling account of boredom: "boredom is not a passive or idle state" but one that engages active cognitive mechanisms (FAITHFUL-Yuvaraj et al., p.12).
- The Conclusion reframes boredom's educational valence: rather than only impeding learning, brief boredom episodes may "activate important brain processes" that improve network connections and thinking efficiency, and the paper claims to "challenge the common belief that boredom only gets in the way" of learning (FAITHFUL-Yuvaraj et al., p.13).
- Limitations conceded by the authors: a single lab-based elicitation stimulus (classroom generalization untested); no correlation analysis between early-termination viewing durations and SAQ ratings; only four graph measures examined; spectral coherence may include spurious zero-lag correlations from volume conduction, for which the authors recommend imaginary coherence or directed-connectivity methods (Directed Transfer Function, partial directed coherence, Granger causality) (FAITHFUL-Yuvaraj et al., p.12).
