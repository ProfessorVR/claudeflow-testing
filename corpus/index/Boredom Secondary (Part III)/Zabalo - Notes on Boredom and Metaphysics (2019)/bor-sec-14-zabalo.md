# bor-sec-14 — Zabalo, Jacobo (2019), "Notes on Boredom and Metaphysics, Sociologically Framed"

**Type:** journal article · **Discipline:** philosophy (Heideggerian phenomenology) read through a sociological / critical-theory lens · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus-role:** Strand A's explicit sociological bridge unit — reads FCM's profound-boredom account through the leisure-economy/spectacle-society tradition (Bloch, Adorno, Debord, Lipovetsky, Sloterdijk), directly grounding the cluster's *situational ↔ trait* debate axis from the humanities side, and supplying the strongest in-cluster parallel to fcm-07's "contemporary Dasein" question.

**Citation:** Zabalo, Jacobo. "Notes on Boredom and Metaphysics, Sociologically Framed." *Ars Brevis* 25 (2019): 307–324. Universitat Pompeu Fabra.

---

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Zabalo's essay holds two projects together. The first is expository: to recover Heidegger's account of boredom as "a feeling of time" (FCM p.80) across its pre-history — the 1924 Marburg lecture *The Concept of Time*, its Augustinian and Kantian backdrop, *Being and Time*'s *Sein-zum-Tode* — into the 1929–30 *Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics* (FCM) lecture course and the contemporaneous "What is Metaphysics?" lecture on anxiety and the nothing (FE, pp.308–312). The second, announced in the subtitle, is the essay's real ambition: to read this ontological analysis "sociologically," tracing how a whole lineage of twentieth-century cultural critique — Bloch, Adorno, Debord, Lipovetsky, Sloterdijk, with an aside to Žižek — diagnoses the leisure/entertainment/spectacle economy as an ever more efficient societal *Zeitvertreib* (pastime) that manages, without dissolving, the "ontological fear" boredom discloses (FE, abstract, p.307). Zabalo's closing thesis, stated in the abstract and reprised via David Foster Wallace at the essay's end, is that the "desperate search for comfort, well-being and fun can mask only partially the reach of that ontological fear" (FAITHFUL-Zabalo, p.307; cf. p.324).

## 2. Structure

1. **"Boredom's equivocal actuality"** (pp.308–312) — opens on Heidegger's own puzzle that boredom is universally familiar yet resistant to definition; glosses *Langeweile* etymologically as a "long while"; establishes boredom as "a feeling of time" against a Kantian/Augustinian backdrop of inner-sense temporality; introduces the *Zeitvertreib* (pastime) vocabulary, *Sein-zum-Tode*, and *horror vacui*; closes by pivoting into the 1929 "What is Metaphysics?" lecture on anxiety and nothingness.
2. **"Metaphysics after the era of metaphysics"** (pp.312–316) — reads anxiety and profound boredom as co-disclosive of nothingness; stages the standard Heidegger/Frankfurt-School antagonism (via Adorno) and argues the two share more common ground, via Nietzsche, than usually assumed; discusses FCM's "Truth of Philosophy and its Ambiguity" passage and Husserl's phenomenological method; closes on the chapter "Awakening a Fundamental Attunement"'s own rhetorical questions and its "silent fog" leitmotif.
3. **"'Free time' and leisure in the spectacle's society"** (pp.316–321) — the sociological turn proper: Bloch's *Principle of Hope* on managed leisure-time as worker "invigoration"; the "permanent vacation" fantasy; digital technology/smartphones extending pastime into every waking moment; Adorno on culture-industry pseudo-satisfaction and art's negative function; Debord's *Society of the Spectacle* and *temps-marchandise*; Lipovetsky's *L'ère du vide* on postmodern meaninglessness; Kierkegaardian "immediacy."
4. **"Roma o morte: ontological dilemmas inside the glasshouse"** (pp.321–324) — names the "boredom or fun" dilemma via Garibaldi's motto; reads Sorrentino's *La Grande Bellezza* (the character Orietta) as an illustration of narcissistic self-representation as boredom-evasion; discusses the postmodern self as "reality show"; imports Sloterdijk's "glasshouse"/"ether of comfort" metaphor (itself quoting Heidegger); closes with David Foster Wallace's *This Is Water* on the unthought boredom of adult routine.

## 3. Constructs

- **profound/deep boredom (FCM 3rd form)** — the essay's ontological anchor throughout, though never distinguished by name from the other two forms; quoted almost verbatim from FCM's own third-form chapter ("the more profound it becomes, the more completely boredom is rooted in time," FCM p.133) and treated as boredom's truest register (FAITHFUL-Zabalo, p.309).
- **being-bored-by (1st form) / being-bored-with (2nd form)** — **not distinguished by name.** Zabalo borrows first-form vocabulary (the "drive away"/"shake off" language of FCM pp.79, 91, the general *Zeitvertreib* gloss) generically, for "boredom" as such, without marking which Heideggerian form is in play (FE, pp.308–311). The screen-mediated "gathering with loved ones but lonely" scene (p.323) reads, unnamed, as an instance of the second form's surface-occupied/depth-hollow structure (P/anticipatory-application — our cross-reference, not Zabalo's).
- **situational vs. trait/state boredom** — the essay's organizing analytical move, though phrased in Zabalo's own idiom rather than the psychological literature's: he treats Heidegger's ostensibly ontological, trait-like "fundamental attunement" as, in the leisure economy, structurally produced and evaded by determinate, situational, industrially supplied pastimes (vacations, sports-watching, smartphones, social media) (FE, pp.316–324).
- **boredom proneness** — **not treated**; no psychometric-trait vocabulary appears.
- **mind-wandering / task-unrelated thought; attentional disengagement / meta-awareness** — **not treated**; these cognitive-science constructs are absent from the essay's philosophical/critical-theory register.
- **boredom-as-regulatory-signal (functional account)** — **not treated, and arguably run against**: pastime/entertainment is diagnosed as a symptomatic masking of boredom's disclosure rather than an adaptive, corrective response to it (FE, pp.311, 324).
- **flow / engagement (contrast pole)** — touched via the "boredom or fun" dilemma (Roma o morte, p.321) and the absorbed-yet-lonely screen scene (pp.323–324), but without Csikszentmihalyi-style flow vocabulary; "fun"/entertainment functions as boredom's consumerist contrary rather than a theorized engagement construct (FE, p.321).
- **monotony / meaninglessness** — treated via *horror vacui*, nothingness anthropologically processed as death (p.311), and Lipovetsky's diagnosis that it is "already possible to live without purpose or meaning" (FAITHFUL-Zabalo, p.320).
- **temporality of boredom (time-drag)** — the essay's second organizing construct after profound boredom itself: boredom as "a feeling of time," the Kantian/Augustinian backdrop, and the claim that depth in boredom is not a matter of duration (FE, pp.309–311).

## 4. Reading of FCM

Zabalo's citations cluster almost entirely around FCM's first-form and third-form material, cited generically as "boredom" without marking the tripartite structure fcm-04/fcm-05/fcm-06 formalize. The "feeling of time" quotation (FCM p.80) and the "drive away"/"shake off" pastime vocabulary (FCM pp.79, 91) fall within fcm-04's own coverage (§§19–23, book pp.78–105); Zabalo uses this material as a general characterization of boredom rather than as specifically first-form (bridges-to-fcm: fcm-04-first-form, FAITHFUL-Zabalo pp.308–311). The essay's load-bearing FCM citation — "the more profound it becomes, the more completely boredom is rooted in time" — quotes fcm-06's own opening move (§29, p.133) verbatim, and this is the passage on which Zabalo's entire sociological extension is built: if depth in boredom is depth *in time*, then a socioeconomic order that engineers ever more efficient techniques for occupying time is, ipso facto, an order engineered to keep profound boredom at bay (bridges-to-fcm: fcm-06-third-form, FAITHFUL-Zabalo p.309, extending into pp.316–324 as P/anticipatory-application on our part, since Zabalo does not himself name "third form").

Two further bridges are ours rather than Zabalo's own citations. First, his entire "free time"/spectacle-society argument — is the leisure economy itself evidence that "man has become boring to himself"? — restates, without citing FCM's chapter five by page, exactly the question fcm-07 poses of "our contemporary Dasein": "Has man in the end become boring to himself?" (fcm-07 §2, p.161). Zabalo's citations never reach FCM pp.160–168; the fcm-07 parallel is this unit's own cross-reference (bridges-to-fcm: fcm-07-particular-profound, P/anticipatory-application). Second, the smartphone/social-media scene of people "gathering with loved ones but lonely, each individual stuck to a screen" (p.323) is an unnamed instance of fcm-05's surface-occupied/depth-hollow "being bored with" structure — outwardly engaged, inwardly hollowed — though Zabalo never invokes the dinner-party example or the "standing Now" vocabulary that define fcm-05 (bridges-to-fcm: fcm-05-second-form, P/anticipatory-application).

A secondary, non-bridge-relevant strand of Zabalo's reading concerns FCM's own methodological self-consciousness rather than the boredom chapters proper: he cites the "Truth of Philosophy and its Ambiguity" passage (FCM p.19, "Everything should start to vacillate") and the closing questions of "Awakening a Fundamental Attunement" (FCM p.77, "Have we become too insignificant to ourselves...") as evidence that Heidegger's own text already gestures toward the sociological diagnosis Zabalo will develop (FAITHFUL-Zabalo, pp.314–315). These loci sit outside the four bridge units named for this cluster and are not separately bridged.

## 5. Findings/Claims

- Boredom's ontological core is a disclosure of nothingness, anthropologically processed as death; the yawn and the *horror vacui* are its bodily and affective symptoms (FE, p.311).
- The free-market leisure economy supplies "plenty of options that pretend to be meaningful" precisely through the separation of working time and leisure time — Bloch's worker "invigoration," the "permanent vacation" fantasy — thereby manufacturing efficient societal pastimes (FAITHFUL-Zabalo, p.307, 316–317).
- Despite their standard opposition, Heidegger and the Frankfurt-School critics (chiefly Adorno) share more common ground than usually granted, traceable to a shared debt to Nietzsche's dissolution of metaphysical certainty (FE, p.313, qualifying the standard antagonism narrative).
- Digital technology and social-media self-representation intensify rather than resolve the boredom-evasion dynamic: representation substitutes for "the real," so that an experience is not complete until shared and "liked" (FAITHFUL-Zabalo, p.323).
- The "boredom or fun" alternative — Zabalo's own formulation, a "consumerist variation" of Garibaldi's *Roma o morte* — names the operative motto of contemporary individualism (FAITHFUL-Zabalo, p.321).
- Sloterdijk's "glasshouse" describes a privileged atmosphere of comfort in which boredom nonetheless persists as "a restless guest," dependent for its very reality on the recognition of others (FAITHFUL-Zabalo, p.324).
- Even at maximal comfort and entertainment, the "desperate search" for well-being and fun "can mask only partially the reach of that ontological fear" boredom discloses — the essay's closing and organizing thesis (FAITHFUL-Zabalo, p.307/324).

## 7. Analytical role

Zabalo's essay is this cluster's clearest humanities-side grounding for the *situational ↔ trait framing* debate axis: it takes Heidegger's profound boredom — presented in FCM as a fundamental attunement of Dasein, i.e., something close to a trait-level, ontologically constitutive structure — and argues it is nonetheless structurally produced and managed by a historically specific, situational apparatus (the industrial leisure/entertainment/digital-media economy), without thereby denying its ontological depth (P/anticipatory-application). For Part III's own reconciliation of trait-style boredom-proneness measures with situational, technology-mediated engagement/disengagement data (the VLE context), Zabalo's account supplies a ready-made vocabulary for treating "engagement" itself as potentially a sophisticated form of *Zeitvertreib* rather than evidence against boredom — a caution structurally identical to fcm-05's surface/depth split, here extended to smartphones and social media rather than a 1929 dinner party (P/anticipatory-application). The essay's central sociological wager — that a society's manufactured pastimes are diagnostic of, rather than a solution to, an underlying ontological condition — offers Part III a way to frame survey findings about student disengagement as potentially reflecting structural (situational, technology-mediated) drivers rather than purely dispositional (trait-boredom-proneness) ones, echoed in the cluster's own prior correction that VLE co-presence and forced grouping shape the "WE unmet want" reading rather than student disposition alone (P/anticipatory-application). No dataset-measure bridge is warranted here: this is a philosophical/critical-theory essay with no physiological or behavioral instrumentation, and forcing a `grounds-measure` pointer to the not-yet-built Boredom Experiment (VR Attention Study) entry would manufacture a connection the source gives no material for (P/anticipatory-application — flagged as a deliberate non-bridge, not an oversight).
