# bor-sec-02 — Elpidorou & Freeman, "Affectivity in Heidegger I: Moods and Emotions in *Being and Time*" (2015)

**Type:** journal survey/synthesis essay (*Philosophy Compass*) · **Discipline:** philosophy (Heideggerian phenomenology of mind) · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** Strand A — establishes the *Being and Time* (BT) architecture of Befindlichkeit/Stimmung that FCM's three-form boredom analysis (fcm-04–fcm-07) presupposes; first half of a two-part study whose companion (bor-sec-03) carries the same authors' account forward into BT's temporality and into boredom proper.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Elpidorou and Freeman reconstruct, in order, (i) the place of mood within Heidegger's project of fundamental ontology in BT, (ii) the relation between Befindlichkeit ("attunement"/"disposition," here left untranslated) and Stimmung ("mood"), (iii) the disclosive function of mood via a sustained case study of anxiety (Angst), and (iv) the place emotions occupy — or fail to occupy — in a text organized around ontological structures rather than ontic affective episodes (FE, p.661). The essay is explicitly the first half of a diptych: a "companion essay" (bor-sec-03, "Affectivity in Heidegger II: Temporality, Boredom, and Beyond") is named in the abstract and reprised in the final endnote as the place where the temporal grounding of Befindlichkeit — and hence Heidegger's specific treatment of boredom — will be worked out (FE, p.661; n.13, p.669–670). This unit's own governing claim is therefore deliberately partial: it builds the general phenomenology of mood-as-disclosure that any subsequent reading of FCM's boredom analysis must presuppose, without itself walking through the three forms.

## 2. Structure

The essay has four titled movements plus endnotes:

1. **§1 Introduction** (p.661) — affective experience as ubiquitous, meaningful, and revelatory; Heidegger's distinguishing claim that having moods is constitutive of Dasein, not a psychological add-on.
2. **§2 The Project of Fundamental Ontology in *Being and Time*** (pp.661–663) — Dasein as being-here/being-there; disclosedness (*Erschlossenheit*) as the alternative to a subject-object/consciousness model; the four co-equal existentials (Befindlichkeit, understanding, fallenness, talk) unified in care (*Sorge*).
3. **§3 Befindlichkeit and Stimmung** (pp.663–668), in three numbered subsections:
   - **3.1 Befindlichkeit and affective disclosedness** (pp.663–664) — the untranslatability of Befindlichkeit; Befindlichkeit/Stimmung as ontological structure/ontic manifestation of one phenomenon; moods as "atmospheres," pre-reflective and unthematic, disclosively prior to cognition.
   - **3.2 Befindlichkeit's three essential characteristics** (pp.664–666) — mood discloses (a) thrownness, (b) being-in-the-world as a whole, (c) what matters.
   - **3.3 Thrownness, anxiety, and fallenness** (pp.666–668) — the evasive "turning-away" that constitutes fallenness; anxiety as the exceptional turning-toward; anxiety as *Grundbefindlichkeit* grounding fear.
4. **§4 Conclusion: The Place of Emotions in *Being and Time*** (pp.668–669) — Heidegger's near-silence on emotion; the ambiguous mood/emotion status of fear; the authors' own interpretative proposal that moods are the background for emotions.
5. **Endnotes 1–15** (pp.669–671), including the translation-history note (n.4) and the explicit deferral of Befindlichkeit's temporal structure — and thus of boredom — to the companion essay (n.13, pp.669–670).

## 3. Constructs

Of the cluster's controlled construct vocabulary, this BT-groundwork essay treats only two directly and gestures at a third; the rest belong to its companion piece or to other strands entirely.

- **profound/deep boredom (FCM 3rd form)** — treated only in a single, general mention: mood discloses thrownness, "sometimes explicitly and rather poignantly as in the case of anxiety and boredom" (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.665, citing BT 173–5/134–6, 315/270, 330/284). Boredom is named as one of BT's own two paradigm poignant thrownness-disclosers (with anxiety) but is not itself analyzed — the authors explicitly reserve that work for bor-sec-03 (FE, p.669–670, n.13).
- **temporality of boredom (time-drag)** — explicitly flagged as out of scope: "we cannot discuss the temporal nature of Befindlichkeit here. We do, however, provide a detailed discussion of this character in a companion piece" (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.669–670, n.13). This is itself a load-bearing claim for the cluster's concordance: it explains why bor-sec-02 and bor-sec-03 must be read as a single argumentative unit for boredom specifically.
- **flow / engagement (contrast pole)** — touched only obliquely, via the general claim that moods "make possible" and are "necessary for" circumspective engagement with the world (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.665–666, citing BT 176/137). No positive or contrastive treatment of flow as such.

Not treated at all: being-bored-by (1st form) and being-bored-with (2nd form) as named FCM constructs; situational vs. trait/state boredom; boredom proneness; mind-wandering/task-unrelated thought; attentional disengagement/meta-awareness (though see the analytical bridge in §7 below); boredom-as-regulatory-signal; monotony/meaninglessness. This is unsurprising — the essay's object is BT's general theory of Befindlichkeit, not boredom, and the authors are explicit that boredom's turn comes in the companion piece.

## 4. Reading of FCM

This essay is not a reading of FCM in the sense of walking through its argument; FCM is cited exactly twice, both as corroborating testimony for claims the authors are building out of BT. But its account of the *general* disclosive structure of mood is precisely the machinery FCM's boredom analysis (fcm-04 through fcm-07) puts to work on a single, extreme case, so four targeted bridges are worth making explicit (all P/anticipatory-application — none is a claim this essay itself makes about FCM's boredom analysis).

First, the essay's own FCM citation is the "atmosphere" passage: "moods [Stimmungen] are not side-effects [of cognition, volition, or action], but are something which in advance determine our being with one another," and "moods are not something merely at hand... a fundamental manner and fundamental way of Being... [that] always directly includes being with one another" (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.663–664, quoting Heidegger 1995, p.67). This passage precedes fcm-04's own coverage (book pp.78–105) — it belongs to FCM's earlier Preliminary Appraisal — so no fcm-0N bridge is claimed for the citation itself; what does bridge forward is the anti-subjectivist method it authorizes. That same refusal to treat attunement as "an object swimming in the stream of consciousness" is the explicit methodological directive fcm-04 §22 derives for boredom specifically (fcm-04, p.90 / GA pp.135–36 / pdf 58): this essay's general BT/FCM claim that moods are irreducible to inner psychological states is the premise fcm-04 spends an entire section re-deriving for the boring-station scene (P/anticipatory-application).

Second, the essay's account of thrownness-disclosure — Dasein "flees to the relief which comes with the supposed freedom of the they-self" (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.666, quoting BT 321/276) — is the general form of which fcm-04's *Zeitvertreib* (passing-the-time) is the boredom-specific instance: Heidegger's station-scene traveler counting trees and checking a watch is, on this essay's own terms, exhibiting exactly the "evasive turning-away" this essay attributes to fallenness generically (bridge to fcm-04, §23a–b, book pp.92–99) (P/anticipatory-application).

Third, the essay's second disclosive characteristic — mood discloses "Being-in-the-world as a whole" equiprimordially with Dasein-with and one's own existence (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.665, quoting BT 176/137) — is radicalized almost verbatim by FCM's third form, where beings become indifferent "as a whole" and Dasein finds itself "in the midst of beings as a whole, i.e., in the whole of this indifference" (bridge to fcm-06, §3, book p.138 / GA p.208 / pdf 80). Profound boredom is this essay's second characteristic pushed to its totalizing limit (P/anticipatory-application).

Fourth, the essay's naming of anxiety as *Grundbefindlichkeit* — the ground-attunement from which fear and other moods derive (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.667–668, quoting BT 230/186) — sits in productive tension with FCM's naming of *profound* boredom as the *Grundstimmung* ("fundamental attunement") of contemporary Dasein (bridge to fcm-07, book pp.160–168). The essay itself flags, in its own footnote, that "the word 'Grundstimmung' appears only once in BT (358/310)" and "becomes much more important in Heidegger's thinking after 1929" (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.662, n.2) — i.e., in FCM itself (WS 1929–30). This essay's BT-era case for anxiety as *the* ground-mood is thus the terminological and structural precedent that FCM will, two years later, contest and displace with profound boredom as the ground-mood proper (P/anticipatory-application) — a debate-axis (anxiety vs. boredom as candidate Grundstimmung) this cluster should track across strands rather than resolve within a single unit.

Finally, the essay's claim that mood "arises out of being-in-the-world, as a way of such Being" rather than from "outside" or "inside" (FAITHFUL-Elpidorou & Freeman, p.665, quoting BT 176/136) anticipates fcm-05's defining contrast for the *second* form of boredom, where "boredom arises out of Dasein itself" rather than from an external situation (bridge to fcm-05, §1, book p.133 / GA p.200 / pdf 78) (P/anticipatory-application).

## 5. Findings/Claims

- Befindlichkeit (ontological structure) and Stimmung (ontic manifestation) name one and the same phenomenon under two aspects; Dasein is always in some mood, "where even indifference is considered to be a mood" (FE, p.663–664, citing BT 173/134).
- Moods are not internal, subjective, causally-inferred mental states; they are "atmospheres" already there, in which Dasein exists, disclosive in a way that is pre-reflective and unthematic, and more "primordially revealing" than theoretical/cognitive disclosure (FE, p.663–664, citing BT 173–177/134–138, and Heidegger 1995, p.67).
- Mood discloses three things equiprimordially: (a) thrownness (*Geworfenheit*) — that Dasein always already finds itself in situations it did not choose; (b) being-in-the-world as a whole, including Dasein-with (*Mitdasein*) and one's own existence; (c) what matters — mattering itself is grounded in Befindlichkeit, so that without mood nothing could show up as valenced at all (FE, p.664–666, citing BT 173–176/134–137).
- Thrownness is for the most part disclosed only in a peculiar, concealing mode: an "evasive turning-away" in which Dasein flees into worldly busyness and "the they" rather than confronting its own facticity — the essay's bridge from Befindlichkeit to fallenness (FE, p.666, citing BT 174–175/135–136, 233–234/189, 321/276).
- Anxiety (*Angst*) is the exceptional turning-away that is not a fleeing: it springs from Dasein itself (not any worldly entity), strips the everyday world of significance without making it absent, and throws Dasein back upon its own "authentic potentiality-for-Being" (FE, p.666–667, citing BT 231–232/186–188, 395/344).
- Anxiety is *Grundbefindlichkeit* — the ground-attunement making other moods, including fear, possible; fear is termed an "inauthentic mode of Befindlichkeit" because its turning-away is a genuine fleeing from oneself, whereas anxiety's is not (FE, p.667–668, citing BT 230/186, 391/341, 234/189).
- Fear's classification is unstable: because what is feared is always a determinate worldly entity approaching from a specific direction, Heidegger's own textual evidence supports treating fear as an occurrent *emotion* rather than a mood — a tension the authors flag rather than resolve, citing Freeman (2015) and Blattner (2006) as holding the same view (FE, p.668, citing BT 181–182/142).
- Heidegger's near-total silence on emotion in BT is explained, not excused, by the text's ontological (not ontic) aim; the authors judge that "an account of affectivity that has no place for emotions" would fail to capture human existence (FE, p.668).
- Authors' own interpretative proposal (FE): moods are the background against which occurrent, intentionally-specific emotions arise; because emotions require a world that already matters, and mattering is only possible on the ground of mood, moods are necessary (though not sufficient) conditions for emotions — a reading the authors present as consistent with, though not made explicit in, Heidegger's own text, and as supported by Ratcliffe (2008, 2013), Slaby (2014), and Elpidorou (2013) (FE, p.668–669).
- This same proposal resolves the fear puzzle: even if fear is an emotion, its possibility is grounded in a mood the authors identify as Heidegger's own "fearfulness" (*Furchtsamkeit*) (FE, p.669, citing BT 182/142).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* This unit's chief service to Part III is infrastructural rather than substantive: it supplies the general theory of mood-as-disclosure — pre-reflective, world-constituting, prior to any subject/object split — that licenses treating student boredom, later in the dissertation, as something other than a private mental episode reportable only by introspection. The essay's insistence that mood is read off comportment and world-disclosure rather than off verbal report is a methodological caution Part III's own survey instrument should sit alongside, not substitute for: self-report captures an ontic trace of an attunement whose primary mode of givenness, on this account, is pre-reflective *(anticipatory-application)*. The essay's three-way disclosure structure (thrownness / world-as-a-whole / mattering) offers Part III a ready-made analytic grid for distinguishing *what* a bored VR or desktop-PC participant's boredom is disclosing — a determinate stimulus (thrownness-adjacent, first-form register) versus a diffuse loss of stakes in the whole task (second/third-form register) — prior to any physiological measurement *(anticipatory-application)*. The essay's own flagged Grundstimmung tension (anxiety in BT vs. profound boredom in FCM two years later) is a debate-axis worth carrying forward explicitly into the cluster's synthesis: Part III's poles (lab/VR vs. deployment/2D-PC) could be read as differentially eliciting situational (first-form-adjacent) versus diffuse (ground-mood-adjacent) boredom, and this unit is the earliest, clearest statement of why those two registers are not the same phenomenon merely differing in intensity *(anticipatory-application)*. Finally, the moods-ground-emotions proposal (§5, FE) is a caution against treating "boredom" and student "frustration" or "disengagement" (emotion-like, object-directed reports) as interchangeable in survey design: on this essay's own model they belong to different ontological registers, with boredom (a mood) the background condition for any more determinate affective complaint a student might voice *(anticipatory-application)*.
