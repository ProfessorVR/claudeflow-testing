# bor-sec-08 — Hughes, "Meaninglessness and Monotony in Pandemic Boredom" (2023)

**Type:** journal article (phenomenology / applied Heideggerian analysis of survey data), *Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences* · **Discipline:** philosophy (phenomenology, philosophy of emotion) applied to first-person Covid-19 survey reports · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** Strand A's dedicated test case for FCM's situative/existential distinction against a mass-scale historical disruption; the cluster's primary source for the monotony/environment-caused vs. meaning/agent-caused debate axis; and, via its critical second aim, a rare Strand-A source that turns Heidegger's own apparatus against its methodological limits rather than simply applying it.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Hughes states two aims (FE, p.3). The first, expository, aim is to give "an original phenomenological interpretation" of the meaninglessness and monotony that mark "pandemic boredom" — her own working term, stipulated to refer to boredom occurring during or because of the Covid-19 pandemic (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.2 n.5) — using Heidegger's FCM as a "unique vantage point" to ask whether this boredom is a situative confrontation with relative meaninglessness that merely sustains everyday absorption, or an existential confrontation with absolute meaninglessness that forces the question of one's own existence (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.3). The second, more critical, aim — and the one carrying the paper's real argumentative weight — is to show that pandemic boredom makes this situative/existential distinction difficult to sustain, and thereby to expose, then exceed, "the distinctive methodological limitations" of Heidegger's own interpretation of boredom (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.3). The paper's overall verdict previews the debate-axis position it argues for: pandemic boredom is essentially environment/situation-caused (an absence of significant intersubjective world-projects), but the totalizing scope of that environmental disruption destabilizes the very distinction Heidegger needs in order to say so cleanly, so that the boredom's residual significance ends up depending on each respondent's differential, agent-relative concrete situation rather than on one uniform mechanism (P/anticipatory-application synthesis of FAITHFUL-Hughes claims across §§3–4).

## 2. Structure

Four numbered sections; the introduction is unnumbered but functions as §1.

1. **§1 Introduction** (p.1–3) — states both aims; situates "pandemic boredom" against the wider, multi-disciplinary "boredom studies" field and its intensification by Covid-19 (citing the International Society of Boredom Studies, the Boredom Lab, and Danckert's "Dissecting Boredom" project); opens with O'Connell's and Grant's journalistic framings of pandemic time-collapse as a "half-assed end of the world" and a "boring apocalypse."
2. **§2 "The philosophy of boredom"** (p.3–7) — exposits Heidegger's FCM typology of three forms via Svendsen's situative/existential terminology; develops the co-constitution of attunement and temporality; traces Heidegger's historical/epochal reading of boredom as the contemporary age's candidate defining mood, then his later, more pessimistic re-reading in the *Contributions to Philosophy*.
3. **§3 "A phenomenology of pandemic boredom"** (p.7–9) — draws on the Froese et al. "Experiences of Social Distancing During the Covid-19 Pandemic" survey (~2,543 respondents, Japan/Mexico/UK, June–July 2020); reads open-ended responses to Question 57 ("Have you noticed changes in your experience of time?") through the meaninglessness/monotony frame; brings in Wittmann's "quarantine paradox."
4. **§4 "The significance of pandemic boredom"** (p.9–13, incl. conclusion) — argues pandemic boredom is essentially situative (first/second-level); argues it nonetheless realizes Heidegger's own *Contributions*-era worry that technologized indifference forecloses boredom's revelatory potential; then executes the paper's critical turn in two steps — the situative/existential distinction is destabilized by the pandemic's totalizing disruption of the everyday world itself, and the relative philosophical significance of different people's pandemic boredom cannot be discriminated without falling back on the heterogeneous social/political/economic conditions Heidegger's train-station and dinner-party examples were never built to address.

## 3. Constructs

- **profound/deep boredom (FCM 3rd form)** — treated as the existential pole pandemic boredom is tested against and ultimately found not to reach: the paper argues pandemic boredom does not involve "the confrontation with absolute meaninglessness" required to take up the question of existence (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.9-10).
- **being-bored-by (1st form)** — cited via Heidegger's train-station example as one of the two situative levels; glossed, following Svendsen, as boredom "directed toward a particular object or situation within the world that has lost its meaning" (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.4).
- **being-bored-with (2nd form)** — cited via the dinner-party example as the second situative level, "more diffuse and yet more pervasive" than the first (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.4).
- **situational vs. trait/state boredom** — invoked twice: early, as a live concern in the psychology literature on pandemic boredom (state/trait, boredom proneness, adherence to distancing) that Hughes brackets as outside her own phenomenological method (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.2 n.6); and again at the close, where she argues Heidegger's account, once its ontological distinction is destabilized, is "forced back onto psychological explanations" that must answer to exactly this state/trait dichotomy and its relatives (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.12).
- **boredom proneness** — noted only as a marker of the wider psychological pandemic-boredom literature (correlation with adherence to social-distancing measures), not adopted as Hughes's own analytic category (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.2 n.6).
- **monotony / meaninglessness** — the paper's two organizing constructs, argued to be ontologically intertwined rather than merely co-occurring: temporality's threefold structure (past/present/future) is what makes attunement possible at all, while attunements in turn "temporalize themselves," so the denial of past and future and the intensification of the present is simultaneously what lets boredom disclose meaninglessness and what contorts time into monotony (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.6-7). Empirically, meaninglessness is read off respondents' loss of anchoring events (past/present/future) and their sense of unaccomplishment despite empty time; monotony is read off the paradoxical experience of days that stretch while weeks and months collapse, and the erosion of chronological structure itself (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.8-9).
- **temporality of boredom (time-drag)** — mapped onto the three-form typology as an escalating series: dragging/paralysing time (1st form), a static "now" that merely persists (2nd form), and an overwhelming, atemporal expanse in which one feels "removed from the flow of time" altogether (3rd form) (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.4-5); then re-grounded empirically via Wittmann's "quarantine paradox," itself built on Pöppel's time paradox — prospective duration is judged slow while the same interval, reconstructed retrospectively, seems to have passed quickly (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.9).
- **boredom-as-regulatory-signal (functional account)** — not endorsed; addressed only via a footnote distancing Hughes's own strictly ontological reading from Elpidorou's drift toward "normative, moral and psychological" theorizing, which she treats as a departure from Heidegger's own framing rather than a development of it (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.6 n.9).
- **flow / engagement (contrast pole)**, **mind-wandering / task-unrelated thought**, and **attentional disengagement / meta-awareness** — not treated; this is a phenomenological/historical-epochal reading, not a cognitive-attentional one, and none of these constructs appear even in passing.

## 4. Reading of FCM

Hughes's exposition (§2, p.3–7) is a compact, faithful restatement of FCM §§19–38, read through Svendsen's situative/existential vocabulary rather than reconstructed section-by-section. The train-station and dinner-party examples are cited as the two situative levels (fcm-04-first-form, FCM §§19-23 book pp.78-105; fcm-05-second-form, FCM §§24-28 book pp.106-131), and profound boredom's world-as-whole "refusal" is cited as the existential level (fcm-06-third-form, FCM §§29-38 book pp.132-159) (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.4). This is restatement, not extension: Hughes adds no new textual material to fcm-04/05/06's own apparatus, though she is unusually explicit that the situative forms are "derived from and grounded in" the existential form, such that being left in limbo or left empty is "profound boredom that has become entangled with the world" (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.5) — a dependency claim fcm-04/05/06 themselves make more implicitly.

Hughes's more substantive contribution to the FCM reading is her account of the historical/epochal register — Heidegger's speculation that boredom, alongside doubt, despair, fear and hope, is a candidate for the fundamental attunement of the contemporary, technologized epoch, quoting Heidegger's own question of whether "a profound boredom draws back and forth like a silent fog" in the abysses of Dasein (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.6, quoting Heidegger 1995 p.77). This material is textually closest to fcm-03's closing §18c (where profound boredom is first named as the age's concealed attunement), but its fuller elaboration — the question of whether *contemporary* Dasein specifically has "become boring to itself" — belongs to fcm-07-particular-profound's territory (FCM §§37-38, book pp.160-168), which this unit treats as the primary bridge for Hughes's epochal reading (P/anticipatory-application mapping, since Hughes's own citation sits at the fcm-03/fcm-04 boundary rather than inside fcm-07's page range).

Hughes then extends the FCM reading by bringing in Heidegger's later, 1936–38 *Contributions to Philosophy (Of the Event)*, where he grows "more hesitant," worrying that the "calculation," "speed" and "massiveness" of homogenized, technologized societies may overwhelm boredom's revelatory capacity altogether and instead produce mere indifference, disillusionment and self-alienation (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.7). Hughes treats this as a genuine self-revision internal to Heidegger's own corpus — profound boredom's status shifts from a potentially revelatory fundamental attunement toward "a necessarily situative one" — and uses it as the more pessimistic reading she ultimately applies to the pandemic case (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.7).

Finally, Hughes contests two methodological features of Heidegger's account directly (§4, p.10-12), and both criticisms press against fcm-06/fcm-07's shared presupposition that there is a stable, intact "everyday world" from which one can be either absorbed or existentially estranged. First, she argues that because the pandemic suspended substantial aspects of the familiar world itself — schools, restaurants, travel, weddings, funerals — rather than merely withholding aspects within an otherwise-intact world, the situative/existential distinction's operative "limit" between familiar and unfamiliar can no longer be presupposed, which "weakens the assumption" that situative boredom cannot be philosophically revelatory (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.10-11). Second, in a footnote she flags as a related but separate concern, she notes that Heidegger's individualistic conception of boredom has no room for a *collective* turning-away from the world, since being withdrawn from "the world" depends on others continuing to be absorbed in it — a structural feature of the account that a pandemic, which withdraws everyone more or less simultaneously, exposes as a genuine limitation rather than an incidental omission (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.11 n.13).

## 5. Findings/Claims

- Pandemic boredom, read through the survey data, converges on the two Heideggerian themes of meaninglessness (loss of anchoring past/present/future events) and monotony (a temporal experience that is simultaneously stretched and collapsed) (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.8-9).
- On its face, pandemic boredom is essentially situative — a confrontation with relative rather than absolute meaninglessness, corresponding to the first and second FCM forms, because what respondents are bored in the face of remains attached to something within the world (the absence of significant intersubjective projects and events), not to the world's meaning as a whole (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.9-10).
- On the straightforward Heideggerian reading, pandemic boredom therefore cannot be philosophically revelatory in FCM's sense; rather, it arguably realizes the more pessimistic worry of the *Contributions*, where technologized, "levelled down" societies produce pervasive indifference rather than an existential confrontation (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.9-10).
- Pandemic boredom nonetheless problematizes the situative/existential ontological distinction itself, because the pandemic destabilized the tacit structure of the everyday world in general, not merely particular contents within an otherwise-intact world — making pandemic boredom "wider-ranging and more diffuse" than ordinary situative boredom and harder to distinguish from the existential form (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.10-11).
- Heidegger's account also cannot discriminate the relative philosophical significance of different people's pandemic boredom, because his train-station/dinner-party examples were never built to register the heterogeneous social, cultural, political and economic conditions that made the pandemic's harms unequally distributed; the survey data shows this bifurcation directly, with some respondents reporting renewed appreciation of relationships ("we seem to be getting along better," EN_UK_0046) and others reporting increased tension and distrust ("more arguments and destructive remarks," EN_UK_1074) under materially similar social-distancing measures (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.11-12).
- Absent a privileged vantage point on a unified world, Heidegger's interpretation is "forced back" onto the ongoing, unresolved psychological attempts to distinguish state from trait, common from modern, exogenous from endogenous, and non-pathological from pathological boredom (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.12).
- Hughes's conclusion is double-edged rather than simply debunking: Heidegger's framework remains valuable for organizing the phenomenon under meaninglessness/monotony, but pandemic boredom demonstrates the need for "a more critical appropriation" of that framework rather than its straightforward application (FAITHFUL-Hughes, p.13).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* Hughes's paper is this cluster's clearest working example of a large-N, open-ended survey being read phenomenologically to characterize a population's boredom, which makes it a direct methodological precedent for Part III's own survey-based interpretation of student boredom, not merely a source of Heideggerian vocabulary *(anticipatory-application)*. Two of her specific cautions transfer with unusual directness. First, her point that the situative/existential distinction presupposes a stable, intact "everyday world" backdrop — and loses explanatory power when that backdrop is itself disrupted rather than merely its contents — is a caution Part III should apply to any VLE/deployment environment that is itself altered (remote instruction, a novel delivery format, a poled desktop-3D vs. video-only comparison) rather than treating student boredom as occurring against a stable, given classroom world *(anticipatory-application)*. Second, her insistence that the relative significance of boredom cannot be read off a uniform mechanism but must be indexed to each respondent's differential concrete situation is a direct precedent for the corrected reading already on file for this dissertation regarding the VLE's proximity-voice and group-assignment structure — that a "we"-unmet-want gap in the survey reflects student-side activation differences within a materially shared design, not a design or grouping failure per se; Hughes's own bifurcated relationship-quality findings under identical social-distancing conditions are a clean parallel case for that same reasoning move *(anticipatory-application)*. Third, Hughes's explicit debate-axis position — that pandemic boredom is primarily environment/situation-caused (an absence of world-sustaining projects) yet its residual significance is agent/situation-relative rather than uniform — offers Part III a ready-made, already-argued position on the monotony/environment-caused vs. meaning/agent-caused axis, rather than one Part III must construct from scratch; it should be cited as a hybrid position, not assimilated to either pole *(anticipatory-application)*.
