# bor-sec-12 — Slaby, "Living in the Moment: Boredom and the Meaning of Existence in Heidegger and Pessoa" (2017)

**Type:** journal article (comparative phenomenology; ideology-critique of Heidegger's Rektoratszeit) · **Discipline:** philosophy (Heideggerian phenomenology; comparative literary-philosophical critique) · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** Strand A companion unit to bor-sec-13 (same author, revisiting the same FCM lecture) — pivots from the 2010 chapter's philosophy-of-emotion framing onto an explicit ideology-critique of Heidegger's "activist" reading of profound boredom's disclosive "message," staged against Fernando Pessoa's *Book of Disquiet* as a non-activist counter-model.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Slaby's announced project is deliberately "simplistic": to contrast how Heidegger and the Portuguese poet Fernando Pessoa each construe the transition out of a boredom-like state, toward what, and on what existential orientation (FE, p.236). The verdict is telegraphed from the outset — Pessoa "will come out on top" (FE, p.236) — and the paper is explicitly framed as a corrective to what Slaby calls an "activist" strain in early Heidegger: existence as a summons to take charge of one's life resolutely, a strain that Slaby traces directly into Heidegger's 1933/34 collaboration as NSDAP-approved Führer-Rektor of Freiburg (FE, p.235–236). The paper's thesis is not merely exegetical. Slaby argues that Heidegger's reading of profound boredom's disclosive "message" in *The Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics* (FCM, the 1929/30 lecture course) is never ideologically neutral: from the moment the third form yields its "message," Heidegger re-absorbs the insight into a demand for resolute, meaning-creating work, a move Slaby reads as continuous with — and an "all too evident foreshadowing" of (FE, p.237) — the Rektoratsrede and the Arbeitsstaat rhetoric of 1933–34. Pessoa's *Book of Disquiet* is then read as a sustained, aestheticized refusal of exactly that demand: an affirmed dwelling within depersonalized, meaningless detachment that never answers boredom's call back to resolute action. Slaby's closing claim generalizes the stakes beyond Heidegger to philosophy's own "habitus of assuredness," for which literary writing — Pessoa above all — supplies a corrective (FE, p.256).

## 2. Structure

The paper follows Slaby's own four numbered divisions.

1. **"Introduction: An antidote to philosophy"** (p.235–236) — frames the *Book of Disquiet* as a deliberate counter-image to philosophy's confident, epistemically sovereign voice; states the anti-"activist" thesis via Adorno's aphorism that philosophy is "the most serious matter" yet "not all that serious" (FE, p.236).
2. **"Boredom and Resolute Being in Heidegger"** (p.237–246) — reconstructs the FCM lecture's basic framing (boredom as a *Grundstimmung* that normally "sleeps" and must be awakened), then moves quickly past the first form to a close reading of the second (the dinner-party passage, quoted in full German) and the third (the "undifferentiated no one," the disclosive "message," the *Augenblick*), culminating in the paper's central polemical claim that Heidegger's own construal of the third form's payoff is an unargued activist imposition, not a textual necessity.
3. **"Interlude: The collapse of dasein and work in the Rektoratszeit"** (p.244–246) — via Werner Hamacher's critique of Heidegger's "Rektorats-Philosophie," traces how "work" (*Arbeit*) comes, in the 1933 "Rede an Arbeitslose" and the 1934 Logic Lecture, to occupy exactly the structural place FCM and *Being and Time* reserve for the *Augenblick*; notes the postwar German continuity of prizing *Arbeit* even after repudiating National Socialism generally.
4. **"Pessoa, or the Ecstasy of Not-Being"** (p.246–256) — introduces Bernardo Soares and Pessoa's heteronyms, then reads the *Book of Disquiet* in three self-declared "stages": Stage 1 (p.249–250), fragments of factlessness, non-existence, and undirected tedium; Stage 2 (p.250–253), fragments of a tentative, self-fashioned passivity in which writing becomes a "sanctuary"; Stage 3 (p.253–256), fragments in which literature is explicitly the "art of making real," compared and ultimately contrasted with Heidegger's account of authentic dying, before a closing reflection on style, seriousness, and philosophy's need for a "friendly antidote."

## 3. Constructs

- **profound/deep boredom (FCM 3rd form)** — the paper's central construct (p.239–245): the "undifferentiated no one," total indifference of beings as a whole, and the disclosive "message" that calls Dasein back into the *Augenblick* (FAITHFUL-Slaby, p.241–242).
- **being-bored-with (2nd form)** — treated at comparable depth via the full German dinner-party passage and its own translation/paraphrase; read as a "standing now" (*stehendes Jetzt*) in which a freely chosen evening becomes a self-forming, lost span of lifetime (FAITHFUL-Slaby, p.239–240).
- **being-bored-by (1st form)** — bracketed deliberately: named via Heidegger's shabby-train-station example but explicitly not developed "in order to have more space for the two deeper manifestations" (FE, p.239).
- **temporality of boredom (time-drag)** — the connective construct: *Langeweile* as lived time's literal "becoming long," and profound boredom as the fusion of past/present/future into an indefinitely stretched horizon (FAITHFUL-Slaby, p.238, 241).
- **monotony / meaninglessness** — central to the third form's "melody of duration has ceased" diagnosis (FAITHFUL-Slaby, p.241) and, in a different register, to Pessoa's "complete and absolute tedium" (FAITHFUL-Pessoa/Slaby, p.249).
- **boredom-as-regulatory-signal (functional account)** — present only as the object of critique: profound boredom "issues a message" (FE, p.241–242), but Slaby contests the further, unargued step from message to a demand for resolute self-transforming action — a proto-functional reading Slaby reconstructs faithfully only to reject its activist extension (FAITHFUL-Slaby, qualified by Slaby's own critique, p.243–245).
- **flow / engagement (contrast pole)** — present in two distinct registers: the dinner party's *outward* liveliness masking an underlying boredom (a surface/depth divergence, p.239–240), and, later, Pessoa's non-instrumental aesthetic absorption in writing as a wholly different, non-activist mode of "engagement" that never resolves into resolute action (FAITHFUL-Slaby, p.251–253).
- **situational vs. trait/state boredom; boredom proneness; mind-wandering/task-unrelated thought; attentional disengagement/meta-awareness** — none of these cognitive-science constructs are addressed in their own idiom. The nearest analogue is literary-phenomenological: Pessoa's fragments of "torpor," being "drunk on not being," and slumber-like sensations that "fill up our mind like a fog" (FAITHFUL-Pessoa/Slaby, p.251) gesture toward attentional withdrawal but are offered as poetic testimony, not as an empirical construct (P/anticipatory-application — any mapping onto meta-awareness/mind-wandering literature is this cluster's own bridge, not Slaby's).

## 4. Reading of FCM

Slaby's engagement with FCM is textually dense and can be triangulated precisely against this corpus's own triple-locus (book/GA/pdf) citations. Slaby quotes and cites throughout from the German original (GA 29/30), cross-referencing the McNeill/Walker translation only once explicitly (FE, p.238 n.6); every German-language locus below has been checked directly against the matching FCM unit and, in every case but one, lands on the identical passage that unit itself already isolates as load-bearing.

Slaby's account of the second form (p.239–240) draws four block quotations and paraphrases from GA pp.165, 170, 180, and 189 — respectively the dinner-party scene proper, the claim that "our entire comportment and behaviour" becomes the passing-of-time, the self-forming emptiness that abandons "the resolute openness of our whole Dasein," and the "standing now" that is "we ourselves." These match fcm-05-second-form's own citations at book pp.110, 112, 119–120, and 126 (GA pp.164–67, 169–70, 179–82, and 189–91) almost word for word (bridges-to-fcm, fcm-05-second-form). Slaby's own gloss sharpens fcm-05's abstract "arises out of Dasein itself" into the more concrete image of a "lost span of lifetime" slipping through the fingers "like sand on the beach" (FE, p.240) — an interpretive intensification, not a departure.

The third-form reading is FCM's own keystone material, read through fcm-06-third-form. Slaby's single most load-bearing citation — Heidegger's claim that "the more profound it becomes" (FAITHFUL-Slaby/Heidegger, GA p.201), boredom is ever more fully rooted in lived time — is the passage fcm-06 itself opens with at book p.133 (GA p.201); Slaby's own footnote even supplies this exact English-page cross-reference (FE, p.238 n.6), independently confirming the corpus's book/GA conversion. The indifference-of-beings-as-a-whole quotation (GA p.208) matches fcm-06's book p.138; the telling-refusal-as-Dasein's-"being delivered over" formula (GA p.210) matches fcm-06's book p.139–140; the "dawning of possibilities left unexploited" (*Ansagen der brachliegenden Möglichkeiten*, GA p.212) matches fcm-06's book p.141; and the *Augenblick* definition — glossed as "the look of resolute disclosedness" opening and holding open the full situation of action (GA p.224) — matches fcm-06's own book p.149 verbatim (bridges-to-fcm, fcm-06-third-form). Slaby reconstructs this sequence faithfully; his departure comes only in how he glosses its upshot, reading Heidegger's insistence on heeding the message as itself already a substantive, contestable existential-political commitment rather than a neutral phenomenological finding (FE, p.242–243).

The cultural-climate condemnation passage Slaby quotes at length — on forgetting that Dasein must be taken up and, as Slaby glosses it, "throw[n] onto his shoulders" (FE/Heidegger, GA pp.246–47) — is the identical passage fcm-07-particular-profound isolates at book p.165 (GA pp.246–47) as the chapter's diagnosis that contemporary Dasein has "slipped out of the danger-zone" (bridges-to-fcm, fcm-07-particular-profound). This is the textual anchor for Slaby's claim that the lecture "crashes down" from about §38 onward into rhetoric he likens to "the convoluted eyewash of an aspiring Nazi party leader" (FE, p.243) — a judgment this unit's own reading confirms is precisely where fcm-07 (Chapter Five, on the fundamental attunement of contemporary Dasein) begins. Slaby's closing citation of the lecture's demand for someone able to "strike terror into our Dasein" (GA p.255) falls just beyond fcm-07's own indexed range (book pp.160–168; GA pp.[240–249, 252–53]) — approximately book p.170, at the Part One/Part Two hinge — and is flagged here as an approximate locus rather than a directly confirmed one.

Slaby does not engage fcm-04-first-form beyond the passing train-station mention (p.239); no German locus is cited for it, and the unit is bridged here only to register that omission as deliberate (bridges-to-fcm, fcm-04-first-form).

## 5. Findings/Claims

- Heidegger's FCM boredom lecture is not merely descriptive but "performative" — it aims to awaken, in the listener/reader, the very attunement it describes (FAITHFUL-Slaby, p.237).
- The second form's emptiness is "self-formed": a freely chosen, outwardly agreeable evening becomes, in retrospect, a lost and self-abandoned span of lifetime (FAITHFUL-Slaby, p.239–240).
- The third form dissolves the bounded "span" of the second form into an indefinitely stretched temporal horizon in which all beings, self included, become uniformly indifferent (FAITHFUL-Slaby, p.241).
- Heidegger's reading is "not neutral": the tone and drive of the relevant passages betray "the nervous unrest of the activist," someone who "can hardly bear even a brief instant of futility" — the insight into meaninglessness is immediately reconscripted into "a mobilizing appeal, a call to arms" (FE, p.243).
- This is not an incidental rhetorical flourish but continuous with Heidegger's subsequent trajectory: the 1933 "Rede an Arbeitslose" and 1934 Logic Lecture install *Arbeit* (work) at precisely the structural place — the *Augenblick* — that FCM and *Being and Time* reserve for resolute, authentic temporality (FE, citing Hamacher, p.244–246).
- Slaby explicitly asks the question Heidegger's own text forecloses: whether lingering in profound boredom, dwelling in depersonalized detachment, might harbor its own "bliss" or "peace of mind" rather than demanding to be overcome (FE, p.244).
- Pessoa's *Book of Disquiet* answers this question in the affirmative through literary practice rather than argument: Bernardo Soares settles into tedium and existential exhaustion as a "wellspring for poetic production" rather than breaking its spell through resolute action (FE, p.250).
- Pessoa's account of merely "perishing" rather than dying existentially parallels — while failing to qualify for — Heidegger's *Being and Time* account of authentic death, since a life without resolute care cannot possess the existential ending Heidegger reserves for Dasein (FAITHFUL-Slaby, p.252–253).
- Despite the sharp contrast, Slaby concedes late in the paper that literature itself becomes, for Pessoa, its own existential commitment — "the end towards which all human effort would have to strive" (FAITHFUL-Pessoa/Slaby, p.255) — so the two authors are, in this one respect, "not all that far apart" (FE, p.255).
- The paper's final generalization: novelists and poets — Pessoa above all — are "the true shepherds of being," offering philosophy a needed corrective to its own over-assured, unitary constructions of the subject (FE, p.256).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* Slaby's central polemical move — showing that Heidegger's own construal of profound boredom's "message" as a demand for resolute, meaning-creating work is an ideological importation rather than a phenomenological necessity — is directly useful as a methodological caution for any Part III framing that reads student or VR-attention boredom as a deficit-signal automatically calling for corrective intervention. The caution generalizes past Heidegger: an instrument or design philosophy that treats disengagement as inherently a problem to be solved should be able to say why, rather than assuming the "message" of low engagement is self-interpreting *(anticipatory-application)*. Pessoa's counter-model — dwelling affirmatively within depersonalized, low-arousal detachment, discovering in it a "wellspring" rather than a deficit — supplies Part III with a second, non-clinical vocabulary for coding VR-attention lapses that are not simply disengagement-awaiting-correction, complementing (without replacing) the cluster's own physiological low-arousal/"zombie stare" readings in Strand D/E *(anticipatory-application)*. Because this unit and bor-sec-13 are the same author reading the same lecture seven years apart, the pairing is useful evidence that a single scholar's own reading of FCM's third form shifted register — from a philosophy-of-emotion reconstruction (2010) to an explicit ideology-critique (2017) — without changing the underlying textual reconstruction; Part III can cite either unit for the phenomenology of the three forms, but should reach for bor-sec-12 specifically when the point being made is about the political stakes of *how* boredom's disclosure gets read, not just *what* it discloses *(anticipatory-application)*. Finally, Slaby's own explicit self-citation (FE, p.237 n.2) licenses treating bor-sec-13 as this unit's own acknowledged predecessor — a genuine authorial continuity, not a retrospective construction of this cluster's — while the reverse direction (reading bor-sec-13 as somehow anticipating bor-sec-12) would be an anachronism this unit does not commit *(anticipatory-application)*.
