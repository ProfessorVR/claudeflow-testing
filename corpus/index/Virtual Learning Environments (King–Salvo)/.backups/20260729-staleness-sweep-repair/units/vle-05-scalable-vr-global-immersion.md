## 1. What the paper is (platform + course facts) [FE]

A scaled redesign of the one-unit **asynchronous online** elective "Addressing Unmet Clinical Needs" — **now a prerequisite for the UCI BME senior capstone**. It delivers *global clinical immersion* to fix the travel/cost/capacity limits of physical immersion, explicitly to satisfy **ABET Student Outcome 2** (global/cultural/economic design factors).

- **Content:** cataract surgeries recorded at **UCI Medical Center + Children's Hospital of Orange County (CHOC)** (initial) and expanded to **Vietnam + Paraguay** (UCI IRB Exempt #5204 filming / #904 assessment), captured with **360-degree stereoscopic + head-mounted cameras** (dual capture: 360 OR view + first-person vantage).
- **Platform:** built in **Unreal Engine (Epic Games)**; **real-time voice chat + screen sharing, "enabling collaborative team viewing"** if students meet in groups in-world. VR-headset users get 3D; desktop users get a 360-degree panoramic 2D view ("greater immersion than standard videos").
- **Fallback:** compatibility-blocked students access the same videos via a **YouTube channel + website (vr.uci.design)** with interactive 360-degree panning.
- **Weekly 10–15-min lectures + quizzes**, Biodesign textbook, innovation notebook, final group report.

## 2. RESULTS — quantitative (Figures 3–4) [FE]  *(emphasis)*

**N = 121 enrolled (Winter 2025); 113 completed the survey** (112 on the two Likert items).
- **83.19% were able to use the virtual platform** (Q1). [FE]
- **62.83% reported increased immersion, presence, or embodiment**, enhancing perception of the OR environment (Q2). [FE]
- **65.49% indicated the clinical-immersion videos substantially enhanced their ability to examine how healthcare is performed** (Q3, 1–5 Likert). [FE]
- **64.6% reported recognizing differences in clinical operations across countries** via the cataract videos (Q4). [FE]

These are the corpus's first published **positive efficacy percentages for the platform itself** (contrast vle-01's deployment-survey emphasis on friction/foreclosure). [P: the efficacy pole of the same VLE the survey corpus reads for foreclosure.]

## 3. RESULTS — qualitative: five themes (thematic analysis of 94 responses) [FE]  *(emphasis)*

1. **Recognition of resource disparities** — technology/OR-condition/infrastructure gaps; global "lack of advanced medical technology," "outdated equipment," multiple simultaneous surgeries vs. U.S. dedicated ORs.
   - [FE-STUDENT] "Vietnam's hospital was very cramped and has a lot of patients in one small room. Paraguay had screen technology which Vietnam didn't seem to have."
   - [FE-STUDENT] "…I learned that certain surgeries can be done in an efficient manner, where particular focus could be put on the rate of patient procedures and to what extent resources should be used to maximize speed without costing effectiveness."
2. **Cultural & systemic factors** — government roles, caregiving norms. In Paraguay "all people are required to be an organ donor"; in Vietnam "family members assume significant caregiving roles."
   - [FE-STUDENT] "Since I am Vietnamese… In Vietnam, nurses only help doctors, while patient care is delegated to the family." (a student pursuing the difference beyond the videos — independent inquiry.)
3. **Enhanced understanding of cataracts/procedures** — the "delicate" nature of eye surgery. [FE-STUDENT] "I have learned a lot more about the process of cataract surgery and how delicate the surgeons need to be…"
4. **Importance of collaboration / team coordination** — "multiple doctors and nurses," "a lot of people in the room."
   - [FE-STUDENT] "I didn't realize how many people there are in the room while the procedure is happening. … a bit of a shock, but it was really interesting to me."
   - [FE-STUDENT] "Seeing how medical teams adapt in different settings has helped me understand the real world needs for biomedical innovations. This experience is crucial for designing solutions that address gaps in care…"
5. **BME innovation connections** — "cost-effective and scalable solutions," "gaps in care."
   - [FE-STUDENT] "I learned here that it is important to see how these devices are used in a medical setting rather than a controlled environment… it allows for gaps and flaws to be rapidly identified."

## 4. RESULTS — barriers to learning [FE]  *(emphasis)*

- **pixelation / poor video quality**, **motion sickness**, and **insufficient narration ("lack of explanation")** hampered learning.
- No advanced video compression → students streamed at **suboptimal resolution (720p or low-bitrate 1080p)** due to bandwidth, **particularly on UCI's campus** → choppy/laggy visuals → motion sickness.
  - [FE-STUDENT] "Honestly, the videos were great, but I personally couldn't focus on the VR video because I get motion sickness."
  - [FE-STUDENT] "…As my laptop is unable to support high resolution, it was difficult for myself to make sense of the video, especially without proper narration from the surgeon or team…"
- Pixelation blocked "seeing the actual or close-up procedure."

## 5. DISCUSSION [FE]  *(emphasis)*

- Survey results "confirmed the virtual platform effectively supported clinical immersion, enhancing students' understanding of global healthcare disparities." [FE]
- **29.20% (N = 33) used YouTube** due to **MacOS compatibility or storage issues** — most still found it valuable for cross-country comparison. [FE]  *(corroborates vle-01 node 2 "video-only stratum" and node 3 "two flight anatomies" at a new site.)*
- Alignment to **ABET SO2** (diverse healthcare contexts) and **SO7** (acquire/apply new knowledge — students ran independent interviews). [FE]
- **Planned fixes:** MacOS/Linux compatibility, better video quality, reduced storage (**currently 3.3 GB**), **mobile (iOS/Android)** priority beyond Windows PC + VR headset.
- **The boredom line [FE] (key Part III hook):** "Interactive features like real-time quizzes, narrated modules, and **engagement alerts will be integrated to address student feedback on boredom**, informed by research on cognitive engagement and retention." → the authors themselves diagnose the passive-playback stall and name interactivity as the remedy. [P: this is vle-01's A₃→A₄ stall (node 4) surfacing in the authors' own discussion, and the pivot that ties efficacy to *interactive* design.]

## 6. Part III integration — pedagogical efficacy ↔ interactive VLE [P]

The user's stated interest is the tie between **pedagogical efficacy and interactive VLEs**, and this paper carries it on both sides at once:

- **Efficacy side (published, quantified):** a credit-bearing, ABET-required, scaled course (N=121) where a strong majority could use the platform (83.19%), felt greater immersion/presence/embodiment (62.83%), and reported enhanced ability to examine healthcare (65.49%) and recognize cross-country differences (64.6%), with rich qualitative learning across five themes. This is the corpus's clearest evidence that the VLE *did pedagogical work*. [FE]
- **Interactivity side (the limiting condition):** where the platform under-affords action it stalls — the barriers (passive playback, "lack of explanation," motion sickness) and the authors' own remedy ("engagement alerts… to address student feedback on **boredom**"; real-time quizzes; narrated modules) locate efficacy's ceiling exactly at **interactivity**. [FE] This is vle-01's **A₃→A₄ stall** and vle-08/09's **screen-route presence** and **optative co-disclosedness** (collaboration wanted) restated by the deployment's own authors — and it is why "collaborative team viewing," voice chat, and screen sharing are the platform's most Part-III-relevant features. [P]
- **The synthesis claim for Part III:** efficacy and interactivity are not separable here — the same deployment that shows a VLE teaching global design skills also shows that its efficacy rises and falls with how much the environment lets students *do* and *be together*, not merely *watch*. [P]
