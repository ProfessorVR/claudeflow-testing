## Section A — Operational Definition: Attitude as Incipient / Suspended Act

**Operational definition (burke-direct, ≤60 words).** Attitude is the dispositional term naming an **incipient act** — a leaning, inclination, or orientation toward action that is not yet (or not necessarily) carried into overt conduct. "Rhetorical language is inducement to action (or to attitude, attitude being an incipient act)" (RM-01 p.42). Attitude is the suspended/rehearsal mode that mediates between potency (*dynamis*) and full act (*energeia*).

**Question answered.** *In what disposition / with what orientation* was (or would) the act be done?

**Two complementary senses (burke-direct):**
1. **Incipient act** — attitude as the *beginning* of an act, the readiness or first stirring that may issue in conduct (RM-02 pp.50–51; the *Grammar*'s Mead/Richards seeds). The arrow points *toward* action.
2. **Suspended act** — attitude as a *held* orientation that may never be consummated, deliberately or constitutively. Kierkegaard's "renunciation and advance fused in one attitude" (RM-06 p.251); the mystic's "attitudinal glow" in which all impulses remain "in a half-way status nascendi" (RM-07 pp.331–332). The arrow is *arrested*.

These are not two terms but two faces of one: an attitude is an act *in potentia* whose actualization is incipient (face 1) and may be perpetually suspended (face 2). This is exactly the "suspended/incipient/symbolic-rehearsal mode mediating between potency and *energeia*" that the Grammar manual's Section A forecast the Rhetoric pipeline would supply.

**Boundary criteria (burke-direct):**
1. **Not yet overt conduct.** If the disposition has issued in a completed external act, code Act, not Attitude (RM-02 pp.50–51).
2. **Distinct from Purpose.** Purpose is the *end toward which*; Attitude is the *disposition with which*. A single purpose can be pursued in many attitudes (eager, reluctant, ironic).
3. **Carries affective/evaluative tone.** Attitude is the term through which emotion and evaluation color action — "the scenic word 'property' translated into terms of an agent's attitude" carries the moral charge of avarice (RM-01 p.24).
4. **Can be the terminal target of rhetoric.** Much rhetoric (and all lyric poetry) aims at attitude *only*, demanding no overt action — persuasion-to-attitude is more general and foundational than persuasion-to-action (RM-02 pp.50–51, T3).

## Section C — How Attitude Modifies Each of the Five Grammar Terms

Attitude is not a sixth *box* alongside the five; it is a **dispositional modifier** that colors each pentad term with the orientation-with-which. The Grammar manual's Section A forward-reference fields are here populated with `burke-direct` content (the patch in `rm-pentad-extension-patch` §II folds these in).

|---|---|---|---|
| **Act** | **Attitude as incipient-act sub-term / symbolic-rehearsal spectrum.** The Act is preceded and shadowed by its incipient form; the same doing can be enacted in different attitudes, and some "acts" are attitudes literally enacted (mountain-climbing, p.302). | RM-01 p.42; RM-07 p.302; GM-06 p.243 | The player's *readiness-to-move* between committed actions; theorycrafting / rehearsed play as symbolic-rehearsal Act. (IG: Kinesthetic readiness-state; Ludic strategy-rehearsal.) |
| **Scene** | **Attitudinal-scene: situation-as-already-evaluated.** The scene is not neutral ground but a *rhetorical scene of address* pre-loaded with evaluative tone (the "scenic word 'property'" carrying avarice; the Urn-as-Attitude). | RM-01 p.24; GM-10 p.458 | The gameworld scene encountered *as* already affectively coloured (atmospherics-as-affect; "I want to live there"). (IG: Affective via Scene; Spatial.) |
| **Agent** | **Attitude as the agent's dispositional stance / identification-disposition.** Acting-together's shared *attitudes* make agents consubstantial (p.21); the agent's stance toward co-agents is an incipient act of identification. | RM-01 p.21; RM-06 p.251 | Player's stance toward avatar/guild/co-players (devotional, competitive, ironic). (IG: Shared + Affective.) |
| **Agency** | **Rhetorical-instrumentality of address (attitudinal use of means).** Means are deployed *in* an attitude — the standoffish, the eager, the ceremonial; the censorial appellative is a tonal gesture (an attitude operating as instrument). | RM-03 pp.91, 98; RM-07 pp.269–271 | The attitudinal coloring of control/rule-use (the standoffish element of skilled performance). | **Purpose** | **Attitudinal-purpose: the end held in a disposition.** Ultimate vocabulary realigns "one's attitude toward the struggles of politics" (p.188); a purpose is pursued *as* devotional, ironic, or completionist. **Why a modifier, not a box.** Burke's pentad answers five questions (what / where / who / how / why); Attitude answers a *sixth, transverse* question — *in what disposition?* — that can be asked of any of the five. This is why the Grammar manual placed "Rhetoric-Attitude" as a **column** across all five terms in its Section-E integration table rather than as a sixth row. The present manual confirms and populates that column.

## Section D — Attitude as the Hinge Between Identification and Affect

Attitude is the term that connects the keystone bridge (consubstantiation↔incorporation) to the player's lived emotional register. The argument (developed in `rm-identification-consubstantiation-manual` §D.4) is:

1. Consubstantiality is **partly constituted by shared attitudes** (RM-01 p.21, `burke-direct`).
2. **Attitude is an incipient act** (RM-01 p.42, `burke-direct`).
3. Therefore a player's *disposition/stance* toward avatar, guild, and gameworld is an **incipient act of identification** [`anticipatory-projection`].

This makes Attitude the dispositional hinge: it links *identification* (the master term, → Shared/Incorporation) to *affect* (the felt register, → Affective). It is also the precise point at which the Burke apparatus meets the Calleja apparatus, because Calleja's manual independently assigns its **Affective** dimension the Layer-B target "**ATTITUDE** (Burke's later sixth term)" — "Attitude captures the emotional-dispositional coloring of action; AFFECTIVE is precisely the response-state dimension" (`ig-pim-operationalization-manual` §A.5, §E.1). The two pipelines converge on Attitude from opposite directions, which is strong evidence the mapping is structural rather than imposed.

## Section E — The Player-Attitude Register → Calleja Affective Involvement

**All mappings `[anticipatory-projection]`.** Burke makes no game claims; the five registers are a typology synthesized from Burke's attitude-concept and projected onto play. Each register is a *sustained dispositional stance* (an incipient/suspended act) toward the gameworld — i.e., Attitude in Burke's sense — and each foregrounds Calleja's **Affective** involvement dimension (the player's emotional-dispositional state), while recruiting a secondary dimension and corresponding to a pentad-term emphasis.

| **Explorer** | Range of mountings (kinesthetic/spatial/anagogic strands); the "imaging of transformation"; scene-as-already-evaluated (RM-07 pp.301–313; RM-00; RM-01 p.24) | Stance of dwelling-toward and ascending-through the gameworld scene; the incipient act of inhabiting place as already-evaluated. | **Affective** (awe/calm) + **Spatial** + **Narrative** | Scene (attitudinal-scene) + Act |

**Register notes.**
- The five registers are **not mutually exclusive**; a single player oscillates among them, and any one act can be performed in more than one register (consistent with Attitude-as-modifier, Section C). The typology is a *coding instrument* for the dissertation's affective-dimension analysis, parallel to Calleja's macro/micro signature scheme.
- The **elation/accidie** arc (RM-07 pp.313–318) cuts across all five: each register has its sustained ("elation," identification holding) and disrupted ("accidie," the self left alone after flow-break) phase — the emotional grammar of engagement vs disengagement (`ig-pim` §A.5 Affective macro/micro).
- The **ironic-meta** register connects to the Grammar manual's Section D irony/dialectic battery (Q40: classic/humble irony = consubstantiality with the toxic optimizer vs. romantic/Pharisaic irony = "not me") — the analyst's attitude and the player's attitude are the same Burkean term operating at two levels.

## How Attitude completes the six-term motive axis

The pentad (Act / Scene / Agent / Agency / Purpose) answers *what / where / who / how / why*. Attitude answers the transverse sixth question — *in what disposition?* — that can be put to any of the five (Section C). It is therefore the term that:
- **closes the potency→act arc** (it is the incipient/suspended mode mediating *dynamis* and *energeia*, Section A);
- **is internal to consubstantiality** (shared attitudes make agents consubstantial, p.21), making it the hinge of the keystone bridge (Section D);
- **is the meeting-point of the two pipelines** (Burke's 6th term = Calleja's Affective Layer-B target, Section D);
- **supplies the player-disposition typology** that grounds Affective involvement (Section E).

With Attitude folded in, the motive axis is complete: five questions of *placement* plus one of *disposition* — the full six-term apparatus the Burke × Calleja integration deploys.
