## 1. The Conceptual Architecture of the Book

*A Rhetoric of Motives* is organized around a single terminological move whose implications Burke unpacks across eight units: replacing *persuasion* as rhetoric's master term with *identification*. The replacement is necessary because persuasion cannot reach the intermediate rhetorical area — expression that is neither fully deliberate nor fully unconscious — where most actual rhetoric operates. The politician who may be sincerely self-deceived, the artist whose work performs political work at one remove, the schoolboy who imitates his social superiors without recognizing the rhetorical charge of the imitation — all of these inhabit an area classical persuasion theory must misclassify.

The book's conceptual architecture is therefore structured by a series of concentric enlargements on a single nucleus. The nucleus (RM-01) defines identification and its substance-theoretic ground, consubstantiality. Each successive unit expands the reach of that nucleus by adding a new species or structural condition: formal appeal as identification through shared pattern (RM-02); mystification as the dyslogistic assessment of identification's ideological operations (RM-03); hierarchy as the scenic condition within which identification is organized into a graduated scale (RM-04); ultimate terms as the terministic form in which identification achieves its widest motivational scope (RM-05); courtship as the rhetorical mode identification takes when it crosses hierarchic difference (RM-06); and pure persuasion as identification's limiting case — rhetoric pursued for its own intrinsic satisfaction, culminating in ultimate identification as the apex of the arc (RM-07).

Cutting across this serial expansion are three transversal concepts that appear in almost every unit: *attitude* (as an incipient act, Burke's seeding of a sixth dramatistic term beyond the Grammar's pentad); *the negative* (division as identification's ironic counterpart, motivationally primary precisely because identification is compensatory to it); and the *terministic screen* (the principle that any vocabulary order directs and deflects attention, making apparent what it simultaneously conceals).

## 2. Tier-1 Spine Concepts (10)

**Identification** is the master term, formally established at RM-01 (p.20) as the replacement for persuasion. A is identified with B when their interests are (or are persuaded to be) joined, making them consubstantial while each remains a distinct locus of motives. The concept operates at every scale Burke examines: property-identification (a man surrounded by properties that 'name his number,' p.24); class-identification through specialty ('Belonging is rhetorical,' p.28); identification between beings of unequal status (the ground of mystery and courtship, p.48); and ultimate identification (the apex of the arc, dissolution of the individual into boundless being, p.333). It also encompasses self-address ('an I addressing its me,' Mead, p.38), unconscious or by-default identification, and the cynically engineered identifications of Iago and Tartuffe.

**Consubstantiality / Consubstantiation** is identification's substance-theoretic ground (RM-01, pp.20–23). To identify A with B is to make them 'substantially one' while each remains a distinct locus — 'both joined and separate, at once a distinct substance and consubstantial with another' (p.21). The ground is action: 'substance was an act; and a way of life is an acting-together; and in acting together, men have common sensations, concepts, images, ideas, attitudes that make them consubstantial' (p.21). Consubstantiality is therefore at once substance-theoretic (carried from the Grammar's paradox of substance), act-based (co-action, not static likeness), and compensatory to division (affirmed because men are apart). Across units it is extended from person/person (RM-01) to imitation by community of ways (RM-04), local participation in universal developmental principle (RM-05), and consubstantiality through shared sacrifice (RM-07, Rattlesnake Club paradigm).

**Attitude (Incipient Act) — 6th Dramatistic Term** seeds a concept beyond the Grammar's pentad. The key formulation appears at RM-01 p.42: rhetorical language is 'inducement to action (or to attitude, attitude being an incipient act).' A leaning or orientation not yet overt conduct, attitude is more general as a rhetorical target than action itself. RM-02 develops it as the ground of 'persuasion to attitude vs. persuasion to action.' RM-03 shows it operating as the tonal charge of the censorial appellative. RM-06's perpetual courtship — 'renunciation and advance fused in one attitude' (p.251) — is its clearest sustained instance. At RM-07's culmination, all nervous impulses 'attitudinally glow' simultaneously in the mystic state — the pure incipience of ultimate identification.

**Mystery** is established in RM-03 (p.115) as the rhetorical condition arising where different kinds of beings communicate across a structural social divide. Not subjective feeling but an objective structural effect of hierarchic difference, it requires both strangeness and the possibility of communion. RM-04 shows mystery to be intrinsic to the persuasive act as such, not merely an ideological mystification of class (p.176–180). RM-06 gives its phenomenology — embarrassment, glamour, military-caste aura, stage fright — and demonstrates the range from Castiglione's classically resolved paradigm to Kafka's grotesque opacity. At RM-07's limit, identification pushed absolutely becomes mystical.

**Hierarchy** is the eulogistic word for bureaucracy (RM-03, p.118) and the universal motivational-symbolic principle organizing existence on a scale of higher/lower. It is inevitable in systematic thought; no particular hierarchy is inevitable, but the principle is indigenous to all well-rounded human thinking (RM-04, p.141). Hierarchy generates courtship (the rhetoric of communications across ranks), mystery (the opacity of hierarchic difference), and mystification (the concealment of specific by generic motive). In RM-05 it becomes the formal structure of an ultimate vocabulary — a guiding-idea or principle of principles that ranks competing dialectical voices in a developmental series rather than leaving them in parliamentary jangle.

**Courtship** is the rhetorical principle operative whenever beings of different social kinds communicate (RM-06, p.208). Not primarily sexual but structural, it uses mystery's opacity as its suasive resource. Castiglione's Book of the Courtier is the classical paradigm (four ascending stages toward a transcendent Platonic oration); Kafka's Castle is the grotesque caricature (formal elements present but distorted; mystery intensified by opacity). Kierkegaard's perpetual courtship — renunciation and advance fused in one attitude — is the dialectical resolution (RM-06, p.251). Courtship is the abstract paradigm of all persuasion where hierarchy is present; at its limit it merges into pure persuasion.

**The Negative** names division as identification's ironic counterpart. Identification is affirmed with earnestness precisely because there is division — 'rhetoric is concerned with the state of Babel after the Fall' (RM-01, p.23). Pure identification would eliminate strife; absolute separateness would also eliminate it; their ambiguous joining is the standing invitation to rhetoric. The negative also structures: victimage and the Kill (hierarchy generates guilt that seeks discharge through scapegoating, RM-07); mystification (the specific concealed by the generic, RM-03); and dialectical terms whose meaning shifts by contrast (RM-05).

**Pure Persuasion** is the limit-concept of rhetoric: the saying of something for a satisfaction intrinsic to the saying, not for extraverbal advantage (RM-07, p.267). Exists nowhere in absolute form but is present as a motivational ingredient in any rhetoric. Its formal hallmark is self-interference or standoffishness — a persisting persuasive act requires its own self-interference, because when the plea is answered persuasion ends and becomes something else. Seeded at RM-04's ten-step synthesis (pp.174–180) and RM-06's perpetual courtship, formally defined at RM-07 and extended into the economic (new needs as perpetual shifting of objectives) and metaphysical (prayer as pure beseeching addressed to the god-term itself).

**Terministic Screen** names the principle that any vocabulary order directs and deflects attention. First operative (unnamed) in RM-00's imaging-of-transformation analysis: the specific transformation-image chosen screens what motivational dimensions appear. Named and systematized at RM-03 (terministic reservation), and fully theorized at RM-05: positive screen is physicalist/perceptual; dialectical screen is partisan/polar; ultimate screen ranks motives hierarchically. Switching screens changes what counts as a motive. The god-term catalog at RM-07 (substance, nature, history, society, logos, hierarchy, authority, consubstantiality, nation, freedom, pure persuasion) presents surrogate god-terms as terministic screens each refracting the same ultimate rhetorical motive.

**Ultimate Identification** is the apex of the identification arc (RM-07, pp.328–333): identification pushed to its dialectical limit becomes mystical union — dissolution of the individual into boundless being, consubstantiality with the universal order. Characterized by William James's composite mystic portrait as the motive heading in 'God as the beloved cynosure and sinecure, the end of all desire' (p.333). Distinguished from Ersatzmystik (substitute mysticisms — speed, sex, money, war) in which means become ends, deforming the same drive into demonic purpose. The principle of the oxymoron — simultaneous glow of contradictory impulses — is its literary analogue.

## 3. Tier-2 Major Structural Concepts (19)

The 23 Tier-2 concepts provide the working scaffolding for the Tier-1 spine. Several deserve particular note for their role in the Burke × Calleja integration:

*Imaging of Transformation* (RM-00): establishes the pre-definitional logic of terministic screening — the specific image chosen for a ground-motive locates and filters what motivational dimensions appear.

*Formal Appeal* (RM-02): the persuasive power of form as such; collaborative expectancy as a species of identification with the speaker's movement rather than ideology. Maps directly to game design's plan-execution-feedback loop.

*Triplicate Vocabulary / Censorial Appellative* (RM-03): Bentham's demonstration that rhetoric's normal condition is evaluative poles, not neutral center; the censorial appellative operates as an attitude, pre-loading conclusion before argument.

*Mystification* (RM-03–04): the dyslogistic assessment of identification's ideological function — specific class interests concealed beneath apparently universal terms. Its eulogistic counterpart is Carlyle's mystery; Burke refuses to adjudicate, treating both as evaluative framings of the same structural condition.

*God-Term* (RM-03, RM-05, RM-07): the operative principle of principles in any ultimate vocabulary; the terminus of dialectical ascent. Subsumes hierarchy, authority, consubstantiality, pure persuasion, and many others as equally god-term-eligible.

*Three Vocabulary Orders* (RM-05): positive, dialectical, ultimate — irreducible and non-conflatable. The ultimate order transforms parliamentary jangle into a developmental hierarchy; the Marxist persuasion case study shows how local participation becomes simultaneously particular and universal.

*Socioanagogic Method* (RM-06, RM-07): secular adaptation of the medieval anagogical sense — reading social hierarchy where divine or erotic imagery appears. The method for disclosing how hierarchic motives are encoded in apparently neutral aesthetic and natural objects (Henry James's 'household gods').

*Kill / Victimage* (RM-07): hierarchy discharges its guilt through scapegoating; common participation in a solemn sacrificial experience is the occasion for consubstantiality, not the kill per se.

*Range of Mountings* (RM-07): the ideal symbolic paradigm when all motivational strands (kinesthetic, erotic, maternal, social, ethical, fecal, anagogic) are simultaneously operative — the model for maximum symbolic vibrancy.

## 5. The Principal Tensions Across the Book

Three tensions run through all eight units without definitive resolution — Burke exploits them as analytic resources rather than closing them:

**Identification as union vs. identification as masking division.** The same mechanism that binds a community (acting-together, consubstantiality) can be 'cloaked malice' (profitable malingering, identification by default, cunning identifications). Whether consubstantiality is primarily ontological or rhetorical-strategic — whether men really are joined-and-separate or merely persuaded they are — is never settled; it is held in productive suspension.

**Hierarchy as universal principle vs. any particular hierarchy as contingent.** The hierarchic principle is inevitable in systematic thought (RM-04, p.141); no particular hierarchy is inevitable. The entelechial tendency — treating the culminating stage as the representative image of the whole — makes hierarchy mystifying by confounding the universal with the particular privilege of the top rank.

**Rhetoric as realistic function vs. rhetoric extending into magic/mystery.** Burke insists rhetoric's function is 'wholly realistic' (inducing cooperation in symbol-responsive beings) and that magic is a faulty derivation. Yet he grants that consubstantiality between beings of unequal status 'may extend far into the realm of the idealistic,' out of which 'a kind of magic or mystery' arises (RM-01, p.48). The realistic-function thesis and the mystery-seed sit in productive tension throughout.

## 6. Cross-Pipeline Architecture

**Grammar of Motives bridge**: The Rhetoric extends and operationalizes the Grammar's 'substance' into identification; the paradox of substance (thing both is and is not what it is) becomes the both-joined-and-separate structure of consubstantiality. The scene-agent and agent-act ratios are deployed analytically in RM-04 (Machiavelli). The attitude-incipient-act seeds the 6th dramatistic term that the 1962 Grammar addendum (GM-06/GM-09) formalizes. Temporizing of essence (Grammar pp.430–440) bridges to RM-00's dramatic/philosophic terms.

**Calleja *In-Game* bridge**: Consubstantiality (RM-01) is the rhetorical/symbolic homologue of Calleja's dual-axis incorporation (IG-10): both name the dynamic of being made substantially one with something other than oneself while remaining a distinct locus. The avatar is precisely the systemically-upheld locus through which the player is joined to the game-world while remaining a distinct substance — Burke's exact both-joined-and-separate structure. The functional homology is strong but not lexical (Calleja never uses substance; Burke never uses embodiment). Courtship / hierarchy / mystery map to Shared-involvement (IG-06); attitude-incipient-act maps to Affective-involvement; pure persuasion maps to Ludic-involvement as the archetype of autotelic play; ultimate identification maps to the apex of Incorporation. All such mappings carry provenance `anticipatory-projection`.

#### 30. The Kill / Victimage
- **canonical_name**: The Kill / Victimage
- **definition**: The mechanism by which hierarchy discharges the guilt it generates: invidious distinction produces accumulated moral pressure, discharged through scapegoating a sacrificial victim. The Kill is not the essence; common participation in a solemn experience is. The Rattlesnake Club anecdote is the paradigm: boys bonded by a shared notable experience, quickly fracturing into hierarchic cliques.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: RM-07
- **centrality**: important

#### 31. Range of Mountings
- **canonical_name**: Range of Mountings
- **definition**: Ideal paradigm for the symbol of ascending: kinesthetic, erotic, maternal, social-careerism, ethical, fecal-scatological, anagogic, and mystical-absolute strands. Maximum symbolic vibrancy obtains when all strands are simultaneously operative in a single symbol. Burke applies this to Hopkins, Yeats, Eliot, and Dante's Purgatorio.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: RM-07
- **centrality**: important

#### 32. Principle of the Oxymoron
- **canonical_name**: Principle of the Oxymoron
- **definition**: The mystic communicates in the oxymoron because mystic experience is the simultaneous glow of contradictory impulses — a condition of radical passivity in which all nervous impulses 'attitudinally glow' at once without the repression required for ordinary purposive action. The oxymoron is the literary analogue of this non-repressive condition.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: RM-07
- **centrality**: important

#### 33. Homo Dialecticus
- **canonical_name**: Homo Dialecticus
- **definition**: Man as the 'classifying animal' whose formal aptitude for classification is prior to social classification; social hierarchy is a special case of this general formal ground. Contrasted with homo faber (tool-using). Grounds Burke's argument that psychoanalytic and institutional explanations conceal the formal/dialectical priority.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: RM-07
- **centrality**: important

#### 34. Grammar–Rhetoric–Symbolic Triad
- **canonical_name**: Grammar–Rhetoric–Symbolic Triad
- **definition**: Burke's three-volume architecture: Grammar built on 'substance' (universal paradoxes/placement); Rhetoric on identification ('substance' applied to persuasion/dissuasion); Symbolic to be built on 'identity' (unique individuals at peace). Consubstantiality bridges Grammar to Rhetoric; identification bridges Rhetoric to Symbolic.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: RM-01
- **centrality**: peripheral

#### 35. Magic as Primitive Rhetoric / Faulty Derivation
- **canonical_name**: Magic as Primitive Rhetoric / Faulty Derivation
- **definition**: Word-magic reverses the true order: the realistic use of addressed language to induce action in people was mistakenly transferred to induce motion in things; magic is a faulty derivation from rhetoric, not its ancestor. Magic's socializing aspect is better read as 'primitive rhetoric' promoting social cohesion (Malinowski, Kluckhohn).
- **type**: concept
- **units**: RM-01
- **centrality**: peripheral

#### 36. Advantage / Agonistic Rhetoric
- **canonical_name**: Advantage / Agonistic Rhetoric
- **definition**: Burke's proposed master-term for subsuming all classical variants of rhetorical purpose and scientific psychological terminologies of drives. Rhetoric shaped by a strongly competitive purpose: proving opposites, countering jest with earnest. Related to Aristotle's view of rhetoric as 'the faculty of discovering persuasive means in a given case.'
- **type**: concept
- **units**: RM-02
- **centrality**: peripheral

#### 37. Literature for Use
- **canonical_name**: Literature for Use
- **definition**: Literary works performing rhetorical-political work: overtly (Areopagitica) or at one remove (Samson — ritual and magic order). Demonstrates that the range of rhetoric extends from bluntest propaganda through courtship, etiquette, education, sermon to 'pure persuasion' — appeal enjoyed for its own sake.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: RM-00
- **centrality**: peripheral

#### 38. Rhetorica Utens / Rhetorica Docens
- **canonical_name**: Rhetorica Utens / Rhetorica Docens
- **definition**: Standard scholastic distinction: rhetoric comprises both the USE of persuasive resources (rhetorica utens, Demosthenes) and the STUDY of them (rhetorica docens, Aristotle's Art of Rhetoric). Burke uses this pair to define rhetoric's scope before expanding it through identification.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: RM-01
- **centrality**: peripheral

#### 39. Context of Situation (Malinowski)
- **canonical_name**: Context of Situation (Malinowski)
- **definition**: Nonverbal situational factors that participate in a rhetorical act's effectiveness. Generalizes the requirement that meaning cannot be separated from nonverbal scene. Applied in RM-03 to qualify the Marxist diagnostic (terministic reservation) and in RM-05 to demonstrate that even positivistic reduction contains a minimal mythic element.
- **type**: concept
- **units**: RM-02, RM-03, RM-05
- **centrality**: peripheral
