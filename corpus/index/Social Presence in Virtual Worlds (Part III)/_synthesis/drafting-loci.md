Every quote below verified via `archon evidence find --mode exact`, accepting only `exact-1.00 (sim 1.00) · bbox ✓`.
Printed pages are offset-confirmed (see `citations.md`); Champion is unpaginated (section + PDF page). These are the
candidate verbatims for revising the passage:

> "until recently, few deployed pedagogical virtual environments had a social interior… Where educators have taught
> inside socially capable virtual worlds in relatively recent times — the Second Life classrooms documented by
> anthropologists and education researchers offer one example — the world has often been a general-purpose social
> platform adapted to teaching rather than an environment authored for a curriculum."

## Confirms: virtual worlds are real, inhabited places (the "social interior" is literal)

- **svw-01 Boellstorff, p.387:** "the myriad ways that the online is real" — the ontological warrant that a virtual world is a real place, not a metaphor. `exact-1.00 · bbox ✓`
- **svw-01 Boellstorff, p.387:** "the false opposition of the digital and the real" — names the misframing the revised passage must avoid. `exact-1.00 · bbox ✓`
- **svw-02 Davis & Boellstorff, p.2099:** "Virtual worlds are places of human culture online" — the single strongest locus for the place / being-together character of a virtual world. `exact-1.00 · bbox ✓`

## Confirms: a Second Life community, documented by anthropologists, on a general-purpose platform

- **svw-02 Davis & Boellstorff, p.2096:** "we analyze the intersection of creativity and agency by examining" (continuing "…disability and the digital… persons living with Parkinson's disease who are active in the virtual world Second Life") — a four-year ethnography; the "Second Life… documented by anthropologists" the passage gestures at, and a general-purpose platform appropriated by a community rather than curriculum-authored. `exact-1.00 · bbox ✓`
- **svw-02 Davis & Boellstorff, ~p.2109:** "digital embodied states and digital objectified states" — the coined cultural-capital constructs showing the depth of social/cultural life inside the world. `exact-1.00 · bbox ✓`

## Sharpens / challenges: does a "social interior" require multiplayer?

- **svw-03 Champion, abstract (PDF pp.1–2):** "convey the impression of shared worlds with social presence and social agency" — single-player worlds can convey social presence; multiplayer is one route, not a precondition. `exact-1.00 · bbox ✓`
- **svw-03 Champion, §2 (PDF pp.2–3):** "It is not clear that we can say social presence is a group of people" — social presence is not reducible to co-located live people. `exact-1.00 · bbox ✓`
- **svw-03 Champion, PDF pp.8–9:** "Oblivion to be considered as a social world" — a navigable single-player world qualifying as a social world on specific authored features. `exact-1.00 · bbox ✓`

## Suggested disposition for the revision

1. Keep the "social interior" phrasing — svw-01 licenses it ontologically; svw-02 instances it empirically.
2. Keep the "general-purpose social platform adapted… rather than… authored for a curriculum" characterization —
   svw-02 is exactly such a case (and svw-03's social-vs-cultural-presence distinction explains what a
   curriculum-authored world would additionally aim at: *cultural* presence).
3. Consider softening the strict equation of "social interior" with "multiplayer capability and spatial
   proximity-voice" — svw-03 shows social presence can be authored without multiplayer. A precise formulation:
   a social interior = authored social presence + place-character, of which multiplayer + proximity-voice is the
   most common but not the only route.
4. Cite svw-02 as evidence that socially-capable virtual worlds were inhabited and anthropologically documented —
   not as a credit-bearing pedagogical deployment (it is a support/creative community, not a classroom).
