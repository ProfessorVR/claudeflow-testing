# Cluster-Level Ontology — Social Presence in Virtual Worlds (Part III)

A compact three-source cluster built 2026-07-20 to revise the Part III introduction's "social interior" passage. The
sources triangulate one question from three disciplines: **is a virtual world a real place where people can be
together, and what makes it so?** Boellstorff (svw-01) supplies the ontological warrant (the digital is real); Davis &
Boellstorff (svw-02) supply the empirical instance (an inhabited Second Life community, anthropologically documented);
Champion (svw-03) supplies the conceptual vocabulary (social presence vs. cultural presence; social presence without
multiplayer). Loci are the cited source's own printed pages where paginated (svw-01, svw-02) or section + PDF page
where not (svw-03, an unpaginated conference paper). Provenance tags: **FE** = source's own claim; **FAITHFUL-\<author\>**
= close paraphrase; **P** = our interpretive link to the drafting purpose, never the source's own claim.

Companion files: `concept-matrix.csv` (source × node grid), `citations.md` (MLA forms + verified pagination offsets),
`drafting-loci.md` (verbatim quote bank keyed to the Part III passage), `README.md` (overview).

## 1. The governing question and the drafting purpose

This cluster exists to serve one revision. The Part III introduction currently hedges: "until recently, few deployed
pedagogical virtual environments had a social interior… Where educators have taught inside socially capable virtual
worlds in relatively recent times — the Second Life classrooms documented by anthropologists and education
researchers offer one example — the world has often been a general-purpose social platform adapted to teaching rather
than an environment authored for a curriculum." The three sources bear on the two halves of that claim: the *existence*
of socially-capable, inhabited virtual worlds (svw-01 ontologically, svw-02 empirically), and the *character* of the
being-together such worlds afford (svw-03 conceptually). See §3 for how each disposes the claim.

## 2. The three anchors

- **Ontological anchor (svw-01).** The digital is real; a virtual world is a genuine modality of the real, so "social
  interior" is literal, not metaphorical. Keeps the passage clear of the "false opposition of the digital and the real."
- **Empirical anchor (svw-02).** A four-year ethnography of a Second Life Parkinson's community: "Virtual worlds are
  places of human culture online." A general-purpose platform inhabited as a real social world — confirming the
  passage's characterization of the recent-history exceptions.
- **Conceptual anchor (svw-03).** Social presence vs. cultural presence; a single-player navigable world can convey
  social presence — complicating the passage's equation of social interior with multiplayer + proximity-voice.

## 3. How the cluster disposes the hedged claim

- **Confirms** that socially-capable virtual worlds existed and were inhabited/documented (svw-02, empirical; svw-01,
  ontological grounding).
- **Confirms** the "general-purpose social platform adapted rather than curriculum-authored" characterization — svw-02
  is exactly such a case (Second Life appropriated by a support/creative community, not a curriculum).
- **Sharpens / partially challenges** the multiplayer equation: svw-03 shows social presence can be authored without
  multiplayer, and separates social from cultural presence — so a "social interior" is better defined as authored
  social presence + place-character, of which multiplayer + proximity-voice is the common but not the only route.
- **Caution:** svw-02 documents a *support/creative* community, not a credit-bearing classroom; cite it as evidence
  that socially-capable virtual worlds were inhabited and anthropologically documented, not as a pedagogical deployment.

## 3A. Canonical Node List

*Registration-format node list (harvested by `scripts/compile-corpus-index.py`). `units` lists the `svw-NN` units in
which the node is genuinely treated.*

#### 1. Reality of the virtual (the digital is real)
- **definition**: The thesis that the online/virtual is a genuine modality of the real, not its negation or a deficient copy; the ontological warrant for treating a virtual world as a real place with a real social interior.
- **type**: THEORY-MODEL
- **units**: svw-01, svw-02
- **centrality**: core

#### 2. Virtual world as place / social interior
- **definition**: A virtual world as an inhabited place where people are together, rather than an interface or mere cyberspace; the "social interior" the Part III passage hedges around. "Virtual worlds are places of human culture online" (svw-02).
- **type**: CONSTRUCT
- **units**: svw-01, svw-02, svw-03
- **centrality**: core

#### 3. Social presence / social agency
- **definition**: The felt presence of other agents as meaningfully "there" in a navigable virtual world; per svw-03, producible by a single-player world's design, not only by live multiplayer co-location.
- **type**: CONSTRUCT
- **units**: svw-02, svw-03
- **centrality**: core

#### 4. Cultural presence / hermeneutic richness
- **definition**: The presence of shared values, beliefs, and identity in a world; distinct from and not entailed by social presence (svw-03's core distinction). A curriculum-authored environment would aim at cultural, not merely social, presence.
- **type**: CONSTRUCT
- **units**: svw-03
- **centrality**: important

#### 5. Being-together / co-presence
- **definition**: The sense of inhabiting a shared world with others; the experiential core of a "social interior." Documented ethnographically in svw-02 and theorized in svw-03.
- **type**: CONSTRUCT
- **units**: svw-02, svw-03
- **centrality**: core

#### 6. Second Life (inhabited virtual world)
- **definition**: The general-purpose social platform (gaming/nongaming) that is the empirical site of svw-02 and a recurrent reference of svw-01; the paradigm case of the passage's "general-purpose social platform adapted" rather than curriculum-authored.
- **type**: TERM
- **units**: svw-01, svw-02
- **centrality**: important

#### 7. Digital embodied states / digital objectified states
- **definition**: svw-02's coined extension of Bourdieu's cultural capital into virtual worlds — capital carried by avatar embodiment and by digital artifacts, beyond social-network density.
- **type**: TERM
- **units**: svw-02
- **centrality**: important

#### 8. Ontology of the virtual (problematizing similitude and difference)
- **definition**: svw-01's positive proposal — an account of the real that treats the virtual as neither a copy of the physical (similitude) nor its Other (difference), drawing on the anthropological ontological turn and Tarde.
- **type**: THEORY-MODEL
- **units**: svw-01
- **centrality**: important

#### 9. Virtual world as ethnographic field site
- **definition**: The methodological stance treating a virtual world as a real place open to participant-observation; svw-02's four-year study, continuous with Boellstorff's Second Life ethnography.
- **type**: TERM
- **units**: svw-01, svw-02
- **centrality**: supporting

#### 10. Single-player vs. multiplayer social world (debate)
- **definition**: The cluster's live tension against the drafting claim: the passage equates social interior with multiplayer capability, whereas svw-03 shows a single-player navigable world can convey social presence — multiplayer is one route, not a precondition.
- **type**: DEBATE-AXIS
- **units**: svw-03
- **centrality**: important
