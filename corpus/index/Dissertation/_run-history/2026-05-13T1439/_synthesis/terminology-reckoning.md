### Q1 — The set-theoretic identity `basic affective valence = pathos simpliciter ∪ resonant orexis`

**Question**: Per Plan §9.1.1 and the §1.4 canonical claim DISS-04-C033 ("Basic affective valence refers to two phenomena: *pathos simpliciter* (object present) and *resonant orexis* (object absent, part of *resonant kinēsis*)"), the relation appears to be:

`basic affective valence` = `pathos simpliciter` ∪ `resonant orexis`

where (a) `pathos simpliciter` covers the present-object case and (b) `resonant orexis` covers the after-object case. **Is this identity the user's intent**, or is `basic affective valence` a wider category that *contains* both `pathos simpliciter` and `resonant orexis` as proper subsets (with additional unnamed scope)?

**Options**:
1. **Strict identity (pipeline default)**: `basic affective valence` = `pathos simpliciter` ⊎ `resonant orexis`. No third case. The two subterms exhaustively partition the phenomenon.
2. **Proper-superset relation**: `basic affective valence` ⊋ {`pathos simpliciter`, `resonant orexis`}; there are basic-valence phenomena not covered by either (e.g., the basic-affective coloring of `nous`-activity considered apart from any *phantasma*).
3. **Non-disjoint partition**: `pathos simpliciter` and `resonant orexis` overlap; e.g., in the kitchen-glass case of §1.3 (DISS-03-C023, C025), the *phantasma*-affect (`resonant orexis`) and the drink-*pathos simpliciter* are *layered*, not mutually exclusive.

**Evidence basis**:
- §1.4 C033 directly states the union (Option 1).
- §1.3 C022–C023 describe *layering* of `resonant orexis` and `pathos simpliciter` in the present-object case (drink-proper-sensible firing + phantasma-affect on the drink-eidos), which could be read as support for Option 3.
- §1.4 C032 explicitly says basic affective valence is "co-given with every perceiving, every thinking, every considering, and with every *pathos* and *pathē*" — the breadth of "every thinking, every considering" could be read as support for Option 2.

**Pipeline recommendation**: **Option 1 (strict identity)**, with the layering observed in §1.3 read as a *temporally simultaneous co-presence of two distinct cases* (the drinker who looks at the drink while remembering it has both an active *pathos simpliciter* AND a persisting *resonant orexis* from the phantasma she had a moment ago) rather than as a single overlapping case. This preserves the architectural cleanness of the basic/articulational distinction the chapter argues for. **If the user prefers Option 2 or 3, the §1.4 canonical definition C032 needs sharpening; if Option 1 is accepted, the §1.3 drinking-case prose can be lightly reworded to make the simultaneous co-presence reading explicit.**

### Q2 — The structural relation between `resonant orexis` and articulationally concrete `pathē`

**Question**: When memory or anticipation elicits articulationally concrete *pathē* — anger at a remembered slight, fear at an anticipated evil, shame before a recalled audience — what is the **structural relation** between `resonant orexis` and that concrete emotion?

**Options** (per Plan §9.1.1):

1. **`resonant orexis` = basic only; concrete *pathē* = `resonant orexis` + *doxa*** (pipeline default). On this reading, `resonant orexis` IS structurally basic throughout; *doxa*'s propositional ratification of the *phantasma* under an evaluative description produces the concrete *pathē* as an *articulational concretion of the underlying basic affective valence (specifically: the `resonant orexis` component thereof)*. The concrete *pathē* is not a higher state of `resonant orexis`; it is a *doxa*-mediated articulation built ON TOP OF the *resonant orexis*.

2. **`resonant orexis` = the unified affective stratum, including the concrete *pathē* that arise from it**. On this reading, `resonant orexis` is a thicker, more inclusive category — it covers everything from the bare basic valence on a *phantasma* through the higher articulated emotional structures of the *Rhetoric*. The concrete *pathē* are themselves higher-degree determinations of `resonant orexis`.

3. **Split the term**: `resonant orexis (basic)` + `resonant pathē (concrete)`, with the bare term `resonant orexis` reserved for the basic case and a parallel coined term introduced for the concrete-on-memory/anticipation case.

**Evidence basis (Phase 2b RETRY — DECISIVE)**: §1.3's 13 in-context occurrences of `resonant orexis` uniformly treat it as basic-only:
- DISS-03-C115 (decisive): "In evaluatively complex cases, *doxa* does engage, and its propositional ratification of an evaluatively loaded presentation articulationally concretizes the *generic resonant orexis* that phantasia has co-presented *into the determinate pathē*."
- DISS-03-C129 (decisive): "*doxa* is the soul's moment of commitment... through which appearance becomes truth-claim *and generic resonant orexis becomes the determinate mobilization of higher-order emotion*."
- DISS-03-C122: "*resonant orexis* responds to the aspectually presented pleasant or painful object and appetite fires, *without any propositional ratification*."
- DISS-03-C153: "It is pre-propositional in not yet involving *logos*-articulation."

The §1.4 canonical claim DISS-04-C034 corroborates: "Perceiving elicits *pathos simpliciter*, whereas thinking and considering — both of which operate on the *phantasma* of the original object of sense — are accompanied with *resonant orexis*." Both elicitations are BASIC; the concrete *pathē* arrive only with *doxa*. The §1.5 use also corroborates: "*Basic* resonant orexis is operative as the perception's pursue-able charge in simple appetition" (DISS-05-C-resonant-orexis).

**Pipeline recommendation**: **Option 1**. The Phase 2 evidence converges with this option without any drift-counterexample in the §§1.0–1.5 corpus. The architectural payoff: the `basic` vs `articulational concretion` distinction stays structurally clean; the concrete *pathē* are not a third coined-term scope but the *doxa*-mediated higher-articulation of *resonant orexis* (= the affective half of basic affective valence). **If the user prefers Option 2 or 3, multiple passages in §§1.3/1.4 require revision; if Option 1 is accepted (recommended), no prose revision is needed — the current text already exhibits the option.**
