## What (primary-text footnote, author-prominent)
> Aristotle's three-factor analysis of locomotion — (i) the unmoved-mover *(desired object)*, (ii) the moved-mover *(orektikon)*, and (iii) the moved *(animal/limbs)* — is given at *De Anima* III.10, 433b13–18: "These three are required: the mover, the means by which it moves, and the moved... thus there are three things: the mover, the means, and the moved." Aristotle reiterates the schema at 433b27–30: "Inasmuch as an animal is capable of appetite it is capable of self-movement; it is not capable of appetite without possessing imagination; and all imagination is either calculative or sensitive."

## Why
The three-factor schema is structurally load-bearing for §1.1/§1.2's analysis of micro-action; *DA* III.10 433b12–30 (the wider 433b10–18 cluster + 433b27–30 confirmation) is the canonical Aristotelian anchor and is well-indexed in the corpus.
