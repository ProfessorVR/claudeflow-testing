## What (compound primary-text footnote; author-prominent)
> The applicability of the *resonant kinēsis* structure to Aristotle's *Rhetoric* II catalogue follows the dual-foundation pattern of (a) *Phys.* III.1's definition of motion as "the fulfilment [*entelecheia*] of what is potentially, as such" (201a10–11) — the source of *kinēsis* as incomplete actuality — and (b) the *Rhetoric* II.2/II.5/II.8 emotion-definitions, in each of which a *phantasma*-mediated *kinēsis* persists in the perceiver. Anger is "a desire accompanied by pain for a conspicuous revenge for a conspicuous slight" (*Rhet.* II.2, 1378a30); fear is "pain or disturbance due to imagining some destructive or painful evil in the future" (II.5, 1382a20); pity is "a feeling of pain at an apparent evil... which we might expect to befall ourselves or some friend of ours" (II.8, 1385b15).

## Why
The applicability claim rests on the Aristotelian doctrine that *pathē* are *psychēs kinēseis* (movements of soul; *Rhet.* II.1, 1378a20). The three *Rhet.* II definitions each foreground a *phantasma*-mediated kinetic-affective structure that is the textual base for the dissertation's `resonant epithymia` / `resonant pathē` apparatus per the FINAL terminology.
