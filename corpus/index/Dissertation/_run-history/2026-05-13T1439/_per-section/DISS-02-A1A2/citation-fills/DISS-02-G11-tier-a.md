## What (paired secondary-source footnote; parenthetical)
> The interpretation of *phantasia* as the mechanism that enables variable, error-prone animal behavior is developed by Nussbaum (1985), who reconstructs Aristotle's positive theory from the action-passages of *De Motu Animalium* (esp. 702a18 ff.) and *DA* III.9–11, treating *phantasia* as preparation-of-desire and the action-enabling vehicle of practical thought (Nussbaum 1985, pp. 221–269, esp. pp. 240–255), and by Caston (1996), who argues that Aristotle introduces *phantasia* in *DA* III.3 precisely to solve the *problem of error* — the divergence of mental content from world-content — that besets the predecessor like-by-like accounts and Aristotle's own causal accounts of sensation and conception (Caston 1996, pp. 20–52, esp. pp. 26–52).
