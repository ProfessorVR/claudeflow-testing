# Phase 2 Narrative Reconstruction — DISS-05-A4

**Section**: §1.5 — *M₃ → M₄ and A₄: The Three Types of Action (Completed Action)*
**Word count of source**: 3,506 | **Word count of narrative**: ~2,800
**Purpose** (per Plan §8.1.2): Reading verification + cross-section synthesis skeleton

---

§1.5 is the chapter's conclusion. It does three things at once: it closes the actualization chain at A₄ (the completed action, *praxis*), it consolidates the three-types-of-action distinction whose elements were laid down in §§1.3–1.4, and it advances the chapter's most novel architectural synthesis — the alignment of the user's three-types catalog with Heidegger's GA 18 §17 distinction between technē-*hexis* and praxis-*hexis*. The section runs only 3,506 words across 28 logical lines and 0 footnotes — markedly under-developed relative to §1.4's gold-standard density — but within those constraints it carries an unusually high architectural load. Per Special Instruction (h) and Plan §11.1's critical path, the section will be revised LAST after §§1.0–1.4 stabilize; the present analysis therefore identifies architectural-mismatch findings carefully, since they will feed §1.5's full rewrite.

The section's first paragraph opens with the architectural thesis: the actualization chain terminates at A₄, reached through the *orektikon* transition M₃ → M₄ in which *orexis* becomes overt motion of the bodily instrument. The user's signature move here is to claim that the kinetic-roles spine of *De Anima* III.10, 433a31-b30 holds invariant across all three types of action Aristotle catalogs in *Movement of Animals*. The DA III.10 schema — unmoved originator (realizable good), moved mover (*orexis*), and moved (animal-via-ball-and-socket-joint) — is preserved across types because it specifies the *fundamental kinetic structure* of any *orectic* actualization. What varies across types is not the schema but the *internal complexity of orexis*: in simple appetition, *orexis* is generic appetite responding to a pleasant or painful object; in habitual-procedural action, *orexis* is articulated by settled *doxa* whose content is craft-procedural; in evaluatively complex action, *orexis* is the emotion-desire composite in which cognitive evaluation, conative orientation, hedonic tonality, and physiological alteration are mutually constitutive aspects of one and the same *pathos*. This opening positions the entire taxonomy as a refinement-within-invariance: same schema, varying internal complexity.

The second paragraph establishes the user's architectural thesis about the somatic preparation of the organic parts: the heating, cooling, trembling, and muscular tension are *internal to* the M₃ → M₄ transition rather than a discrete subsequent stage. The user grounds this in the *MA* 702a17-19 passage — "the organic parts are suitably prepared by the affections, these again by desire, and desire by imagination" — and argues that this textual evidence licenses preserving the chain's A₀ → A₄ macro-skeleton without adding a fifth node. The *MA*'s "affections" name a moment *within* the *orektikon* transition, not a separate macro-node. The temporal duration of the transition itself varies with complexity and material resistance, but Aristotle's speed-claim — "thinking that one ought to go and going are virtually simultaneous, provided that there is nothing to compel or to prevent" (*MA* 702a15-17) — establishes a structural ceiling: under normal conditions the transition fires with rapidity, rendered possible by the "natural correspondence of the active and passive" (*MA* 702a21), each level of the chain already disposed in its first actuality to receive the impulse transmitted from the level above.

The third paragraph pivots to the section's most substantively novel architectural move: the recovery of *hexis* through Heidegger's GA 18 §17. Heidegger's reading is "indispensable" because it sets *hexis* in a non-moralizing register — *hexis* is "the determination of the genuineness of being-there in a moment of being-composed as to something" (GA 18, p. 119). On Aristotle's own terms in *Metaphysics* Δ 20, 1022b 4, *hexis* is the *energeia* of having — the genuine being-present of having as such, not a stored possession awaiting use. From this Heidegger draws the structurally decisive distinction between technē-*hexis* and praxis-*hexis*. Where the *ergon* (work, product) is decisive (technē), "training has the precise sense of reducing deliberation insofar as it is through training that the completedness of attaining a result comes about" (GA 18, p. 127): cultivated technē-*hexis* fires as routine. Where the action itself is decisive (praxis and *aretē*), "the how is only appropriated in such a way that the human being enables himself to be composed at each moment; not routine but holding-oneself-open" (GA 18, p. 128); "repetition does not mean the bringing-into-play of a settled completedness, but rather acting anew in every moment on the basis of the corresponding resolution" (GA 18, p. 128). The two modes differ structurally: technē-*hexis* reduces deliberation toward routine; praxis-*hexis* cultivates the capacity for fresh resolution in the *kairos*. The three-types distinction the section develops *tracks* this bivalence: simple appetition does not engage *hexis* in either mode; habitual-procedural action operates through technē-*hexis*; evaluatively complex action — when the agent has cultivated the relevant *aretē* — operates through praxis-*hexis*. This synthesis is the section's most consequential interpretive move.

The fourth paragraph treats Type 1 (simple appetition). The chain runs A₀ → A₁ → *orexis* → action without engaging *doxa* or the *pathē*. Aristotle's paradigm is the drinking case of *MA* 701a29-b1, quoted in full: "I want to drink, says appetite; this is drink, says sense or imagination or thought: straightaway I drink." *Phantasia* is operative throughout — the *phantasma* identifies the present object as drink — and basic *resonant orexis* is operative as the perception's pursue-able charge. What is absent is *doxa*'s engagement: either no *doxa* forms or the *doxa* that forms ("this is drink") is too thin to engage the evaluative categories of the *Rhetoric*. The action is "emotionally transparent" — it passes through the system without engaging the structures that produce fear, anger, or pity. The user sharpens this with three observations. First, Aristotle says explicitly that "the actualizing of desire is a substitute for inquiry or thinking" — the *orectic* actualization *positively occupies* the structural slot inquiry would otherwise fill. Second, the disjunction "sense or imagination or thought" explicitly includes perception alone as sufficient for the recognition that triggers desire-actualization, *for the rational human as much as for the brute*. Third, "what we do without reflection, we do quickly" frames this unreflective swiftness as *normal* for rational-animal appetitive action, not as an aberration from a *doxa*-mediated default. Position-taking remains (perception is *kritikon*, basic valence is a *phasis*-like stance), but the doxastic articulation does not concretize into determinate *pathē*. The kinetic roles for Type 1: unmoved originator = realizable pleasant object as apprehended through the *phantasma*; moved mover = *orexis* in its basic-appetitive form; animal-via-bodily-instrument is moved straightaway, the transition firing with the rapidity that the somatic correspondence (*MA* 702a21) makes possible.

The fifth paragraph treats Type 2 (habitual-procedural action) — the technē-mode of *hexis*-grounded action. *Doxa* is fully operative, but the *doxa*'s content is craft-procedural rather than evaluatively complex, and so no *pathē* concretize in the middle of the chain. Aristotle's paradigm is the practical syllogism (*MA* 7, 701a7-16, quoted in full): "whenever one thinks that every man ought to walk, and that one is a man oneself, straightaway one walks." The chain runs basic *pathos* → *doxa* → *orexis* → action, without concretion. The good premise is a settled *doxa* ("every man ought to walk"); the possible premise is a perceptual self-recognition ("I am a man"); the conclusion is the action itself. *Doxa* is fully present at every step; what is absent is evaluatively-complex *doxa*-content. The pianist case sharpens this: the pianist has internalized many settled *doxai* ("this passage takes the soft pedal," "voice the inner line under the melody," "release the chord before the next downbeat") and each fires almost automatically as the keyboard's unfolding demands present themselves perceptually. Yet — barring a memory slip or a broken string — no *pathē* arise. What makes the practical syllogism fire "straightaway" without fresh deliberation is that the universal premise functions as a technē-*hexis*: a settled disposition that fires routinely. The structural condition for "what we do without reflection" is *not* the absence of *doxa* but the *presence* of *hexis*-grounded *doxa*. Heidegger's gloss is sharp: in technē-cultivation, "the completedness of attaining a result comes about" through training; the *grammatikos* who has internalized the prescription writes "not by chance, but according to a prescription...from out of himself," but precisely because the prescription has been "put further and further out of play" through repetition. The kinetic roles for Type 2: unmoved originator = realizable good as articulated by settled *doxa* (health, musical excellence); moved mover = *orexis* shaped by the technē-*hexis*-grounded *doxa*; animal-via-bodily-instrument moves with the rapidity that the somatic correspondence makes possible. The structural finding consolidates the *variable-presence thesis*: *doxa* is necessary but not sufficient for *pathē*; the gate is *content-conditional*, not *doxa*-presence-conditional. *Doxa* with non-evaluative content (Type 2) does not produce *pathē*; *doxa* with evaluative content (Type 3) does — and this holds whether the Type 3 chain is spontaneous or virtuously habituated, since virtuous habituation operates as praxis-*hexis* rather than technē-*hexis*, and praxis-*hexis* does not reduce deliberation toward routine but cultivates the capacity for fresh resolution in the *kairos*.

The sixth paragraph treats Type 3 (evaluatively complex action). *Doxa*'s content engages the evaluative categories of the *Rhetoric* — slight, threat, undeserved suffering, public falling-short — and the *pathē* concretize. The chain runs basic *pathos* → *doxa* → *pathē* → action, with the somatic preparation internal to M₃ → M₄ taking the specific form the emotion calls for: heated blood for anger that prepares the body for aggressive action, cooled blood for fear that prepares for flight, sympathetic pain for pity that prepares for relief. Emotion (as §1.8 established) is not a separate antecedent stage but *is* the form desire takes when *doxa* has disclosed evaluatively complex content. Anger *is* the desire to retaliate insofar as it is structured by the perception of an unjustified slight and experienced in the somatic alteration of heated blood; fear *is* the desire to flee insofar as it is structured by the cognitive evaluation of an anticipated destructive evil and experienced in the somatic alteration of chilled blood. Aristotle indicates that the affective force of *doxa*-mediated desire is both immediate (*euthus*, DA III.3, 427b23) and involuntary: "it is not in our power to form opinions as we will" (427b20). The soul that believes something dangerous is already mobilized toward avoidance before deliberation can intervene, precisely because *doxa*, unlike *phantasia*, is not subject to the will. The involuntariness is not a *defect* of evaluative complex action but its *structural signature*: the agent who *takes the object to be* fearful does not first decide to fear and then flee; the fear arises with the *doxa*, and the desire to flee arises as the form the fear takes in the *orektikon* transition. The kinetic roles for Type 3: unmoved originator = realizable good as articulated under the evaluative category; moved mover = emotion-desire composite (anger-as-desire-to-retaliate, fear-as-desire-to-flee, pity-as-desire-to-relieve); animal-via-bodily-instrument moves with the somatic preparation specific to the emotion's eidetic structure.

The seventh paragraph — the section's longest — develops the *hexis*-bivalence in Type 3. In Type 3, *hexeis* operate in a register distinct from Type 2. Where Type 2 *hexis* is technē-*hexis* (settled *doxa* furnishing the universal premise of the practical syllogism, fired routinely), Type 3 *hexeis* are *praxis-hexeis*: sedimented dispositions of emotional comportment, the diachronic residue of repeated *pathos*-actualizations, cultivated as the standing capacity for rightly-disposed comportment toward the *pathē* the situation demands. Heidegger states the structural relation directly: "*Hexis* is nothing other than a how of *pathos*, being-out-of-composure, in relation to being-composed-as-to" (GA 18, p. 125). The Aristotelian texts confirm the bivalent character: *hexis* qua *dynamis* is both active power and passive power (*Met.* Δ 12, 1019a 15 ff.); the same settled capacity exercised as *praxis* is what is struck or displaced in *pathos*. *Hexis* and *pathos* are accordingly co-original structures of the same human being-in-the-world. Courage is the praxis-*hexis* of fear; magnanimity the praxis-*hexis* of anger; modesty the praxis-*hexis* of shame; *aretē* more generally is the cultivated *hexis* that fits the *kairos*, defined as Aristotle has it by "the *phronimos* as the prudent one would define it" (*NE* B 6, 1106b 36). The praxis-*hexis* structure of virtuous action explains why courage is not the absence of fear but the cultivated comportment-toward-fear: the brave person fears the genuinely fearsome (*NE* III.7, 1115b 7-13), and what *aretē* cultivates is the *how* of the fearing — fearing rightly, in the right manner, at the right moment, before the right matter. These *hexeis* modulate Type 3 chains in two distinct ways: (1) they modulate the *input* to *doxa* (the courageous person's basic valence is differently shaped than the cowardly person's; the cowardly person sees enemies in shadows where the courageous person sees only shadows; the perceptual encounter is conducted under the resulting *Stimmung*-shading); and (2) they modulate the *gating* of *doxa* into *pathē* (the courageous person's *doxa*-gate produces a regulated, action-prepared fear rather than a paralyzed fear; the irascible person's gate fires anger more readily on thinner evaluative content; the modest person's gate produces shame on circumstances the shameless person's gate ignores). The *Rhetoric* II analyses presuppose this *hexis*-modulation throughout — Aristotle's diagnostic categories specify "those of such-and-such a character feel anger toward such-and-such persons," which is a description of *hexis*-conditioning of the *doxa*-gate. The structural distinction matters: Type 2 *hexis* operates at the *doxa* level; Type 3 *hexeis* operate at *both* the basic-valence level (perceptual-affective biasing) and the *doxa*-gate level. The same Aristotelian term names two structurally distinct dispositional functions across the two action types, and the recognition of the distinction is what enables the chain-apparatus to handle both rationally articulated craft-action and emotionally complex situational action without collapsing the two into a single dispositional model.

The eighth paragraph consolidates the role of *phantasia*, *phantasma*, *doxa*, and *pathē* in articulating every action the chain produces. Aristotle defines *phantasia* as "a movement resulting from actual perception" (DA III.3, 429a1-2), a residual motion that persists, recombines, and re-presents, supplying the condition for emotional response in the absence of immediate stimuli. *Phantasia* operates in all three action types but differently in each: as the immediate *phantasma* identifying the present object in Type 1; as the settled *phantasma* informing the universal premise of the practical syllogism in Type 2; and as the projected *phantasma* over which *doxa* operates in Type 3. "Both the man who remembers and the man who hopes," Aristotle observes, "will be attended by an imagination of what he remembers or hopes" (*Rhetoric* I.11, 1370a28-30): the temporal depth of emotional life is supplied by *phantasia*. The *phantasma* is what *doxa* operates upon; what *doxa* ratifies as true is the *phantasma*'s taking-as. The *doxa*-gate is content-conditional across all three types: absent in Type 1, present-but-non-evaluative in Type 2, present-and-evaluative in Type 3. Emotion enters the chain wherever the appearance discloses evaluatively complex content that *doxa* ratifies as true, and it enters as the form desire takes under that disclosure. The paragraph closes with the section's most rhetorically marked sentence — a four-part climactic gradatio: "What *phantasia* presents, *doxa* commits to; what *doxa* commits to, emotion mobilizes; what emotion mobilizes, action completes."

The final paragraph (line 29) consolidates the recursive closure. The chain closes at A₄ but does not stop: the completed action transforms the perceptual environment, producing new sensible conditions that serve as A₀ for a new cycle. The chain is recursive, not linear: A₄ produces A₀', and the cycle resumes with the world now altered. This recursivity is what makes the chain a chain rather than a sequence, and what makes the analysis of action a *generative apparatus* rather than a one-pass diagnostic. The recursive structure secures Heidegger's reading of *hexis* as the determination of the genuineness of being-there in a moment of being-composed: each pass through the chain re-cultivates the dispositional ground, and the cumulative trajectory of the agent's chain-actualizations *is* their *hexis*-formation. The threefold structure of the *pathē* that Aristotle isolates in *Rhetoric* B 1, 1378a 24 — "how we find ourselves, what we are attuned to, with respect to whom" — is, on Heidegger's reading, the proto-form of what *Being and Time* §29 will name *Befindlichkeit* (state-of-mind, "finding-oneself," attunement). Emotion is not, accordingly, an add-on to rationality, nor a disruption of cognition, but the way the soul's self-movement discloses significance at the precise point where significance must become motion. The chain analysis is complete; subsequent chapters can take up the rhetorical cultivation of *hexeis*, the *kairos*-relativity of appropriate *pathē*, and the further questions the chain-analysis opens for finite, embodied, speaking beings whose way of being is *zōē praktikē meta logou*.

## Architectural critical notes (for revision)

The section carries unusual interpretive weight for its length. Four dissertation-novel moves merit special attention, all currently unflagged in the prose:

(1) The alignment of the user's three-types catalog with Heidegger's GA 18 §17 technē-/praxis-*hexis* bivalence (C031) is the section's master synthesis. Neither Aristotle nor Heidegger draws this alignment; it is the user's. The current prose treats the alignment as if it followed directly from Heidegger's reading, but the move from "Heidegger distinguishes technē-*hexis* from praxis-*hexis*" to "the user's three-types-of-action catalog tracks this distinction" is interpretive and requires explicit marking.

(2) The double claim that Type-3 evaluatively-complex action includes both spontaneous evaluative response AND virtuously habituated comportment (C058, C059) is similarly dissertation-novel. The standard Aristotelian distinction would set virtuous action apart from spontaneous emotional response, but the user collapses both under Type 3 on the strength of the praxis-*hexis* analysis (because praxis-*hexis* cultivates fresh resolution rather than reducing deliberation). This collapse is the section's most substantive architectural choice and needs explicit defense.

(3) The two-level modulation thesis (C076, C081) — that Type 3 *hexeis* operate at BOTH the basic-valence level (perceptual-affective biasing) and the *doxa*-gate level (which *pathē* arise and with what intensity) — is the section's most substantive elaboration beyond §1.4. Special Instruction (f) directs verification that this is consistent with §1.4's *hexis*-bivalence treatment. Provisional finding: §1.4 establishes the bivalence in principle; §1.5 elaborates the operational two-level structure. The two treatments are consistent but the §1.5 elaboration is novel and needs §1.4 cross-reference.

(4) The recursivity-as-*hexis*-formation thesis (C095) — that each pass through the chain re-cultivates the dispositional ground and the cumulative trajectory of chain-actualizations IS the agent's *hexis*-formation — is the section's bridge from chain-analysis to character-formation. This is the dissertation's most consequential link to the rhetorical-soul thesis (its way of grounding the soul's rhetorical constitution in the chain's iterated structure). Currently unflagged.

The section's primary structural defect is at the architecture-vs-introduction interface. §1.0's PROSE at line 45 names A_4 as "completed cognitive actuality," but §1.0's DIAGRAM at line 176 names A_4 as "Completed Action (*praxis*)" — and §1.5 uses §1.0's diagram convention. This is a §1.0 internal inconsistency that §1.5 inherits; per critical path it must be resolved in §1.0's revision before §1.5 can be finalized.

The section's secondary structural defect is the four occurrences of the OLD M_n → M_{n+1} convention (lines 1, 3, 5, 23). Per the locked decision in Plan §3.1, all instances must migrate to M_n → A_{n+1}. The migration is mechanical but must be coordinated with the §1.0 architectural resolution to avoid introducing new inconsistencies.

These are the conclusion's rhetorical signature and one of the chapter's strongest stretches. The temptation in revision to soften the parallel structures should be resisted.
