§1.5 claims the "good-as-major-premise" structural role in the practical syllogism is occupied by a *settled doxa* — a doxastic uptake of the apparent good that has consolidated into the agent's character. The Aristotelian locus is NE VII.3 (the akrasia analysis); the secondary anchor is Frede 1992 on the cognitive role of phantasia in practical reasoning.

## 3. Verbatim from corpus/index

### NE VII.3, 1147a25–28:

> "The one belief is universal, the other is concerned with the particular facts, and here we come to something within the sphere of perception; when a single opinion results from the two, the soul must in one type of case affirm the conclusion, while in the case of opinions concerned with production it must immediately act."

### Frede 1992 (from `phx-03-frede.md` summary): 

Frede's "Cognitive Role of *Phantasia* in Aristotle" develops the role of *phantasiai* in storing the universalized doxastic contents that feed the major premises of practical syllogisms. The settled-doxa argument finds its strongest contemporary defense in Frede's analysis of how the *phantasma* of the apparent good is propositionally articulated in the major-premise position of practical reasoning.
