## 1. Claim and anchor

§1.5 claims that *pathē* have somatic-specificity — each pathos has its proper bodily realization (the heated blood of anger, the cooled blood of fear, the sympathetic pulse of pity). The Aristotelian loci are DA I.1 (the *pathē*-of-the-soul-are-also-of-the-body argument) and MA 8 (animal-motion physiology).

## 2. Tier A loci

**Pipeline**: `corpus/index/Aristotle - Complete Works/`

- **DA I.1, 403a16–b19**: the *pathē* of the soul are inseparable from the natural matter of animals; "anger is a boiling of the blood and warm substance surrounding the heart"
- **MA 8, 701b33–702a21**: the physiology of animal motion; warm/cold movements of pneuma in the body

## 3. Verbatim from corpus/index

### DA I.1, 403a16–b19 (canonical somatic-specificity passage):

> "It seems that all the *pathē* of soul involve a body — passion, gentleness, fear, pity, courage, joy, love, and hate; in all these there is a concurrent affection of the body... For example, anger: the dialectician would define anger as a desire for retaliation, or something of the sort, while the natural scientist would define it as a boiling of the blood and warm stuff around the heart" (403a16–b1).

### MA 8, 701b33–702a3:

> "The bodily parts are altered by being warmed and cooled. The warming and cooling, by means of the pneuma which is itself a kind of bodily part, are caused by the *phantasmata* and aisthēseis."
