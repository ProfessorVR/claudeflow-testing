## 3. Verbatim from corpus/index

### From SZ §65, H.328–329 (per BT-D2-U3 §65 anchored content):

The three-ecstases-of-temporality formulation: *Zeitlichkeit* is the unity of having-been (*Gewesenheit*), present (*Gegenwart*), and projected-futural (*Zukunft*). The canonical M-R rendering at H.329: "Temporality is the primordial 'outside-of-itself' in and for itself. We therefore call the phenomena of the future, the character of having been, and the Present, the 'ecstases' of temporality" (M-R p. 377).

### From NE II.1, 1103a14–b25 (canonical *ethismos* passage):

> Moral virtue comes about as a result of habit, whence also its name (*ēthikē*) is one that is formed by a slight variation from the word *ethos* (habit). From this it is also plain that none of the moral virtues arises in us by nature; for nothing that exists by nature can form a habit contrary to its nature. … Neither by nature, then, nor contrary to nature do the virtues arise in us; rather we are adapted by nature to receive them, and are made perfect by habit (*ethismos*).

And NE II.1, 1103b21–25:

> But the virtues we get by first exercising them, as also happens in the case of the arts as well. … so too we become just by doing just acts, temperate by doing temperate acts, brave by doing brave acts.

### From BCAP §17b-alpha (pp. 122–124), the explicit temporality-of-hexis-formation:

> *Aretē* as *hexis* is not a property or possession brought from without; it is a *mode of being-there itself*. The peculiar category of the "how" — how being-there is *determined by its being*, **characterized by *temporality*, by the stretching across time**. *Aretē* comes to be *di' ethous*, "through habit" (NE B 1, 1103a 25 sq.).
