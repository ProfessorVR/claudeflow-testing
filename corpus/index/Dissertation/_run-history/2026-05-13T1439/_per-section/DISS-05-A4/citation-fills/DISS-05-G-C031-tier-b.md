### Hit 2 — phronēsis as practical wisdom (Umsicht); contrast with technē/poiēsis (chunk 426, pp.301)

**Distance (cosine)**: ~0.43

> "In 'The Question of Technik' Heidegger is focused only on knowing-how: calculative thinking or instrumental/means-ends rationality. [...] The first instance, φρόνησις, is the capability to act well in human affairs both individual and social — for example, in ethical, political, or economic matters. In this case, one can fore-see the goal to be achieved as well as the best means for achieving it. In human affairs the goal is some kind of human good, τὸ ἀνθρώπινον ἀγαθόν [...] This calculative virtue of φρόνησις is what Being and Time calls Umsicht, the existential capability to look ahead to both an existentiel goal and the means to attain it. [...] That which is guided by fore-sightful φρόνησις is the kind of activity that Aristotle calls πρᾶξις, not just any action but only rational action (μετὰ λόγου), action that has to do with achieving, for ourselves or others, some perceived form of the human good. In the second instance (and this is Heidegger's specific focus here), fore-sightful or calculative thinking is directed at changing not persons but things — for example, converting a pile of wood into a bookcase, or transforming a mountain into a strip mine: in short, producing a product. In this kind of calculative thinking, the foresightful know-how — the skill or practical cognition that guides the production process — is called τέχνη (Latin ars), whereas the actual pro-duction or bringing forth of the product is called ποίησις."

— Sheehan (2015), p. 301 (ChromaDB chunk index 426).

**Why relevant**: Provides the canonical Sheehan formulation: φρόνησις guides πρᾶξις (rational action for human good); τέχνη guides ποίησις (production of products). This is the structural distinction the DISS-05-C031 three-types catalog tracks. The footnote ties this to NE VI.4, 1140a7 (the precise Bekker locus where Aristotle distinguishes τέχνη from πρᾶξις).

> "'The Question of Technik' traces the current mode of disclosure back to Aristotle's notion of τέχνη, the human capacity to bring things into meaningful presence by producing or constructing them, as contrasted with φύσις, whereby natural things emerge of and by themselves. ## Recommended LaTeX form (combine with Tier A NE VI and Tier C Sherman)

> Heidegger's reading of Aristotle's distinction between τέχνη and φρόνησις in NE VI grounds the three-types architecture of action developed here. As Sheehan summarizes, "the two capabilities of knowing-how" are "φρόνησις and τέχνη" — the former guiding πρᾶξις, the latter guiding ποίησις (Sheehan 2015, p. 300; cf. NE VI.4, 1140a7). On the architectural reading developed here, Type 2 actions operate through technē-hexis ("the habitual practical cognition that creates the programs and manages the machines," Sheehan 2015, p. 297) — Aristotle's intellectual virtue in the order of production. Type 3 actions, by contrast, operate through praxis-hexis: a sedimented disposition of emotional comportment, what Aristotle calls "the habitual capability of foresightful knowing-how" in the order of πρᾶξις (NE VI.5, 1140a24–b30). Simple Type-1 appetition engages no hexis, technical or practical.
