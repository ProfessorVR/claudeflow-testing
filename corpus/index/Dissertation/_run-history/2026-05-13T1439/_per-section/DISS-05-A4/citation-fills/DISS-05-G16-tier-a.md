## 3. Verbatim from corpus/index

### Rhet. II.5, 1382a21–25 (canonical definition):

> "Let fear be defined as a pain or disturbance (*lypē tis ē tarachē*) due to imagining (*ek phantasias*) some destructive or painful evil in the future. For there are some evils, e.g. wickedness or stupidity, the prospect of which does not frighten us; I mean only such as amount to great pains or losses."

The crucial element for the dissertation's argument is the *ek phantasias* clause: fear is keyed not to a present perception but to a *phantasma* of a future evil. This is the canonical site for the dissertation's `resonant pathē` analysis — fear as a phantasma-attached articulationally-concrete *pathos* (per `terminology-decisions-final.md`).
