### Hit 1 — Rhetoric as "the interpretation of concrete being-there"; pathē as Dasein's self-interpretation (chunk 3, p.4)

**Distance (cosine)**: 0.3596

> "[Heidegger's reading of Aristotle's rhetoric] occurred at a time in which rhetoric had fallen into deep obscurity. Heidegger specifically refers to this obscurity in the beginning of the third chapter of the aforementioned lectures of the summer semester of 1924, where he characterizes rhetoric as 'the discipline in which the self-interpretation of being-there is explicitly fulfilled. Rhetoric is nothing other than the interpretation of concrete being-there, the hermeneutic of being-there itself. That is the intended sense of Aristotle's rhetoric' (GA 18: 110/75). Starting from the fundamental possibility of being-together, Heidegger carries out an interpretation of Dasein which adopts Aristotelian rhetoric as its guiding thread. Coexistence or being-together is possible only within the framework of communicability or, as Ricoeur formulates it, in the intersubjective and dialogical dimension of the public use of language. Rhetoric basically appeals to communication among people: 'Again, it is absurd to hold that a man ought to be ashamed of being unable to defend himself with his limbs, but not of being unable to defend himself with speech and reason (λόγος), when the use of rational speech is more distinctive [than physical defense]'"

— Costache (2013), p. 4 (ChromaDB chunk index 3).

**Why relevant**: Establishes that on Heidegger's reading (GA 18: 110/75), the pathē-catalog of Aristotle's *Rhetoric* II is the "interpretation of concrete being-there" — that is, the articulation of Dasein's comportment. Costache's framing supports the dissertation's claim that praxis-hexeis are "sedimented dispositions of emotional comportment" because the pathē themselves are already comportment-disclosive on Heidegger's reading.

### Hit 2 — δόξα as starting point of moral / philosophical / political discourse (chunk 5, p.5)

**Distance (cosine)**: 0.4152

> "δόξα, as the common voice of all individuals in a community, constitutes the starting point of moral discourse, of philosophical arguments, scientific disputes, political discussions, etc. Therefore, δόξα expresses a true basis of wisdom in an already constructed and recognizable language. In this sense, rhetoric possesses a clear political aspect that refers to the space of coexistence that is intersubjectively shared with others. Contrary to those interpretations which maintain that Heidegger simplifies the analysis of ethical virtues and that he detaches Aristotelian πρᾶξις from its connection to the political community, we find in his commentary on Rhetoric an unusual interest in the political aspect. Heidegger explains that this rhetorical-political aspect shows a different side of the ζῷον λόγον ἔχον: 'Insofar as the human being lets something be said, he is λόγον ἔχον in a new respect. He lets something be said insofar as he hears. He does not hear in the sense of learning something, but rather in the sense of having a directive for concrete practical concern' (GA 18: 111/76)."

— Costache (2013), p. 5 (ChromaDB chunk index 5).

**Why relevant**: Heidegger's "directive for concrete practical concern" is precisely the operating-mode of praxis-hexis. δόξα is community-grounded; cultivated into hexis through habituation, it becomes the standing-capacity for rightly-disposed comportment.
