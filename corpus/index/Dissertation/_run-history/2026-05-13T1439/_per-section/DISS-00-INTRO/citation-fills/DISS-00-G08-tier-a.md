## What (paired primary-text footnote, parenthetical compound citation)
> Aristotle treats animal locomotion as proceeding jointly from practical thought and appetite, with the desired object as the unmoved mover that sets the chain in motion (*De Anima* III.10, 433a13–15, 433a17–20; *De Motu Animalium* 6, 700b15–22).

## Why
Two complementary loci: *DA* III.10 establishes the *orektikon/dianoia praktikē* coupling, while *MA* 6 supplies the species-level cataloguing of *orexis* into *epithymia/thymos/boulēsis*. Both are required to underwrite the introduction's framing of animal motion as desire-actualization.

## Verbatim (primary text excerpts, ≤25 words each)
- "Both thought and appetite are capable of originating local movement: practical thought... differs from speculative thought" (*DA* III.10, 433a13–15).
- "All living things both move and are moved for the sake of something" (*MA* 6, 700b15–17).
- "Wish, however, impulse, and appetite, are all three forms of desire" (*MA* 6, 700b22–23).
