## What (interpretive-flag footnote + primary citation; author-prominent)
> *Interpretive*: I take Aristotle's argument at *Physics* VII.3 (245b3–246b3) — that alterations of figure are not strictly *alloiōseis* because shape-completion produces a new substance-predicate, whereas the affective and dispositional changes ("excellences and defects") *are* a special kind of qualitative alteration — to license treating psychic events as a sub-species of natural *kinēsis*. The Aristotelian text neither asserts nor denies this generalization in so many words; I draw out the implication.

## Why
The dissertation's claim that psychic events are a species of natural motion is interpretively load-bearing and must be flagged. *Physics* VII.3 (with explicit treatment of *hexis*/*aretē* at 246a1–b3) is the textually closest Aristotelian anchor for ascribing motion-status to soul-states.

## Verbatim (primary text, ≤25 words)
"Excellences and defects are not alterations but perfections and departures; they exist in virtue of particular relations" (*Phys.* VII.3, 246a1–b3).
