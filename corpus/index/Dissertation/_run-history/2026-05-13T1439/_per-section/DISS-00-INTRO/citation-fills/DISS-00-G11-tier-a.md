## What (paired primary-text footnote)
> Aristotle defines motion as "the fulfilment of what is potentially, as such" (*Phys.* III.1, 201a10–11) — the *energeia* of the potential *qua* potential, an incomplete (*ateles*) actuality — and grounds time as "the number of motion in respect of the before and after" (*Phys.* IV.11, 219b1–2).

## Why
The continuous-unfolding thesis requires both (a) the *energeia-ateles* definition (Phys. III.1) and (b) the time-as-number-of-motion canonical anchor (Phys. IV.11). The pair is what makes "continuous unfolding" Aristotelian rather than a free-standing user construct.
