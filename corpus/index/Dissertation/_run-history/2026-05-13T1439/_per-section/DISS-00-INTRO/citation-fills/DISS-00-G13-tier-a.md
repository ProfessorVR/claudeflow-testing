## What (primary-text footnote)
> Aristotle marks off *phantasia* from perception, opinion, and discursive thought across *De Anima* III.3, 427b14–428a18: "Imagination is different from either perceiving or discursive thinking, though it is not found without sensation, or judgement without it" (427b14–16); imagination "takes place in the absence of [sense]" (428a5–9); imagination "may be false, but knowledge and intelligence are never in error" (428a16–18).

## Why
The introduction's claim that phantasia is the temporally-distinguishing faculty (i.e. the one that operates in the absence of the live object) requires the full 427b15–428a18 cluster, since that is where Aristotle isolates phantasia's mode-of-operation from each of the neighbouring faculties.
