## What (primary-text footnote)
> The etymological-Aristotelian bridge runs through *De Anima* III.10's three-factor analysis of locomotion. The *orektikon* is the moved-mover — itself moved by the desired object (the unmoved mover) and in turn moving the animal (433a17–18, 433b13–18). Etymologically, *orexis* (ὄρεξις) and *epithymia* (ἐπιθυμία) share the kinetic core: a reaching-out-toward (*orexis*) and an upward-yearning (*epi-thymos*) that are constitutive of the soul's being-disposed-toward an object.

## Why
The "etymology-Aristotle bridge" — translating from Greek etymology to Aristotle's positive doctrine — is best anchored at *DA* III.10 433a17–18 + 433b13–18, where *orexis* is given the technical "moved mover" role that the etymological reading prepares for.
