## What (paired secondary-source footnote)
> The "rhetoric-as-phantasmatic-reshaping" thesis runs through two recent works. Hawhee (2011) reads Aristotle's *Rhetoric* II–III as governed by a *phantasia bouleutikē* that lets rhetors "bring before the eyes" composite images (*pro ommatōn poiein*; *Rhet.* III.10, 1410b33–35; III.11, 1411b22–23): "*phantasia* moves closer to the way Nussbaum (1985) figures it — as interpretation, but it is interpretation that is grafted onto direct perception: rhetorical vision depends upon both simultaneously" (Hawhee 2011, p. 152). Rickert (2013, *Ambient Rhetoric*) reframes this at the ontological level: rhetoric is "the always-ongoing disclosure of the world that shifts our manner of being in that world, rather than a discrete symbolic act performed by autonomous human agents upon passive audiences" (Preface xii). Both authors converge on a *phantasmatic*-reshaping account, though Hawhee remains text-immanent (Aristotelian *Rhetoric*) while Rickert routes through Heideggerian ontology (*Stimmung*, dwelling, attunement).

## Why
The phantasmatic-reshaping thesis is best anchored by Hawhee (for the *phantasia*-rhetoric textual analysis) plus Rickert (for the ambient-ontological extension). The paired citation is canonical for the dissertation's claim.
