## What (paired primary+secondary footnote)
> Aristotle establishes the universalist thesis at *De Anima* II.2: every animal with touch (and therefore every animal whatsoever) has pleasure and pain co-given with sensation; thereby "wherever the senses are found, there too is pleasure and pain, and wherever these are found, *epithymia* must also be present" (DA II.2 cluster, 413b22–25; cf. DA II.3, 414b1–6 for the species-of-orexis schema). Heidegger develops the parallel "co-givenness" thesis at BCAP p. 115: "the *pathē* are co-given (*hois hepetai*) with *lupē* and *hēdonē* — not 'following' but a 'being-disposed-as-higher-or-lower' of the being-there in question."

## Why
The hedonic-universalist claim integrates the Aristotelian doctrine (DA II.2–II.3 on the tactile/hedonic minimum) with Heidegger's ontological reading at BCAP p. 115 (co-givenness as constitutive of being-disposed). Both anchors are needed.
