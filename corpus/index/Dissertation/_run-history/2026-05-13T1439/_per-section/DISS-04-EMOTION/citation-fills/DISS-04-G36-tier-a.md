## What (secondary-source footnote)
> The dissertation's overlap-of-*pathos*-senses warning is grounded in Heidegger's four-meaning analysis of *pathos* at BCAP §SS18b (pp. 131–132), expounding *Met.* Δ 21 (1022b15–22): (i) *poiotes kath' hen alloiousthai endechetai* — pathos as constitution-allowing-alteration; (ii) *energeia* of such constitution; (iii) *blaberai alloioseis kai kineseis, kai malista ai luprai* — pathos as harmful occurring-to-one, especially the painful; (iv) *ta megethē ton sumpheron kai luperon pathē* — pathos as the "size" of what occurs to me. Heidegger extracts the "*genuine relatedness* of pathos: it is related to the *being of living things*" (p. 132). This four-fold dispersion is precisely the dissertation's warning-target: *pathos* names neither a unified affect-concept nor a well-formed Aristotelian category.
