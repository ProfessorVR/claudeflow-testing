## What (compound primary-text footnote)
> The perception-vs-thinking distinction is Aristotle's own at *De Anima* III.3, 427b6–14: "Thinking and perceiving are not the same. Perceiving is universal in the animal world, thinking only in a small division of it." The distinction is then refined at *DA* III.7, 431a8–17: perception is *like* judgment (it makes "a sort of affirmation or negation"), but thinking proper "is necessarily aware of [something] along with an image" — i.e., requires *phantasma*-mediation.

## Why
The mapping requires both DA III.3 (the bare perception/thinking distinction) and DA III.7 (the more refined "thinking-requires-phantasma" thesis). The dissertation's perception-vs-thinking grid is structurally built on these two textual moments.
