## What (primary-text footnote)
> Aristotle's account of *aisthēsis* as a non-ordinary alteration is at *De Anima* II.5, 417a14–417b28: he distinguishes two senses of alteration — one a destruction-by-contrary (ordinary *alloiōsis*) and one a "preservation or fulfilment of a potentiality by what is actual" — and locates sense-perception in the second, preservative sense. The alterability at *aisthēsis* is thus an alterability that *fulfils* rather than *destroys* the bearer; this is the textual base for the dissertation's account of how perception is the live phase in which valence becomes susceptible to articulation.
