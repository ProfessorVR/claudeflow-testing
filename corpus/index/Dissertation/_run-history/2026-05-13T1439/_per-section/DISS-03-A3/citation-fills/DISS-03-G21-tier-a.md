## What (compound primary+secondary footnote)
> The three characteristics of *phantasmata* — (i) derivation from sensation as a quasi-causal residue (κίνησις ὑπὸ τῆς ἐνεργείας τῆς αἰσθήσεως, *DA* III.3, 428b13); (ii) capacity for falsehood (where sensation is always true; 428a11–12, 428b17–18); (iii) freedom from belief-conviction (we can imagine fearful things without being affected; 427b21–24) — are Aristotle's own distinguishing marks at *DA* III.3, 427b14–429a9. The recent literature converges: Frede (1992) reads *phantasia* as supervenient on but irreducible to sensation; Caston (1996, pp. 26–52) reads Aristotle as introducing *phantasia* precisely to solve the *problem of error*, with the *phantasma* functioning as a second, causally-mediated change ("the echo," Caston 1996, p. 47 diagram).

## Why
The three-characteristics scheme requires both the *DA* III.3 anchors and the canonical Anglophone secondary readings. Frede + Caston are the two most-cited interlocutors in the cluster.

## Verbatim (≤25 words primary)
"Phantasia is necessarily caused by the actuality of sense; from this it follows that it may be false as well as true" (*DA* III.3, 428b13–17).
