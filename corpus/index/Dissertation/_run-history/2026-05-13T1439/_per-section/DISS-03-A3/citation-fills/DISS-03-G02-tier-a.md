## What (paired-secondary footnote in dissertation house style)
> *Phantasia* names the faculty; *phantasma* its product. The distinction is canonical in Aristotle (*De Anima* III.3, 428a1; III.7, 431a17) but is unpacked most fully in two strands of recent scholarship: Frede argues that *phantasia* is supervenient on but irreducible to sensation, with *phantasmata* functioning as the cognitive vehicle of all post-sensory and non-perceptual thought (Frede 1992); Papachristou refines the picture by distinguishing *three* kinds or grades of *phantasia* — indefinite, sensitive, and calculative — corresponding to imperfect animals, perfect animals, and rational animals respectively (Papachristou 2013, esp. pp. 19–27).
