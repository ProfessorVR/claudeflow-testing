## What (compound secondary-source footnote; author-prominent)
> Heidegger gives three load-bearing statements on the *logos*/*pathē* relation in the rhetoric chapters of BCAP. At p. 95, *Rhetoric* itself is defined as "the interpretation of being-there with regard to the basic possibility of speaking-with-one-another," and the three *pisteis* are aligned with three aspects of speaking: about-something / to-someone / speaking-being-itself. At p. 110, in the analysis of deliberative oratory, the three factors are made explicit: "(1) *ethos*, the 'comportment' of the speaker; (2) *pathos*, the 'disposition' of the hearers; (3) the matter itself." At p. 176, the master claim of Chapter 3 lands: "the πάθη are not merely an annex of psychical processes, but are rather *the ground out of which speaking arises, and which what is expressed grows back into*."

## Why
The dissertation's claim about logos/speech as grounded in *pathē* requires all three loci: p. 95 (rhetoric-as-interpretation-of-being-there), p. 110 (ethos/pathos/matter triad), and p. 176 (pathē-as-ground-of-speech master claim). The three are mutually reinforcing and cannot be reduced to a single citation.
