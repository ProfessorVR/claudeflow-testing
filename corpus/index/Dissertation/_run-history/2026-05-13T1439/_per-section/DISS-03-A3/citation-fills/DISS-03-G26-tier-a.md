## What (paired primary+secondary footnote)
> Aristotle treats the universal as that which "holds across instances" in *Metaphysics* Z.10 (1035b27–1036a25), where he distinguishes the universal-as-form (logos) from the particular composite. The Castonian reading frames this in terms of the *phantasma*'s capacity to bear content "in a neutral way" without conviction (πίστις), which permits a more basic form of intentionality than the conceptual: "Even deliberation and higher cognition, which DO require concepts and inference, *build on phantasia*: the forms which are thought are 'in' phantasmata" (Caston 1996, citing *DA* III.7, 431b2; III.8, 432a4–6, 432a12–14; 431a14–16).

## Why
The "universal-holds-across-instances" claim integrates an Aristotelian primary citation (*Met.* Z.10 on the universal-form-vs-particular-composite question) with a recent secondary reading (Caston's *phantasma*-as-vehicle-of-pre-conceptual-intentionality) — together they support the dissertation's framing of universal-judgment as *phantasma*-grounded.

## Verbatim (≤200 words secondary)
