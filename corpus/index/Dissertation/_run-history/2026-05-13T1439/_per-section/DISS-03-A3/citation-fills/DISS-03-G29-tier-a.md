## What (primary-text footnote)
> At *De Anima* III.10, Aristotle stipulates that the object of practical desire is "the real or apparent good" (*to prakton agathon ē phainomenon*; cf. 433a27–29): "The object of desire is the start of practical thought, but the end of practical thought is the start of action" (433a17–18); and "what is the object of appetite is the start of practical thought... what is last in the process of thinking is the beginning of action" (433a15–18). The "apparent good" component is critical: it is what allows the renumbered A₃ phantasma to function as ratifying-articulation even when the *prakton* is only apparently good.

## Why
The dissertation's identification of A₃ as the apparent-good site needs *DA* III.10 433a27–29 (where the "real-or-apparent-good" object is named) plus 433a15–18 (where the practical-thought chain is laid out). The pair is the canonical locus for the *phainomenon agathon* doctrine.

## Verbatim (≤25 words)
"Appetite is in every form relative to an end; the object of appetite is the stimulant of practical thought" (*DA* III.10, 433a15–18).
