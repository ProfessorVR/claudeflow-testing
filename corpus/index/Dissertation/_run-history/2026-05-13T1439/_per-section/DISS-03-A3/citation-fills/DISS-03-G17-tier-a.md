## What (recommended integration; secondary-citation footnote)
> Heidegger characterizes Aristotle's ethical analysis as "a sharp contrast between *legein* about ethics-related problems and real philosophizing — an advance against the misuse of Socratic method" (BCAP p. 124). The point is that *aretē* is not a *technē*: "These determinations are 'not ascribed to a *technē*'" (*NE* B 3, 1105b 1, quoted at BCAP p. 124). The triad of conditions on the *one acting* — *eidos* (knowing, oriented toward *kairos* with *phronēsis*); *proairoumenos* (acting from out of himself); *bebaios kai ametakinētos echōn* ("stable and not to be brought out of composure") — opposes the sophists' and moralizers' public-orientation by re-locating ethical practice in the agent's settled comportment (BCAP p. 123–124).
