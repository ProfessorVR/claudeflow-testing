## What (paired primary+secondary citation, author-prominent)
> Aristotle's most quoted thesis on the *phantasma*-grounding of thought appears at *De Anima* III.7, 431a16–17: "the soul never thinks without an image" (*οὐδέποτε νοεῖ ἄνευ φαντάσματος ἡ ψυχή*). Heidegger develops the same claim in his 1924 *Basic Concepts of Aristotelian Philosophy*: "*phantasia* is 'the making-present-to-itself of the world, in which what is made present is not actually there'... 'insofar as *noesis* is the highest possibility for the being of human beings, the entire being of human beings is determined so that it must be apprehended as the *bodily being-in-the-world*'" (BCAP p. 134).

## Why
The claim is doubly anchored: at *DA* III.7 431a16–17 (primary) and at BCAP p. 134 (Heidegger's appropriation). The pair supplies both the Aristotelian source and the Heideggerian framing-claim the dissertation builds on.
