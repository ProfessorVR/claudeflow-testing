## 1. Final terminology architecture

The dissertation's coined-term vocabulary is reorganized around a single structural rule:
- **Unqualified Aristotelian term = live / present-object case** (the canonical Aristotelian usage; default)
- **`resonant <term>` = user's analytic extension** to the residual / phantasma-attached phase
- **No "vivid" or other live-case qualifier** anywhere — the unqualified term carries the default sense.

### 1.1 Three-axis × three-phase architecture

| Phase / Dimension | Formal-Eidetic Axis | Affective-Valence Axis | Articulationally-Concrete Axis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Live phase** (A₁→A₂; object present + *aisthēsis* active) | *aisthēma* | ***epithymia*** | *pathē* (live-elicited) |
| **Residual phase** (A₂-residue; object departed, *kinēsis* persists) | `resonant aisthēma` | **`resonant epithymia`** | — (no concretion at this layer; *doxa* not yet engaged) |
| **Phantasmatic-engagement phase** (A₃; *doxa*-mediated articulation) | *phantasma* | (re-elicited at A₃ via *doxa*-articulation) | **`resonant pathē`** (memory- or expectation-phantasma-attached) |

### 1.2 Over-arching umbrella term

**`basic affective valence`** survives as the synoptic umbrella for the whole basic-valence stratum across the live + residual temporal phases. Specifically:
> `basic affective valence` = *epithymia* (live phase) ∪ `resonant epithymia` (residual phase)

The chain-level analytic work is done by the more specific *epithymia* / `resonant epithymia` pair; `basic affective valence` is retained for synoptic moves (e.g., "what gets articulationally concretized into *pathē* is the underlying basic affective valence, in either of its temporal phases").

### 2a. New term to introduce

**`resonant pathē`** (REFINED 2026-05-19) — coin and use for the past articulationally-concrete-emotional content **preserved in memory itself** (the past anger preserved with the memory of the slight, the past joy preserved with the memory of the pleasure). The temporal-object of the emotion (past slight, future evil) is distinct from the temporal-location of the emotional experience (always now). `resonant pathē` names what is STORED, not what is FELT.

## 3. Footnote insertions at first use

Phase 5 revision-roadmap will queue these as discrete user-actionable tasks:

### 3.1 Footnote at first use of ***epithymia*** (likely §1.2, ~line 41 area where the Met. fourfold relocation will land)

> Aristotle defines *epithymia* (ἐπιθυμία) at *De Anima* II.3, 414b5–6 as "*orexis* of the pleasant" (τοῦ γὰρ ἡδέος ὄρεξις αὕτη) — the species of *orexis* (genus) that all sense-perceiving animals possess insofar as they have touch + pleasure/pain (cf. 413b22–25; 414b1–6 distinguishing *epithymia* / *thymos* / *boulēsis* as the three species of *orexis*). I use *epithymia* in this dissertation specifically for the basic hedonic valence (good/bad, pursue/avoid) co-given with live *aisthēsis* at the present-object phase. Aristotle's broader applications of *epithymia* to objects no longer present — e.g. *Rhet.* I.11, 1370a25–b18 on the present-felt pleasure of remembering past drinks during fever-thirst — I treat as the re-engagement of *resonant epithymia* (the past basic-valence-content preserved in the residual *kinēsis*) producing live *epithymia* in the present; the articulationally-concrete parallel — re-engagement of past *pathē*-content preserved in memory producing a live *pathē* — I name *resonant pathē*. (REFINED 2026-05-19.)

### 3.2 Footnote at first use of `resonant epithymia` (likely §1.2, dual-trace subsection ~line 63 area)

> I extend Aristotle's analyses of residual *kinēsis* — which treat the formal-imagistic trace (*phantasma* as *eikōn / typos*, per *On Memory* 450a26–b11; *On Dreams* 459a24–460b3) as what persists in the sense-organs after the object departs — to claim that the residual *kinēsis* also carries an affective-valence dimension I call *resonant epithymia*. *Mem.* 453a14–32 (the kinetic persistence of already-excited anger and terror) supplies partial textual support for affective-kinetic persistence; the dual-trace thesis generalizes this to the dormant-residual mode of basic hedonic tonality. The pair *epithymia* (live) / *resonant epithymia* (residual) follows Aristotle's own temporal contrast at *On Dreams* 460b3–16 between live, *enargēs* phantasia-perception and residual *phantasmata* of dreams.

### 3.3 Footnote at first use of `resonant pathē` (likely §1.3 Memory subsection, or §1.4 Feedback Loop)

> *Pathē* in Aristotle's *Rhetoric* II.2–11 catalog (anger, fear, shame, pity, etc.) are articulationally-concrete emotions that I treat throughout as essentially present-tense: a *pathos* is felt in the moment of its actualization, however that actualization is triggered. At *Rhet.* I.11, 1370a25–b18, Aristotle establishes that present-felt pleasure and pain attend both remembering and expecting — the pleasure of anticipated vengeance felt now by the angry man (1370b14), the pleasure of remembered drinks felt now by the fever-thirsty (1370b15–18). Aristotle's "temporal depth of emotion" is not, therefore, a distinction between live and non-live emotion but a distinction in what *triggers* the present-felt experience: present perception, future expectation, or memory re-engagement. I coin *resonant pathē* for a structurally distinct phenomenon: the past articulationally-concrete-emotional content preserved alongside the memory itself — the past anger preserved with the memory of the slight, the past joy preserved with the memory of the pleasure. This preserved past-emotional content is the "movement caused by the object preserved internally" of *De Memoria* 450a20–25 at the articulationally-concrete-emotional layer. *Resonant pathē* parallels *resonant epithymia* (the past basic-valence-content preserved in the residual *kinēsis*) but at the *doxa*-articulated-emotional layer rather than the basic-affective-valence layer. (REFINED 2026-05-19: prior draft treated *resonant pathē* as phantasma-attached *present-felt* emotion; refined to stored-past-content distinct from present-felt experience.)

### 3.4 Footnote at first use of `state-of-mind (*Befindlichkeit*)` (likely §1.2 or §1.4)

> Heidegger's *Befindlichkeit* is rendered throughout this dissertation as "state-of-mind (*Befindlichkeit*)," following Macquarrie & Robinson, with italicized German parenthetical retained after every occurrence to preserve the technical anchor. *Stimmung* is rendered as "mood (*Stimmung*)" by default, or as "Being-attuned (*Stimmung*)" when the tuning root-metaphor of *Gestimmtsein* is foregrounded. "Attunement" as a standalone noun appears in this dissertation only within direct quotations or within explicit glosses that list alternative English renderings; in user prose, *Befindlichkeit* is always rendered "state-of-mind (*Befindlichkeit*)". (REFINED 2026-05-19 per BT glossary verification at pp. 172–173.)
