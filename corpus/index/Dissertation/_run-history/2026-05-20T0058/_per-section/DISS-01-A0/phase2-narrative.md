## §1.1 in one sentence

The actualization chain that organizes the dissertation's analysis of desire and animal locomotion begins not with the perceiving organism but with the *ontological conditions* perception presupposes — motion (*kinēsis*) and time (*chronos*) as co-implicated structures whose prior actuality must already be in act before the chain's downstream nodes (perception, *phantasia*, articulated desire, and the *orektikon* transition to overt action) can be exercised. The section names this prior actuality A₀ and develops it through six successive analytic moments: (i) A₀ as the chain's first actuality and the limit case of the priority of actuality; (ii) *kinēsis* as *energeia ateles* (incomplete actuality), the peculiar mode of being whose *telos* lies beyond itself; (iii) the priority of actuality and the iterated structure of the chain it licenses; (iv) the relational dyadic structure of *kinēsis* (mover and moved as one *energeia* under two *logoi*); (v) time as the number of motion with the *now* as bounding-and-joining principle; and (vi) the soul's constitutive role in numbering motion — culminating in the threshold of A₁ at which the natural beings and rational souls A₀'s motion-time horizon licenses begin to enter the chain.

## Why this section runs the way it does

The chapter (of which §1.1 is the opening) has a *twofold project*: it develops the actualization chain of animal desire (*De Motu Animalium* 702a17-19 — sense-perception or thought → imagination → desire → affections → organic parts → movement) AND demonstrates how Aristotle's *phantasia*, evolved by Heidegger in his analyses of productive imagination and ecstatic temporality, is the human capacity through which time becomes a coherent narrative of past, present, and future (memory, present perception, anticipation). These are not two parallel projects but one. Every cognitive act — including pure intellection — is itself a *kinēsis* of the soul, since "being pained or pleased, or thinking" are themselves modes of motion (DA I.4, 408b5-7); and *orexis* is the moved mover that produces all such motion (DA III.10, 433b17-18). The chain A₀ → A₁ → A₂ → A₃ → A₄ names this one structure, with A₀ as motion-time horizon and successive stages unfolding the single chain through which desire moves the animal via perception, *phantasia*, evaluative articulation, and bodily preparation.

Because the section is the opening of a chapter whose payoff is *phantasia*'s temporal-narrative-synthesizing capacity, §1.1 cannot merely posit motion and time and move on. It must establish their ontological texture in enough depth that the subsequent sections (on *aisthēsis*, *phantasma*, evaluative articulation, completed action) inherit the right substrate. The whole section is therefore architectural-foundational — every term, every formal feature, every interpretive move at A₀ has consequences downstream. The Heideggerian register is not an addition to an Aristotelian baseline but operates from the section's opening, on the principle that motion as *kinēsis* and *Bewegtheit* names the same ontological structure under two thematizations.

## Subsection-by-subsection reconstruction

### S1: A₀ as First Actuality (lines 3-12)

The chain begins with a *first actuality* that is itself a process. The chain cannot begin with a static potentiality awaiting external awakening; if motion and time are jointly the substrate of all subsequent actualizations, they must already be in act, on pain of infinite regress. The section establishes the chain's notation: A_n names actualization stages (nodes), M_n → M_{n+1} names the transitions between them (the user has flagged the migration M_n → A_{n+1} as the eventual canonical form; §1.1 retains the old M_n → M_{n+1} convention throughout).

The architectural claim that motion and time are jointly the first actuality rests on Aristotle's threefold priority of actuality (*Metaphysics* IX.8, 1049b12-17, 1049b17-1050a3, 1050a4-16): in account (the potential is intelligible only through the actuality it is potential for), in time (any individual potentiality presupposes a prior actuality capable of bringing it into play), and in substance (the *telos* is more fully real than the process directed toward it). Burke's *Grammar of Motives* glosses the formal dimension: "man is 'prior' to boy because man has already attained its complete form whereas boy has not" — the priority is not first temporal but formal, the entelechial completion that organizes the iterated chain (Burke, *Grammar*, pp. 280-281 [VERIFY: corpus locus is pp. 253, 261-262]).

A₀ is the limit case: an actuality without prior actuality, since motion and time are co-eternal. Aristotle insists on the co-eternity at *Metaphysics* XII.6, 1071b6-8 ("it is impossible that movement should either come into being or cease to be; for it must always have existed") and yokes motion and time at 1071b8-11 ("nor can time come into being or cease to be; for there could not be a before and an after if time did not exist"). The section's Heideggerian register operates from the opening, not adjacent to it: Heidegger glosses Aristotelian motion in the *Basic Concepts of Aristotelian Philosophy* as the structural-existential determination of a being's *there*; if a being is determined as Aristotle determines *kinēsis*, then it is in movement, and *kinēsis* names what Heidegger develops as *Bewegtheit* — the mode of existence of beings whose way of being is to be underway toward what they are not yet.

### S2: *Kinēsis* as *Energeia Ateles* — Being-in-Movement (lines 13-26)

Aristotle's compact definition of motion is the term whose ontological texture this subsection specifies: "the fulfilment of what is potentially, as such, is motion — e.g. the fulfilment of what is alterable, as alterable, is alteration..." (*Physics* III.1, 201a11-14). Motion is the actuality (*energeia*) of the potential (*dynamis*) qua potential; it is a genuine actuality, and yet it is incomplete because its *telos* lies outside itself. Aristotle's pairing in *Metaphysics* IX.6 (1048b23-30) sharpens the cut between *energeia* (seeing, thinking — contain their *telos* within themselves) and *kinēsis* (building, learning, curing — terminate when their external *telos* is reached).

The peculiar ontological texture: motion is at once genuinely present (the builder is at work, the house is rising) and incomplete in respect of what it is becoming. Hawhee's reading of *energeia* — "*energeia* names something like physical presence: *energeia* means the presence of the thing" (Hawhee, *Bodily Arts* p. 154 [VERIFY: corpus has this verbatim at Hawhee 2011 *Rhetorical Vision* p. 154]) — supplies the lexical-rhetorical register. If *energeia* names the presence of the thing, then *kinēsis* as *energeia ateles* names a peculiar mode of presence: the presence of something that is not yet fully what it is becoming. The qua-structure governs everything: what is actualized in motion is actualized *as* potential, not as achieved completion (the bronze statue example, *Physics* III.1, 201a28-33).

The world is always already in motion; there is no moment at which potentiality sits inert (*Physics* V.2, 226a23-30). A₀ is always already actual, and it is actual as ongoing. The two-modes-of-being-affected distinction from *De Anima* II.5 (417b2-5) reinforces the point: in one sense being-affected is destruction; in the other it is *preservation* in which a potential is brought into its actuality. Hearing a sound is being-affected of the second sort. The same structural point governs A₀: the motion-time horizon is not exhausted in producing the next stage of the chain but *preserves itself* as the ground on which the next stage stands.

Heidegger's reading transposes this analysis from natural-philosophical category to fundamental ontology. Motion is a *Seinscharakter*; *kinēsis* accordingly names what Heidegger develops as *Bewegtheit* (*BCAP* / *GA* 18, p. ****** — placeholder awaiting verbatim from corpus). The structural payoff: Dasein is not a static substance subsequently entering into changes; it is a being whose mode of existence is being-underway-toward, the ahead-of-itself structure that *Sein und Zeit* §41 develops as *Sorge*. "Dasein is its possibility" (SZ §9, H.42; Eng. 67) — Aristotelian motion recovered at the level of fundamental ontology. The intermediate-register character of *energeia ateles* — genuinely actual yet structurally incomplete — is therefore the primitive ontological pattern from which the entire chain inherits its iterated structure: each completed actuality at A₁, A₂, A₃ is both terminus of its antecedent motion and unmoved originator of its successor, an *entelecheia* in relation to what precedes it and an *archē* in relation to what follows.

### S3: Priority of Actuality and the Chain Structure (lines 27-42)

The chain's iterated structure inherits its logic from the priority of actuality over potentiality. Aristotle's most concentrated statement (*Metaphysics* IX.8, 1050a7-11): "Everything that comes to be moves towards a principle, i.e. an end... and the actuality is the end, and it is for the sake of this that the potentiality is acquired." Sight is not a capacity that becomes actualized as seeing; seeing is the actuality for the sake of which sight exists as a capacity. The three senses of priority each carry architectural consequences for the chain:

- **In account**: the potential is intelligible only through the actuality it is potential for; the chain's apparatus is therefore intelligible only through the completed A₄.
- **In time**: any individual potentiality presupposes a prior actuality capable of bringing it into play; A₀'s motion-time horizon brings into play the natural beings of A₁.
- **In substance**: the *telos* is more fully real than the process directed toward it; the completed action at A₄ is more fully real than the chain's earlier phases.

The principle by which motions are named in the chain: "it is that to which rather than that from which the motion proceeds that gives its name to the change" (*Physics* V.1, 224b7-8). Each M_n → M_{n+1} takes its name from its terminus, not from its starting point. Aristotle's geometer illustration (*Metaphysics* IX.9, 1051a29-33) sharpens the relation: the potentialities are not discovered by introspection but by being brought to actuality. The actualization makes them manifest; the principle generalizes to the chain.

The transitional character of A₀ is articulated via *Metaphysics* XI.6, 1063a19-21 [VERIFY chapter]: motion is the medium of passage; the chain's iterated structure is a sequence of such passages, each instantiating the same transitional ontology. The relation between stages is hermeneutical, not causal-mechanical. The completed actuality at each stage — the for-the-sake-of-which — is always already operative in the understanding that guides the actualization. Aristotle articulates this as the operation of the *telos*; Heidegger generalizes the structure at the level of Dasein's interpretive comportment as the hermeneutical circle. Understanding projects itself upon possibilities that are themselves disclosed by the fore-structure of understanding: *Vorhabe* (fore-having), *Vorsicht* (fore-sight), and *Vorgriff* (fore-conception) jointly articulate the as-structure of interpretation (SZ §§32-33, H.150-159). "What is decisive is not to get out of the circle but to come into it in the right way" (SZ §32, H.153; Eng. 195). The chain's iterated structure — *energeia ateles* generating *entelecheia* generating new conditions for *energeia ateles* — is the proto-form of the hermeneutical circle Heidegger generalizes for Dasein's interpretive comportment.

### S4: The Relational Structure of *Kinēsis* (lines 43-54)

A feature of A₀ that the chain inherits at every subsequent stage is the relational character of *kinēsis*. Motion is not a property inhering in a single substance but a relation between mover and moved. Aristotle: "motion is in the movable. It is the fulfilment of this potentiality by the action of that which has the power of causing motion; and the actuality of that which has the power of causing motion is not other than the actuality of the movable; for it must be the fulfilment of *both*... Hence there is a single actuality of both alike" (*Physics* III.2, 202a13-20).

Aristotle specifies the dyadic structure in categorial terms: the agent's actuality is *poiēsis*; the patient's actuality is *pathēsis* (*Physics* III.3, 202a21-24). The Thebes-Athens analogy clarifies: "Thebes and Athens are different cities, yet the road from Thebes to Athens and the road from Athens to Thebes are the same" (*Physics* III.3, 202b13-14). One road, two descriptions. One motion, two formal aspects. The distinction is in account, not in substrate.

The dyadic architecture supplied by A₀ propagates through every subsequent stage of the chain. At A₁ the sensible object's active potency meets the sense-organ's receptive potency in a single perceptual actualization (DA II.5, 416b33-417a20; III.2, 425b26-27 on the single-event-under-two-*logoi*). The same schema iterates at the cognitive-motor stages: what the realizable good moves at M_3 → M_4 is *orexis*, the moved mover that is at once agent (of the animal's locomotion) and patient (of the apparent good). The chain is dyadic at every node because A₀ establishes dyadic relationality as primitive. Burke captures the consequence: the motion of A by B determines both A and B, assigning each a role and a trajectory — "conditions are likewise contextual, as with the conditions of an organism's existence" (Burke, *Grammar*, pp. 214-215 [VERIFY]).

Heidegger's existential radicalization is the analysis of Dasein as constituted by its *In-der-Welt-sein*. Dasein does not first exist as substance and then enter into relations; it is constituted by relational being-in-the-world, in which entities are encountered as ready-to-hand (*Zuhandenheit*), as members of equipmental contexts (*Zeug*) articulated by chains of involvement (*Bewandtnis*). The relevant SZ §18 passage on H.84 is currently a ****** placeholder. The Aristotelian relational *kinēsis* is, on this reading, the proto-form of Heideggerian worldly involvement: motion is always a being-toward, an articulation between an active power and a passive power that are not first independent substances and then jointly active but co-constituted in the actuality of the motion they share.

### S5: Time (*Chronos*) as the Number of Motion (lines 55-70)

Aristotle's definition: "time is the number of motion in respect of the before and after" (*Physics* IV.11, 219b1-2). Time is not an independent substance — not a container within which events transpire, not an autonomous metaphysical entity — but an aspect of motion: motion insofar as it admits of enumeration. Where there is no motion there is no time; where motion can be numbered there is time as that numbering. The continuity and directional structure of time are inherited from motion, and motion inherits these from spatial magnitude (*Physics* IV.11, 219a10-13). Time is accordingly "either the same thing as movement or an attribute of movement" (*Metaphysics* XII.6, 1071b10-11).

The architecturally load-bearing distinction: number is used in two ways — of what is counted (or countable) and of that with which we count. Time is the counted number of motion, not the counting activity (*Physics* IV.11, 219b5-10). This allows time to be located in the structure of motion itself — as what *can* be counted — while still requiring a counter for the full actualization of time as numbered. We perceive movement and time together (*Physics* IV.11, 219a4-8 [VERIFY Bekker]); the reciprocity is ontological as well as epistemic.

The *now* (*to nun*) is the principle by which time's continuum is articulated and divided: "the now determines time, insofar as time involves the before and after" (*Physics* IV.11, 219b12-13). The body-carried-along analogy (*Physics* IV.11, 219b22-25 [VERIFY Bekker]): the now corresponds to the body that is carried along, as time corresponds to the motion. The moving body is always the same body even as it traverses different positions; the now is always the same now even as it marks different moments. *One in substrate, different in being* — the formula that subsequent sections will deploy to articulate the dual structure of *resonant kinēsis* at A₂.

Heidegger reads the Aristotelian analysis in *Being and Time* Division Two, Chapter VI as the most rigorous articulation of *Innerzeitigkeit* — time as publicly encountered, the time-in-which entities and events occur. Aristotle's analysis is correct as far as it goes; what it does not thematize is the more primordial structure of temporality (*Zeitlichkeit*) and historicity (*Geschichtlichkeit*) from which *Innerzeitigkeit* is derived. The relevant SZ §81 H.421-422 passage is currently a ****** placeholder. The structural-philosophical point: Aristotle has captured the *Innerzeitigkeit*-stratum without thematizing its grounding in originary temporality; the chapter's apparatus deploys *Innerzeitigkeit* as the time-stratum within which the chain unfolds, while the more primordial temporality the chain-actualizations presuppose is held open by the Heideggerian framework. The two registers operate together.

The implication for the chapter's broader project is direct: the cognition of time as a coherent narrative of past, present, and future is not delivered by the Aristotelian definition alone. The definition captures countable succession; it does not, on its own, articulate the synthetic activity through which the three temporal modes are knit into a unified phenomenal manifold. That synthetic activity is the work of *phantasia*, which the subsequent sections develop. The A₀ motion-time stratum is the substrate within which *phantasia*'s temporal-synthesizing activity unfolds. Aristotle himself prepares the thesis: "imagination is a feeble sort of sensation, and memory and expectation involve imagination" (*Rhetoric* I.11, 1370a28-33).

### S6: The Soul's Constitutive Role (lines 71-80)

The soul is "a substance in the sense of the form of a natural body" (DA II.1, 412a19-20); it is the first actuality of a natural body that has life potentially. The soul does not exist apart from the body; it is the body's organizing principle. Hence the soul is itself an instance of the priority of actuality. The motion-time parallel runs explicitly: just as motion is the actuality of the potential qua potential, so the soul is the actuality of the body's potential for life; just as time is the numbered aspect of motion, so the rational soul is the numbering principle that articulates temporal flow. [INTERPRETIVE BRIDGE: the four-term proportional analogy is the chapter's synthesis, not directly stated by Aristotle.]

Aristotle's three-grade schema (DA II.5, 417a21-b2): first potentiality is the bare capacity, second potentiality / first actuality is the developed capacity, second actuality is the exercise. A₁ corresponds to first actuality. The hierarchical ordering of psychic capacities (DA II.3) reinforces the iterated structure.

The architectural weight of A₀ becomes most explicit at the soul's relation to time: "If nothing but soul, or in soul reason, is qualified to count, it is impossible for there to be time unless there is soul, but only that of which time is an attribute — i.e., if movement can exist without soul" (*Physics* IV.14, 223a25-27 [VERIFY: corpus has 223a21-26]). The conditional is precise. Motion may exist without soul: natural bodies move whether or not anyone observes them. But time — the numbered aspect of motion — requires a counter, and where there is no counter there can be no number counted.

**THE STRONG READING.** The chapter adopts a stronger interpretive reading of the conditional than Aristotle's text strictly compels: *time without a counting soul is not merely unnumbered but ontologically incomplete* — present as countability, structurally real as the before-and-after of motion, yet not fully actualized as time. The reading is interpretive rather than textually compelled, and the chapter flags it as such. It coheres with Aristotle's broader priority-of-actuality commitments: if time is fully actual only as numbered, what makes the numbering possible is constitutive of time's full actuality. The textual warrant is cautious-conditional, and extended engagement with the alternative reading (which would treat time as fully actual independently of soul) is deferred to subsequent sections, with Coope and Broadie as principal interlocutors.

The Heideggerian radicalization: originary temporality (*Zeitlichkeit*) is the unity of three ecstases (*Ekstasen*) — having-been (*Gewesenheit*), present (*Gegenwart*), and projected-futural (*Zukunft*) (SZ §65, H.328-329 — currently ****** placeholder). The Aristotelian insight that time requires soul as counter is secured at the existential-ontological level: Dasein's ecstatic temporality *is* the originary phenomenon from which the public-time of the now-sequence derives. The strong reading of Phys. IV.14 aligns most cleanly with the Heideggerian recovery, since on the Heideggerian reading a motion-time stratum without any soul to count it would lack the originary temporality that makes it intelligible as time at all.

### S7: The Threshold of A₁ (lines 81-86)

The architectural pivot: Aristotle's *phantasia* and Heidegger's productive imagination name the same temporal-synthetic capacity through which a finite, embodied, comporting being cognizes time as a coherent narrative of past, present, and future. Aristotle states the foundation: "all memory implies time; therefore only animals that perceive time can remember" (*De Memoria* 449b28-30); "imagination is a feeble sort of sensation, and memory and expectation involve imagination" (*Rhetoric* I.11, 1370a28-30). *Phantasia* is the capacity that ranges across all three temporal modes — it presents the present object, retains the absent past object as memory, and projects the not-yet object as anticipation. Without *phantasia*, the soul could be moved only by what is perceptually present; with *phantasia*, the soul can be moved by absences, possibilities, appearances, and counterfactuals — which is to say by the full range of objects required for the actualization chain to operate across memory and anticipation rather than within the immediate now alone.

Heidegger's analysis of productive imagination in *Kant and the Problem of Metaphysics* (GA 3) radicalizes this Aristotelian capacity as the temporalizing-synthesis that knits the three ecstases into the unity of originary temporality. A₀'s motion-time horizon licenses A₁'s natural beings: sensible objects exercising their sensible powers, sense-organs holding first actuality as capacity, rational souls capable of articulating temporal succession. Motion being actual is what allows sensible objects to actually exercise their sensible powers; motion being actual is what allows the medium to transmit the sensible form; time being actual is what allows the temporal continuum within which the transmission unfolds; time becoming fully actual with the arrival of rational soul is what allows the sequential articulation of perceptual, imaginative, and cognitive acts the subsequent sections will trace. The chain begins.

## What this section does for the chain

§1.1 establishes A₀ as the chain's first actuality and the limit case of the priority of actuality. Three structural features are bequeathed to all subsequent stages:
1. **Iterated structure** (from priority of actuality): each A_n is both *entelecheia* in relation to what precedes it and *archē* in relation to what follows.
2. **Dyadic relationality** (from the joint actualization of mover and moved): each motion in the chain is one *energeia* under two *logoi* — agent and patient, active and passive capacity.
3. **One-in-substrate, different-in-being** (from the now's dual character as continuity-principle and division-principle): each A_n is one substrate that can be analyzed under multiple formal aspects.

The Heideggerian register operates throughout: Aristotelian motion as *Bewegtheit*; Aristotelian dyadic *kinēsis* as proto-form of *In-der-Welt-sein*; the iterated chain as proto-form of the hermeneutical circle; the *now*-sequence of *Innerzeitigkeit* as derived from the originary *Zeitlichkeit* of the three ecstases. These are interpretive bridges that the chapter explicitly flags as such only at the strong-reading-of-Physics-IV.14 moment. The other Heideggerian-Aristotelian transpositions are presented as if architecturally settled; a Phase 2 revision should add interpretive flags at the framing level of these bridges (see citation gap table G10, G11, G13).
