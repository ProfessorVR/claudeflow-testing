## The hinge problem

Section §1.3 sits at the architectural hinge of the dissertation. §1.2 ended with the *resonant kinēsis* of A₂ — the dual-trace residue (*resonant aisthēma* + *resonant orexis*) that persists after the sensible object has withdrawn. §1.4 will begin with the articulational concretion of basic affective valence into the determinate *pathē* of the *Rhetoric*. Between them must lie the moment at which (i) the residual *kinēsis* hardens into a *determinate* presentational image, (ii) the rational soul takes that image up under cognitive operations, and (iii) the propositional ratification through which determinate *pathē* will subsequently arise becomes possible. §1.3 names this hinge A₃ and identifies its content as the *phantasma* proper, addressed under three orientational modes and orthogonally overlaid by *doxa*.

## The phantasia / phantasma distinction

The section's terminological opening makes a distinction that is load-bearing throughout. *Phantasia* names the soul's capacity and activity of generating presentation-images from perceptual residue. *Phantasma* names the determinate image that results from that activity. The distinction tracks Aristotle's *DA* III.3, 428a1–3 ("that in virtue of which an image arises for us") read against itself: the same word names a faculty, an act, and a product. Papachristou is invoked as the secondary scholar who insists on leaving *phantasia* untranslated for exactly this reason — "imagination" obscures the tripartite ambiguity (capacity/activity/product) that Aristotle's analysis requires.

A further internal grading is then asserted: Aristotle distinguishes *sensory* *phantasia* (which all perceiving animals possess and which generates *phantasmata* that are direct residues of perception) from *deliberative* *phantasia* (which belongs only to rational animals and synthesizes multiple *phantasmata* into composite images of future possibility). The *phantasma* itself is characterized by three essential features: it is *determinate* (it bears a definite formal structure), *available* (it stands before the cognitive faculties as something contemplable, recallable, combinable, or judgeable), and *representational* (it bears a structural likeness to the sensible particular from which it derives). These three features are not extrinsic; they follow from *phantasia*'s essential character as a motion arising from actual sensation. Aristotle's famous claim that "the soul never thinks without a *phantasma*" is read here as a statement of architectural necessity: the higher operations of memory, intellection, deliberation, and *doxa* all presuppose an image that already possesses determinacy-availability-representationality.

Burke's deployment of Hazlitt's notion of "identification" — that the only reason one prefers one's future interest to the interests of others is that one anticipates the future with greater warmth of present imagination — is introduced here to extend the *phantasma* beyond the epistemological into the motivational. The image does not merely present content for contemplation; it engages the *orectic* dimension of the soul, moving desire toward or away from what the image represents.

## The Heideggerian gloss: phantasia as poiein paron

The user reads Heidegger's *Basic Concepts of Aristotelian Philosophy* (BCAP) 132–137 as transforming the three features just enumerated. Heidegger reads *phantasia* as *ποιεῖν παρόν* ("making-present"): a making-present that takes up the *eidos* (the "look") of what has been perceived and holds it in availability for *doxa*, memory, and deliberation. Crucially, this making-present is not the activity of a faculty producing inner copies; it is a *mode of being-in-the-world*. The three features — determinacy, availability, representationality — are accordingly recast: determinacy becomes the *eidos*-character of the look itself (the *phantasma* presents *this* being and not another, and so can be true or false), availability becomes the very making-present that grounds remembering-thinking-deliberation, and representationality becomes the *uncovering* function in which the *phantasma* makes the thing manifest in a non-sensory mode.

What *phantasia* makes present, on this reading, is the *eidos* (the "what it is") together with hedonic tonality / *resonant orexis* (the "how it matters to me"). The *phantasma* is the way in which *both* the *eidos* and the affective tonality of a being are taken up and held. Heidegger's light-and-illumination metaphor (BCAP 135) is mobilized: every being that is there as a being requires a fundamental illumination, and "this possibility is nothing other than *nous*." The *phantasma*, then, is structurally the same *eidos* that perception first received, now held as the illuminated presence in which the thing can be seen and thought.

Heidegger's grounding of higher cognition in bodily being-in-the-world is then taken up explicitly: even *nous* "of human beings is not pure" — it is grounded, bodily, in *phantasia*. Aristotle's claim that the soul never thinks without an image is read not as a contingent psychological condition but as the indication that all higher cognition is grounded in the image-based, pre-propositional mode of being-in-the-world that *phantasia* discloses. A₃ is precisely the moment at which the soul holds the *phantasma* of the perceived particular under the description "image-for-the-mind": the moment at which every higher cognitive act shows itself as bodily through *phantasia*.

## The three thirst cases: taking-as-something across faculties

The section's most pedagogically distinctive move is the deployment of three thirst-cases keyed to Aristotle's *MA* 7, 701a32–33: "I want to drink says appetite; this is drink, says sense or imagination or thought: straightaway I drink." The three cases isolate the structural fact that the *phantasma*'s *taking-as-something* can be activated by any of the three discriminative faculties:

**Case 1 (aisthēsis):** The thirsty hiker returns home, walks into the kitchen, sees the cool glass of water. Visual proper-sensibles fire; *pathos simpliciter* is co-given (the clear cool-look is intrinsically pleasant); *resonant orexis* is engaged as *dunamis* (drink-as-realizable-good-to-be-pursued is anticipated). The hiker grabs the glass and drinks. *Pathos simpliciter* of taste and tactile activates; *resonant orexis* transitions from *dunamis* to *energeia*. The *resonant orexis* has not disappeared or been replaced — it has shifted from potentiality to actuality.

**Case 2 (phantasia):** The hiker is mid-summer thirsty with no water in sight. A canteen-with-water *phantasma* arises from memory. No drink-proper-sensibles are firing; environmental proper-sensibles (heat, throat-distress) and their *pathos simpliciter* are firing instead. This is *resonant orexis* in its purest mode: hedonic tonality riding on the *phantasma* alone, layered with thirst-state *pathos simpliciter* rather than drink-*pathos simpliciter*. The *resonant orexis* motivates reaching into the pack.

**Case 3 (nous):** The hiker is thirsty in unfamiliar terrain. *Nous* runs deliberative inference (recent rain + clean rock-basin + rainwater = drinkable) and concludes this-is-drink. The *eidos* "drink" is held under universal premise; no specific *phantasma* of any particular drink-source is present. The abstract *resonant orexis* (drink-as-good-held-universally) is the motivational driver. When the inference completes and the puddle is seen, the abstract *phantasma* applies to the perceived particular and the case collapses into case 1.

Across all three cases the user identifies a structural invariant: *resonant orexis* on the *phantasma* "drink" is present everywhere the *eidos* is held — presently deployed, retained from memory, prospectively applied, atemporally held by *nous*.

## The three orientational modes

The three thirst cases serve as the launching point for the section's principal architectural distinction. Rather than being discharged immediately into action, the *phantasma* can equally be *held* by the soul and engaged cognitively. The three modes in which it can be held are: **intellection** (*nous*), **memory** (*mnēmē*), and the **discursive mode** (deliberative *phantasia* / *phantasia bouleutikē*) which subdivides into practical (deliberative) and speculative (theoretical) sub-domains. The three share a common structural character — each is a *non-committal* cognitive engagement with the *phantasma*, in the precise sense that none of them, taken on its own, makes a truth-claim — but they differ in intentional direction and temporal orientation. Intellection holds the *eidos* atemporally as universal. Memory deploys the *phantasma* retrospectively as past. The discursive mode projects it prospectively, whether toward future action (practical) or toward contemplated unchangeable truth (speculative).

**Intellection** is treated under the rubric "the *phantasma* as bearer of the universal." Aristotle's claim that "the thinking faculty thinks the forms in the images" (*DA* III.7, 431b12–13) is read as specifying that *nous* operates *upon* the *phantasma* by abstracting from its particular determinations the universal form it instantiates. The *phantasma* is the substrate; the universal is what *nous* extracts. Crucially, intellection is *atemporal*: the universal, once grasped, is not indexed to past, present, or future. Heidegger is invoked again to add the qualification that even atemporal intellection is grounded bodily in the temporally-available *phantasma*. *Nous* abstracts; *doxa* asserts; these are distinct operations, and the boundary between them is one of the section's load-bearing structural insistences.

**Memory** is treated under the rubric "the *phantasma* as likeness of the past." *On Memory* 449b25–27 is the textual ground: the object of memory is past, just as the object of perception is present and the object of expectation is future. Memory requires not merely the *phantasma* but the recognition that the *phantasma* is the likeness of something one has formerly experienced. As Frede observes, memory's object is "a past experience *qua* past." Heidegger's existential analytic of *Zeitlichkeit* (ecstatic temporality) is then folded in: *Gewesenheit* (having-been) is not a dead past but a living dimension of *Dasein*'s being that continues to determine present understanding and future projection. The Aristotelian memory-*phantasma* is read in the same register: a living *resonant kinēsis* that continues to orient the soul's cognitive engagement toward the past from which it derives. The temporal orientation of memory is *retrospective*. Memory furnishes the materials on which the other modes operate; a settled memory may function as an additional unmoved originator that shapes how a current *phantasma* is taken up.

**The discursive mode** is treated under the rubric of the synthetic capacity. Unlike intellection and memory (which operate on a *single* *phantasma*), the discursive mode operates on *multiple* *phantasmata*, synthesizing them into a unified object of thought. *DA* III.11, 434a5–10 ("there must be a single standard to measure by, for that is pursued which is greater. It follows that what acts in this way must be able to make a unity out of several images") is the textual ground. Within the discursive mode two sub-domains are distinguished. **Deliberative *phantasia*** is practical: it operates by a *projective-synthetic* sub-function (recombining *phantasmata* into images of states not yet actual) and a *calculative-practical* sub-function (measuring projected possibilities against a single standard). Its temporal orientation is *prospective*; it terminates in a determination of the greater good and initiates motion at M₃ → A₄. **Speculative thinking**, by contrast, synthesizes *phantasmata* toward the unchangeable — toward what is and cannot be otherwise. Aristotle's distinction between "thought concerned with action" (which calculates means to an end) and "thought which is speculative and not concerned with action" (which "moves nothing"; *DA* III.10, 433a13–15) is here load-bearing. Speculative thinking is movement *as* thinking-through-*phantasmata*, but it is not movement toward a realizable good. Its temporal orientation is *atemporal-contemplative*: its object stands outside the practical temporal horizon altogether. The chain terminates at A₃ in the speculative case. Heidegger's analysis of deliberation as "seeking a definite view" toward the *telos* of a *doxa* (BCAP 96) is integrated.

## The unified cognitive apparatus

The three modes, the user insists, should not be mistaken for three separate faculties operating independently. They are analytical descriptions of a single rational cognitive apparatus operating on the *phantasma* under distinct intentional directions. They share a common structural role within M₂ → A₃ (note: the section uses both M₂→M₃ and M₂→A₃ formulations in different places — see §1.3's mixed numbering): the *phantasma* (or repertoire of *phantasmata* for the synthetic case) is the unmoved originator; the rational cognitive apparatus in its respective orientation is the moved mover; the cognitively transformed rational animal is what is moved. Each orientation takes up the same formally determined image under a different aspect. These modes may operate simultaneously or in succession; the same *phantasma* can simultaneously bear a universal for intellection, a temporal index for memory, a deliberative projection for practical reasoning, and a formal structure for speculative contemplation. The conclusion to the modes-section maps the user's "orientational modes" onto Heidegger's "modes of disclosure" and the temporal orientations onto Heidegger's "ecstases of *Zeitlichkeit*."

## Doxa as orthogonal committal layer

The section's most distinctive architectural move now arrives. *Doxa*, the user insists, is not a fourth cognitive mode parallel to intellection, memory, and the discursive mode. It is an *orthogonal dimension* that attaches to any content produced by those three orientations. The argument proceeds from Aristotle's contrast at *DA* III.3, 427b17–21: imagination is *ἐφ' ἡμῖν* (within our power) — we can call up a *phantasma* by mnemonic practice — but in forming opinions we are *not* free: "we cannot escape the alternative of falsehood or truth." The asymmetry is total. The capacity-to-summon (*phantasia*) and the inability-to-decide (*doxa*) arise from a single source: *doxa* is constitutively truth-or-falsehood-bearing, and answerable accordingly to how things *are* rather than to how we might wish them to appear.

Heidegger is again integrated. *Phantasia* "has both possibilities" (true or false) but "only *from without*"; *doxa* has the possibility "*in itself*" — within the sense of opining itself lies the "can" of true or false (BCAP 93–94). Where *phantasia* is simple making-present (taking-as-something), *doxa* is the "yes-saying" that adds *logos*-dependent ratification. Rationality is precisely what makes the *taking-as-something-true-or-false* possible and why *doxa* is present only in rational beings.

Aristotle's three conditions on *doxa* (*DA* III.3, 428a19–24) — belief, conviction, discourse of reason — are read as a layered specification of what *doxa* must possess. Belief is the assent that distinguishes *doxa* from non-committal entertainment of an image. Conviction is the binding force of that assent. Discourse of reason is the medium through which assent and binding force operate. The sun passage (*DA* III.3, 428b2–9) is read as demonstrating *doxa*'s ontological distinctness from *phantasia*: the *phantasma* of the foot-wide sun persists *alongside* the *doxa* that the sun is larger than the inhabited earth. The simultaneity proves the structural distinctness.

The *taking-as-true-or-false* function is then treated as the chain's hinge. Two operations must be distinguished. First, *taking-as*: the presenting of an object under a determinate form together with its affective valence — *phantasia*'s accomplishment. Second, *propositional* *taking-as-true-or-false*: the ratification of the *phantasma* as a truth-claim — *doxa*'s accomplishment. The first is pre-propositional and accomplished by *phantasia* alone (so non-rational animals navigate without it); the second is *logos*-dependent and operates exclusively in rational animals. O'Gorman is invoked for the external grounding of *doxa*: opinions are formed by the world about us through *phantasia*. The crucial textual ground for the propositional-ratification step is *DA* III.3, 427b21–24: when the soul merely imagines something fearful we remain unaffected; only when we *take-as-fearful* (when *doxa* ratifies the aspectual presentation as disclosing a genuine threat) does emotion immediately arise (εὐθὺς πάσχομεν).

The orthogonality of *doxa* follows: *doxa* engages upon any of the three orientational modes, adding to each the dimension of involuntary assertoric commitment. *Doxa* cuts across, applying its verdict of truth or falsity to whatever content the orientational modes produce. This orthogonality preserves the autonomy of *phantasia* from *doxa* (the *phantasma* can be generated, manipulated, combined without commitment to its truth; *doxa* enters only when the soul takes a stand). Burke's observation that rhetoric "proves opposites" is integrated: the rhetorical capacity to argue both sides presupposes that propositions are neutral with respect to truth-value until *doxa* commits the soul to one side. Heidegger's reading of rhetoric as aimed at *krisis* — the formation of a definite view in the sense of a *doxa* — locks the architecture together.

## The transition into action

The conclusion gathers everything: A₃ is a multi-dimensional achievement at which several distinct operations converge upon the *phantasma* stabilized at A₂. The rational cognitive apparatus engages the *phantasma* along three orientational axes (atemporal-intellection, retrospective-memory, discursive); alongside, and structurally distinct from them, the orthogonal *doxa*-dimension adds propositional *taking-as-true-or-false*. The structural consequence: A₃ serves *conditionally* as the unmoved originator of the subsequent motion at M₃ → A₄, and the condition is that A₃'s content disclose a realizable good. Where the actuality is the contemplation of unchangeable truth (speculative case), no realizable good is disclosed and the chain terminates at A₃. Where the actuality bears on a practical possibility, the chain continues. The *phantasma* is the principle of cognitive and practical motion in the rational soul.

## What §1.3 promises and what §1.4 receives

§1.3 hands §1.4 a fully-articulated A₃: a *phantasma* with a *resonant aisthēma* (the formal-eidetic "what it is") and a *resonant orexis* (the affective "how it matters") that can be engaged orientationally and ratified doxastically. §1.4 takes up the doxastic-ratification step as the trigger that articulationally concretizes the generic *resonant orexis* into the determinate *pathē* of the *Rhetoric*'s catalog. The pre-propositional / propositional bifurcation that §1.3 establishes is the single most consequential architectural delivery to §1.4. The dissertation's main thesis — emotion is the form desire takes under doxastic ratification of an evaluatively-loaded phantasmic presentation — is set up here in §1.3 but consummated in §1.4.
