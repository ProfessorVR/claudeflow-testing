# Contested Readings — Aristotelian Emotion Secondary Cluster (1975–2015)

Phase-3 synthesis. The cluster is organized by a single dispute — **what kind of cognition is constitutive of a *pathos*: a belief (*doxa*) or an appearance (*phantasia*)?** — and its ramifications. Each entry: *reading* · *surface convergence* · *underlying disagreement* · *evidence* (≥2 units, citing unit reading-IDs) · *implication*. Units: **F** = Fortenbaugh (aes-01, doxastic), **K** = Konstan (aes-02, social-cognitive/judgmental), **MA** = Moss-Akrasia (aes-03, phantastic), **MG** = Moss-Apparent-Good (aes-04, phantastic), **D** = Dow (aes-05, affirmation). A closing **Bridge to the dissertation's Section 5** maps the disputes onto the advisor-review findings.

---

## 1. The constitutive cognition of *pathos*: belief vs. appearance vs. affirmation *(the cluster's master axis)*

**Surface convergence.** All five reject the non-cognitivist/"feeling" view: every *pathos* has an evaluative-cognitive component built into its essence, and all anchor on the *Rhetoric* II.1–II.11 definitions (anger = pain at an apparent slight; fear = the appearance of impending evil).

**Underlying disagreement.** Four distinct positions on *what kind* of cognition:
- **F (doxastic, maximal):** the cognition is a **belief/judgment (*doxa*/*krisis*)** functioning as the *efficient cause* in the essential definition; *phantasia* is not deployed as a category at all (F-R1, F-R2). Extreme pole.
- **K (judgmental, social):** the cognition is a **supposition (*hupolēpsis*)** — a social construal of another's act bearing on standing — siding with Andronicus over Aspasius's bare-*phantasia* reading (K-R1, K-R6). Judgmental, but "judgment" = social appraisal, not technical *doxa*.
- **MA / MG (phantastic):** the cognition is a non-doxastic, quasi-perceptual ***phantasia***-appearance of something as good/bad; the passionate part is *incapable of belief* (MG-R4, MG-R5; MA-R3).
- **D (affirmation):** the representational capacity is *phantasia*, but the subject **affirms** its content as the way things are — a treating-as-true that is **neither bare appearance nor full belief** (D-R3, D-R4, D-R5).

**Evidence.** F-R2 (constitutive *doxa* "open to persuasive *logos*"; no *phantasia* in his index); K-R6 (*hupolēpsis*, contra Aspasius); MG-R4/R5 (the seat argument: *pathētikon = orektikon = aisthētikon = phantastikon*, *DA* 431a13–14, incapable of belief); D-R4 (subject-level affirmation as the keystone).

**Implication.** The cluster does not converge; it *spans* the judgmental→phantastic spectrum, with **Dow's "affirmation" the deliberately intermediate term** (his stated advance beyond both Fortenbaugh's belief and Moss's sub-personal acceptance, D-R5). This is the single most important structural fact about the cluster — and the lever for the dissertation (see Bridge §A).

## 2. What the sun-illusion (DA III.3 428b) proves — *the same passage, opposite verdicts*

**Surface convergence.** MA, MG, and D all treat *DA* III.3 428a18–b9 (the sun appears a foot wide though believed larger than the earth) as the decisive test passage for the *phantasia*/*doxa* relation.

**Underlying disagreement.** **Moss reads it *for* the phantastic account; Dow reads it *against*.** For Moss the sun-case is the paradigm of an appearance that persists *contrary to belief*, proving *phantasia* and *doxa* are distinct faculties and that motivation tracks the appearance (MA-R3, MA-R4; MG-R1, MG-R8). For Dow the sun-case is a **disanalogy**: it involves *no subject-level inconsistency* (the belief "the sun is huge" is wholeheartedly endorsed), so a belief/affirmation theory would be *bad* for the sun — but a recalcitrant *emotion* (fear of the spider believed harmless) *is* conflicted/irrational, which shows the passions, *unlike* the sun-illusion, carry subject-level affirmation (D-R6).

**Evidence.** MG-R1/R8 vs. D-R6 (explicit inversion); both cite *DA* III.3 428a18–b9 and the *Sophist* 263d–264b mixture-view background.

**Implication.** The cluster contains a clean, fully-articulated reversal on its key text. An examiner who knows this literature will expect a dissertation that leans on the sun/rope-snake cases to declare which reading it takes — and Dow's disanalogy is the resource for *keeping* a taking-as-true thesis while conceding the bare illusions.

## 3. The seat of the passions: can the part that feels also believe?

**Surface convergence.** All agree Aristotle locates the passions with the *orektikon*/*aisthētikon* complex and that *DA* III.7 431a8–14 ("one in substrate, different in being") is central.

**Underlying disagreement.** **Is that seat capable of *doxa*?** MG: **no** — the passionate part exercises perception and *phantasia* but is *constitutively incapable of belief*, so the cognition cannot be doxastic (MG-R5). F: the alogical part *can* hold habituated **opinions/judgments "open to persuasion"** that furnish a true evaluative verdict (F-R7) — i.e. the seat is *not* belief-incapable in his sense. D: the seat is the *non-reasoning* part, which *affirms* but does not *believe*; affirmation is precisely the non-reasoning part's "treating as veridical" (D-R3, D-R4).

**Evidence.** MG-R5 vs. F-R7 vs. D-R3 — all reading the same *DA* III.7 / *NE* I.13 bipartition, with opposite conclusions about whether the lower part can bear *doxa*.

**Implication.** This is where the dissertation's own **tri-identity** (*aisthētikon = phantastikon = orektikon*, "one substrate two in being," diss. 863–876) meets the literature: **Moss deploys the very same identity (MG-R5)** to argue the *opposite* of the dissertation — that the seat *cannot* believe. The dissertation both gains an ally for the identity-claim and inherits Moss's conclusion as an objection.

## 4. The mode of "acceptance": default yielding vs. person-level affirmation vs. Stoic assent

**Surface convergence.** MA, MG, and D all distinguish *being subject to* an appearance from *accepting* it, and all invoke the Stoic *phantasia*/*synkatathesis* contrast as a foil.

**Underlying disagreement.** Three models of acceptance: **Moss** — passive, sub-personal **"default yielding"**: the appearance is accepted because reason *fails to oppose* it (MA-R6, MG-R6); Aristotle's passions are exactly the default acceptances the Stoics *refuse* to call passions (MG-R9). **Dow** — **person-level, gradable affirmation**, constituted by the *absence of override* by a more authoritative faculty, and explicitly his *advance beyond* Moss's sub-personal acceptance (D-R5). **Both deny** it is Stoic rational assent, but for different reasons (Moss: it's non-rational; Dow: it's not a separate act).

**Evidence.** MA-R6 / MG-R6 / MG-R9 vs. D-R4 / D-R5; shared anchor *Insomn.* 459a6–8, 461b29–462a8 + Origen/SVF on Stoic assent.

**Implication.** "Taking-as-true" is *not* a single thing in this literature. The dissertation's "involuntary *positive* taking-as-true" must choose among default-yielding (Moss), person-level affirmation (Dow), and full assent — and Dow's affirmation is the model that both is positive *and* explains recalcitrance.

## 5. Do animals (and children) have *pathē*?

**Surface convergence.** All grant animals *phantasia*-driven pursuit/avoidance and agree humans differ.

**Underlying disagreement.** **The judgmental camp denies animals genuine *pathē*; the phantastic camp grants them the same basic processes.** K: animals and small children lack *pathē* "in the proper sense"; beast-uses of *orgē*/*phobos* are *analogical* (K-R3, explicitly rejecting Nussbaum). F: emotions (and *akrasia*) are denied to animals because they lack *doxa* "open to persuasive *logos*" (F-R9). Against this, **D**: human and non-human passions deploy **the same kinds of process** (common *phantasia*), differing only "by the more and the less" (D-R10); and **Moss**: animals have the phantastic value-appearances that ground appetite, lacking only the universal (MA on beasts-not-akratic).

**Evidence.** K-R3 + F-R9 (analogical/denied) vs. D-R10 + MA (same processes, by degree).

**Implication.** This *is* the dissertation's animal-*pathos* problem (diss. 1079 fn), already adjudicated four ways. It also underwrites the **two-tier** reading: a *phantasia*-based lower tier shared with animals + a *doxa*/*logos*-articulated higher tier proper to rational, *polis*-dwelling beings — exactly the dissertation's articulational-concretion split, with Konstan/Fortenbaugh manning the upper boundary and Moss/Dow the lower.

## 6. The boundary of the *pathos*-category: is grief a *pathos*?

**Surface convergence.** All treat the *Rhetoric* II catalogue (anger, fear, shame, pity, indignation, envy, *philia*…) as the paradigm list.

**Underlying disagreement.** **Konstan alone thematizes the boundary and excludes grief** (K-R4): *lupe*/*penthos* is absent from the catalogue; *lupe* is Aristotle's word for "pain," a *constituent sensation* of *pathē*, not itself a *pathos*; grief lacks the relational/intentional structure (no judgment of intentions, desert, or standing), has no answering action and no responsible agent — "the natural fact of death stands outside this system." The others' frameworks bear on this only by implication: a Moss-style *phantasia* of loss-as-bad would seem to qualify grief as affect, and a Dow-style affirmation likewise — so the exclusion is *contestable* from the phantastic side.

**Evidence.** K-R4 (the explicit exclusion); MG-R3 (a pleasurable/painful *phantasia* just *is* an evaluative cognition — which would *include* grief), in latent tension.

**Implication.** Direct warrant for the dissertation's T1-4 worry: the *Rhetoric*-catalogue model (which the dissertation universalizes) was built around *relational, action-oriented, agent-directed* emotions, and Konstan shows grief — the dissertation's own counter-case — sits at or beyond the category's edge.

## 7. The *Rhetoric* II.1 generic definition vs. the per-emotion definitions

**Surface convergence.** All use *both* the II.1 generic definition (emotions = what changes judgments, attended by pleasure/pain) and the II.2–II.11 per-emotion definitions.

**Underlying disagreement.** **Which is doing the defining?** K: the II.1 definition (by *effect on judgment*, *krisis*) is Aristotle's *real* definition of emotion (K-R2). F: the *per-emotion* definitions carry the weight (each names a constitutive thought as efficient cause); II.1 is a generic frame (F-R1). D: II.1 is read through the pleasures/pains thesis — passions *are* pleasures and pains *with* intentional content (D-R2). Moss reads II.1's judgment-change as a downstream *effect* of the phantastic appearance, not its definition.

**Evidence.** K-R2 vs. F-R1 vs. D-R2 — all on *Rhet.* II.1 1378a19–22 + the II.2ff definitions.

**Implication.** The dissertation leans hard on II.1's "judgement-modifying" clause for its *pathetic* loop (diss. 1138–1140); this cluster shows that clause is variously read as *the definition* (Konstan), a *generic frame* (Fortenbaugh), or a *symptom* (Moss) — the dissertation should say which.

## 8. Against Hume — but by which mechanism?

**Surface convergence.** F, MA, MG, D all reject the Humean division (desire sets ends, cognition finds means): evaluative cognition itself engages desire.

**Underlying disagreement.** **The anti-Humean mechanism differs by pole.** Moss: practical cognition is *in the first place* cognition of things *as good* — a *phantasia* of the good *is* a desire-inducing state (MA-R2; MG-R3). Fortenbaugh: a constitutive *belief* is the efficient cause, and virtue makes the *end* right via habituated true judgment (F-R7). Dow: evaluative *phantasmata* of the pleasant/painful have *necessary* motivational consequences regardless of belief (D-R8).

**Evidence.** MA-R2 / MG-R3 vs. F-R7 vs. D-R8.

**Implication.** Convergence on the conclusion the dissertation needs (perception is already desire-laden; diss. dual-resonance / "hedonic tonality co-given," 922–934) — but the *cluster's* preferred mechanism is the *phantastic* one (Moss/Dow), which the dissertation's own dual-resonance thesis closely matches and should cite.

---

## 9. The motivational force of recalcitrant affect: "default yielding" vs. necessary phantasmatic motivation

**Surface convergence.** MA, MG, and D all explain why a *pathos* keeps moving us against contrary judgment by appeal to *DA* III.3 429a5–8 + *de Insomniis*' "covering over" of intellect.

**Underlying disagreement.** **Moss:** the appetite wins by *impairing* intellect's contradicting-function, so the appearance is followed by *default* once nothing opposes it (MA-R5, MA-R6). **Dow:** the persisting pull is the *positive* output of evaluative *phantasmata* whose motivational consequences are **necessary** (*DMA* 8 701b33–702a2), with reason "covered over" *by degrees* — not a mere default (D-R8). Same texts, opposite emphasis (privative vs. positive).

**Evidence.** MA-R5/R6 (privative: undermining) vs. D-R8 (positive: necessary motivation); shared *Insomn.* 460b3–16, *DMA* 8.

**Implication.** This is the heart of the dissertation's *pathetic*-loop (T2-1). Note both read the *de Insomniis* mechanism as operating **within a single episode** (the appetite vies → impairs reason → one acts) — *synchronic*, not diachronic — corroborating the advisor-review's T2-1 challenge.

## 10. Is the evaluative appearance a *separate* representation, or is the pleasant *phantasia* itself the cognition-of-good?

**Surface convergence.** MA and MG agree the apparent good is not a neutral image plus a tag.

**Underlying disagreement (a refinement, MG-internal but cluster-relevant).** MG: a *pleasurable, desire-inducing phantasia* **is** an evaluative *phantasia* — there is no separate representation of "good" over and above the affectively-charged appearance (MG-R3, MG-R2). This is stronger than F's "a belief *that* it is good" and than a layered model. It coincides exactly with the dissertation's claim that hedonic tonality (*epithymia*) is *co-given* with the *aisthēma* "as a single disclosure," not added by a later operation (diss. 1038, 922–934).

**Evidence.** MG-R2/R3; convergence with diss. dual-resonance.

**Implication.** The dissertation's most defensible lower-tier claim has its closest scholarly ally in Moss — which is ironic, since Moss is its T1-2 *rival*. The dissertation should cite MG-R3 *for* its co-givenness thesis even while answering MG-R4/R5 on the *doxa*-gate.

## Bridge to the dissertation's Section 5

**A. T1-2 (the *doxa*-gate).** The cluster delivers a ready-made repair. Reading 1 shows four positions; Readings 2–4 show that the right model for "taking-as-true" is **Dow's affirmation** (positive, person-level, *weaker than conviction*, able to stand against belief), *not* the dissertation's "*doxa* = belief + *pistis* + *logos*" — which a 200 ms startle cannot satisfy. Recommended rewrite: **(i)** concede the basal tier (hedonic tonality / *resonant epithymia*) to the **phantastic** model (cite MG-R2/R3, which *matches* the dissertation's own dual-resonance, Reading 10); **(ii)** recast the higher-order civic emotions' cognitive gate as **Dovian affirmation** that, for *those* emotions, is *constitutive* rather than a "normal accompaniment" (contesting MG-R7's separability); **(iii)** name Moss as the rival, Dow/Fortenbaugh/Konstan as the allies. This converts the advisor-review's biggest vulnerability into a defended two-tier contribution.

**B. T1-4 (grief / non-realizable goods).** Reading 6 (Konstan) is the dissertation's primary witness: the *Rhetoric*-catalogue emotions are relational, agent-directed, action-ready; grief lacks all three. The dissertation should either exclude grief (with Konstan) from the catalogue it universalizes, or explicitly argue grief in via the phantastic route (Reading 6's latent tension) — but not silently treat grief as a paradigm *pathos*.

**C. Animal-*pathos* (diss. 1079 fn).** Reading 5 supplies the four-way map and underwrites the **two-tier** split directly.

**D. *toward-whom* / *Mitsein* (five-fold element 2).** Konstan (Reading 1, K-R1/R5/R7) is the classicist witness that each emotion is constitutively a social construal *toward/before* determinate others — non-Heideggerian support for what the dissertation currently carries on Heidegger alone.

**E. T2-1 (*pathetic* loop, synchronic vs. diachronic).** Reading 9: both Moss and Dow read the *de Insomniis* mechanism as a *within-episode* (synchronic) feedback, corroborating the advisor-review's challenge to the dissertation's diachronic-only assignment.

**F. The tri-identity (advisor-review T2-3).** Reading 3: Moss (MG-R5) deploys *DA* III.7 431a13–14 ("one substrate, two in being") — the dissertation's own keystone — to the *opposite* conclusion. The dissertation gains a co-deployer of the identity but must answer Moss's "belief-incapable" inference.
