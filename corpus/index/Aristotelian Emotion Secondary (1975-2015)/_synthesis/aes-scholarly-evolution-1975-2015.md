# Scholarly Evolution of the Cluster, 1975–2015

A chronological trajectory of the debate the cluster reconstructs: how the question "what cognition is constitutive of a *pathos*?" moved from **belief** (1975) through **social judgment** (2006) and a **phantastic challenge** (2009–2012) to an **affirmation reconciliation** (2015). Every move is anchored to a unit; nothing here is conjecture beyond the units' own positioning.

## 1975 — Fortenbaugh, *Aristotle on Emotion*: the cognitivist founding
Fortenbaugh sets the terms. Against the Platonic legacy that treats emotion as a non-rational disturbance, he argues that Aristotle's decisive advance was to build a **thought/belief into the *essential definition* of each emotion as its efficient cause** (anger = pain *on account of* an apparent unjust slight). Because the cognition is *constitutive*, emotions become intelligible, teachable, persuasion-responsive, and the proper matter of rhetoric, poetics, and a bipartite (alogical/logical) ethical psychology. Crucially for the cluster's later development, Fortenbaugh frames the constitutive cognition as ***doxa*/*krisis*** and **never deploys *phantasia* as a category** — he reads every *phainomenon* in the *Rhetoric* definitions simply as *the thought that p*. He thus occupies the doxastic extreme and leaves *un-theorized* exactly the gap (appearing-vs-believing) that the later debate will prise open.

*(Between 1975 and 2006 the wider debate developed through interlocutors the cluster repeatedly cites — Nussbaum's *De Motu* commentary (1978) and *Upheavals of Thought* (2001), Cooper, Sorabji (2000), Striker, and the *phantasia* commentators (Schofield, Modrak, Frede) — setting up both the social-emotions turn and the *phantasia* turn.)*

## 2006 — Konstan, *The Emotions of the Ancient Greeks*: the classics / social-cognitive turn
Konstan relocates the question from philosophy-of-mind into classics and the history of emotions. He preserves the **cognitivist core** — *pathē* are acts of **judgment**, not states of excitation — but reframes the judgment as **social and relational**: each emotion is a construal of another agent's act or intention as it bears on one's **standing** (anger answers a *slight*, *oligoria*, by those "not fit to slight one"; pity requires "distance"; shame is loss of esteem before a community). Two consequences resonate through the cluster: (i) because emotions are construals, they are **changeable by persuasion**, and (ii) **animals and small children lack *pathē* "in the proper sense"** (beast-uses are *analogical*) — Konstan explicitly **rejects Nussbaum's** ascription of emotion to animals. He sides with Andronicus's *hupolēpsis* over Aspasius's bare *phantasia*, keeping him on the judgmental side, and — uniquely — polices the category's boundary, arguing **grief is not a *pathos*** at all.

## 2009 — Moss, "Akrasia and Perceptual Illusion": the phantastic challenge opens
Moss launches the non-judgmental turn from an unexpected angle: *akrasia*. Reading *DA* III.9–10, she argues that the cognition which *moves* the akratic — the appetite's object — is a **non-doxastic *phantasia*-appearance** of the pleasant *as good*, which can run **contrary to belief**, exactly as the sun *appears* a foot wide though believed vast (*DA* III.3 428b; *EE* VII.2 1235b26–29). The motivating evaluative cognition is therefore *phantasia*, not *doxa*; following it is **"default yielding,"** not assent. This directly threatens the Fortenbaugh–Konstan settlement: if what moves us in conflict cases is appearance, the constitutive cognition of desire/affect need not be belief.

## 2012 — Moss, *Aristotle on the Apparent Good*: the phantastic account generalized
Moss generalizes the 2009 result into a full theory and names the dispute. The book's Ch. 4 — **"Passions and the Apparent Good," §4.1 "Doxastic vs. phantastic accounts of the passions"** — argues the passions *quite generally* are based on **literal *phantasiai*, not beliefs**: the passionate part of the soul (*pathētikon = orektikon = aisthētikon = phantastikon*, *DA* III.7 431a13–14) exercises perception and *phantasia* but is **incapable of belief**. Her **accepting-vs-believing** distinction (§4.6) makes "acceptance" a genus covering both rational assent and the non-rational part's *default* acceptance, so emotion requires acceptance *without* belief; and her thesis-(b) — that a *phantasia* normally *triggers* a corresponding belief — quietly installs a **two-tier architecture** (phantastic basis + doxastic accompaniment). This is the cluster's fully-developed non-judgmental pole and the dissertation's named centerpiece rival.

## 2015 — Dow, *Passions and Persuasion*: the affirmation reconciliation
Dow closes the arc by occupying the middle and, notably, **correcting himself** (he had defended a "mere appearance" reading in Dow 2009). Retaining the *phantasia*-basis (passions are pleasures/pains exercising *phantasia*, not *doxa*), he argues that the subject **herself affirms** *phantasia*'s evaluative content as the way things are — a **person-level, gradable affirmation** that is *weaker than belief/conviction* yet positive enough to let the arousal of passion supply genuine grounds for *pistis* (rhetorical proof). He explicitly positions this as his **advance beyond Moss's sub-personal "acceptance"** (Ch. 10 n.48), and inverts Moss's use of the sun-illusion via a **disanalogy** argument: recalcitrant emotions, *unlike* the sun-case, *are* conflicted, which shows they carry subject-level affirmation. Recalcitrant passions become genuine, explicable *malfunctions* rather than counterexamples.

## The arc, and its lesson for the dissertation
**belief (1975) → social judgment (2006) → appearance (2009–2012) → affirmation (2015).** The cluster's own trajectory converges on **affirmation** as the term that holds the positive, "taking-as-true" intuition of the judgmental camp *together with* the recalcitrance-explaining power of the phantastic camp. That is precisely the resource the dissertation needs for Section 5's T1-2: not "belief + conviction + *logos*" (Fortenbaugh's extreme, which a startle cannot satisfy) and not bare appearance (Moss, which dissolves the gate), but **Dovian affirmation over a phantastic basis**, pitched as *constitutive* for the higher civic emotions and as a *normal accompaniment* for the basal ones. The 40-year debate effectively pre-builds the dissertation's two-tier repair.
