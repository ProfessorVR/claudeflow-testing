# De Anima: Cross-Pipeline Hooks

**Date**: 2026-03-09
**Source work**: Aristotle, *De Anima*, trans. J. A. Smith (Barnes ed.)
**Target pipelines**: BCAP (Heidegger GA 18), B&T (Heidegger *Being and Time*), Uexkull (*Foray into the Worlds of Animals and Humans*), Rickert (*Ambient Rhetoric*)

All hooks are tagged **[INTERP-high]** unless otherwise noted. These are synthetic connections requiring interpretive framing; they are not claimed as textual parallels.

## Hook 1: ψυχή (DA) → ζωή πρακτική (BCAP)

**DA source**: Soul is the first actuality of a natural organized body (412a27); soul is cause in three senses -- efficient, final, formal (415b8--28); the faculty hierarchy orders psychic powers serially, with each higher faculty presupposing the lower (414b20--32).

**BCAP target**: Heidegger reads *psuchē* not as "consciousness" but as "the doctrine of the being of living things" (BCAP C4: ψυχή = "ontologically basic determination of living"). The *entelecheia hē prōtē* formula is interpreted as the being-character of the living, where *zōē* is a mode of being-in-a-world (*ζῷον λόγον ἔχον* presupposes *ζωή*, BCAP P3). Heidegger's ψυχή is closer to *ζωή πρακτική* -- practical life, the soul as it operates in the domain of *praxis* and *hexis* -- than to any theoretical or biological reading.

**Bridge structure**: DA's faculty hierarchy (nutritive → sensitive → appetitive → locomotive → intellectual) maps onto BCAP's ontological interest in the *praktikē tou logon echontos* (the practical exercise of the being that has logos). Where DA treats the hierarchy as a serial biological ordering, BCAP reads it as an ontological *Aufbau* in which each level discloses a richer mode of being-in-the-world. The DA II.4 doctrine that soul is the final cause of the body (415b16--18: "nature acts for an end") becomes in BCAP the claim that the being of the living is essentially *teleological* -- not in a biological-functional sense but in the existential sense of being-toward-an-end.

**Dissertation relevance**: This hook grounds the argument that Aristotle's psychology is already proto-existential in its teleological structure. The faculty hierarchy is not merely a taxonomy of biological capacities but an account of how the living being is disclosed to itself through progressively richer modes of engagement with its world. [INTERP-high]

## Hook 2: φαντασία (DA) → Erschlossenheit / Being-in-the-World (B&T)

**DA source**: Phantasia is a movement resulting from actual sense (429a1--2); it is neither perception nor opinion nor knowledge (427b14--428b10); it persists in the absence of the sensed object and guides animal action (429a4--8). The soul never thinks without a phantasma (431a17). Sensitive imagination (all animals) vs. deliberative imagination (calculative animals only, 434a5--10).

**B&T target**: Heidegger's *Erschlossenheit* (disclosedness) is the equiprimordial structure of *Befindlichkeit* (attunement), *Verstehen* (understanding), and *Rede* (discourse) through which Dasein is always already "outside" alongside entities (B&T section 28, H.132--134). The "cabinet model" of mind -- where representations are stored inside a subject and then projected outward -- is precisely what Heidegger rejects.

**Bridge structure**: DA III.8's thesis that "the soul is in a way all existing things" (432a1--3) is a locus Heidegger repeatedly engages. Both DA's phantasia and B&T's *Erschlossenheit* deny the representationalist model: for Aristotle, the soul does not contain copies of things but *becomes* its objects through formal reception; for Heidegger, Dasein is not enclosed in a mental space but is always already alongside the things it encounters. Phantasia's intermediate position between *aisthēsis* and *nous* -- causally derived from sense yet functionally autonomous, operating in the absence of the object -- structurally parallels the way *Erschlossenheit* operates prior to the theoretical attitude: it is the pre-thematic disclosure of a meaningful world that is neither pure sensory input nor fully articulated thought.

The distinction between sensitive and deliberative imagination (434a5--10) can be mapped onto the B&T distinction between pre-thematic circumspective concern (*Umsicht*, section 15) and explicit thematic apprehension (*Erkennen*, section 13). Sensitive imagination is the animal's being-alongside-entities prior to any deliberative stance; deliberative imagination introduces the "measuring by a single standard" that corresponds to the shift into theoretical comportment.

**Dissertation relevance**: This hook is load-bearing for the dissertation's central argument. If phantasia is read not as "mental imagery" (the cabinet model) but as the pre-theoretical disclosure of a world of meaningful appearances, then the Aristotelian psychology already contains the structural resources that Heidegger will radicalize as *Erschlossenheit*. The gap between DA and B&T is not between a naive realism and a phenomenological hermeneutics but between two different articulations of the same structural insight: the living being is always already *outside itself*, in the midst of a meaningful world. [INTERP-high]

## Hook 3: αἴσθησις (DA) → Befindlichkeit (B&T)

**DA source**: Perception is a transition from potentiality to actuality through the agency of an external object (II.5, 416b33--418a6); it is a *paschein* (being-affected) that is not genuine alteration but the "development into its true self" (417b2--16); the sense receives form without matter (424a17--28); the sense-organ is a mean (*mesotēs*) between contraries (424a2--7). Touch grounds intelligence: "man is the most intelligent of all animals" because touch is most discriminating (421a22--26).

**B&T target**: *Befindlichkeit* (attunement, state-of-mind, findingness) is the existential through which Dasein is disclosed to itself as always already thrown into a situation (B&T section 29, H.134--140). Moods are not internal states caused by external objects but modes of being-in-the-world that disclose the world in particular ways. The analysis of *Furcht* (fear, section 30) is directly connected to Aristotle's *Rhetoric* II analysis of *phobos*.

**Bridge structure**: The critical parallel lies in the *receptive* character of both *aisthēsis* and *Befindlichkeit*. DA II.5's insistence that perception is a *paschein* -- a being-affected from without -- that is nonetheless not a destruction but a "preservation and development" (417b2--7) structurally anticipates Heidegger's claim that *Befindlichkeit* is a *Geworfenheit* (thrownness) that is simultaneously disclosive. In both cases, the being is *affected by* its world in a way that reveals the world's character. The wax-signet analogy (424a17--28) can be read as an early attempt to articulate what Heidegger calls the "world-entry" (*Welteingang*) of beings into the space of meaning.

The *mesotēs* doctrine adds a further dimension: the sense-organ is already attuned to a specific range of contraries (hot/cold, white/black) by being a mean between them. This structural attunement -- the organ must be "neither hot nor cold" in order to discriminate heat and cold -- parallels the way *Stimmung* (mood) in B&T pre-structures the range of what can show up as significant. Neither the Aristotelian sense-organ nor Heideggerian Dasein encounters a "raw" given; both are always already calibrated to a field of contraries or significances.

The touch-intelligence thesis (421a22--26) strengthens this hook: if intelligence is grounded in the refinement of embodied sensory discrimination, then the embodied, world-directed character of perception is not opposed to but *constitutive of* cognitive capacity -- a thesis that runs parallel to Heidegger's insistence that theoretical cognition is a deficient mode of circumspective concern, not its foundation.

**Dissertation relevance**: This hook supports the argument that *aisthēsis* is proto-attunement: an embodied, world-directed, receptive mode of being that discloses the environment's character through the organism's own structural calibration. The transition from DA to B&T is not a leap from naturalism to phenomenology but an intensification of the same insight. [INTERP-high]

## Hook 4: φάντασμα (DA) → Merkbild (Uexkull)

**DA source**: The *phantasma* is the product of phantasia that persists in sense-organs after sensation ceases (428a1); it serves the thinking soul as quasi-perceptual content (431a17, 432a8--10); animals "in their actions are largely guided by" imaginations that "remain in the organs of sense and resemble sensations" (429a4--8). Sensitive imagination is found in all animals; in imperfect animals it is "indefinite" (434a5--7).

**Uexkull target**: The *Merkbild* (perceptual image / cue-image) is the organism-side correlate of the *Merkmal* (perceptual cue-carrier) in Uexkull's functional circle (*Funktionskreis*). The *Merkbild* is not a passive copy of external reality but a species-specific perceptual construction that constitutes the organism's *Umwelt* (environment-world). The *Suchbild* (search image) extends this: the organism actively projects a perceptual template onto its environment, recognizing only what matches the template. The *Merkwelt* (perceptual world) is the totality of *Merkbilder* available to a given organism.

**Bridge structure**: The structural parallel is precise: both *phantasma* and *Merkbild* are organism-internal perceptual residues that guide action in the absence of current stimulation. Aristotle's claim that brutes "are largely guided by" phantasmata "because of the non-existence of thought" (429a5--7) maps directly onto Uexkull's account of how animals navigate their *Umwelt* through species-specific *Merkbilder* rather than through anything resembling conceptual thought. The DA distinction between sensitive imagination (all animals, indefinite in imperfect animals) and deliberative imagination (calculative animals only) parallels Uexkull's distinction between organisms with simple, rigidly determined *Funktionskreise* (the tick's three-cue world) and organisms with complex, flexible perceptual worlds (the dog's rich *Merkwelt*).

The key divergence is ontological: for Aristotle, the *phantasma* is a residual movement *in the sense-organ* -- a physiological trace whose formal character derives from the sensible object. For Uexkull, the *Merkbild* is a species-specific construction that does not "receive" an external form but *constitutes* the object as a cue-carrier within the organism's *Umwelt*. The Uexkullian organism is not a passive recipient of form (contra the wax-signet model) but an active projector of *Bedeutung* (meaning). This divergence is productive: it marks the transition from Aristotelian form-reception to the biosemiotic paradigm where meaning is an organism-environment relation, not a property of objects.

**Dissertation relevance**: This hook supports the argument that phantasia is the Aristotelian ancestor of the biosemiotic *Merkbild* -- but with a crucial ontological difference. Where Aristotle's phantasma retains the form-reception structure of aisthēsis (it is a kinēsis *from* actual sense), Uexkull's Merkbild is constitutive rather than derivative. The dissertation can use this divergence to argue that the ambient-rhetorical framework requires a move beyond Aristotelian form-reception toward a more radically relational account of perceptual meaning -- a move that Heidegger's *Befindlichkeit* and Uexkull's *Umwelt* theory independently accomplish. [INTERP-high]

## Hook 5: ὄρεξις + φαντασία → κίνησις (DA) → Ambient Rhetoric (Rickert)

**DA source**: Animal movement originates from appetite (*orexis*) together with practical thought or imagination (433a9--b30). The good (real or apparent) moves as unmoved mover; the faculty of appetite moves as moved mover; the animal is the thing moved. Appetite cannot function without imagination (433b28--29). Phantasia produces affective responses (pleasure/pain → pursuit/avoidance, 431a8--12).

**Rickert target**: Rickert's concept of "ambient rhetoric" -- rhetoric as environmentally embedded and affectively attuned -- draws on exactly this circuit. Rhetorical situations "move" bodies prior to deliberative judgement through the affective-imaginative complex that DA III describes.

**Bridge structure**: The DA III.9--11 circuit (phantasia → orexis → kinēsis) is the Aristotelian mechanism by which appearances produce bodily movement through the mediation of desire. This is the core of what Rickert calls "ambient persuasion": the environment (rhetorically structured) presents appearances (phantasmata) that activate appetitive responses (orexis) and produce movement (kinēsis) -- all prior to or beneath the level of deliberative judgement. The DA III.3 insight that imagination produces affective responses "only under belief conditions" (427b21--24) is relevant to Rickert's account of how rhetorical situations establish the belief-conditions under which environmental cues become persuasive.

The distinction between sensitive and deliberative imagination (434a5--10) maps onto Rickert's distinction between ambient rhetoric (which operates through sensitive imagination -- pre-deliberative, environmental, affective) and classical rhetoric (which operates through deliberative imagination -- argument, evidence, explicit persuasion). DA thus provides the psychological infrastructure for the ambient/classical distinction.

**Dissertation relevance**: This hook establishes that the phantasia-orexis-kinēsis circuit of DA III is the Aristotelian foundation for Rickert's ambient rhetoric. The dissertation can argue that Aristotle's own psychology already contains the resources for understanding rhetoric as a bodily, environmental, pre-deliberative phenomenon -- not as a later addition from the phenomenological tradition. [INTERP-high]

## Hook 6: αἴσθησις as Form-Reception (DA) → Weltarmut / Weltbildend (GA 29/30 via Uexkull)

**DA source**: Sense receives form without matter (424a17--28); plants lack perception because they have "no mean" and are affected by forms *with* matter (424b1--3). The distinction between genuine perceptual reception and mere physical alteration is the criterion separating animal from plant.

**Uexkull/Heidegger target**: Heidegger's *Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics* (GA 29/30) takes up Uexkull's *Umwelt* theory to develop the tripartite thesis: the stone is *weltlos* (worldless), the animal is *weltarm* (poor in world), the human is *weltbildend* (world-forming). The animal is "poor in world" because it is captivated (*benommen*) by its environmental cues without access to beings *as* beings.

**Bridge structure**: DA's distinction between plant (affected by forms with matter = no perception) and animal (receives forms without matter = genuine perception) is a precursor to Heidegger's stone/animal distinction. The Aristotelian criterion is formal: the capacity to receive form *qua* form, without material alteration. The Heideggerian criterion is ontological: the capacity to encounter beings in their being. But both mark the same structural threshold -- the point at which an organism's relation to its environment becomes more than mere physical causation. The DA plant/animal distinction maps onto the *weltlos/weltarm* threshold; the DA animal/human distinction (sensitive vs. deliberative imagination, 434a5--10) maps onto the *weltarm/weltbildend* threshold.

**Dissertation relevance**: This hook positions the wax-signet principle as the Aristotelian version of the *Weltbildung* threshold. The capacity to receive form without matter is the capacity to have a world -- even if, for animals, that world is limited to the *Umwelt* of species-specific cue-responses. [INTERP-high]
