# Rhetoric: Cross-Pipeline Hooks

**Date**: 2026-03-09
**Source**: Phase 3 synthesis of RHET-01 through RHET-06.
**Convention**: All hooks tagged [INTERP-high] per Phase 3 requirements. These represent interpretive connections requiring scholarly argument, not textually explicit claims.

## 1. πάθος (Rhetoric) --> Befindlichkeit (BCAP and B&T)

**Tag**: [INTERP-high]
**Rhetoric locus**: II.1--II.11 (1377b--1388b), especially 1378a20: "All those feelings that so change men as to affect their judgements."
**BCAP target**: U-FP3a--c (GA 18, SS 16--21). Heidegger's 1924 lectures read Aristotle's πάθη not as subjective psychological states but as modes of disclosive attunement (Befindlichkeit). The three-headed analysis of each emotion (state of mind / persons toward whom / grounds) becomes the structural template for Heidegger's claim that the Rhetoric is "the first systematic hermeneutic of the everydayness of Being-with-one-another."
**B&T target**: BT-D1-U5 (SS 29--30, H.134--142). The Befindlichkeit analysis in B&T SS 29 explicitly refers back to Rhetoric II as the most penetrating interpretation of affects in the Western tradition. The Rhetoric's claim that emotions are judgement-altering and attended by pain/pleasure anticipates B&T's insistence that Stimmung is not merely subjective but discloses the factical situation of Dasein.
**Specific hook**: The Rhetoric's twelve-emotion catalogue (anger, calm, friendship, enmity, fear, confidence, shame, kindness, pity, indignation, envy, emulation) maps onto Heidegger's existential analytic as a pre-ontological inventory of Being-in-the-world. Each emotion's three-headed structure (state/person/grounds) corresponds to what Heidegger will call the existential structure of Befindlichkeit: it always discloses (a) how one finds oneself (Sich-befinden), (b) one's being-with-others (Mitsein), and (c) the factical situation (Faktizitat).

## 2. φόβος (Rhetoric II.5) --> Furcht / Angst (B&T SS 30, 40)

**Tag**: [INTERP-high]
**Rhetoric locus**: II.5 (1382a20--1383a15). Fear defined as "pain or disturbance due to imagining (φαντασία) some destructive or painful evil in the future." Key conditions: fear must be near at hand, not remote; the person must retain a faint expectation of escape.
**B&T target**: BT-D1-U5 (SS 30, H.140--142). Heidegger's analysis of Furcht (fear) in B&T SS 30 explicitly draws on Rhetoric II.5's threefold structure: (1) the before-which of fear (das Wovor: the threatening entity), (2) fearing as such (das Furchten: the affective disclosure), (3) the about-which of fear (das Worum: Dasein itself). This maps onto Aristotle's three heads: grounds / the emotion itself / persons toward whom.
**BCAP target**: U-FP3c (SS 19). Heidegger reads φόβος as disclosing the futural character of being-there: fear is always ahead-of-itself.
**Specific hook**: The Rhetoric's requirement that fear involve φαντασία (imagination of future evil) bridges to the B&T distinction between Furcht (fear of an intraworldly entity) and Angst (anxiety before Being-in-the-world as such). Aristotle's "faint expectation of escape" condition corresponds to Heidegger's observation that Furcht discloses Dasein as endangered but still capable of fleeing -- in contrast to Angst, which has no determinate "before-which." The transition from Aristotelian φόβος to Heideggerian Angst passes through the removal of the determinate object.

## 3. ἦθος as πίστις (Rhetoric I.2, II.1) --> Heidegger's Existential Reading of ἦθος (BCAP)

**Tag**: [INTERP-high]
**Rhetoric locus**: I.2 (1356a1--13): ethos must be produced by the speech itself, not prior reputation. II.1 (1378a5--15): three sources of trust -- φρόνησις, ἀρετή, εὔνοια.
**BCAP target**: U-FP3d--e. Heidegger reads ἦθος not as moral character in the conventional sense but as the speaker's "way of being-disposed" (Weisedesgestelltseins). The three sources of trust become existential rather than ethical categories: φρόνησις = circumspective insight, ἀρετή = having one's being-possibility at one's disposal, εὔνοια = attunement to the other's situation.
**Specific hook**: The Rhetoric's insistence that ethos be "achieved by what the speaker says" (1356a8--10) is radicalized by Heidegger: ἦθος is not a character-trait possessed by a subject but a mode of disclosive comportment enacted in λόγος. This connects to the B&T doctrine that Dasein "is" its disclosure (SS 28--29).

## 4. λέξις / μεταφορά (Rhetoric III) --> Rickert's Ambient Rhetoric

**Tag**: [INTERP-high]
**Rhetoric locus**: III.2 (1404b--1405b): metaphor gives "clearness, charm, and distinction"; III.7 (1408a10): appropriateness "makes people believe in the truth of your story."
**Rickert target**: Rickert chapters on "Ambience" and "Attunement" (Phase 2 units from Rickert pipeline). Rickert's redefinition of rhetoric as "ambient" challenges the speaker-centered, intentional model of persuasion. Aristotle's λέξις doctrine, however, contains a latent ambientism: the principle that art must conceal itself (1404b18--19) and that naturalness is persuasive means that the most effective rhetoric operates below the threshold of conscious recognition. Appropriate language works not by making an argument explicit but by attuning the audience to a situation.
**Specific hook**: Rickert argues that rhetoric is not fundamentally a matter of conscious persuasion between speaker and audience but of environmental attunement. Aristotle's τὸ πρέπον (appropriateness) doctrine -- where language that "corresponds to its subject" produces belief without the audience recognizing the mechanism (1408a20--23) -- provides the ancient prototype for ambient persuasion. The "false conclusion" the audience draws (believing the speaker trustworthy because his language fits) is exactly the kind of non-deliberate, environment-level influence Rickert theorizes.

## 5. πρὸ ὀμμάτων ποιεῖν (Rhetoric III.10--11) --> φαντασία (DA III.3) --> Erschlossenheit (B&T)

**Tag**: [INTERP-high]
**Rhetoric locus**: III.10--11 (1410b33--1412a10). "Setting before the eyes" through ἐνέργεια: metaphor that represents things "in a state of activity." Homer paradigmatic.
**DA target**: DA III.3 (DA-04). φαντασία as the psychological faculty that mediates between αἴσθησις (perception) and νοῦς (thought). The Rhetoric's πρὸ ὀμμάτων ποιεῖν activates the audience's φαντασία -- it produces quasi-perceptual experience through language alone.
**B&T target**: BT-D1-U4 (SS 28--29). Erschlossenheit (disclosedness) as the fundamental structure of Dasein's Being-in-the-world. The Rhetoric's doctrine that metaphor makes the audience "see" is a pre-ontological description of what Heidegger will call the disclosedness achieved through Rede (discourse).
**Specific hook**: The chain: μεταφορά --> πρὸ ὀμμάτων ποιεῖν --> ἐνέργεια --> φαντασία traces the mechanism by which language discloses world. Aristotle holds that metaphor works by transferring the structure of activity (ἐνέργεια) to the audience's imagination (φαντασία), producing quasi-perceptual "seeing." This is a rhetorical-poetic account of what Heidegger describes as the disclosive function of λόγος. The Rhetoric does not treat this as mere ornament but as the highest achievement of style -- the moment where language stops describing and starts showing. This connects to Heidegger's Aufweisung (pointing-out) as the proper mode of phenomenological λόγος (B&T SS 7).

## 6. μεταφορά and αἴσθησις (Rhetoric III.2) --> Rickert's Suasive Materiality

**Tag**: [INTERP-high]
**Rhetoric locus**: III.2 (1405b18--20): "The materials of metaphor must be beautiful to the ear, to the understanding, to the eye or some other physical sense."
**Rickert target**: Rickert's chapters on materiality and the non-human dimensions of rhetoric. The Rhetoric's insistence that metaphor must be grounded in sensory experience -- "beautiful to the ear, to the understanding, to the eye or some other physical sense" -- locates rhetorical effect not in abstract argument but in perceptual experience. This is the ancient anchor for Rickert's claim that rhetoric operates through material, sensory, and environmental channels, not only through propositional content.
**Specific hook**: Aristotle grounds metaphor in αἴσθησις (perception), not merely in νοῦς (intellect). The "intuitive perception of the similarity in dissimilars" (Poetics 1459a7--8) that metaphor requires is a perceptual-cognitive act, not a purely logical one. Rickert's argument that rhetoric is "ambient" -- operating through sensory environments rather than conscious argumentative exchange -- can be read as radicalizing this Aristotelian insight: if metaphor already works through sensory channels, then rhetoric was never purely "propositional" in the first place.

## 7. ῥητορική as τέχνη --> Uexkull's Naturtechnik

**Tag**: [INTERP-high]
**Rhetoric locus**: I.1--2 (1354a1--1356a25). Rhetoric defined as a τέχνη: a systematic, teachable art. Yet its supreme instrument (metaphor) cannot be taught (1405a9), and its highest principle demands art conceal itself (1404b18). The tension between rhetoric as τέχνη and its dependence on nature (φύσις) is constitutive.
**Uexkull target**: Uexkull's concept of Naturtechnik (natural technology / technique of nature) from *A Foray into the Worlds of Animals and Humans* (Phase 2 units from Uexkull pipeline). Uexkull argues that organisms exhibit "plan-conformity" (Planmassigkeit) that mirrors but does not derive from conscious design -- a technique of nature that operates without a technician.
**Specific hook**: Aristotle's rhetoric occupies the boundary between τέχνη and φύσις. Rhetoric is a τέχνη (systematic, teachable), but its highest operation -- metaphor -- depends on natural genius that cannot be taught, and its highest principle demands that τέχνη appear as φύσις (naturalness). Uexkull's Naturtechnik names an analogous boundary: biological organisms exhibit plan-conformity that looks like τέχνη but operates through φύσις. The rhetorical speaker who conceals art behind naturalness enacts, at the human level, what Uexkull describes at the biological level: a technique that works by appearing natural. This connection runs through the concept of Umwelt (functional environment): the rhetor shapes the audience's perceptual environment just as the organism's body-plan shapes its Merkwelt (perceptual world).

## 8. φιλία (Rhetoric II.4) --> Miteinandersein (BCAP)

**Tag**: [INTERP-high]
**Rhetoric locus**: II.4 (1380b35--1382a15). Friendship defined as "wishing good for someone for their sake, with reciprocity." Parallels NE VIII.2.
**BCAP target**: U-SP1. Heidegger discusses Aristotle's φιλία in connection with Miteinandersein (being-with-one-another) and λόγος. Friendship is not a psychological bond between two subjects but a mode of shared world-disclosure.
**Specific hook**: The Rhetoric's treatment of φιλία is embedded in the πάθη catalogue (II.4), not in the ethical treatises. This placement reveals that friendship, for the purposes of rhetoric, is an emotional disposition that can be produced, strengthened, or undermined through speech. Heidegger reads this as evidence that even the most apparently "personal" affect (friendship) is constituted in and through λόγος -- it is a mode of being-with, not a private sentiment.
