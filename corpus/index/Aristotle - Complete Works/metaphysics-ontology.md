## 2. Internal Trajectory

The Metaphysics develops its key themes across 14 Books in a cumulative but non-linear progression:

1. **Books Alpha and alpha (I-II)**: The programme is established. Wisdom is knowledge of first causes; philosophy begins in wonder. The four-cause scheme (material, formal, efficient, final) is introduced and confirmed by a survey of predecessors who grasped causes only partially. Causes must be finite in series. Method must match subject matter.

2. **Book Beta (III)**: The aporetic agenda. Fifteen puzzles set the programme for the central books: Is there one science or many for the causes? Are genera or elements principles? Are being and unity substances? Are universals knowable but not substantial? These aporiai structure everything that follows.

3. **Book Gamma (IV)**: The foundational breakthrough. First philosophy is defined as the science of being qua being. The focal meaning (*pros hen*) structure shows how multivocal "being" can be studied by a single science — all senses refer to substance. PNC is established as the firmest principle and defended elenchically.

4. **Book Delta (V)**: The philosophical lexicon. Thirty key terms are disambiguated, mapping the conceptual vocabulary presupposed by the central books. The entries on substance (two primary senses: substratum and form), being (four senses), and capacity (active/passive) are foundational.

5. **Books Epsilon + Zeta i-iii (VI + VII.1-3)**: Narrowing the inquiry. The senses of being are sorted: accidental being and being-as-truth are dismissed from first philosophy. Substance's primacy is established (in formula, knowledge, and time). Four candidates for substance are identified: essence, universal, genus, substratum.

6. **Book Zeta iv-xvii (VII.4-17)**: The heart of the Metaphysics. Essence belongs primarily to substance. Each primary thing is identical with its essence. Form is not generated — only the composite comes into being. No universal is substance. The genus is matter relative to the differentia. **Conclusion: substance is the formal cause — the "why" that makes matter a determinate "this."**

7. **Books Eta + Theta (VIII-IX)**: The actuality-potentiality framework. The unity of definition is resolved: matter and form are one and the same, the one potentially, the other actually. Potentiality is systematically defined. Actuality is defined by analogy. The kinesis/energeia distinction separates complete actualities from incomplete movements. Actuality is prior to potentiality in formula, time, and substance. Being in the strictest sense is truth.

8. **Books Iota + Kappa (X-XI)**: Unity, contrariety, and recapitulation. Unity is not a substance but follows the categories like being. Contrariety = complete privation = the greatest difference within a genus. Perishable and imperishable differ in kind (against Platonic Forms). Kappa recapitulates earlier arguments and the Physics definition of movement.

9. **Book Lambda (XII)**: The theological culmination. Three kinds of substance are distinguished. The argument for an eternal unmoved mover: movement is eternal, therefore its cause must be purely actual. The mover produces motion by being loved — as object of desire and thought. God's life is thought thinking itself (*noesis noeseos noesis*). "On such a principle depend the heavens and the world of nature."

10. **Books Mu + Nu (XIII-XIV)**: The anti-Platonist conclusion. Mathematical objects exist by abstraction, not separately. The theory of Forms is refuted again (third man, causal impotence, participation as empty metaphor). Ideal number theory is dismantled. The One is a measure, not a substance. The relative (great-and-small) cannot be a material principle. Numbers are not causes.

## 4. Work-Level Tensions (De-duplicated)

### T1: Form as universal vs. form as individual
**Locus**: Z.viii vs. Z.xiii
Z.xiii argues no universal can be substance. Z.viii shows form is not generated (man generates man — same in form). If the form of man is numerically the same across all humans, it looks universal; if numerically distinct, what individuates it? This tension is never fully resolved.

### T2: The composite as paradigm substance vs. form as primary substance
**Locus**: Categories vs. Z.xvii, Z.xv
The Categories treats individual composites (Callias, this horse) as primary substance. Zeta shifts primacy to form as the substance of each thing. The concrete composite is the *tode ti* par excellence yet is dismissed as "posterior" (Z.iii) and perishable (Z.xv). The relationship between form-substance and composite-substance is left in productive tension.

### T3: Matter as unknowable vs. matter as indispensable
**Locus**: Z.x vs. Z.vii-ix
"Matter is unknowable in itself" (1036a9), and the formula of the form does not include material parts. Yet generation requires matter as that from which things come to be. The epistemological opacity of matter and its ontological indispensability are never reconciled.

### T4: First philosophy as both particular and universal
**Locus**: E.1, 1026a23-32
First philosophy is universal *because* it is first — it deals with one particular kind of substance (immovable, eternal) and this priority makes it universal. How a science of one genus can be the universal science of being qua being is programmatic rather than fully resolved.

### T5: Actuality defined only by analogy
**Locus**: Theta.6, 1048a35-37
Aristotle explicitly says we must "be content to grasp the analogy" — actuality resists genus-species definition. The priority thesis (Theta.8) rests on this analogically defined concept. Whether *energeia* has specifiable content or is a proto-concept graspable only through instances is left open.

### T6: One unmoved mover vs. 55 movers
**Locus**: Lambda.7 vs. Lambda.8
Chapter 7 argues for a single first principle; chapter 8 introduces 55 (or 49) unmoved substances. Whether the subsidiary movers are dependent on, co-ordinate with, or exemplary of the single supreme mover is never fully resolved.

### T7: God's self-thinking vs. knowledge of the world
**Locus**: Lambda.9 vs. Lambda.10
If God thinks only himself, God has no cognition of the world he orders. Yet the good of the universe depends on God as both separate principle and immanent order. How a self-thinking god can govern worldly order without cognizing it is left unresolved.

### T8: PNC as indemonstrable yet elenchically defended
**Locus**: Gamma.3-4
Aristotle insists PNC cannot be demonstrated, yet proceeds to "demonstrate" it elenchically. Whether elenctic argument genuinely establishes PNC or merely silences opponents remains contested.

### T9: Perishable and imperishable differ in kind
**Locus**: Iota.10 vs. Lambda.1
Iota.10 argues that perishable and imperishable must differ in kind (ruling out common Forms). But Lambda.1 classifies all three substance-types under a single inquiry. Whether they share a genus "analogically" is a point of cross-book tension.

### T10: Abstraction vs. real mathematical structure
**Locus**: Mu.3
Mathematics treats objects by abstraction (not separate entities), yet order, symmetry, and definiteness are "causes of many things" (1078b1). If mathematical properties are only abstracted, their genuine causal status is in question.
