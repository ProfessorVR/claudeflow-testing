## Section 3C: Tension Layer

1. **The transparent as universal medium vs. element-specific media** (SS-01, chs. 2-3): Aristotle insists the transparent is a "common nature and power" not peculiar to any element (439a25-b5), yet he also correlates specific sense organs with specific elements (sight = water, sound = air, smell = fire, touch = earth) at 438b30-439a5. Whether the transparent genuinely transcends the elements or is simply a shared property of water/air remains unresolved. [INTERP-low]

2. **Mixture vs. juxtaposition and the status of numerical ratios** (SS-01, ch. 3): Colours involving numerical ratios (3:2, 3:4) are said to be most agreeable, analogous to musical concords (440a1-5), but some ratios are acknowledged as incommensurable (440a28-30). Whether all colours reduce to ratios, and whether the concord analogy is structural or illustrative, is left open. [INTERP-low]

3. **Seven species — empirical or Pythagorean?** (SS-01, ch. 3-4): Aristotle asserts seven species of colour and seven of savour (442a18-25), paralleling the musical scale. Whether this is empirical observation, Pythagorean inheritance, or structural requirement of the ratio-theory is not made explicit. [INTERP-low]

4. **The unity of the perceptual faculty** (SS-02, ch. 7): The faculty is described as "numerically one" but "different in being" (449a5-20), analogous to a point serving as boundary of two lines. How a single faculty can be simultaneously unified and differentiated — and what this means for the phenomenology of cross-modal perception — is a persistent difficulty. [INTERP-med]
