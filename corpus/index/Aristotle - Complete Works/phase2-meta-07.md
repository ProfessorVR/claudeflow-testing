# Phase 2 Analysis: META-07 — Actuality and Potentiality

- **bekker_range**: 1042a–1052a
- **PDF pages**: 96–119 (formula: (Bekker−980)×1.566+3; verified against visible Bekker margins)
- **translator**: W. D. Ross (rev. J. Barnes)
- **cluster**: First Philosophy
- **dissertation_critical**: Yes — ἐνέργεια/δύναμις pair is the ontological backbone for motion theory (Physics III), soul as first actuality (De Anima II.1), and the "setting before the eyes" doctrine (Rhetoric III.11).
- **unit_thesis**: Book VIII summarizes the inquiry into sensible substance by identifying three kinds of substance (matter, form, the composite), argues that the matter–form composite requires no external cause of unity because one element is matter and the other actuality, and resolves the problem of definition by showing that the proximate matter and the form are "one and the same thing, the one potentially, the other actually." Book IX then provides the systematic treatment of δύναμις (potentiality/capacity) and ἐνέργεια (actuality/fulfillment), distinguishing rational from non-rational potentialities, refuting the Megaric denial of unexercised capacities, defining actuality by analogy rather than genus, establishing the absolute priority of actuality over potentiality (in formula, in time, and in substance), differentiating complete actualities (ἐνέργειαι) from incomplete movements (κινήσεις), and concluding with the thesis that being and non-being in the strictest sense are truth and falsity, with incomposite substances admitting only contact (truth) or non-contact (ignorance), never error.

## Section 2: Hierarchical Outline

### 2.1 Book VIII (Η): Summary of the substance inquiry (1042a3–1042b8)

- Recapitulation of results from Books VII (Z): substance is sought through the four causes; recognized substances include natural bodies (fire, earth, water, air, plants, animals, heavens) plus the essence and the substratum. 1042a3–25 (PDF 96)
- Three senses of substance: (1) the matter (that which, not being a 'this' actually, is potentially a 'this'); (2) the formula or form; (3) the complex of matter and form, which alone is generated and destroyed. 1042a26–32 (PDF 96)
- Matter is substance because it underlies all opposite changes (place, increase, alteration, generation/destruction); substance in the sense of form can exist apart in some cases. 1042a32–b8 (PDF 96–97)

### 2.2 Substance as actuality: differentiae as causes (1042b9–1043b4)

- The substance of sensible things in the sense of actuality must be identified: Democritus posited three kinds of difference (shape, turning, inter-contact); Aristotle generalizes to many kinds of differentiae — mixture, position, time, place, hardness/softness, density/rarity, excess/defect. 1042b9–1043a1 (PDF 97)
- The word 'is' has as many meanings as these differentiae: a threshold *is* by lying in such a position; ice *is* by being solidified in such a way. These differentiae are the causes of each thing's being. 1043a2–12 (PDF 97)
- The differentiae are substance in the sense of actuality, even when coupled with matter; in each case what is predicated of the matter is the actuality itself. 1043a12–28 (PDF 97)

### 2.3 The composite, the formula, and the soul–body analogy (1043a29–1043b32)

- Ambiguity of names: 'house' may mean the composite (bricks and stones laid thus) or the actuality/form (a covering). 'Animal' is a soul in a body. Soul and body are the same: one potentially, the other actually. 1043a29–1043b4 (PDF 98)
- The syllable is not its letters plus juxtaposition; there is "something else" — the substance — that is neither an element nor composed of elements but is the cause of the thing's being. This is the primary cause of each thing's being. 1043b4–14 (PDF 98)
- Substances must be either eternal or destructible without being destroyed (i.e., never in course of coming to be or being destroyed — the form is never generated). The difficulty raised by Antisthenes: the 'what' cannot be defined (definition is a long formula), but one can say *what sort* something is. 1043b23–32 (PDF 98)

### 2.4 Matter, potentiality, and the causes of sensible substance (1044a1–1045a6)

- Material substance: even if all things have the same primary constituent, there is a proximate matter proper to each (e.g., phlegm, bile); one thing comes from another in two senses (developmental or analytic). 1044a15–b3 (PDF 99)
- When one inquires what the cause is, one should state all the possible causes: material, formal, moving, final. The formal and final cause are often the same. We must state the *proximate* causes. 1044b1–1045a6 (PDF 100)
- Regarding *generable* natural substances: if the causes are really these, we must inquire about eternal substances differently; some have no matter, or matter only for change in place. 1044b6–15 (PDF 100)
- Potentiality and actuality in matter: some things are and are not without coming to be (points, forms in general); the matter of contraries (health/disease, white/black) is related to both, and all things which change reduce to their matter. 1044b21–1045a6 (PDF 100)

### 2.5 The unity of definition: matter and form as one (1045a7–1045b23)

- The problem: what is the cause of the unity of a definition (e.g., 'biped animal')? Why is this one and not many? Participation does not solve it (what is the cause of participating?). 1045a7–b7 (PDF 101)
- **Solution**: if one element is matter and another is form, and one is potentially what the other is actually, the difficulty vanishes. The unity of definition is the same as asking the cause of unity in general: each thing is a unity because matter and form are "one and the same thing, the one potentially, the other actually." 1045b7–23 (PDF 101)
- There is no other cause of unity unless there is something which caused the movement from potentiality into actuality. Things that have *no* matter are *without qualification* essentially unities. 1045b18–23 (PDF 101)

### 2.6 Book IX (Θ), chs. 1–2: Potentiality defined; rational vs. non-rational capacities (1045b27–1046b28)

- Potentiality and actuality extend further than the mere sphere of motion (the primary focus), but Aristotle begins with the motion-related sense. 1045b32–1046a4 (PDF 102)
- **Potentiality (δύναμις)** defined: a starting-point (*ἀρχή*) of change in another thing or in the thing itself *qua* other. One kind is a potentiality for being acted on; another is a state of insusceptibility to change for the worse. All definitions contain the formula of potentiality in the primary sense. 1046a4–19 (PDF 102)
- The potentiality of acting and of being acted on is in a sense one (a thing is capable because it can be acted on, or because something else can be acted on by it), but in a sense the potentialities are different. 1046a19–28 (PDF 102)
- **Rational vs. non-rational potentialities** (1046a36–1046b28, PDF 103): rational potentialities (all productive arts, all sciences) are in the rational part of the soul and can produce contrary effects (medicine produces both health and disease). Non-rational potentialities produce only one effect (the hot can only heat). The rational formula explains a thing and its privation; denial and removal of the contrary is the primary privation. 1046b4–24 (PDF 103)

### 2.7 Θ.3–5: The Megaric refutation, the possible, and the acquisition of potentialities (1046b29–1048a24)

- **Megaric refutation** (Θ.3, 1046b29–1047a29, PDF 104): the Megarics say a thing can act only when acting. Aristotle's reductio: on this view a builder is not a builder unless building; arts would be lost and reacquired each time; nothing would be cold or hot or sweet unless perceived (Protagorean relativism follows); what is blind is what *has* sight but lacks it, so the same people would be blind many times a day. 1047a4–10 (PDF 104)
- Potentiality and actuality are evidently different; the Megaric view annihilates both change and becoming. 1047a14–20 (PDF 104)
- **The possible and the impossible** (Θ.4, 1047a24–1047b2, PDF 105): if what we have described is the possible, evidently it cannot be true to say 'this is capable of being but will not be' — nothing is incapable of being (*nothing impossible follows from supposing a possibility realized*). The proof: if A is possible, then when A is supposed actual nothing impossible results; if B need not be possible whenever A is, nothing prevents B's being impossible. 1047a24–b2 (PDF 105)
- **Acquisition of potentialities** (Θ.5, 1048a2–24, PDF 105): potentialities are either innate (the senses) or acquired by practice (flute-playing) or by learning (the arts). Those acquired by practice or rational formula require prior exercise; those non-rational and those implying passivity do not. 1048a2–16 (PDF 105)

### 2.8 Θ.6–7: Actuality defined by analogy; the kinēsis/energeia distinction (1048a25–1049b2)

- **Actuality (ἐνέργεια) defined by analogy, not by genus** (Θ.6, 1048a25–b9, PDF 106): "We must not seek a definition of everything but be content to grasp the analogy — that as that which is building is to that which is capable of building, so is the waking to the sleeping, and that which has its eyes open to that which has sight but has them shut, and that which has been wrought out of the matter to the matter." Actuality is defined by one member of the antithesis, potentiality by the other. 1048a35–b6 (PDF 106)
- Not all things are said to exist actually in the same sense — only by analogy: as A is in B or to B, C is in D or to D. Some are as movement to potentiality, others as substance to some sort of matter. 1048b6–9 (PDF 106)
- The infinite, the void, and similar things exist potentially and actually in a different sense from ordinary things; the infinite does not exist potentially in the sense that it will ever actually have separate existence — its separateness is only in knowledge. 1048b9–17 (PDF 106)
- **The kinēsis/energeia distinction** (Θ.6, 1048b18–35, PDF 107): actions that have a limit are not ends but are *relative to* the end (e.g., making thin) — these are *movements* (κινήσεις), incomplete. Those in which the end is present are *actualities* (ἐνέργειαι): "at the same time we are seeing and have seen, are understanding and have understood, are thinking and have thought; but it is not true that at the same time we are learning and have learnt, or are being cured and have been cured." Every movement is incomplete — making thin, learning, walking, building. The latter sort — where at the same time one is seeing and has seen — "I call an actuality, and the former a movement." 1048b18–35 (PDF 107)
- **Θ.7: When is something potentially a given thing?** (1048b37–1049b2, PDF 107–108): not at any and every time — earth is not yet potentially a man; it must first become seed. A thing is potentially X when, through its own motive principle, if nothing external hinders, it will become X. The earth must undergo a change in a foreign medium; the seed already has its own motive principle. 1049a1–18 (PDF 108)
- The subject and substratum of accidents is an individual (body and soul); the "of" construction applies to matter and is only rightly used with reference to matter and to accidents. Prime matter: that which is no longer called "of" something else (e.g., fire is not earthen nor earth fiery; fire is prime matter, not a 'this'). 1049a18–b2 (PDF 108)

### 2.9 Θ.8–9: The priority of actuality; actuality and the good (1049b3–1051a2)

- **Actuality is prior to potentiality in three ways** (Θ.8):
  1. *Prior in formula*: the potential is defined through the actual (the capable of building = that which can build; capable of seeing = that which can see); the formula and knowledge of the actual must precede knowledge of the potential. 1049b12–17 (PDF 108)
  2. *Prior in time* (in a qualified sense): in the individual, the potential precedes the actual (the matter and seed are prior to this man); but from the potential the actual is always produced by an actual thing (man from man, musician by musician; there is always a first mover, and the mover already exists actually). 1049b17–1050a3 (PDF 109)
  3. *Prior in substance*: (a) things posterior in becoming are prior in form and substance (man is prior to boy, human to seed, because the one already has its form); (b) everything that comes to be moves toward a principle, i.e., an end (*telos*), and the actuality is the end; (c) the potentiality is acquired for the sake of the actuality, not conversely — animals have sight not *in order that* they may have sight but sight was acquired *for the sake of* seeing; (d) matter exists in a potential state just *because* it may attain its form, and when it exists *actually*, then it is in its form. 1050a4–b6 (PDF 109)
- **Priority extends to eternal things**: actuality is prior in a still higher sense, because eternal things are prior in substance to perishable things, and no eternal thing exists potentially. Every potentiality is at the same time a potentiality for the opposite (what is capable of being is also capable of not being); but imperishable things exist actually, without qualification. The sun, stars, and whole heaven are ever active; movement does not imply potentiality for opposites in their case, so their motion is not laborious. 1050b6–1051a2 (PDF 110)
- **Actuality and the good** (Θ.9, 1051a4–21, PDF 111): the good actuality is better and more valuable than the good potentiality. The capacity for contraries is at the same time a potentiality for health *and* illness; but contraries cannot be present at the same time; the actuality is better. In the case of bad things, the end (actuality) must be worse than the potentiality. The bad does not exist apart from bad things; in eternal things there is nothing bad, nothing defective.
- Geometrical relations are discovered by being brought to actuality (the reason being that thinking *is* the actuality of thought); potentiality is discovered from actuality. 1051a21–33 (PDF 111)

### 2.10 Θ.10: Being as truth; incomposites and truth as contact (1051a34–1052a11)

- Being and non-being in the strictest sense are **truth and falsity** (1051a34–b9, PDF 111): truth = the subject and attribute are really combined; falsity = they are really separated. Truth in thinking = thinking what is combined as combined, and what is separated as separated. "It is not because we think you are white that you *are* white, but because you are white we who say this have the truth." 1051b3–9 (PDF 112)
- Regarding **composites**: some things are always combined, others always separated, others admit of both; being is combination, non-being is separation. For contingent facts, the same opinion can be sometimes true and sometimes false; for necessary facts (things that are always so), the same opinions are always true or always false. 1051b9–17 (PDF 112)
- Regarding **incomposites** (ἀσύνθετα): truth or falsity is not combination/separation but **contact (θιγεῖν) and assertion** = truth; non-contact = ignorance (not error). It is not possible to be *in error* about incomposites; one either thinks them or does not. They all exist actually, not potentially (otherwise they would come to be and cease to be, but being itself does not come to be). Inquiry about them is whether they are of such and such a nature or not. 1051b17–1052a4 (PDF 112–113)
- Regarding unchangeable things, there can be no error in respect of time. 1051a4–11 (PDF 113)

## Section 6: Tensions & Open Questions

1. **The analogy-only definition of actuality (Θ.6)**: Aristotle explicitly says we must not seek a definition of actuality but "be content to grasp the analogy" (1048a35–37). This raises the question of whether ἐνέργεια is a genuine concept with specifiable content or a proto-concept graspable only through its instances. If the latter, the priority thesis (Θ.8) rests on an analogically defined base term. The dissertation must decide how this non-definability affects the ontological status of actuality as a first principle. [INTERP-high] — Linked to: META-07-P5, META-07-P6.

2. **The kinēsis/energeia distinction and its scope**: Aristotle provides paradigm cases (seeing vs. learning, living well vs. building), but the boundary is contested. Is *perceiving* (αἴσθησις) a complete actuality or an incomplete movement? De Anima II.5 treats perception as a kind of alteration, yet the Θ.6 tense test suggests perceiving qualifies as an ἐνέργεια (one is perceiving and has perceived). This tension is directly relevant to the dissertation's treatment of phantasia and perceptual activity. [INTERP-high] — Linked to: META-07-P7, DA units.

3. **The priority of actuality and the eternal prime mover**: the argument that actuality is prior "in a still higher sense" for eternal things (1050b6–28) depends on the thesis that the sun, stars, and heaven are "ever active" and their motion is not laborious. This cosmological commitment is load-bearing for the priority thesis but is the most historically vulnerable premise. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: META-07-P6, META-08 (Lambda).

4. **Truth as contact (θιγεῖν) for incomposites**: Aristotle asserts that for non-composite substances, truth is contact and ignorance is non-contact, with *no possibility of error* (1051b17–25). What counts as an incomposite substance, and whether the soul's "contact" with such substances is itself an actuality (thus linking Θ.10 back to Θ.6–8), remains underdeveloped. This connects to the De Anima's account of νοῦς grasping indivisibles (DA III.6). [INTERP-medium] — Linked to: META-07-P8, DA III.6.

5. **Rational potentiality and the role of desire/choice (προαίρεσις)**: Aristotle argues that rational potentialities require desire or choice to determine which contrary they actualize (1048a8–16). But the exact relationship between δύναμις μετὰ λόγου and προαίρεσις is compressed: does choice *trigger* the potentiality or does it *specify* which contrary the potentiality realizes? The former is compatible with a faculty model, the latter with a more integrated agent-causal account. [INTERP-medium] — Linked to: META-07-P3.

6. **Matter–form unity vs. hylomorphic dualism**: the Η.6 solution (matter and form are one and the same, the one potentially the other actually) elegantly resolves the unity problem, but it raises the question of whether matter retains any independent ontological status or is entirely absorbed into the form–actuality. If the former, then the unity remains explanatorily basic; if the latter, the matter-concept collapses. [INTERP-low] — Linked to: META-07-P1.

## Section 9: Dissertation Relevance

### 9.1 Direct Contributions to the Dissertation Framework

1. **ἐνέργεια/δύναμις as the ontological backbone of motion theory**: Physics III defines motion (κίνησις) as "the actuality of that which exists potentially, insofar as it is potential" (Phys. 201a10–11). The systematic treatment in Θ.1–6 provides the conceptual infrastructure that makes Physics III's definition intelligible: what potentiality *is* (Θ.1–2), why it must be real even when unexercised (Θ.3), and how actuality relates to it by analogy (Θ.6).

2. **The kinēsis/energeia distinction (Θ.6) and perception**: The distinction between complete actualities and incomplete movements is critical for classifying perception (αἴσθησις) and phantasia. If perceiving is an ἐνέργεια (the tense test: one is seeing and has seen), then perception is not a κίνησις but a complete actuality — supporting the De Anima II.5 characterization of perception as a "second actuality" rather than an ordinary alteration. This directly affects how the dissertation theorizes the perceptual conditions of rhetorical experience.

3. **Rational potentiality and rhetorical art**: The Θ.2 distinction between rational and non-rational capacities provides the ontological ground for rhetoric as a τέχνη (art/skill). Rhetoric, as a productive art accompanied by a rational formula, is capable of producing contrary effects (persuasion and dissuasion); what determines the outcome is choice (προαίρεσις). This maps onto Rhetoric I.1's characterization of rhetoric as the faculty of observing the available means of persuasion.

4. **Priority of actuality and "setting before the eyes"**: The Θ.8 priority thesis (actuality is prior in substance because it is the τέλος) connects to Rhetoric III.11's doctrine of "setting before the eyes" (πρὸ ὀμμάτων ποιεῖν): metaphor's power lies in presenting things "as actual" (ἐνεργοῦντα), i.e., in actuality. The ontological priority of actuality grounds the rhetorical efficacy of ἐνέργεια-style metaphor.

5. **Truth as contact (Θ.10) and noetic apprehension**: The Θ.10 doctrine that truth for incomposites is θιγεῖν (contact) connects to De Anima III.6's account of νοῦς grasping indivisibles. This provides a model for non-propositional, non-discursive apprehension that may illuminate the pre-reflective dimension of rhetorical experience (ambient attunement rather than explicit judgment).

### 9.2 Cross-Unit Dependencies

- **META-07 → PHYS-03** (Physics III): The Θ.1–6 analysis of δύναμις/ἐνέργεια is presupposed by the Physics III definition of motion.
- **META-07 → DA-02** (De Anima II): Soul as "first actuality (ἐντελέχεια) of a natural body that has life potentially" (DA 412a27) depends on the Θ framework for both ἐντελέχεια and δύναμις.
- **META-07 → DA-03** (De Anima III): The Θ.10 contact-truth doctrine parallels DA III.6 on νοῦς and indivisibles.
- **META-07 → RHET-05** (Rhetoric III): The ἐνέργεια concept in "setting before the eyes" (Rhet. 1411b24–1412a10) draws on the Θ.6 definition of actuality.
- **META-07 → META-08** (Metaphysics Λ): The unmoved mover as pure actuality (Λ.6–7) presupposes the Θ.8 priority thesis.
