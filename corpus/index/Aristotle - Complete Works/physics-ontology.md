**Internal trajectory**: Named as prerequisite for understanding nature at III.1 (200b14: "if motion were unknown, nature too would be unknown"). Formally defined at III.1 (201a10). The strict/broad distinction between kinēsis (subject-to-subject) and metabolē (including generation/corruption) creates a systematic gap: the most fundamental natural changes fall outside the kinetic framework.

## Work-Level Tensions (Synthesized)

### WT-1: Incomplete actuality (energeia atelēs)
Motion is defined as a peculiar kind of actuality — actual yet not fully actual (III.2, 201b31). Whether this constitutes a genuine third ontological category between potentiality and completed actuality, or is merely a conceptual device, is the central interpretive crux of the Physics. Connects to the De Anima's use of entelecheia for the soul and to Metaphysics Theta on the priority of actuality. [INTERP-high]

### WT-2: The potential infinite without possible actualization
The infinite exists only potentially (III.6), yet the general principle "what can potentially be, can actually be" (207b17) is used *against* actual infinity. This requires a special mode of potential existence — processual, successive, never completed — that has no parallel elsewhere in Aristotle. [INTERP-high]

### WT-3: Teleology without deliberation
Nature acts for an end but does not deliberate (II.8, 199b26). The analogy with art presupposes a deliberating craftsman; stripping deliberation while retaining purposiveness is the central contested move. [INTERP-high]

### WT-4: Time as objective vs. time as soul-dependent
Time is grounded in motion (IV.11, 219b1) but may require soul as counter (IV.14, 223a21--26). The hedged formulation leaves time's ontological status ambiguous — mind-independent feature or product of psychic activity? [INTERP-medium]

### WT-5: Place as boundary vs. place as having power
Place is defined as a geometrical boundary (IV.4, 212a20) yet is said to have "power" (dunamis) over natural locomotion (IV.1, 208b10). Whether the power belongs to place itself or to the containing body is never resolved. [INTERP-medium]

### WT-6: The strict/broad distinction — kinēsis vs. metabolē
Motion (kinēsis) is restricted to subject-to-subject change (V.1); generation and corruption are changes (metabolai) but not motions. This excludes the most fundamental natural transformations from the kinetic framework, creating a systematic gap between the classification of motion and the ontology of substance. [INTERP-low]

### WT-7: Self-motion vs. the unmoved mover
The self-mover is posited at VIII.5 but analyzed at VIII.6 as containing an unmoved component. Whether genuine self-motion is ontologically possible, or whether "self-motion" reduces to one part moving another, connects to De Anima I.3--4 on whether the soul is in motion. [INTERP-medium]
