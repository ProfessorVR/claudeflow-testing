# Cross-Pipeline Hooks — Movement of Animals

**Source**: Phase 3 ontology of *Movement of Animals* (MA-01).
**Date**: 2026-03-09

---

## 1. κίνησις in MA → BCAP on κίνησις [INTERP-high]

**Aristotelian concept**: κίνησις (C3) — animal locomotion as a specific instantiation of the general concept of movement; requires both an internal immovable point and an external immovable ground; caused proximately by desire.

**BCAP connection**: Heidegger in GA 18 treats κίνησις as ἐντελέχεια τοῦ δυνάμει ὄντος (Physics Gamma 1) — the being-at-work of what is potentially. MA provides the zoological concretization: the animal's locomotion is the actualization of its capacity to move, conditioned by the cosmological structure of moved-and-unmoved. The MA requirement that animal motion needs an external immovable ground (698b8-9) parallels Heidegger's insistence that Dasein's being-in-the-world is always already thrown into a situation — the "ground" is not chosen but given.

**Key passages**: 698a1-698b5 (internal immovable point in joints), 698b8-9 (external immovable required), 699a14-20 (cosmic unmoved mover), 700b15-17 (all movement is for the sake of something).

**Integration priority**: HIGH — MA is the zoological-cosmological complement to BCAP's ontological reading of κίνησις.

---

## 2. φαντασία as practical mover → BCAP on φαντασία [INTERP-high]

**Aristotelian concept**: φαντασία (C2) — presents objects to desire as a "faculty of discrimination" (700b19-21); can produce involuntary movements "without express mandate of the intellect" (703b6-8); mediates between perception and action.

**BCAP connection**: Heidegger reads φαντασία in GA 18 as "making-present-to-itself" — the showing-itself of what is not yet there. MA concretizes this: φαντασία in the practical domain *makes present* the object of desire, and this making-present has immediate causal efficacy. The involuntary movements of the heart and genitals (ch. 11) show that φαντασία can operate below the threshold of rational control — supporting Heidegger's claim that φαντασία is not "imagination" in the modern sense but a bodily being-in-the-world.

**Rickert connection**: The sub-rational motive power of φαντασία supports Rickert's "ambient rhetoric" thesis: rhetorical persuasion operates through images that bypass deliberation. MA ch. 7's drinking example — "I want to drink, says appetite; this is drink, says sense or imagination: straightaway I drink" (701a32-33) — is the locus classicus for phantasia's role in pre-deliberative action, structurally parallel to the rhetorical arousal of πάθη through φαντάσματα.

**Key passages**: 700b17-21 (phantasia as discriminative faculty), 701a29-33 (phantasia in rapid action), 703b3-21 (involuntary movement from phantasia).

**Integration priority**: HIGH — central to the dissertation's argument about phantasia as sub-rational practical mover.

## 3. ὄρεξις and the practical syllogism → BCAP on ὄρεξις and πρᾶξις [INTERP-high]

**Aristotelian concept**: ὄρεξις (C1) as the proximate efficient cause of action; the practical syllogism (C4) as the logical structure of desire-driven action.

**BCAP connection**: Heidegger in GA 18 treats ὄρεξις in the context of the Rhetoric and NE, emphasizing that the πάθη are modes of ὄρεξις — ways of being affected that dispose the agent toward or away from objects. MA provides the physiological-mechanical account that Heidegger's existential appropriation deliberately leaves aside: desire literally moves the body through the connate spirit (703a9-23). The practical syllogism shows how the universal (the good) and the particular (this thing here) are synthesized in action — a structure Heidegger reads as the formal indication of practical engagement with the world.

**Key passages**: 700b15-701a5 (the desire-phantasia-action chain), 701a7-b1 (the practical syllogism), 701a24 ("the premises of action are of two kinds, of the good and of the possible").

**Integration priority**: HIGH — the MA practical syllogism is the stripped-down version of the NE/Rhetoric account that BCAP interprets.

## 4. σύμφυτον πνεῦμα → Embodied action [INTERP-med]

**Aristotelian concept**: σύμφυτον πνεῦμα (I2) — the connate spirit as the bodily medium transmitting desire's motive force to the limbs through expansion and contraction.

**Cross-pipeline relevance**: The pneuma doctrine locates the mechanism of action in a material medium — neither purely psychic nor purely somatic. This intermediate status resonates with the hylomorphic psychology of De Anima and challenges any dualistic reading of Aristotle. For the dissertation, the pneuma shows that even the most "rational" moment of Aristotelian action theory (the practical syllogism) has a material substrate.

**Uexkull connection**: The pneuma as the organism's internal medium for transmitting action parallels Uexkull's *Wirkwelt* (effector world) — the organism's capacity to act on its environment is mediated by specific bodily mechanisms.

**Key passages**: 703a9-23 (pneuma as expansion/contraction), 703a13-14 (pneuma stands to soul as moved joint-point to unmoved), 703a29-35 (the commonwealth analogy).

**Integration priority**: MEDIUM — supports the embodied-action argument but is not directly cited in B&T or BCAP.

---

## 5. The puppet analogy → Automaticity and ambient action [INTERP-med]

**Aristotelian concept**: The puppet (αὐτόματον) analogy — "the movements of animals may be compared with those of automatic puppets" (701b2); a tiny occasion sets off cascading movement.

**Rickert connection**: The puppet analogy captures the automaticity of habituated action — small triggers producing large-scale coordinated movement without deliberation. This is directly relevant to Rickert's account of ambient rhetoric as operating through habituated response patterns rather than conscious argumentation. The analogy suggests that even Aristotle recognized a domain of action below full rational control, governed by mechanism rather than deliberation.

**Key passages**: 701b2-9 (puppet analogy), 701b16-32 (imaginations and sensations cause qualitative bodily change), 703b6-8 (involuntary movements from images).

**Integration priority**: MEDIUM — the automaticity theme connects MA to the ambient-rhetoric framework.

## 6. τὸ ἀγαθόν as final cause of movement → Teleological structure of action [INTERP-low]

**Aristotelian concept**: τὸ ἀγαθόν (C5) — the good (actual or apparent) as the final cause initiating desire and thus movement; "not every intelligible object, but only the end in the domain of conduct" (700b25).

**B&T connection**: The claim that only the practical good — not every intelligible object — initiates movement anticipates Heidegger's distinction between present-at-hand (Vorhandenheit) and ready-to-hand (Zuhandenheit). Objects move us only insofar as they appear under the aspect of the good (i.e., as relevant to our practical concerns), not insofar as they are merely known. The qualification that "a seeming good may take the room of actual good" (700b28-29) adds that the practical world is constituted by appearances, not by objective states of affairs.

**Key passages**: 700b24-29 (the good initiates movement), 700b28-29 (apparent good substitutes for actual good).

**Integration priority**: LOW — supports the B&T reading but the connection is general rather than specific.
