# Phase 2 Analysis: DA-01 --- De Anima I

## Section 1: Unit Metadata

- **unit_id**: DA-01
- **work**: *De Anima* (On the Soul)
- **author**: Aristotle
- **translator**: J. A. Smith (Revised Oxford Translation, ed. Barnes)
- **chapters**: I.1--I.5
- **Bekker range**: 402a1--411b30
- **PDF pages**: 3--18 (Barnes vol. II, pp. 641--656)
- **text note**: TEXT: W. D. Ross, OCT, Oxford, 1956
- **tier**: Light
- **cluster**: psychology
- **dissertation_critical**: Moderate --- methodological prolegomenon, soul-body relation, critique of predecessors; no direct phantasia doctrine but frames the inquiry that Books II--III resolve
- **Unit thesis**: De Anima I establishes the methodological parameters for investigating soul (*psuche*), surveys and systematically refutes the doctrines of predecessors who define soul primarily through movement or elemental composition, and concludes that soul can be neither a self-moving number, a harmony of the body, nor a magnitude moved in a circle, thereby clearing the ground for Aristotle's own hylomorphic definition in Book II.

```

    +-- Dignity and difficulty of the inquiry into soul 402a1--10 (p. 3)
    +-- Methodological puzzles: genus, method, starting-points 402a10--402b16 (p. 3)
    +-- What is soul? Substance, quality, quantity? Potentiality or actuality? 402b1--16 (p. 3)
    +-- The affections of soul: shared with body or proper to soul alone? 403a3--403b19 (pp. 4--5)
    +-- The physicist vs. the dialectician on anger: matter + form 403a29--403b9 (p. 5)
    +-- Conclusion: affections of soul are enmattered accounts (logoi enuloi) 403b17--19 (p. 6)

I.2  Survey of Predecessors: Soul as Movement and Cognition (403b20--405b30)
    +-- Two marks predecessors assign to soul: movement and sensation/cognition 403b25--28 (p. 6)
    +-- Democritus: soul = spherical fire-atoms producing movement 403b31--404a16 (pp. 6--7)
    +-- Pythagoreans: soul = motes in air, or what moves them 404a16--20 (p. 7)
    +-- Anaxagoras: soul/mind as mover; distinguished from Democritus 404a25--405a3 (pp. 7--8)
    +-- Empedocles: soul composed of elements; like known by like 404b8--405a13 (pp. 7--8)
    +-- Plato in the Timaeus: soul fashioned from elements 405a15--29 (p. 7)
    +-- Thales, Diogenes, Heraclitus, Alcmaeon, Hippo, Critias 405b1--406a12 (pp. 8--9)
    +-- Summary: three traditional definitions 405b31--406a12 (p. 9)
        (a) self-mover, (b) subtlest/most incorporeal body, (c) composed of elements

I.3  Critique of Soul as Self-Movement (406a12--407b26)
    +-- Four species of movement: locomotion, alteration, diminution, growth 406b12--15 (p. 9)
    +-- If soul moves itself, it has place and can be moved by force --- absurdities 406b15--407a2 (pp. 9--10)
    +-- Against the Timaeus: soul is not a magnitude moved in a circle 407a2--407b11 (pp. 10--11)
    +-- Thought is one and continuous; circular revolution is not 407a20--407b5 (pp. 10--11)
    +-- If soul were a magnitude, thought could not think with any part indifferently 407b5--26 (p. 11)

I.4  Soul as Harmony and as Self-Moving Number (407b27--409b18)
    +-- Against harmony: admits of degrees, soul does not; harmony cannot move 407b27--408a30 (p. 12)
    +-- It is the man who is angry, not the soul; thought is impassible 408b1--30 (p. 13)
    +-- Against soul as self-moving number: units/points cannot bear movement 408b30--409b18 (pp. 13--14)
    +-- Combining movement and number yields the same absurdities 409b1--18 (p. 14)

I.5  Soul as Composed of Elements; Final Difficulties (409b19--411b30)
    +-- Against like-by-like: soul would know only elements, not composites 409b19--410b15 (pp. 15--16)
    +-- Empedocles' god lacks Strife and so cannot know all things 410a27--b15 (p. 16)
    +-- Panpsychism of Thales generates difficulties 411a7--24 (p. 17)
    +-- Unity problem: is soul divisible or partless? What holds its parts together? 411a24--b18 (p. 17)
    +-- Segmented animals retain soul in each part --- soul is homogeneous 411b19--30 (pp. 17--18)
```

## Section 3: Key Concepts and Positions

### Key Concepts

| Concept | Greek | Definition | Bekker (PDF) |
|---------|-------|------------|-------------|
| soul | *psuche* (ψυχή) | The subject of the entire inquiry; predecessors define it through movement, cognition, or elemental composition; Aristotle frames it as potentially substance, quality, or quantity, and as either potentiality or actuality | 402a7 (p. 3) |
| enmattered accounts | *logoi enuloi* (λόγοι ἔνυλοι) | The affections of soul are inseparable from the natural matter of animals; a definition of anger must include both the form (desire for retaliation) and the matter (boiling of blood around the heart) | 403a25--403b19 (pp. 5--6) |
| movement / self-motion | *kinesis* (κίνησις) | The most commonly attributed mark of soul among predecessors; Aristotle argues systematically that soul cannot be self-moved, since movement implies place, force, and divisibility incompatible with soul's nature | 403b25--28 (p. 6), 406a12ff (p. 9) |
| like-by-like cognition | *homoioi to homoion* (ὁμοίῳ τὸ ὅμοιον) | The principle that soul knows each thing by being composed of the same element; attributed to Empedocles and the elemental theorists; Aristotle shows it entails that soul cannot know composites or forms | 404b8--405a13 (p. 7), 409b26--410b15 (pp. 15--16) |
| harmony | *harmonia* (ἁρμονία) | The view that soul is a ratio or blending of bodily contraries; refuted on grounds that harmony admits of degrees while soul does not, and that harmony cannot originate movement | 407b27--408a30 (p. 12) |
| self-moving number | --- | The Xenocratean or Pythagorean-Platonic view that soul is a number that moves itself; refuted on grounds that units/points cannot be assigned movement without generating absurdities about place, magnitude, and infinity | 408b30--409b18 (pp. 13--14) |

### Main Positions

| ID | Claim | Bekker (PDF) |
|----|-------|-------------|
| DA-01-P1 | The affections of soul are not separable from the physical matter of animals; consequently the study of soul falls to the natural philosopher, and adequate definitions must include both form and matter (*logoi enuloi*). The physicist defines anger as a boiling of blood around the heart; the dialectician defines it as a desire for retaliation; the complete account requires both. | 403a3--403b19 (pp. 5--6) |
| DA-01-P2 | Soul cannot be a self-mover: if soul moves, it must have place and be susceptible to forced movement; moreover, if soul moved the body by moving itself, it could also be moved in reverse --- all of which are absurd. It is more correct to say "the man is angry" than "the soul is angry." | 406a12--407a2 (pp. 9--10); 408b1--15 (p. 13) |
| DA-01-P3 | Soul is not a magnitude moved in a circle (contra the *Timaeus*): thought is one and continuous, whereas circular revolution is divisible, repeatable, and infinite; and a magnitude cannot think indifferently with any of its parts. | 407a2--407b26 (pp. 10--11) |
| DA-01-P4 | Soul is not a harmony of bodily constituents: harmony admits of more and less, but soul does not; harmony is a static ratio, not a source of movement; and the harmony theory cannot explain how soul governs the body or why particular parts have particular functions. | 407b27--408a30 (p. 12) |
| DA-01-P5 | The like-by-like doctrine of cognition --- that soul knows each element by being composed of it --- is refuted because the soul would then know only simple elements, not their ratios, composites, or forms. What in the soul perceives God, or discord, or bone? Each element would need its own principles of knowledge. | 409b26--410b15 (pp. 15--16) |

## Section 4: Interlocutors and Cross-Work References

| Name | Role | Key Engagement | Bekker (PDF) |
|------|------|---------------|-------------|
| **Democritus** | Major target. Soul = spherical fire-atoms; soul and mind identified ("truth is what appears"). | Refuted: spherical atoms cannot explain rest or directed movement; Philippus' puppet-analogy (Daedalus pouring quicksilver into his wooden Aphrodite) mocked as inadequate. | 403b31--404a16 (p. 7); 406b15--407a2 (p. 10) |
| **Plato / *Timaeus*** | Major target. Soul fashioned from elements; soul-of-the-world moves in a circle, divided by harmonic numbers. | Refuted: circular movement of thought generates absurdities about magnitude, contact, and infinite repetition; if thinking is a movement of revolution, the soul that thinks must be the circle whose movement is thinking. | 404b16--405a29 (p. 7); 407a2--407b26 (pp. 10--11) |
| **Empedocles** | Soul composed of all elements; like known by like; verse quoted: "For 'tis by Earth we see Earth, by Water Water." | Refuted via like-by-like critique; also, if god (the Sphairos) lacks Strife, god cannot know all things --- an absurdity. | 404b8--405a13 (p. 7); 410a27--410b15 (p. 16) |
| **Anaxagoras** | Mind (*nous*) as mover, simple, unmixed, pure. | Distinguished from Democritus: Anaxagoras separates mind from soul, but in practice treats them as a single substance. His account remains obscure. | 404a25--405a3 (pp. 7--8); 405b19--23 (p. 8) |
| **Pythagoreans** | Soul = motes in air, or self-moving number. | Motes-in-air theory seems arbitrary; self-moving number refuted through absurdities about units, points, and magnitude. | 404a16--20 (p. 7); 408b30--409b18 (pp. 13--14) |
| **Thales** | Soul as motive force; the magnet has soul because it moves iron. | Introduced as earliest known psychologist; his panpsychism (soul intermingled with the whole universe) raises difficulties about why soul does not form an animal when in air or fire. | 405a19--21 (p. 8); 411a7--24 (p. 17) |
| **Heraclitus** | Soul = warm exhalation, the first principle. | The warm exhalation is incorporeal and in ceaseless flux; what is in movement knows what is in movement. | 405a25--28 (p. 8) |
| **Alcmaeon** | Soul resembles the immortals; immortal through ceaseless motion. | Moon, sun, planets, and the whole heavens are in perpetual movement --- so soul is immortal by analogy. | 405a29--b1 (p. 8) |
| **Diogenes** | Soul = air, finest in grain and first principle. | Air is cognitive and motive because finest in grain; all other things derived from it. | 405b21--25 (p. 8) |
| **Critias** | Soul = blood; perceptiveness is due to blood's nature. | Mentioned briefly; takes perception as the most characteristic attribute of soul. | 405b5--8 (p. 8) |
| **Hippo** | Soul = water; the more "superficial" thinker. | Argued from the fact that seed is fluid; Hippo tries to refute those who say soul is blood. | 405b2--5 (p. 8) |
| ***Physics* VIII** | External cross-reference. | "We have already pointed out that there is no necessity that what originates movement should itself be moved" --- presupposes Physics VIII.5 on the unmoved mover. | 406a2 (p. 9) |
| **DA-02 (Book II)** | Downstream dependency. | Book I's systematic aporiai --- clearing the ground by refuting all predecessor accounts --- motivate the "completely fresh start" of II.1, where soul is defined as the first actuality of a natural organized body. | 411b30 (p. 18) to 412a1 (p. 18) |
