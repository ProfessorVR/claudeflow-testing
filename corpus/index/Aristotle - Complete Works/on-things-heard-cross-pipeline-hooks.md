# Cross-Pipeline Hooks — On Things Heard

**Source**: Phase 3 ontology of *On Things Heard* (AUD-01).
**Date**: 2026-03-09

---

## 1. ψόφος / πληγή → De Anima II.8 sound theory [INTERP-high]

**Aristotelian concept**: ψόφος (C1) — all sounds arise from the impact (πληγή) of air against bodies; the De Audibilibus provides the detailed material acoustics.

**De Anima connection**: DA II.8 (419b4-421a6) establishes the general theory that sound is an impact of air and that hearing operates through the air as medium. De Audibilibus extends this into a systematic treatment of vocal production (lungs, windpipe, mouth), instrumental acoustics (horns, reeds, strings), and qualitative differentiation (hard, soft, clear, rough, thick, thin). The De Audibilibus account is more empirical and less ontological than the DA treatment, focusing on material causes rather than formal/final causes.

**Key passages**: 800a1-5 (all sound from impact), 800a18-30 (mouth as primary differentiator), 803b10-25 (lungs as the real cause of vocal hardness/softness).

**Integration priority**: HIGH — required for a complete Aristotelian acoustics bridging DA to the material details.

---

## 2. σαφές (clearness) across modalities → Common sensibles and cross-modal analogy [INTERP-med]

**Aristotelian concept**: σαφές (C4) — clearness in sound analogous to clearness in colour; the claim that the strongest, purest stimuli produce the most distinct impressions across both modalities.

**Sense and Sensibilia connection**: The clearness analogy presupposes the De Sensu doctrine of common sensibles — properties (figure, magnitude, etc.) perceived by multiple senses. If clearness is genuinely cross-modal, it is a candidate for a common sensible not listed in the standard catalogue. The De Audibilibus goes further than De Sensu in making the analogy explicit: "grey" voices are harsh and confused, "white" voices are clear (802a10-b1) — using colour terms for sound qualities.

**Cross-pipeline relevance**: The colour-sound analogy raises the question of synaesthetic unity at the level of the common sense. If the same quality (clearness) applies to both colour and sound, the common sense must synthesize not just different sense-data but cross-modal qualitative properties — a richer unification than the De Sensu account explicitly develops.

**Key passages**: 802a1-10 (clearness analogy), 802a10-b1 ("grey" and "white" voices).

**Integration priority**: MEDIUM — enriches the common-sensibles doctrine but the text is Light-tier and disputed in authorship.

## 3. τραχύς (roughness) → Touch and cross-modal perception [INTERP-med]

**Aristotelian concept**: τραχύς (I2) — rough sounds arise when the impact is divided and dispersed, "each portion striking separately"; compared to rough objects touching the skin.

**Cross-pipeline relevance**: The explicit comparison of rough sound to rough tactile surfaces is one of the clearest examples of cross-modal analogy in the Aristotelian corpus. It suggests that the qualitative space of perception is not modality-specific but organized by properties (rough/smooth, clear/confused) that span multiple senses. This supports the Uexkullian thesis that the organism's Merkwelt is a unified perceptual field, not a collection of isolated sense-channels.

**Uexkull connection**: Uexkull's functional circle unifies perception and action into a single organism-environment loop. The De Audibilibus cross-modal analogies — roughness spanning touch and hearing, clearness spanning sight and hearing — show Aristotle's own commitment to a unified perceptual field, even if he does not develop it into a full theory of organism-environment coupling.

**Key passages**: 803b40-804a15 (roughness analogy to touch), 802a1-10 (clearness analogy to colour).

**Integration priority**: MEDIUM — supports the perceptual-unity argument.

## 4. πνεῦμα (breath) → MA σύμφυτον πνεῦμα [INTERP-low]

**Aristotelian concept**: πνεῦμα (C3) — breath as the material cause of vocal sound; its force, quantity, and manner of expulsion determine vocal quality.

**Movement of Animals connection**: The De Audibilibus πνεῦμα (breath in vocal production) is distinct from the MA σύμφυτον πνεῦμα (connate spirit as bodily medium for transmitting desire to limbs), but both share the root concept of πνεῦμα as a material medium through which psychological states produce bodily effects. In De Audibilibus, the psychology is implicit (desire to speak → breath → sound); in MA, it is explicit (desire → pneuma → locomotion).

**Key passages**: 800a5-b5 (breath production in lungs and windpipe), 803b10-25 (force of breath determines vocal quality).

**Integration priority**: LOW — the connection is terminological and structural rather than thematic.

---

## 5. συμφωνία (concord) → Ratio-theory in De Sensu [INTERP-low]

**Aristotelian concept**: συμφωνία (I3) — musical concords arise from overlapping note-impacts causing intermediate sounds to escape notice.

**Sense and Sensibilia connection**: De Sensu ch. 3 compares the generation of intermediate colours to musical concords: "colours involving numerical ratios (3:2, 3:4) are most agreeable, like musical concords" (440a1-5). The De Audibilibus gives the acoustic side of this analogy: concords are explained by the temporal overlap of impacts, not by abstract ratios. This provides a mechanistic grounding for the De Sensu ratio-theory — if concords are fundamentally about the temporal structure of impacts, the colour-concord analogy is more than decorative.

**Key passages**: 804b1-10 (concord as overlapping impacts).

**Integration priority**: LOW — enriches the ratio-theory but does not directly connect to external pipelines.
