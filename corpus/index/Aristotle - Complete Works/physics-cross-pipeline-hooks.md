## 1. κίνησις (Physics III) --> BCAP (GA 18) Sections 25--28

### Connection

The BCAP analysis covers Aristotle's *Rhetoric* lectures (GA 18, Grundbegriffe der aristotelischen Philosophie), where sections 25--28 treat κίνησις as a fundamental concept (Grundbegriff) in relation to δύναμις and ἐνέργεια. The Physics III definition of motion as "the fulfilment of what is potentially, as such" (201a10--11) is the primary text behind the BCAP analysis of these sections. The BCAP sections examine how Heidegger reads Aristotle's kinēsis-definition as revealing the ontological structure of Dasein's being-in-the-world — motion not as physical displacement but as the fundamental way in which beings are disclosed.

### Specific hooks

- **Physics III.1 (201a10--11)**: The canonical definition. BCAP SS25--28 provide Heidegger's phenomenological reinterpretation of this definition, treating entelecheia tou dunamei ontos as the self-showing of a being in its being.
- **Physics III.2 (201b31)**: Motion as "incomplete actuality" (energeia atelēs). The BCAP analysis should reveal Heidegger's treatment of this as a fundamental ontological category — not a deficient mode of presence but a way of being that is irreducible to either pure potentiality or completed actuality.
- **Physics VIII.4 (255b1--256a3)**: The dynamis/energeia analysis in the context of natural vs. violent motion. BCAP sections on δύναμις and ἐνέργεια should connect to this passage's analysis of the removal of hindrance as the condition for actualization.

## 2. χρόνος (Physics IV) --> B&T Zeitlichkeit (Being and Time)

### Connection

Physics IV.10--14 provides Aristotle's canonical account of time as "the number of motion in respect of before and after" (219b1--2). Heidegger's Being and Time (SS78--83) contains an explicit confrontation with this Aristotelian definition. Heidegger argues that Aristotle's account of time is the origin of the "vulgar" (ordinary/inauthentic) concept of time — time as a sequence of nows, measured by motion — which B&T seeks to ground in the more primordial temporality (Zeitlichkeit) of Dasein.

### Specific hooks

- **Physics IV.11 (219b1--2)**: "Time is the number of motion in respect of before and after." B&T SS78 directly engages this definition, arguing it captures only "world-time" (Weltzeit) — time as public, measurable, and derived from Dasein's primordial temporality.
- **Physics IV.11 (219b12--15)**: The now as "same in substratum but different in being." B&T SS81 treats the Aristotelian nun as the basis of the "now-time" (Jetzt-Zeit) that Heidegger calls the vulgar concept. The now-sequence (past-now, present-now, future-now) is the derivative form; authentic temporality is ecstatic (Ekstasen: Gewesenheit, Gegenwart, Zukunft).
- **Physics IV.14 (223a21--26)**: Time and soul — "if soul did not exist, would time exist?" B&T SS80--81 take this passage as evidence that Aristotle glimpsed the dependency of time on Dasein but failed to radicalize it, treating soul as a counter rather than as the being whose being is constituted by temporality.
- **Physics IV.12 (220a5)**: "Time is both made continuous by the now and divided at it." The dual character of the nun connects to Heidegger's analysis of the Augenblick (moment of vision) in B&T SS65, which is the authentic counterpart of the derivative "now."
- **Physics VIII.1 (251b11)**: "Time is an affection of motion." Heidegger argues this is the fundamental limitation of the Aristotelian account: it makes time dependent on motion (kinesis) rather than recognizing temporality as the horizon of being as such.

### Integration points

| Physics Concept | B&T Section | Hook Type |
|----------------|-------------|-----------|
| χρόνος = ἀριθμὸς κινήσεως (219b1) | SS78 (Aristotle's time-definition) | genealogical_target |
| τὸ νῦν (219b12--15) | SS81 (Jetzt-Zeit, now-time) | genealogical_target |
| ψυχή and time (223a21--26) | SS80 (time and Dasein) | conceptual_contestation |
| Before-and-after (219a14) | SS79 (datability, Datierbarkeit) | structural_parallel |
| Time as continuous (220a5) | SS65 (Augenblick vs. now-sequence) | contrasts_with |

[INTERP-high] — This is the most direct cross-pipeline connection. Heidegger's entire "destruction of the history of ontology" in B&T Division Two rests on a confrontation with the Aristotelian concept of time. The Physics IV analysis is not merely background but the explicit target text.

## 3. φύσις vs. τέχνη (Physics II) --> Uexkull, *A Foray into the Worlds of Animals and Humans* (Naturtechnik)

### Connection

Physics II.1 establishes the foundational distinction between nature (φύσις) and art (τέχνη): nature has its principle of motion and rest in the thing itself; art has its principle in the maker (192b20--33). Uexkull's concept of Naturtechnik ("nature-technology" or "natural technique") — the idea that organisms are constructed according to a Bauplan (blueprint) that integrates perceptual and effector organs into an integrated functional circle (Funktionskreis) — sits at the intersection of this distinction. For Uexkull, the organism is neither purely natural (a product of blind causation) nor purely artificial (a product of external design), but exhibits a technical character intrinsic to its nature.

### Specific hooks

- **Physics II.1 (192b20--23)**: Nature as internal principle. Uexkull's Umwelt theory treats the organism as self-organizing — its perceptual world (Merkwelt) and effector world (Wirkwelt) are generated from within, not imposed from without. This aligns with the Aristotelian internal principle but adds a subjective dimension: the organism does not merely have nature, it has a *world*.
- **Physics II.8 (199a12--18)**: "If a house had been a thing made by nature, it would have been made in the same way as it is now by art." Uexkull's Bauplan concept inverts this: natural organisms *are* constructed as if by art — the organism's functional plan is isomorphic with a technician's blueprint. The analogy runs in Uexkull's direction, not Aristotle's.
- **Physics II.8 (199b26--30)**: Purposive action without deliberation (spider, ant, swallow). Uexkull's functional circle provides the mechanism: the organism's Merknetz (perceptual net) and Wirknetz (effector net) form a closed loop that produces purposive behavior without requiring deliberation or representation in the cognitive sense.
- **Physics II.9 (200a14--15)**: "Necessity is in the matter, while that for the sake of which is in the definition." Uexkull's Bauplan is the analogue of the definition/form; the physical substrate is the analogue of matter. Hypothetical necessity in Uexkull: the organism's bodily structure is necessary *for* the functional plan, not the plan *because of* the structure.

### Integration points

| Physics Concept | Uexkull Concept | Hook Type |
|----------------|----------------|-----------|
| φύσις as internal principle (192b20) | Umwelt as self-generated perceptual world | structural_parallel |
| τέχνη: principle in maker (192b28) | Bauplan / Naturtechnik | conceptual_contestation |
| Teleology without deliberation (199b26) | Funktionskreis (functional circle) | structural_parallel |
| Hypothetical necessity (200a14) | Bauplan constrains substrate | structural_parallel |
| Art imitates/completes nature (199a12) | Nature exhibits technique intrinsically | conceptual_inversion |

[INTERP-high] — Uexkull's Naturtechnik challenges the Aristotelian phusis/technē distinction by arguing that organisms exhibit a technical character that is *natural* — not imposed from without but generated from within according to a plan. This is not a simple borrowing or break but a reconfiguration of the terms. The cross-pipeline integration must track how Uexkull transforms the Aristotelian categories.

## 4. ἐνέργεια / ἐντελέχεια (Physics III) --> De Anima II.1 (Soul as First Entelechy)

### Connection

Physics III.1 uses entelecheia in the definition of motion (201a10). De Anima II.1 uses the same term to define the soul: "the first actuality [entelecheia] of a natural body that has life potentially" (412a27). The Physics establishes the sense of entelecheia as *process* of actualizing (in motion), while De Anima uses it as *completed state* (first actuality, as opposed to second actuality = exercise). This semantic shift within the same technical term is a critical interpretive problem.

| Physics Usage | De Anima Usage | Tension |
|--------------|----------------|---------|
| entelecheia = process (building, not the house) | entelecheia = state (possessing knowledge, not exercising it) | same term, different aspect |
| energeia atelēs (incomplete actuality) | First entelechy (complete possession, not yet exercised) | "incomplete" vs. "first" |

[INTERP-high] — The cross-work semantic range of entelecheia/energeia is central to the dissertation's account of phantasia. If phantasia is "a kind of motion" (kinēsis tis, DA III.3 429a1), it inherits the Physics' definition of motion as incomplete actuality. But if the soul is a *first* actuality (a state, not a process), then the relation between the soul's being and the motions it undergoes requires careful articulation.

## 5. κίνησις and the Mover-Moved Relation (Physics VII.2) --> De Anima II.5--7 (Perception)

### Connection

Physics VII.2 argues that the mover and the moved must always be in contact, and demonstrates this for alteration by induction from sense-perception: "air is continuous with colour, colour with sight" (244b2--245a5). This passage directly anticipates De Anima II.7's medium theory of perception: perception occurs through a medium (air, water) that transmits the form of the sensible object to the sense organ. The Physics passage treats perception as an *example* of the general contact thesis; the De Anima treats it as the central topic.

| Physics VII | De Anima II | Connection |
|------------|------------|------------|
| Contact between alterer and altered (244b2) | Perception requires medium (DA II.7, 418a26-419b2) | same phenomenon, different emphasis |
| Air continuous with colour, colour with sight (244b5) | Air/water as medium for colour/sound (DA II.7) | direct parallel |
| Alteration restricted to sensible qualities (VII.3, 245a10) | Perception is reception of form without matter (DA II.12, 424a17) | same restriction, different formulation |

[INTERP-high] — The Physics provides the general causal framework (contact, alteration); the De Anima applies it specifically to perception. The dissertation's account of how rhetorical persuasion works through perceptual channels depends on this Physics-to-De Anima pathway.

## 6. Time, Soul, and the Now (Physics IV.14) --> On Memory and Recollection

### Connection

Physics IV.14's claim that time depends on soul as counter (223a21--26) connects directly to On Memory's account of temporal perception: memory is always of the past and involves a lapse of time (449b25). The successive infinity of time (III.6, 206a26--27: "in time, in the generations of man, and in the division of magnitudes") provides the ontological framework for the temporal structure of memory and recollection. The now as boundary between past and future (IV.13, 222a10) is the structural condition for the possibility of remembering: one can only remember what is *past*, i.e., what is on the far side of the now.

[INTERP-high] — The Physics' temporal ontology is the necessary background for On Memory's phenomenology of temporal experience.

## 7. The Unmoved Mover (Physics VIII) --> Metaphysics Lambda (XII)

### Connection

The unmoved mover argument of Physics VIII is restated and deepened in Metaphysics XII.6--7 (1071b--1072b), where the unmoved mover is identified with pure thought thinking itself (noēsis noēseōs) and is said to move as an object of desire (kinei hōs erōmenon, 1072b3). Physics VIII provides the kinematic proof; Metaphysics Lambda provides the theological/noetic characterization. The cross-pipeline integration must track the transformation from a physical principle (that which causes continuous circular motion) to a metaphysical/theological principle (pure self-thinking thought that moves by being desired).

[INTERP-high] — The relation between the kinematic and theological characterizations of the unmoved mover is one of the most debated issues in Aristotle scholarship.
