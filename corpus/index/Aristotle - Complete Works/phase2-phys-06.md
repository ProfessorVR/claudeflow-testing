## 3. Key Concepts + Positions

| # | Concept / Position | Type | Definition or Claim | Key Passage |
|---|-------------------|------|---------------------|-------------|
| 1 | the continuous (to suneches) | concept | That whose extremities are one; infinitely divisible into always-divisible parts; nothing continuous can be composed of indivisibles | 231a21–b18 (PDF 78–79) |
| 2 | the indivisible / partless (to adiaireton) | concept | That which has no parts; points and nows; cannot compose a continuum because indivisibles lack extremities for contact or succession | 231a24 (PDF 78), 240b8 (PDF 93) |
| 3 | the now (to nun) | concept | The indivisible limit between past and future; inherent in all time; nothing moves or rests in a now | 233b33–234a31 (PDF 83) |
| 4 | the quicker (to thatton) | concept | That which traverses a greater magnitude in equal time, or an equal magnitude in less time; the quicker-slower distinction proves time is continuous | 232a25–233a12 (PDF 80–82) |
| P1 | Magnitude, motion, and time are co-divisible: if any one is composed of indivisibles all are, and if any one is infinitely divisible all are | position | If magnitude ABC is composed of indivisibles A, B, C, the motion DEF over it must consist of indivisible motions D, E, F; but then a thing could complete a passage without being in motion, which is absurd | 231b18–232a22 (PDF 79–80) |
| P2 | Zeno's four paradoxes rest on false assumptions about the composition of continua | position | The Dichotomy and Achilles falsely assume infinitely many sub-distances cannot be traversed in finite time; the Arrow falsely assumes time is composed of indivisible nows; the Stadium confuses relative and absolute motion | 239b5–240a18 (PDF 92–93) |
| P3 | That which is without parts cannot be in motion except accidentally | position | A partless thing in motion would need to traverse a distance less than itself, but nothing is less than the partless; the line would then be composed of points, which has been shown impossible | 240b8–241a26 (PDF 93–94) |
| P4 | No change is infinite except rotary locomotion | position | Every non-rotary change is from something to something between contraries and is therefore finite; only circular motion can continue without limit | 241a26–b20 (PDF 95) |
