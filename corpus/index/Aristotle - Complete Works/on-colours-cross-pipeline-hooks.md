# Cross-Pipeline Hooks — On Colours

**Source**: Phase 3 ontology of *On Colours* (COL-01).
**Date**: 2026-03-09

---

## 1. χρῶμα → Sense and Sensibilia colour theory [INTERP-high]

**Aristotelian concept**: χρῶμα (C1) — simple colours derived from the four elements; intermediate colours from mixture and blending.

**Sense and Sensibilia connection**: De Coloribus provides the empirical-observational complement to the more ontologically sophisticated colour theory of De Sensu ch. 3. Where De Sensu defines colour as "the limit of the transparent in a determinately bounded body" (439b10-14) and develops the three hypotheses of colour generation (juxtaposition, superposition, mixture), De Coloribus grounds colour in elemental composition and extends the analysis to biological chromatic change (plants, animals). The De Coloribus lacks the concept of τὸ διαφανές entirely, which either indicates an earlier stage of the theory, a different author, or a different level of exposition.

**Key passages**: 791a1-12 (simple colours from elements), 792a5-b25 (intermediate colours from blending), 794b10-799b (biological colour changes).

**Integration priority**: HIGH — internal to the Aristotelian colour theory; required for a complete account.

---

## 2. πέψις (maturation) → Biological teleology [INTERP-med]

**Aristotelian concept**: πέψις (C4) — the maturation process driving chromatic change in plants and animals through heat, nutriment, and moisture.

**Cross-pipeline relevance**: The maturation framework is a specific application of Aristotelian teleology to biological phenomena: colour changes are not random but follow a determinate sequence (herb-green → yellow → crimson → wine-colour → black) governed by the organism's natural telos. This connects to the broader question of whether perception (αἴσθησις) and its objects (χρῶμα, ψόφος) are themselves teleologically organized — a question that bears on both the De Anima psychology and the Uexkullian functional circle.

**Uexkull connection**: The De Coloribus observation that different species take different colours from the same maturation process — "each species takes colour from its juice as dyed material takes colour from dye" (795a25) — resonates with Uexkull's thesis that the same environment presents different Merkwelten to different organisms. Colour is not a fixed property of objects but an organism-relative phenomenon.

**Key passages**: 794b10-796a30 (plant maturation and chromatic sequence), 797a1-799b (animal colour and nutriment).

**Integration priority**: MEDIUM — connects to Uexkull but the link requires mediation.

---

## 3. λευκόν as deviation/weakness → Privation ontology [INTERP-low]

**Aristotelian concept**: White as the colour of "deviation from natural colour" (798a25-b5), associated with weakness, ageing, and premature maturation.

**BCAP connection**: Heidegger in GA 18 treats στέρησις (privation) as "positively constitutive of being — not mere absence." The De Coloribus characterization of white as a deviation-colour (not a pure positive quality) resonates with this: white in the biological domain is not the presence of a quality but the absence of proper colouring, i.e., privation of the colour that would be natural to the organism. This contrasts with the De Sensu account where white is one of two positive extremes of the colour spectrum.

**Key passages**: 798a25-b10 (white as deviation; white animals weaker than black), 797a1-10 (white when moisture dries up in maturation).

**Integration priority**: LOW — the connection to BCAP's privation ontology is structurally interesting but the texts are far apart.

---

## 4. Dyeing analogy → Techne and nature [INTERP-low]

**Aristotelian concept**: βαφή (I3) — dyeing as the paradigm case of colour transference; used as analogy for natural processes (fruit maturation, animal colouring).

**Cross-pipeline relevance**: The use of a τέχνη (dyeing) as model for understanding φύσις (natural colour change) instantiates the broader Aristotelian principle that art imitates nature and nature operates like art. This τέχνη-φύσις analogy is central to Physics II.1-2 and has implications for the dissertation's argument about the relationship between rhetorical τέχνη and the natural dispositions (πάθη, ἕξεις) that rhetoric engages.

**Key passages**: 794a15-b10 (dyeing as colour transference), 795b5-20 (dyeing analogy for fruit maturation: "as moisture is heated and boiled, hues separate and recombine").

**Integration priority**: LOW — supports the techne-physis argument but indirectly.
