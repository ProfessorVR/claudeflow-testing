# Cross-Pipeline Hooks — On Memory

**Source**: Phase 3 ontology of *On Memory and Reminiscence* (MEM-01).
**Date**: 2026-03-09

---

## 1. φάντασμα as memory-image → B&T temporality [INTERP-high]

**Aristotelian concept**: φάντασμα (C3) — the dual-aspect memory-image that functions simultaneously as an object of contemplation in itself and as a likeness (εἰκών) of the thing remembered.

**B&T connection**: The φάντασμα's dual nature — present affection *qua* likeness of an absent original — raises the question of temporal transcendence that is central to B&T's analysis of Dasein's temporality. Memory is essentially directed at the past (449b10-15), but its medium is a present image. This structure of making-present-what-is-absent parallels Heidegger's analysis of Gegenwartigung (making-present) in B&T Section 69, where Dasein's present is always structured by retention (Gewesenheit) and anticipation. The wax-seal model (τύπος, 450a27-b1) provides the Aristotelian material for Heidegger's claim that the past is not simply gone but retained in a present disposition.

**Key passages**: 450b11-451a2 (the dual-aspect puzzle), 449b24-25 (memory as ἕξις/πάθος conditioned by time), 452b7-24 (time as magnitude in recollection).

**Integration priority**: HIGH — φάντασμα as retention-of-the-past-in-the-present is directly relevant to the dissertation's argument about temporal experience.

## 2. μνήμη / ἀνάμνησις → B&T retention and retrieval [INTERP-high]

**Aristotelian concept**: μνήμη (C1) as passive retention conditioned by time; ἀνάμνησις (C2) as active inferential recovery through sequential reactivation of movements.

**B&T connection**: The μνήμη/ἀνάμνησις distinction maps onto Heidegger's analysis of two modes of temporal comportment: (1) the tacit retention of the past in present disposition (Gewesenheit), parallel to μνήμη as ἕξις that simply persists; (2) the explicit retrieval (Wiederholung) of the past through deliberate engagement, parallel to ἀνάμνησις as ζήτησις requiring the βουλευτικόν. The Aristotelian thesis that recollection is "as it were, a mode of inference" (453a10) anticipates the hermeneutic-deliberative character of Heidegger's Wiederholung.

**Key passages**: 449b24-25 (μνήμη as dispositional state), 451a18-22 (ἀνάμνησις distinguished from μνήμη), 453a10-14 (recollection as inference/investigation).

**Integration priority**: HIGH — the μνήμη/ἀνάμνησις distinction structures the argument about passive vs. active temporal experience.

## 3. ἀνάμνησις as ζήτησις → Rhetorical τόποι [INTERP-high]

**Aristotelian concept**: ἀνάμνησις operates through "places" (τόποι) — starting-points from which recollection proceeds by hunting up associations (similarity, contrariety, contiguity).

**Rhetorical connection**: The recollective τόποι are structurally parallel to the τόποι of rhetorical invention (Rhetoric I-II). In both cases, the agent begins from a starting-point and moves through a network of associated items to arrive at the sought conclusion. The De Memoria account makes explicit that this process is governed by custom (ἔθος/συνήθεια, 451b28-452b1), which creates quasi-natural pathways — parallel to the habituated pathways of rhetorical invention.

**Rickert connection**: This link supports Rickert's "ambient rhetoric" thesis: rhetorical invention is not a deliberate, fully conscious process but operates through habituated associative networks — the same networks that govern recollection. The De Memoria account shows that even Aristotle's own psychology locates the inferential process of recovery at the boundary of rational deliberation and quasi-mechanical association.

**Key passages**: 452a12-16 (method of places: milk → white → mist → moist → Autumn), 451b28-452b1 (custom creates quasi-natural sequences), 452b1-7 (hunting up the series from similar, contrary, or contiguous items).

**Integration priority**: HIGH — directly bridges Aristotelian psychology to rhetorical theory via the shared τόποι structure.

## 4. κίνησις (residual movements) → BCAP on κίνησις [INTERP-high]

**Aristotelian concept**: κίνησις (C5) — residual physiological movements left by perception; their sequential reactivation constitutes recollection; movements succeed one another by nature, custom, or association.

**BCAP connection**: Heidegger in GA 18 treats κίνησις as the culminating concept of Aristotelian ontology (defined as ἐντελέχεια τοῦ δυνάμει ὄντος). The De Memoria deployment of κίνησις for residual perceptual movements shows a specific application: the past is retained not as a static record but as a dynamically reactivatable movement-chain. This resonates with Heidegger's emphasis that κίνησις is not merely physical motion but a mode of being-at-work (ἐνέργεια). Memory-movements are ἐνέργεια in miniature: the past persisting in the present as a movement capable of being reactivated.

**Key passages**: 451b10-16 (movements succeed one another by nature or custom), 452a7-12 (remembering = existence of a movement capable of stimulating the desired movement), 452a30-b1 (frequency creates nature).

**Integration priority**: HIGH — κίνησις as the ontological category uniting physics, psychology, and memory theory.

## 5. τὸ πρῶτον αἰσθητικόν (common sense) → Sense and Sensibilia [INTERP-med]

**Aristotelian concept**: τὸ πρῶτον αἰσθητικόν (I1) — the primary perceptive faculty / common sense, which cognizes time, magnitude, and motion; the organ to which memory essentially belongs.

**Sense and Sensibilia connection**: The De Memoria account that memory belongs to the common sense (450a12-14) presupposes the De Sensu analysis of the common sense as "numerically one but different in being" (449a5-20). Memory is thus a temporal function of the same unified faculty that enables simultaneous cross-modal perception. This places memory within the perceptual apparatus rather than the intellect — a claim with consequences for the dissertation's argument that phantasia and memory are embodied, environmental phenomena.

**Key passages**: 450a9-14 (memory belongs to the primary perceptive faculty), 450a15-19 (not only humans but certain other animals possess memory).

**Integration priority**: MEDIUM — bridges De Memoria to De Sensu and De Anima III.1-2.

---

## 6. τύπος (wax-seal model) → Embodied cognition [INTERP-med]

**Aristotelian concept**: τύπος (I3) — the impression stamped in the soul and its bodily seat; the wax-seal analogy (450a27-b1); physiological conditions (moisture, hardness) explain individual differences.

**Cross-pipeline relevance**: The τύπος model grounds memory in the body's material conditions — moisture, hardness, age, passion. This is the strongest textual support for reading Aristotelian memory as embodied cognition rather than disembodied information storage. The claim that "those strongly moved by passion, the very young, and the very old are defective" because the receiving surface is too fluid or too hard (450b1-11) makes memory a function of bodily constitution, not merely cognitive capacity.

**Uexkull connection**: The body-dependent character of memory reinforces the Uexkullian thesis that the organism's world is constituted by its bodily capacities. Different bodily constitutions yield different mnemonic worlds.

**Key passages**: 450a27-b11 (wax-seal analogy and physiological conditions), 453a14-b7 (corporeal substrate of recollection; melancholic persons moved by images).

**Integration priority**: MEDIUM — supports the embodied-cognition argument across multiple pipelines.
