## Supports the framing — presence is not captured by a score

- **Riva p.159:** "a core neuropsychological phenomenon whose goal is to produce a sense of agency and control" — presence is cognition (enacting intentions), not a media effect a score can capture.
- **Riva p.159:** "any environment, virtual or real, does not provide undifferentiated information" — the world is a differentiated field of opportunities, not uniform information.
- **Riva p.159:** "It offers different opportunities and produces presence according to its ability in supporting the users and their intentions."
- **Mantovani & Riva `****** UNVERIFIED:`** "From the viewpoint of ingenuous realism, 'real' presence designates a state of things, the way an object is…" — the naive-realist criterion the paper rejects (OCR-only; re-verify).
- **Spagnolli p.138:** "as a method to investigate presence and social presence" (full: "…the analysis of participants' action as a method…") — the field itself turns to *action*, not only self-report.

## Sharpens / complicates — an instrument that does track world-openness

- **Lepecq p.141:** "This behavioral adjustment is thus assumed to be an objective indication of presence" — a behavioral index (afforded action) that measures presence *through the body's disclosure of the world*.
- **Lepecq p.141:** "every afforded action could be a potential tool for sensorimotor assessment of physical presence" — generalizes the disclosure-faithful measure.

## Part II bridge (world-disclosedness) — see `bridge-to-part-ii.md`

- **Lepecq (PRIMARY)** and **Mantovani & Riva** carry presence-as-embodied/ontological-disclosure into the Uexküll/Umwelt neighborhood.

## Suggested disposition
1. Use Riva (p.159) as the theoretical warrant that presence is enacted intention in a disclosed world, not a scored feeling.
2. Use Mantovani & Riva (once re-verified) for the deeper point that the *criterion* itself is ontology-relative.
3. Use Lepecq (p.141) as the honest counter-weight: a behavioral measure *can* track disclosure (for physical presence) — so the introduction's claim should target self-report scoring specifically, not all measurement.
