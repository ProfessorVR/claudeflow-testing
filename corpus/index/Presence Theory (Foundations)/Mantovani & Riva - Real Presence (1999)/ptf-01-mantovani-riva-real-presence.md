## 1. Thesis / Aim (paraphrase)

Mantovani & Riva argue that what counts as "real" presence is **not fixed** but is generated by the **ontology** the observer presupposes. On a **naive / "ingenuous" realist** ontology, presence means the faithful reproduction of a mind-independent physical state of affairs — presence is high when the mediated environment corresponds to how things "really" are. On a **cultural-constructivist / situated-action** ontology, reality (and therefore presence) is socially and culturally mediated — always an interpretation, never a bare copy — so the reference point for "real" presence is not the physical environment but the **ecology of relations between actor and (culturally mediated) environment**. Different ontologies thus generate different, non-interchangeable criteria for presence, telepresence, and virtual presence. [paraphrase — FE-substance]

## 2. Key constructs (paraphrase)

- **Ontology-dependence of presence criteria** (core) — the article's central claim: no criterion of presence is ontology-neutral.
- **Ingenuous/naive realism vs. cultural constructivism** (core) — the two ontologies contrasted; the former equates presence with faithful physical reproduction, the latter with culturally-mediated, situated action.
- **Presence as social construction / situated action** (core) — presence is always mediated by physical *and* conceptual tools and is constituted in a culturally-defined situation, not read off a mind-independent world.
- **Actor–environment ecology as the reference of presence** (important) — the criterion of "real" presence is the actor's meaningful engagement within a situation, not correspondence to the physical world.

## 5. Relevance to the Part III presence framing

The Part III introduction frames presence as a feeling the field's instruments "score" but which cannot "say how a world is opened to its inhabitants." Mantovani & Riva **sharpen and support** that framing at the level of *criteria*: they show the very yardstick of presence is ontology-relative, so a single scored number ("presence = X") already presupposes an ontology and cannot, by itself, say *how* the world is disclosed.
