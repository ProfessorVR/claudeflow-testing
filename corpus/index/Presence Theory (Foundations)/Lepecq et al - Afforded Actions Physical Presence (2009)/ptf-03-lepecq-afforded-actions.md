## 1. Thesis / Aim

Lepecq et al. propose an **objective, behavioral** measure of **physical presence** in VR, grounded in a **Gibsonian affordance**. Subjects walk through virtual apertures of varying width; in the presence of a genuinely disclosed spatial layout, body orientation while walking should be **adapted to the aperture width relative to the walker's own shoulder width**. Most subjects show exactly this — a **behavioral transition from frontal walking to shoulder-rotation** as apertures narrow, the same transition Warren & Whang (1987) documented in *real* environments. This adjustment "is thus assumed to be an objective indication of presence" (FE, p.141), and the paper generalizes: "every afforded action could be a potential tool for sensorimotor assessment of physical presence" (FE, abstract, p.141).

## 2. Structure

Abstract + Introduction (pp.141–143): the polysemy of presence; the ISPR definition; the proposal to assess presence not "as a whole" but via a specific afforded action (aperture-crossing). Method (pp.143–146): virtual apertures, body-scaled ratios, walking task. Results (pp.146–149): the frontal-to-rotation transition tracks both body architecture and aperture width; common to both genders. Discussion/Conclusion (pp.149–151): the afforded action "behaviorally objectivates physical presence in VR"; calls for psychometric-validity work. Runs pp. 141–151.

## 3. Key constructs

- **Afforded action as behavioral presence measure** (core, FE) — presence indexed by an action the environment affords, not by self-report.
- **Physical / spatial presence** (core, FE) — the specific presence-type at issue (body-in-space), distinct from social presence.
- **Body-scaled affordance (aperture-crossing)** (core, FE) — the Warren & Whang shoulder-rotation transition, reproduced in VR.
- **Objective vs. self-report assessment** (important, FE) — a falsifiable behavioral index against the presence field's questionnaire tradition.

## 5. Bridge to Part II world-disclosedness (PRIMARY — record only, no new cross-entry edges)

**This is the cluster's strongest Part II bridge** (user-flagged). Lepecq operationalizes presence as **attunement of the lived body to what the environment affords** — the walker rotates because the aperture *is disclosed as passable-or-not relative to this body*. That is world-disclosedness in a psychophysical register: the environment shows up as a field of bodily possibilities (an Umwelt of affordances), and presence is the body's responsive coping with it, not a mental representation. This is the concrete, empirical hinge to the **Von Uexküll / Umwelt** material (`corpus/index/Von Uexkull - A Foray into the Worlds of Animals and Humans/`) and to Part II's disclosedness vocabulary: Gibsonian affordance ↔ Uexküllian Umwelt ↔ Heideggerian readiness-to-hand / world-disclosedness. ## 6. Relevance to the Part III presence framing

Cuts closest to the introduction's contrast ("instruments score [presence] but cannot say how a world is opened to its inhabitants"). Lepecq offers an *instrument* — but one that, unlike a questionnaire, measures presence *through the body's disclosure of the world* (the afforded action). It thus both **supports** the introduction (a behavioral index is more faithful to how a world opens than a self-report score) and **complicates** it (here is a rigorous measure that does track world-openness, at least for physical presence). Useful in Part III as the empirical counter-weight showing what a presence measure would have to look like to register disclosure.
