## 1. Thesis / Aim

Riva argues presence is **not fundamentally a technology issue.** Against the ISPR/Sheridan lineage that ties presence to media ("the effect felt when… immers[ed] in virtual environments"), he draws on cognitive science to redefine presence as a **core neuropsychological phenomenon** whose function is to produce a **sense of agency and control**: one is "present" to the degree one can **enact one's intentions** in an external world — virtual *or* real (FE, abstract, p.159). It follows that no environment delivers ready-made, uniform presence; an environment "offers different opportunities and produces presence according to its ability in supporting the users and their intentions" (FE, p.159).

## 3. Key constructs

- **Presence as cognition, not technology** (core, FE) — presence is a neuropsychological function, not a property of a medium.
- **Presence = enacting intentions (agency/control)** (core, FE) — one is present insofar as one can realize one's intentions in a world.
- **Differential opportunity structure of the environment** (core, FE) — an environment offers differentiated action-opportunities, not undifferentiated, equal-for-everyone information.
- **Presence in any environment (virtual or real)** (important, FE) — presence is not exclusive to mediated settings.

## 5. Bridge to Part II world-disclosedness (record only)

Riva's "differentiated opportunity structure" and "enacting intentions" map onto **world-disclosedness / Umwelt** (Von Uexküll entry): the environment is not a neutral container of equal information but a field of action-opportunities *disclosed relative to an agent's intentions* — the cognitive-science cousin of the phenomenological point. ## 6. Relevance to the Part III presence framing

Directly supports the introduction's framing ("presence names a feeling; the field's instruments score it but cannot say how a world is opened to its inhabitants"). Riva **relocates the very object**: presence is not a media effect to be scored but the achievement of enacting intentions in a disclosed world — so a presence score misses the action-and-intention structure that constitutes it.
