## 1. Unit Metadata

**One-sentence thesis**: Heidegger reads *De Partibus Animalium* A1 to establish that the methodological disposition of paideia (assurance of manner of treatment) is more decisive than episteme (concrete knowledge) for investigating phuseia ginomena, that the ou heneka (the "for-the-sake-of-which") as the primary logos secures eidos over hule and thereby reveals psuche as the genuine topic of the phusikos within restricted limits, and then pivots to *NE* K1-5 to reinterpret hedone and lupe not as "pleasure and pain" but as ontological determinations of being-in-the-world -- modes of how living beings are at each moment in their being-there, complete en toi nun ("in the moment"), grounded not in circumstance but in being-there itself.

The section opens with a transitional question from the pathe analysis of SS18: since pathe involve corporeality, and living bodies are the domain of the phusikos, in what field does the investigation of beings with the "character of living things" belong? (p. 140). The answer requires a sustained digression into the methodology of natural philosophy, drawing on *De Partibus Animalium* Book 1, Chapter 1.

**SS19a** (pp. 140-142): The two types of hexis theorias -- (1) episteme, concrete knowledge of a subject, and (2) paideia, assurance of the manner of treatment. The pepaideumenos can judge *how* a matter is treated without specialist knowledge of the subject itself.

**SS19b** (pp. 142-145): The decisive paideia for investigating phuseia ginomena. The primary question is the dia ti -- the "wherefore" of beings. Two possibilities: the ou heneka (the "for-the-sake-of-which") and the arche tes kineseos (the "whence of movement"). Aristotle determines that the ou heneka is primary, and that the ou heneka is a logos. Extended discussion of logos as "addressing" (*Ansprechen*): logos as access (apophainesthai), as answer (how beings address themselves to us), and as both exhibiting and expressing. The significance: psuche emerges as the topic of the phusikos because both being-present and being-produced are determinations of living beings as zoa. The look (eidos) of a being, not its material substrate, is the primary respect.

**SS19c** (pp. 146-156): The determination of independent logos in relation to phuseia ginomena. Three sub-questions: (alpha) how techne looks in relation to its erga -- the "if-then" structure of the logos of techne, where eidos is anticipated and hule is co-addressed as entirely determinate; (beta) the being-characters of phuseia ginomena as self-moving, self-completing beings whose primary experience is "something is in movement in such a way that it comes to an end"; (gamma) criticism of the ancient physiologists who investigated phusis from hule rather than eidos.

**SS19d** (pp. 156-161): The dual proof of the restricted scope of the phusikos. (alpha) Indirect proof: if the phusikos studied all living things, "there would be no philosophy beside this science" (p. 157); (beta) Proof from being-moved itself: three modes of movement (auxesis, alloiosis, phora) define the actual scope.

**SS19e** (p. 161): Science advances by being "compelled by truth itself" -- Empedocles "led by truth itself," Democritus "borne by the matter itself." The Socratic turn from phusis-research to politike was a fundamental oversight.

**SS20** (pp. 162-166): Hedone is not subjective pleasure but "a fundamental determination of being-in-the-world" (p. 163). Hedone is en toi nun -- "in the moment" -- not a kinesis but a determination of the presentness of being-there. The pathe are possessed in the dual possibilities of hairesis (seizing) and phuge (fleeing), the basic motivations of being-there.

## 3. Key Concepts

### 3.1 phusikos
- **Greek form**: phusikos
- **Definition**: The natural philosopher; one whose investigation treats the phuseia ginomena and must therefore also treat psuche within certain limits
- **Type**: investigator-type / methodological concept
- **Dependencies**: episteme, paideia, logos, phuseia ginomena
- **Function**: Heidegger uses the phusikos as the exemplary case for showing how the scope and manner of an investigation is determined by the being-character of what is investigated. The phusikos must draw psuche into consideration because every living soma is a phuseia on whose being-character corporeality (pathe) depends.
- **Pages**: 140, 148, 152, 156-161

### 3.2 episteme
- **Greek form**: episteme
- **Definition**: Concrete knowledge of a subject area; the first of two types of hexis theorias
- **Type**: epistemic disposition
- **Dependencies**: hexis, theoria
- **Function**: Distinguished from paideia; episteme is knowledge *about* the subject, whereas paideia is assurance of the *manner of treatment*. The more decisive disposition for research is paideia, not episteme.
- **Pages**: 140-141

### 3.3 paideia
- **Greek form**: paideia
- **Definition**: Assurance of the manner of treatment; the proper methodological instinct for how to approach a subject area; the second and more decisive type of hexis theorias
- **Type**: epistemic-methodological disposition
- **Dependencies**: hexis, theoria, episteme
- **Function**: The pepaideumenos (one possessing paideia) can judge whether a speaker treats the matter "from the proper basic relation to the matter" or merely from hearsay. Paideia is characterized as hexis, being-free, the peculiarly reflective being-open for a definite concrete content. It is "entirely neglected" today, "even more difficult to obtain" (p. 141).
- **Pages**: 141-142

### 3.4 ou heneka
- **Greek form**: hou heneka
- **Definition**: The "for-the-sake-of-which"; the telos as the primary respect under which phuseia ginomena are to be investigated
- **Type**: causal-ontological principle
- **Dependencies**: logos, telos, arche, phuseia ginomena
- **Function**: The decisive paideia for investigating phuseia ginomena is that the ou heneka is the primary logos. The ou heneka is the primary respect because from the matter itself it appears that "the wherefore is the first dia ti" (p. 142). This is not teleology in the modern sense but the claim that phuseia ginomena are primarily intelligible from their being-completed.
- **Pages**: 142-145, 150-151, 160

### 3.5 logos (triple sense)
- **Greek form**: logos
- **Definition**: In this context, Heidegger identifies three senses: (1) logos as apophainesthai -- exhibiting a being in the sense of access; (2) logos as answer -- how beings "address themselves" to us, how a matter calls; (3) logos as *both* exhibiting and expressing, where what is expressed and what is exhibited may not coincide
- **Type**: ontological-disclosive concept
- **Dependencies**: apophainesthai, eidos, arche
- **Function**: The triple determination establishes that the "wherefore" of investigation is decided by how beings show themselves. Logos is often identical to eidos (p. 143). The dual meaning grounds the entire analysis of how the phusikos's investigation proceeds.
- **Pages**: 142-146, 148-149, 152-153, 160

### 3.6 phuseia ginomena
- **Greek form**: phusei ginomena
- **Definition**: Beings that come to be by nature; natural beings characterized by self-producing, being-there as aei
- **Type**: ontological category
- **Dependencies**: phusis, genesis, ou heneka, telos
- **Function**: Distinguished from pragmata (products of techne) by the fact that they do not require production by others. Their genesis is their being-character. They are "already there for us" and their being is self-producing. The primary experience of them is of a "self-moving being-completed."
- **Pages**: 142-145, 147, 150-152

### 3.7 techne
- **Greek form**: techne
- **Definition**: Art/craft; the mode of producing guided by a knowing-the-way-around; characterized by poiesis as the logos of its erga
- **Type**: productive mode of being / analogical foil
- **Dependencies**: logos, eidos, hule, poiesis
- **Function**: Provides the proximate analogy for understanding the logos of phuseia ginomena. In techne, the logos is logos tou ergou -- the anticipation of the completed work. But the phuseia ginomena differ fundamentally: they do not require production by another. The comparison to techne reveals the distinctive being-characters of natural beings.
- **Pages**: 146-150, 153-156

### 3.8 eidos
- **Greek form**: eidos
- **Definition**: The look of a being; what determines a being in its being-there; "outline of a being in the way that it is furnished with respect to its look"; not Platonic "idea" but "look" (a front against Plato, p. 154)
- **Type**: ontological determination
- **Dependencies**: logos, hule, morphe, schema
- **Function**: Eidos is authoritative for the investigation of nature: only when eidos is made visible can one investigate the out-of-which of lasting (genesis). With respect to logos, hule is dependent on eidos. The single use of "idea" in Aristotle means "nothing other than 'look'" (p. 154).
- **Pages**: 149-150, 153-156, 162

### 3.9 hule
- **Greek form**: hule
- **Definition**: Material/stuff; not indeterminate stuff but the determinate suitability for application; dunamis, the positive possibility visible only from eidos
- **Type**: ontological determination
- **Dependencies**: eidos, logos, dunamis
- **Function**: Heidegger insists hule is not "indefinite stuff or limit of form" but the determinate -- "this or that suitability, on whose basis it comes into consideration as hule" (p. 153). In living beings, hule is organon -- the stuff of self-producing being. The soma must be organized, characterized by the determinate being of being-able (dunamis), and this means: determined by psuche as sarx ("flesh"), not mere material.
- **Pages**: 148-149, 152-153, 155-156, 160

### 3.10 telos
- **Greek form**: telos
- **Definition**: Not "purpose" or "aim-directedness" but "being-there-completed," "being-completed"; the genuine logos of a being; = peras
- **Type**: ontological-causal determination
- **Dependencies**: ou heneka, logos, genesis, eidos
- **Function**: "Telos = peras. These are clues for the basic sense of Greek ontology" (p. 144). The path of coming-into-its-being of a being, the telos, is the ou heneka. With respect to the phuseia onta, telos is the way the animal is there in its being-there, moving itself.
- **Pages**: 143-144, 149, 151, 160

### 3.11 psuche
- **Greek form**: psuche
- **Definition**: Soul; not a metaphysical substance but phainomenon psuches -- the phenomenal fact that must become visible because it belongs to the being-there of living beings (zoa)
- **Type**: phenomenon / object of the phusikos's investigation
- **Dependencies**: phusikos, zoa, pathos, eidos, soma
- **Function**: Psuche emerges as the genuine topic of the phusikos because the phusikos wants to see living things *as living things*. If being in a soma belongs to living things, then the soma and thus the pathe belong to the correct grasping of the basic phenomenon. What the ancients had not observed "appears as a *being-able*, which determines the being of hule, what we designate as soul" (p. 156).
- **Pages**: 140, 145, 148, 152, 156-158

### 3.12 hedone
- **Greek form**: hedone
- **Definition**: Not "pleasure" but a fundamental determination of being-in-the-world -- the genuine being-character of living beings as such; the tendency toward being-there-completed; a determination of the presentness of being-there
- **Type**: ontological-affective determination / pathos
- **Dependencies**: pathos, energeia, phusis, zoe, aisthesis
- **Function**: Every living thing tends toward its oikeion agathon -- this is hedone. It is not a psychological "pleasure" but the being-character of finding-oneself in being-in-the-world. Hedone is identified with zoe, "living." It is en toi nun -- "in the moment," complete at each moment like aisthesis or horasis, not a kinesis. "Hedone is in itself already there with being as living" (p. 165).
- **Pages**: 162-166

### 3.13 lupe
- **Greek form**: lupe
- **Definition**: Not "pain" but the mode of finding-oneself as losing-composure, being-toned-down-in-attunement; the counter-determination to hedone
- **Type**: ontological-affective determination / pathos
- **Dependencies**: pathos, hedone, phusis
- **Function**: Lupe is the dispositional counterpart to hedone. Every hedone is a definite one, every lupe is a definite one (p. 165). Together with hedone, lupe constitutes the basic mode of finding-oneself (sich-Befinden) in being-in-the-world.
- **Pages**: 162, 165-166

### 3.14 pathos/pathe
- **Greek form**: pathos/pathe
- **Definition**: Not "emotion" or "affect" but modes of being-taken with respect to being-in-the-world; determinations of the being-possibility of living beings co-determined by soma
- **Type**: ontological category of living being
- **Dependencies**: hedone, lupe, psuche, soma, eidos
- **Function**: The pathe are (1) things occurring in the soul, in living-being -- being-taken, losing-composure, kineisthai; (2) modes of being-taken with respect to being-in-the-world; (3) that through which "the possibilities of orienting oneself in the world are determined essentially" (p. 162). Being-out-of-composure is in itself related to being composed (hexis). The pathe are possessed in the dual possibilities of hairesis (seizing) and phuge (fleeing).
- **Pages**: 140, 152, 156, 162-163, 166

## 4. Main Positions

### U-FP3d-P1: The phusikos must draw psuche into consideration within certain limits
Every soma of the phusikos is a toiouton, determined as such, and so the phusikos is obligated to determine this toiouton "from the outset, to determine the hule in its positive sense" (p. 140). But the obligation extends to psuche only insofar as psuche belongs to the being of the phuseia onta under investigation -- not to psuche in its entirety.
- **Source**: p. 140; De part. an. A1
- **Status**: Explicitly stated

### U-FP3d-P2: Episteme and paideia are two fundamentally distinct types of hexis theorias
Concrete knowledge of a subject (episteme) is categorically different from assurance of the manner of treatment (paideia). The pepaideumenos can judge whether a speaker treats a matter from the proper basic relation to it -- whether access is originary or hearsay -- without possessing specialist knowledge. This is not technique or method in the modern sense but "being-free, the peculiarly reflective being-open for a definite concrete content" (p. 141). Heidegger emphasizes this hexis is "entirely neglected" today.
- **Source**: pp. 140-141; De part. an. A1, 639a1 sqq.
- **Status**: Explicitly stated

### U-FP3d-P3: The decisive paideia for investigating phuseia ginomena gives primacy to the ou heneka over the arche tes kineseos
When investigating the dia ti of natural beings, there are two possible respects: the ou heneka and the hothen he arche tes kineseos. Aristotle decides that the ou heneka is first "from the matter itself" -- from looking at the phuseia onta as zoa. The reasoning: the ou heneka is a logos, and "for logos is, in the domain of beings, what exists, what is there, in like manner within the orbit of beings of production, as within the beings that are there as phuseia on, the arche" (p. 142, citing 639b14 sqq.).
- **Source**: pp. 142-143; De part. an. A1, 639b14 sqq.
- **Status**: Explicitly stated and grounded

### U-FP3d-P4: The ou heneka is a logos, and logos means "address" in the double sense of access and answer
Heidegger unpacks logos through three senses: (1) logos as apophainesthai -- exhibiting a being in the sense of access; (2) logos as how beings "address themselves" to us, how a matter calls and one answers; (3) logos as *both* exhibiting and expressing, where what is expressed and what is exhibited may not coincide ("I thereby do not genuinely bring expressing to completion," p. 146). This triple determination establishes that the "wherefore" of investigation is decided by how beings show themselves, not by a prior theoretical framework.
- **Source**: pp. 143, 146; De part. an. A1, 639b14 sqq.
- **Status**: Heidegger's interpretive construction; draws on but extends Aristotle

### U-FP3d-P5: Being-there (being-present) for the Greeks means having-come-into-the-present through production (poiesis); telos = peras = being-there-completed
"The primary sense of being as being-present" receives precise determination: "having-come-into-the-there, and specifically through pro-duction" (p. 144). The genuine sense of poiesis is bringing into the present. "Telos = peras. These are clues for the basic sense of Greek ontology" (p. 144). Ousia as "possessions and goods" -- what is there at one's disposal -- is recapitulated as the primary sense from which Greek being-concepts derive.
- **Source**: pp. 144-145
- **Status**: [INTERP-high] Heidegger's interpretive claim; central to his destructive ontology

### U-FP3d-P6: Techne provides the proximate analogy for understanding the logos of phuseia ginomena, but the phuseia onta are precisely what we do not produce
The erga technes are intelligible through the anticipation of the eidos in production -- the master builder deliberates with the "if-then" structure of logos. But the phuseia onta are "already there for us," already in the world; they do not require production by others. This establishes a fundamental contrast: techne's logos is aneu tes hules (without stuff), while phusis's logos must account for beings that self-produce.
- **Source**: pp. 144, 147-149; De part. an. A1, 639b26 sqq., 640a31 sq.
- **Status**: Explicitly stated; the analogy-and-contrast is structurally decisive

### U-FP3d-P7: In techne, the eidos is anticipated through logos, and the hule is entirely determinate in relation to the logos
Production is characterized by "if-then": "if such and such is to be completed, *then* such and such must occur" (p. 148). The hule must be procured in advance; it is co-addressed (aneu tes hules does not mean hule is absent but that it is not addressed in itself). The eidos of the house is anticipated in the builder's deliberation; genesis is founded in the telos. Key passage: he de techne logos tou ergou ho aneu tes hules estin (De part. an. A1, 640a31 sq.).
- **Source**: pp. 148-149; De part. an. A1, 639b26 sqq., 640a16 sqq.
- **Status**: Explicitly stated

### U-FP3d-P8: The phuseia onta are self-moving, self-completing beings whose primary experience is: "something is in movement in such a way that it comes to an end"
The constitutive aspect of phusis: a being-that-is-complete whose being-completed or having-become is sublated in its having-come-out-of as self-producing. Wherever something shows itself as coming-to-the-end, the primary experience is "the seeing of a self-moving being-completed" (p. 150). This is not dark teleology but a phenomenal finding. The phuseia on "in a certain sense runs ahead of itself" (p. 151). [INTERP-high] The "runs ahead of itself" phrasing adds existential overtones that structurally anticipate Vorlaufen.
- **Source**: pp. 150-151; De part. an. A1, 641b12, 23 sqq.
- **Status**: Explicitly stated; existential resonance is Heidegger's own

### U-FP3d-P9: The ancient physiologists fundamentally erred by treating phuseia onta through their hulike arche rather than their eidos
The ancients asked about aitia of movement and answered: the stuff. "They believed that this was an answer to the question: what is being-there?" (p. 152). But for-being-wood also determines the being-there of the table; they "have not inquired into the *being* of the table" (p. 152). Aristotle argues that eidos must be researched first; only through eidos can hule be properly addressed. The eidos is schema but not merely the outline -- "the hand of wood is no hand" because it lacks the ergon and dunamis that belong to the eidos (p. 155).
- **Source**: pp. 152-156; De part. an. A1, 640b26 sqq.; Met. Z10, 1035a7 sqq.
- **Status**: Explicitly stated; Heidegger presents this as Aristotle's own argument

### U-FP3d-P10: Democritus advanced beyond the other physiologists by arriving at schema, but this remains insufficient because the deceased has the same schema as the living
Democritus was "the first to arrive at the schema, the 'figure' of things" (p. 154). He correctly saw that schema and chroma determine a being as phuseia on. But Aristotle objects: "the deceased also has the same look and figure but is not the human being" (p. 155). A hand made of wood looks like a hand but cannot fulfill its specific function; the ergon and dunamis belong to the eidos, not mere schema.
- **Source**: pp. 154-155; De part. an. A1, 640b26 sqq.
- **Status**: Explicitly stated

### U-FP3d-P11: The hule of living things must be organon -- organ, not mere material -- characterized by the determinate being of being-able (dunamis), and this means: determined by psuche as sarx
The hule of living things "has the character of accomplishment" (p. 160); it is not mere wood or stone. "What hitherto had been not observed by the ancients appears as a *being-able*, which determines the being of hule, what we designate as soul" (p. 156). The psuche as the organizing principle of sarx is not a separate substance but the being-character of the living body's material.
- **Source**: pp. 155-156, 160; De part. an. A1, 641a17 sqq.
- **Status**: Explicitly stated; crucial for the restricted scope argument

### U-FP3d-P12: The phusikos's scope is restricted: studying psuche in its entirety would collapse all philosophy into phusike (indirect proof)
If the phusikos studied all living things including their being-living, "then there would be no philosophy beside this science, the phusike" (p. 157). The argument: noeton = panta; if nous were phuseia, phusike episteme would encompass all beings. But living originarily means being-in-a-world, being-by-something; the basic determination of living beings is pros allela (toward each other). "The living thing can only be determined in its being if the being that it is with is understood in its being" (p. 157, paraphrase). Aristotle concludes the phusikos studies only a "determinate extract" of life.
- **Source**: p. 157; De part. an. A1, 641a32 sqq.
- **Status**: Explicitly stated as Aristotle's argument; Heidegger endorses it

### U-FP3d-P13: Proof from being-moved itself: phuseia onta are characterized by three modes of movement (auxesis, alloiosis, phora), each studied from its arche
Plants: auxesis only (no aisthesis). Animals: aisthesis and phora. Humans: from nous. Heidegger notes that Aristotle's treatment of plant-movement "is almost phenomenological" -- plants grow "out toward opposed places, directions" and nourish themselves from all sides (De Anima 413a26 sqq.). The being-possibilities are hierarchically ordered by their arche. The being-possibility of phuseia onta has its limits in aisthesis and phora.
- **Source**: pp. 159-160; De part. an. A1, 641b5 sqq.; De Anima B2, 413a26 sqq.; B4, 415a16 sqq.
- **Status**: Explicitly stated

### U-FP3d-P14: The advancement of science is not a matter of argumentative acuity but of the "correct relationship to the matters" -- being compelled by truth (aletheia) itself
Science "can already be operating for a long time, that material is collected and certain properties and theories are unearthed; and yet this science does not at all need to be by its object" (p. 161). The critical factor is not logical skill but originary access. Empedocles was "led by truth itself" (hup' autes tes aletheias); Democritus was "borne by the matter itself" (hup' autou tou pragmatos). [INTERP-high] Heidegger uses this passage to establish a proto-Heideggerian notion of aletheia as that which shows itself and compels the researcher.
- **Source**: p. 161; De part. an. A1, 642a18 sq., 642a26 sqq.
- **Status**: Explicitly stated; Heidegger uses Aristotle's own formulations

### U-FP3d-P15: The Socratic turn from phusis-research to politike was a "fundamental oversight," not an incidental omission
Socrates promoted arete and politike, and the zetein peri phuseos was left behind. "That is not an incidental omission, perhaps due to their having pursued the human sciences more than the natural sciences. Instead, it is a fundamental oversight" (p. 161). Even the concepts of being-in-the-polis have their foundation in concepts of nature. Aristotle "shifted the weight of his work primarily onto the examination of phusis as being" (p. 161).
- **Source**: p. 161; De part. an. A1, 642a28 sqq.
- **Status**: Explicitly stated; a strong evaluative claim

### U-FP3d-P16: Being-with-one-another (Miteinandersein) involves two determinations: encountering-one-another is being-there-for-one-another in the world; being-with-one-another means having the same world
The transition from SS19 to SS20 sharpens the concept of living as "being-in-a-world": (1) eidos as dunamis of being-in-the-world = the being-determination of beings; (2) encountering from out of the world = belongingness-to-the-world. The Greeks do not recognize the distinction between internal and external. "Encountering-one-another is being-there-for-one-another, such that every being that is for another is in the world" (p. 162). And: "we have the same world with one another" (p. 162).
- **Source**: p. 162
- **Status**: [INTERP-high] Heidegger's interpretive construction

### U-FP3d-P17: The pathe are modes of being-taken with respect to being-in-the-world; they determine the possibilities of orienting oneself in the world essentially
The pathe occur "in the soul" but this means: in living-being, as being-taken, losing-composure, kineisthai. "Through the pathe, the possibilities of orienting oneself in the world are determined essentially" (p. 162). "Being-out-of-composure is in itself related to being composed, hexis" -- the pathe are intrinsically related to hexis as the ground of comportment.
- **Source**: pp. 162-163
- **Status**: Heidegger's interpretive claim, grounding the BT notion of Befindlichkeit

### U-FP3d-P18: Hedone is not subjective pleasure but a fundamental determination of being-in-the-world: the tendency toward being-there-completedness that belongs to being as living
The basic determination: hedone is "a being-possibility, ti phusikon, even in what is inferior, which belongs to their being, that is better than they are in themselves" (p. 163, citing NE K2, 1153b29 sqq.). All living things pursue hedone because "they are all after the same thing" -- theion, the genuine being of being-always. Heidegger: "hedone is nothing other than a fundamental determination of being-in-the-world" (p. 163).
- **Source**: pp. 163-164; NE K2, 1173a2 sq., 1153b29 sqq.
- **Status**: Explicitly stated; central interpretive claim

### U-FP3d-P19: Hedone is en toi nun -- "in the moment" -- and thus no kinesis but a determination of the presentness of being-there as such
Comparison with aisthesis (NE K3): hedone is complete "in the moment," not in time as a determinate span. "It is in itself completed, has no movement" (p. 164). Heidegger insists this is not merely negative (not-in-time) but positive: hedone characterizes the presentness of being-there. In *Rhetoric* A11, hedone is called kinesis tis, but this means it is a determination of being -- change as the character of being, not a process in a span.
- **Source**: p. 164; NE K3, 1174a13 sqq., 1174b9; Rhet. A11, 1369b33
- **Status**: Explicitly stated; decisive for the temporality analysis

### U-FP3d-P20: Hedone as disposition has the character of co-being-there (hepetai): the hedone means nothing other than the co-being-there of being-there itself
The hedone is said to hepetai -- not "follow" temporally but "be there also." "Closer consideration shows that the co-being-there of hedone means nothing other than the co-being-there of being-there itself" (p. 165). Hedone is identified directly with zen ("living"). This grounding of hedone in being-there-itself -- not in objects or circumstances -- is the most radical claim.
- **Source**: p. 165; NE K4, 1175a10 sqq.
- **Status**: [INTERP-high] Heidegger's interpretive construction

### U-FP3d-P21: The pathe are possessed in the dual possibilities of hairesis (seizing/choosing) and phuge (fleeing), and these are the basic motivations of being-there
Disposition through hedone has a dual possibility: (1) hairesis, insofar as finding-itself is psuche; (2) phuge. "Hairesis and phuge are the characteristics that characterize the basic possibility of living as a way of being with itself" (p. 166). This pairs with the earlier analysis of orexis (SS9-10) and anticipates BT's analysis of Befindlichkeit.
- **Source**: p. 166; NE B2, 1104b30 sq.
- **Status**: Explicitly stated

### U-FP3d-P22: The pathe are co-given with every pathos: hedone and lupe accompany every perceiving, thinking, and considering as inseparable companions
"Hedone and lupe are co-given; with every pathos, but equally with every perceiving, every thinking, considering, with theoria, to the extent that they are basic modes of living, hedone is an inseparable companion" (p. 166). This is the ground-claim for the universality of affect: no mode of being-there is without hedone/lupe.
- **Source**: p. 166; NE K4, 1174b20 sqq.
- **Status**: Explicitly stated

## 4b. BT Anticipation

"No explicit BT anticipation in this unit."

[INTERP-high] However, several significant implicit anticipations can be identified:

**BT-A1. Befindlichkeit (Attunement/State-of-mind) -- BT SS29.** U-FP3d-P17 through P22 constitute the most direct anticipation. The claim that pathe are "modes of being-taken with respect to being-in-the-world" that "determine the possibilities of orienting oneself in the world essentially" (p. 162) is structurally identical to BT's thesis that Befindlichkeit discloses Dasein's thrownness. The dual structure of hairesis/phuge (P21) anticipates BT's emphasis that attunement always involves being drawn toward or fleeing from the disclosed situation.

**BT-A2. The Existential Concept of Science -- BT SS69b.** U-FP3d-P14 (science compelled by truth) and P2 (episteme vs. paideia) anticipate BT's existential concept of science as a mode of Dasein's being. The "correct relationship to the matters" prefigures BT SS69b's thesis that science is a derivative mode of being-in-the-world.

**BT-A3. The Phenomenological Method ("to the things themselves") -- BT SS7.** Paideia as non-technical, reflective "being-open for a definite concrete content" (p. 141) anticipates the phenomenological maxim. The pepaideumenos who judges whether access is originary prefigures the BT distinction between genuine and derivative modes of access.

**BT-A4. Readiness-to-hand (Zuhandenheit) and Present-at-hand (Vorhandenheit) -- BT SS15-16.** P5-P7 (being-present = poiesis, techne analogy, eidos anticipated) anticipate the BT analysis of equipment and the transition from readiness-to-hand to present-at-hand. The "if-then" structure of techne and the contrast with phuseia onta "already there" prefigures BT's distinction between the equipmental context and breakdown.

**BT-A5. Being-with-Others (Mitsein) -- BT SS26.** P16 (Miteinandersein as encountering-for-one-another in the world, having the same world) is a direct precursor to BT SS26.

**BT-A6. Worldhood of the World and Nature -- BT SS14-15.** The entire SS19 analysis of phuseia ginomena as self-completing beings, contrasted with techne products, anticipates BT's treatment of "Nature" as discovered within the world (BT SS15). The criticism of the phusiologoi (P9) prefigures BT's critique of "Nature" as Vorhandenheit.

**BT-A7. Temporality and the "Now" -- BT SS65, 81.** P19 (hedone as en toi nun) anticipates BT's analysis of the "now" (Jetzt). The determination that hedone is "in the moment" and is a "determination of the presentness of being-there" (p. 164) prefigures BT's distinction between the ecstatic present (Gegenwart) and the leveled-down "now" of ordinary time-reckoning.

## 5. Tensions & Open Questions

**T1. Tension between the ou heneka as logos and the modern concept of teleology.** Heidegger insists that the ou heneka is not "aim-directedness" or "teleology" in the modern sense but "being-there-completed" (pp. 143, 160). Yet the very notion of the phuseia on as "running ahead of itself" toward its telos risks reintroducing a proto-teleological structure. The question is whether Heidegger's existential reinterpretation (telos as being-completed rather than purpose) genuinely avoids teleology or merely renames it. This tension recurs in the BT discussion of Being-toward-death as a "not-yet" that belongs to Dasein's being.

**T2. The phusikos's scope and the problem of nous.** The indirect proof (SS19d-alpha) restricts the phusikos to anoeta -- beings not guided by nous. But Heidegger simultaneously claims that psuche as such is the phusikos's topic. The tension: if psuche includes nous (as Aristotle says in *De Anima*), how can the phusikos study psuche while excluding nous? Heidegger's answer -- that the phusikos studies psuche "within certain limits" -- leaves open exactly where those limits fall. This connects to the broader BT problem of the relationship between Dasein's existential understanding and theoretical knowledge.

**T3. The status of the ancient physiologists' "failure."** Heidegger presents the phusiologoi's focus on hule as a failure to reach the being-question. But he also acknowledges they were investigating "the being of nature" (p. 152) -- they simply asked the wrong question (what moves? rather than what is?). Is the phusiologoi's error a genuine methodological failing, or is it a historically necessary stage in the unfolding of truth itself? SS19e suggests the latter with the claim that truth *compels* the researcher -- Empedocles and Democritus were "led" and "borne" by truth despite their errors. This creates a productive ambiguity about the relationship between error and disclosure.

**T4. Hedone as both energeia and pathos.** In SS20, hedone is characterized as not-kinesis, as energeia-like (complete at each moment, en toi nun). But hedone is also characterized as pathos -- a mode of being-taken, of losing-composure. The tension: energeia suggests actuality, completeness, self-sufficiency; pathos suggests passivity, being-affected, vulnerability. How can hedone be both the self-sufficient presentness of being-there and a mode of being-taken? [INTERP-high] This tension may be productive: hedone as the always-already-there-ness of finding-oneself is precisely what makes being-taken (pathos) possible. One is always already disposed before any particular affect befalls.

**T5. The identification of hedone with zoe.** Heidegger claims hedone can be "directly identified with zoe, 'living'" (p. 165). But this risks collapsing all distinctions within the affective life -- if hedone = living, what distinguishes hedone from lupe, since lupe too is a mode of living? The text suggests the answer lies in the hairesis/phuge structure (going-toward vs. fleeing), but this needs further development in the subsequent unit on phobos (U-FP3e).

**T6. The "fundamental oversight" of the Socratic turn.** Heidegger's claim (P15) that the turn from phusis to politike was a "fundamental oversight" because even polis-concepts have their foundation in concepts of nature is a remarkably strong claim. Yet Heidegger's own later work (BT and beyond) proceeds primarily through the analysis of Dasein's practical-social existence rather than through natural philosophy. The tension: is Heidegger's praise of Aristotle's return to phusis a program he himself follows, or is it an insight he acknowledges but does not pursue?

## Methodological Notes

**Heidegger's strategy in U-FP3d**: The section operates through a distinctive two-stage movement. First (SS19), a sustained methodological digression into *De Partibus Animalium* establishes the phusikos's scope and thereby the primacy of eidos over hule, the role of psuche as the organizing principle of living matter, and the restricted character of natural-philosophical inquiry. This is not merely a historiographical exercise; Heidegger uses the Aristotelian analysis of techne and phusis to exhibit the Greek sense of being as presence-through-production, which is the ontological ground of the entire course. Second (SS20), the return to the pathe via hedone and lupe completes the affective dimension of being-there: the pathe are not psychological appendages but the mode in which being-there finds itself in each moment, oriented through hairesis and phuge. The arc from SS16 (pathos in rhetoric) through SS18 (pathos in Met. Delta 21) to SS20 (pathos as hedone/lupe) is now complete.

**Distinctive features of the SS19 analysis**: Heidegger's reading of *De Partibus Animalium* A1 is remarkable for its sustained attention to a text rarely treated as philosophically central. The entire analysis is oriented toward making visible (a) the Greek sense of being as production/presence, (b) the priority of eidos over hule as a consequence of this sense of being, and (c) the restricted scope of the phusikos as a consequence of the ontological structure of living beings. The Democritus discussion (P10) is particularly notable: Heidegger credits Democritus with genuine philosophical advance while showing the limits of his approach.

**The hedone analysis (SS20) as pivotal**: The determination of hedone as en toi nun (P19) is one of the most significant claims in the entire course for the later development of Heidegger's thought. It connects the affective dimension of being-there directly to temporality -- the pathe are not in time but constitute the very presentness of being-there. This is the clearest bridge from the First Part's rhetorical-practical analysis to the Second Part's ontological analysis of kinesis and, beyond the course, to BT's existential temporality.
