**One-sentence thesis**: Heidegger argues that Aristotle's Physics is not natural science but *ἀρχή*-research -- critique of inherited interpretedness -- whose central achievement is the definition of κίνησις as ἐντελέχεια τοῦ δυνάμει ὄντος (Physics Γ1, 201a10), a formula that discloses movement as the *presence of a being in its ability-to-be precisely insofar as it is an ability-to-be*, grounded in the threefold articulation of ἐντελέχεια/ἐνέργεια, στέρησις, and δύναμις and in the twofold (διχῶς) structure of each category, such that κίνησις is revealed not as a regional phenomenon but as the fundamental mode of being of worldly beings -- the 1924 anticipation of *Zuhandenheit* and the existential analytic.

    §26d-α  ἐντελέχεια and ἐνέργεια (pp. 200--201)
      ├─ φύσει ὄντα characterized by ἐντελεχείᾳ μόνον ("as pure presence")
      │   and by δυνάμει ("as possibility *and* presence")
      ├─ A being that is present is not merely δυνάμει; it is still δυνάμει in this being-present
      │   → always δύναμις of an ἐντελεχείᾳ ὄν; third member (δύναμει alone) superfluous
      ├─ Two possibilities: (1) as *pure* presence = ἐντελεχείᾳ; (2) as ἐντελεχείᾳ *and in addition* δυνάμει ὄν
      ├─ Met. Θ3 passage: ἐνέργεια "came to be carried over" to ἐντελέχεια
      │   from the region of κινήσεις; ἐνέργεια specifically "is related in itself
      │   πρὸς τὴν ἐντελέχειαν" (sunteinei pros)
      ├─ DISTINCTION:
      │   ├─ ἐντελέχεια = ἐντελές + ἔχειν = "possession of completion"
      │   │   = presence of a being as end (τέλος), being-completed,
      │   │     maintaining-itself-in-being-completed
      │   │   = what *excludes every δύναμις*; simply present, never produced
      │   │   = seen in the heavens, *experienced* in fear (not just observed)
      │   └─ ἐνέργεια = "stretches itself toward" (συντείνει πρός) ἐντελέχειαν
      │       = being-grasped-in-becoming-completed
      │       = character of being-there where beings are *not there in their being-completed*
      ├─ Consequence: ἐνέργεια is κίνησις, but not ἐντελέχεια
      │   κίνησις = a mode of being-there displayed in ἐνέργεια
      ├─ Word-formation: ἐντελ(ές)ἔχεια; Diels: ἐντελόμισθος in Demosthenes = "full pay"
      │   → ἐντελέχεια = "possession of completion"
      ├─ ἐντελέχεια μόνον: that which *only* maintains itself in being-completed
      │   = excludes every δύναμις; always already completed, never produced, simply present
      │   = seen in the movement of the heavens; experienced in fear, not merely observed
      └─ "This being-that-is-always-there did not remain standing, vanished from there"
          → connection to the threat motivating φόβος (§26b)

    §26d-β  στέρησις (pp. 201--203)
      ├─ Physics A8: three ἀρχαί (δύναμις, ἐνέργεια, στέρησις)
      │   → definition of movement requires all three, "just as Plato had defined it,
      │     but carried through fundamentally differently, with the help of στέρησις"
      ├─ στέρησις obtained from beings "in themselves non-being":
      │   αὐτό μὴ ὄν = being-absent, not nothingness
      │   "Negation is a position" (p. 202)
      ├─ Non-being is the "there of a *definite there*, the there of absence"
      │   → from this, μεταβολή becomes intelligible
      ├─ Surprising claim: something can come from non-being
      │   (contra the dictum that nothing comes from nothing)
      │   → only possible because στέρησις is a *determinate* non-being
      ├─ One way of making γένεσις intelligible = μεταβολή;
      │   another way: looking back to δύναμις and ἐνέργεια
      ├─ Relation to Aristotle's development: Physics things said regarding ὄν and ἔν
      │   are at the level of the Metaphysics
      ├─ Through στέρησις (= δύναμις + ἐνέργεια) the difficulties of predecessors resolved
      └─ Aristotle's own high estimate of this discovery (Phys. A8, 191b30 sqq.)

    §26d-γ  δύναμις (pp. 203--204)
      ├─ δύναμις does NOT mean "possibility" (modal-logical sense)
      │   = not "that which at some time can be there at all"
      ├─ δύναμις = already the determination of an ἐντελεχείᾳ ὄν
      │   = "a being-that-is-there already"
      ├─ Illustration: tree trunk in the forest
      │   ├─ As ἐντελέχεια: present there for me as a tree
      │   ├─ As δυνάμει: encountered as fallen tree, available for shipbuilding
      │   └─ "The tree trunk has the character of *being-serviceable for*...,
      │       of *usability for*..." = the *mode of its being*
      ├─ The being in the surrounding world has the character of συμφέρον
      │   ("being-what-is-conducive-to")
      ├─ δυνάμει-being = "a *positive determination of the mode of its there*"
      │   → "For a long time I have been designating this being-character
      │     of being-there as *meaningfulness*" (p. 203) [KEY BT ANTICIPATION]
      ├─ δύναμις is not empty/formal but determinate with determinate conditions
      │   (Met. Θ7: πότε δὲ δυνάμει ἐστὶν ἕκαστον καὶ πότε οὔ, διοριστέον)
      ├─ Illustration: σπέρμα is δυνάμει ἄνθρωπος only when it has become (γένηται)
      │   → "the earth in its ability-to-be [is not] something like a human being"
      ├─ Crucial ontological point: δυνάμει as constituting-along-with-being
      │   ├─ Wood changes to being-there of a chest; "in what way is wood,
      │   │   being-wood also constitutive of the being of the being-there of the chest?"
      │   ├─ Plato: the chest *has* wood; wood is an Idea; chest *participates* in wood
      │   │   → τόδε τι: the chest is not the wood in the sense of "this here"
      │   │   but ἀλλ᾽ ἐκεῖνον: "co-related" to the wood
      │   └─ Aristotle: the chest is not wood but wooden; being-wooden = another mode
      │       of being-there as being-wood; out-of-which (ἐνεργείᾳ) not itself there in chest
      ├─ Key: κινούμενον is ontologically apprehended as ἐκεῖνον
      │   ├─ Chest/wood: the being that is moved is always ἐκεῖνον (remotely related)
      │   ├─ Stone that falls: κίνησις is there in a certain mode
      │   └─ "The stone *does not participate* in movement" (contra Plato)
      │       → κίνησις, unlike wood, is not there in the mode of ὕλη
      └─ "The fundamental clue to the phenomenon of movement can only be
          approached by way of the being-that-is-moved" (p. 204)

    §26d-δ  Being in the Sense of the Categories (pp. 205--206)
      ├─ A being within constancy and δύναμις = being-there-character of meaningfulness
      │   = "this here" or "so much" or "a such" → the categories of being
      ├─ τουτόνδε, τοσόνδε, etc. from δή: "manifestly there," "present"
      ├─ Categories are not a "system" but are simply introduced as self-evident
      │   wherever they are spoken of in the writings
      ├─ Category (κατηγορία) derives from ἀγορεύειν:
      │   ├─ = "to talk in the market-place, to talk publicly"
      │   ├─ κατηγορεῖν = "to publicly reply to each about something"
      │   │   = "to accuse, to draw out a definite fact"
      │   └─ κατηγορία = "modes of addressing beings in their being"
      ├─ Previous emphasis on λόγος now connected to categories:
      │   ├─ λόγος as being-in-the-world: discoveredness, uncoveredness, being-present of world
      │   └─ Categories = the being-discovered-of-the-world showing itself in its basic respects
      ├─ Warning: categories are not logical forms or a system for classifying statements
      │   = must be understood from λόγος in its distinctive mode:
      │   "the being-discovered of the world, which is constitutive in such a way
      │    that this discoveredness shows the world in its basic respects" (p. 205)
      ├─ κατηγορία with dual meaning: λόγος as λέγειν — λόγος as λεγόμενον
      │   κατηγορήματα = the other side where this dual meaning is explicit
      ├─ ζωὴ πρακτική: the world lies in the ζωὴ πρακτική
      │   → τὰ μετὰ λόγου = the categories
      │   → πρᾶξις: the ζωὴ πρακτική is the type of being at each moment
      │   → πρακτόν oriented to καιρός
      └─ Κινησία = being-moved as a mode of the being-there of the world (p. 206)
          → movement itself must be genuinely named: κινησία

  §26e  Movement as the Being of Beings of the World Itself:
        Critique of the Platonic ἀγαθὸν καθόλου (Nicomachean Ethics A4) (pp. 206--208)
    ├─ ἀγαθόν as πέρας of πρᾶξις: characterizes being of the world as being-there
    │   at each moment → discourse of ἀγαθὸν καθόλου makes no sense
    ├─ ἀγαθόν ≠ "value" in the generic sense; = "a particular mode of being-there
    │   of those beings with which we have to do in πρᾶξις, oriented to the καιρός"
    ├─ NE A4: there is no ἀγαθὸν καθόλου; ἀγαθόν is always πρακτόν
    │   characterized through τόδε τι, ποσόν, πρός τι → oriented to καιρός
    ├─ No good that "hovers over" (κατ᾽ οὐσίαν) the definite situation
    ├─ Aristotle's self-objection: perhaps ἀγαθὸν καθ᾽ αὑτό as συμφέροντα?
    │   → No: ἀγαθὰ καθ᾽ αὑτά (φρονεῖν, ἡδοναί, τιμαί) are for their own sakes
    │   → λόγοι about them are ἕτεροι καὶ διαφέροντες (different and divergent)
    │   → If Idea, then εἶδος would be "empty" (μάταιον τὸ εἶδος)
    ├─ Aristotle sharply separates ἰδέα and εἶδος:
    │   ├─ By εἶδος: the "appearing" of a concrete being
    │   └─ ἀγαθὸν καθ᾽ αὐτό as Idea = empty if not connected to definite πρᾶξις
    ├─ κοινόν is not universal (not γένος) but κατ᾽ ἀναλογίαν (by analogy)
    ├─ PARALLEL to κίνησις:
    │   ├─ οὐκ ἔστι δὲ κίνησις παρὰ τὰ πράγματα (Phys. Γ1, 200b32)
    │   ├─ κίνησις too is no γένος: it is not a way of being *alongside* beings
    │   └─ κίνησις = being-determination of the beings of the world,
    │       insofar as they *always are determined as these*
    ├─ Four possible movements only: τόδε τι, ποιόν, ποσόν, κατὰ τόπον
    │   grounded through ἐπαγωγή (Physics E1), not arbitrary
    └─ Categories = "possibilities of being-moved are primarily determined by
        the characteristic being-there of the world" (p. 208)

  §26f  The διχῶς of the Categories (pp. 210--211)
    ├─ ἕκαστον δὲ διχῶς ὑπάρχει πᾶσιν (Phys. Γ1, 201a3):
    │   "every one of the categories is there in a twofold manner"
    ├─ The διχῶς: τὸ μὲν γὰρ μορφὴ αὐτοῦ, τὸ δὲ στέρησις
    │   = each category contains within itself morphē and steresis
    ├─ A being appears as εἶδος but is at the same time characterized by στέρησις:
    │   "an 'absence': a being-present of something of whose presence an absence
    │    is constitutive" (p. 210)
    ├─ Illustration: "I miss him very much" = not "he is not at hand"
    │   but "a completely determinate way of his being-there for me" (p. 210)
    ├─ The being-there of the world: things are never *fully* there;
    │   always characterized by absence, by *not-being-thus as they genuinely
    │   could and should be* → averageness (λευκόν/μέλαν, light/dark)
    ├─ This διχῶς "belongs to the basic categories themselves"
    │   = "itself fundamental for movement"
    ├─ The categories, insofar as determined as this διχῶς, show in themselves
    │   being-possibility: "to be something that is 'from...toward...'
    │   of something like a change" (p. 211)
    ├─ "From...toward..." structure (ἐκ τίνος εἴς τι):
    │   ├─ grounded in the doubling of the categories themselves
    │   ├─ τόδε τι, ποιόν, ποσόν, κατὰ τόπον each admit this structure
    │   └─ Aristotle: ὑποκείμενον visible in κατάφασις, convertible
    │       (ὑποκείμενον ↔ non-ὑποκείμενον)
    └─ Wider determination: ὥστ᾽ οὐδὲ κίνησις οὐδὲ μεταβολὴ οὐθενὸς ἔσται
        παρὰ τὰ εἰρημένα (201a4) → no movement beyond the said categories

  §26g  The First Definition of Movement and Its Illustration (pp. 211--212)
    ├─ Recourse to the first determination: a being as being-there is there
    │   in such a way that it *can* be something → a piece of wood *can* be a chest
    ├─ The formula: διηρημένου δὲ καθ᾽ ἕκαστον γένος τοῦ μὲν ἐντελεχείᾳ,
    │   τοῦ δὲ δυνάμει, ἡ τοῦ δυνάμει ὄντος ἐντελέχεια, ᾗ τοιοῦτον, κίνησίς ἐστιν
    │   (Phys. Γ1, 201a10 sq.)
    ├─ Heidegger's rendering: "Movement is ἐντελέχεια, presence of beings
    │   that are beings able to be there, indeed insofar as they can be there"
    ├─ τέλειον comprises both aspects: (1) *there* (presently), (2) *being-from-out-of*...
    ├─ Being-there itself as *presence*: being-there-now (praesens) =
    │   both spatial presence and temporal now (αἴσθησις always in the now)
    ├─ Illustration: wood in a workshop
    │   ├─ Wood lies there with determination of usability for a chest
    │   ├─ δύναμις: the wood is "not yet" a chest but is usable for making one
    │   ├─ Movement = the presence of the ability-to-be-there as such
    │   ├─ Cabinet-maker at work: wood is in its ability-to-be, present in
    │   │   the being-at-work (ἐνέργεια) insofar as the cabinet-maker has it in hand
    │   └─ "Aristotle can also define movement in what follows as ἐνέργεια":
    │       ἐνέργεια as mode of being-there = being-at-work of something
    ├─ Usability as the being-character: not genuine merely as usability
    │   (deceptive: wood only genuinely there insofar as wood is at work)
    ├─ REST (ἠρεμία) as extreme case of movement:
    │   ├─ Cabinet-maker gone: chest begun, wood not at hand in movement
    │   │   but also not as it was before → merely δυνάμει = *at hand in rest*
    │   ├─ "Rest is only an *extreme case of movement*"
    │   ├─ Only something that has the being-determination of being in movement
    │   │   or being able to be in movement can rest
    │   └─ "We encounter many things of the world -- most of those with which
    │       we have to do -- for the most part as resting"
    └─ Unit concludes mid-paragraph: analysis continues in §27

## 4. Main Positions

**U-SP2a-P1. The Aristotelian Physics is not "physics" in the modern sense but ἀρχή-research: the setting-forth of the basic respects (ἀρχαί) in which the being of beings shows itself.**

Heidegger opens the unit by situating ἀρχή-research within the existential structure already developed in the First Part: the interpretedness of being-there (conveyed by λόγος as idle chatter) is authoritative for the world-conception itself. The ἕξις of ἀληθεύειν -- genuine knowing as a possibility of being-there -- is fulfilled concretely as ἐπιστήμη, or more precisely as πρώτη φιλοσοφία and σοφία. This "science" is not a scrutinizing of beings in their concrete determinations but a setting-forth of the basic respects guided by the question: "τί τὸ ὄν? What are beings as beings? What is being?" (p. 193). ἀρχή-research is thus ontological from the start; it asks not about this or that being but about being as such.

**U-SP2a-P2. The investigation is περὶ φύσεως (about being), not περὶ τῶν φύσει ὄντων (about beings that are natural): the Physics is ontological, not ontic.**

This distinction, introduced at p. 197, is decisive. Heidegger insists that once Aristotle begins investigating κίνησις, "the being-characters are derived from beings themselves: περὶ φύσεως, not περὶ τῶν φύσει ὄντων -- an investigation of *being*, not of *beings*; not an investigation of the *ontic*, such that it would be pursued with respect to individual beings, but rather an investigation into the *ontological*, insofar as beings are addressed *in their being*." The Physics is thus not a forerunner of natural science but a contribution to first philosophy.

**U-SP2a-P3. Critique is the primary mode of ἀρχή-research: it is "nothing other than the bringing-to-itself of the past" and simultaneously "research about access."**

Heidegger characterizes ἀρχή-research as essentially critical. It does not begin from nothing but from the existing interpretedness of being -- the way beings have already been understood. Critique means deconstructing this interpretedness: "what was always already interpreted, what was already seen in early conceptions, is to be brought to its genuine rightfulness, to transparency" (p. 193). The critique of the Eleatics in Physics Book 1 is the first stage: Parmenides' determination ἓν τὸ ὄν ("being is one") denied κίνησις and forced beings to a standstill. By drawing the Eleatics into the circle of his critique, Aristotle shows that one "can hardly determine beings in their being if one insists that there is only *one* ἀρχή" (p. 194). The sense of ἀρχή as such requires a manifoldness (πολλά) -- minimally two (δύναμις and ἐνέργεια), but not more than three (adding στέρησις).

**U-SP2a-P4. The number of ἀρχαί is not an arbitrary question but internally connected to the phenomenon of κίνησις: insofar as κίνησις is determined by δύναμις and ἐνέργεια, these two constitute two ἀρχαί, and στέρησις provides a third.**

This emerges from the discussion of Physics A 8, where Aristotle shows that the definition of movement "must come about without δύναμις and ἐνέργεια; and only by way of the ἀρχαί, just as Plato had defined it, but carried through in a fundamentally different way than Plato, with the help of στέρησις" (p. 201). The three ἀρχαί are not external principles imposed on κίνησις but are read off from the phenomenon itself.

**U-SP2a-P5. Fear (φόβος) is the affective ground -- the Grundstimmung -- of the ancients' ἀρχή-research, not a merely psychological accompaniment.**

Section 26b contains a remarkable claim: the ἀπορῆσαι of the ἀρχαί -- the genuine puzzlement that opens up the region of κίνησις -- was "at its basis, guided by *fear*" (p. 196). Heidegger cites Met. Θ 8 (the heavenly bodies are "always at work": διὸ ἀεὶ ἐνεργεῖ ἥλιος καὶ ἄστρα καὶ ὅλος ὁ οὐρανός) and Eth. Nic. K 7 (διαγωγή as the highest possibility, threatened by the fear that the constant rotation of the stars might cease). The fear is not about this or that danger but about the possibility that "what is there in the way of being-always-present might stay away" -- that the familiar world might vanish. This fear is the motive of inquiry: "the interpretation of the ἀρχαί, and thereby of beings themselves, is conveyed in a determinate *being familiar* with the being-there of the world itself" (p. 196). The abolition of fear is διαγωγή: pure θεωρεῖν as the secure "stay" in which nothing more threatens.

**U-SP2a-P6. κίνησις is not locomotion (φορά) but the total phenomenon of change (μεταβολή); modern physics *presupposes* Aristotle's concept without understanding it.**

At p. 199, Heidegger issues a sharp methodological warning: "it is really not a question of *defining* movement in some sense, but of *making* beings as moved *visible* in their being-there and holding fast to them." The modern formula c = s/t already presupposes everything Aristotle said about movement. What modern physics set up -- the question of the *system-of-relations* of movement (Galileo, Copernicus) -- concerns measurability, not the being of movement itself. Aristotle's question is more fundamental: not the system-of-relations but "movement itself with a view to the system-of-relations from which it is to be *measurable*." The absolute/relative distinction for this system-of-relations remains undiscussed by modern physics because it already presupposes κίνησις as uniform speed.

**U-SP2a-P7. ἐντελέχεια and ἐνέργεια are not synonyms but name two distinct modes of presence: ἐντελέχεια = being-completed (maintaining-itself-at-the-end), ἐνέργεια = being-grasped-in-becoming-completed.**

This is the conceptual heart of the analysis (§26d-α, pp. 200--201). Heidegger traces the word-formation: ἐντελ(ές) + ἔχειν = "possession of completion" (citing Diels on Demosthenes' ἐντελόμισθος, "full pay"). ἐντελέχεια is "presence, being-present of a being as end, in the sense of the final point that is completed, that has itself in itself in its 'end' -- τέλος as character of being-there, which constitutes being-completed." It is what *excludes every δύναμις*: a completed being that was "always already completed, which was never produced, which never would be but is simply present" -- seen in the heavens, "not simply in mere observation, but *experienced* in fear" (p. 201). ἐνέργεια, by contrast, "stretches itself out toward (συντείνει πρός) ἐντελέχειαν" -- it is a character of being-there but one in which beings "are *not there in their being-completed*." ἐνέργεια is the "being-character of *being-grasped in becoming-completed*" (p. 200). The critical consequence: κίνησις is a mode of being-there displayed in ἐνέργεια, not ἐντελέχεια. "ἐνέργεια is κίνησις, but not ἐντελέχεια. κίνησις is a mode of being-there displayed in ἐνέργεια" (p. 201).

**U-SP2a-P8. στέρησις is not mere negation or nothingness but a positive ontological determination: "absence" as constitutive of the being of a being.**

Heidegger develops στέρησις (§26d-β, pp. 201--203) as a concept Aristotle obtains from beings that are "in themselves non-being" -- characterized as being-absent (αὐτό μὴ ὄν). "Negation is a position" (p. 202). This non-being is not nothing but the "there of a *definite there*, the there of absence. On the basis of this being-that-is-not, the there is in the character of a determinate being-absent, from which 'something can become,' that is, with the help of this peculiar non-being, 'becoming,' μεταβολή, can become intelligible" (p. 202). Aristotle valued his own discovery highly; Heidegger shows this by quoting Aristotle's claim that "if this way of being would have become obvious to them, then every unclarity about these beings would have vanished for them" (p. 203, citing Phys. A 8, 191b30 sqq.). The illustration at p. 210 is decisive: "If I say of a human being: 'I miss him very much,' I, precisely, do not say that he is not at hand, but assert *a completely determinate way of his being-there for me*." στέρησις is thus a mode of being-there, not a deficiency of being.

**U-SP2a-P9. δύναμις does not mean "possibility" in the modal-logical sense but the being-character of meaningfulness: being-there-already as available-for, serviceable-for, usable-for.**

Section 26d-γ (pp. 203--204) recasts δύναμις entirely. "δύναμις does not have the sense of the 'possible': that which at some time can be there at all. δύναμις is already the determination of an ἐντελεχείᾳ ὄν, that is, of a being-that-is-there already" (p. 203). A tree trunk in the forest is ἐντελέχεια, present for me as a tree. But it can also be encountered as "fallen tree, tree trunk," available for shipbuilding: "The tree trunk has the character of *being-serviceable for*..., of *usability for*..., not in such a way that I thus apprehend it as first, but rather it is the *mode of its being*" (p. 203). δύναμις is thus a *positive* determination of the mode of being-there: "δυνάμει-being is a *positive determination of the mode of its there*. For a long time I have been designating this being-character of being-there as *meaningfulness*" (p. 203). This is the primary mode in which the world is encountered. The passage explicitly connects δύναμις to the existential analytic: meaningfulness (*Bedeutsamkeit*) as a fundamental character of worldhood.

**U-SP2a-P10. The categories are not a logical system but modes of addressing beings in their being, derived from the publicness of speaking (ἀγορεύειν = to talk in the marketplace).**

Section 26d-δ (pp. 205--206) grounds the categories in the phenomenon of λόγος as public speaking. κατηγορεῖν stands in the closest connection with λέγειν; ἀγορεύειν means "to talk in the market-place, to talk publicly, where being-with-one-another takes place, where everyone understands" (p. 205). κατηγορεῖν means "to publicly reply to each about something, that it is this or that, to accuse, to draw out a definite fact." Categories are thus "modes of addressing beings in their being" -- not logical forms imposed on experience but the ways in which the being-discovered of the world constitutively shows the world in its basic respects. Heidegger explicitly warns against treating categories as a "system": "No discussion of a system of categories! ... Wherever they are spoken of in the writings handed down to us, they are spoken of in this way" (p. 205).

**U-SP2a-P11. There is no ἀγαθὸν καθόλου (universal good); the ἀγαθόν is always πέρας of πρᾶξις, always κατὰ τὸν καιρόν -- at each moment, in the definite situation.**

Section 26e (pp. 206--208) deploys the critique of the Platonic ἀγαθὸν καθόλου from NE A 4 to establish that κίνησις is the being of worldly beings, not something "alongside" (παρά) them. The ἀγαθόν is πέρας of πρᾶξις; πρᾶξις is always "this here" at each moment (κατὰ τὸν καιρόν). There is no good "in general" hovering above the definite situation. The argument proceeds through the categories: the πρακτόν is characterized through τόδε τι, ποσόν, πρός τι, and oriented to καιρός (p. 207). If ἀγαθόν were an Idea (a γένος, a universal), "then the look would be empty" -- there would be nothing for πρᾶξις to be concerned about (p. 208). Aristotle sharply separates ἰδέα and εἶδος: by εἶδος he understands the "appearing" of a concrete being, not an Idea. The being-character of κοινόν is not universal (not γένος) but κατ᾽ ἀναλογίαν -- by analogy (p. 208).

**U-SP2a-P12. κίνησις too is "no γένος" (οὐκ ἔστι δὲ κίνησις παρὰ τὰ πράγματα) -- movement is not a way of being *alongside* beings (Plato) but the being of beings of the world, insofar as they are always determined as these.**

This is the direct parallel to the ἀγαθόν-critique (p. 208). Just as ἀγαθόν is not καθόλου but always at each moment, κίνησις is not παρὰ τὰ πράγματα but a "being-determination of the beings of the world, insofar as they always *are determined as these*." The categories distribute κίνησις into four possible movements only: in relation to τόδε τι, ποιόν, ποσόν, and κατὰ τόπον. Aristotle grounds this number not arbitrarily but through ἐπαγωγή (induction from the phenomena) in Physics E 1 (p. 210).

**U-SP2a-P13. The διχῶς (twofoldness) of each category is the ontological condition for movement: every category "is there in a twofold manner" (μορφή/στέρησις), and this twofoldness is the "from...toward..." structure that makes κίνησις possible.**

Section 26f (pp. 210--211) makes the crucial link. "Every one of the categories ὑπάρχει διχῶς, 'is there in a twofold manner': the being of the categories, every category as a mode of being-there, of encountering the world, intends, contains in itself a διχῶς: τὸ μὲν γὰρ μορφὴ αὐτοῦ, τὸ δὲ στέρησις" (p. 210). A being that is there, that I characterize in its being as this here, has a διχῶς in itself: it appears as εἶδος but is at the same time characterized by στέρησις -- "by an 'absence': a being-present of something of whose presence an absence is constitutive, absence in the sense of deficiency, of lack" (p. 210). This twofoldness "belongs to the basic categories themselves" and is "itself fundamental for movement": the being of the surrounding-world, insofar as it is determined as this διχῶς, "shows in itself being-possibility, to be something that is 'from...toward...' of something like a change" (p. 211). The "from...toward..." (ἐκ τίνος εἴς τι) structure is not imposed on beings but read off from their categorial twofoldness.

**U-SP2a-P14. The first definition: "Movement is ἐντελέχεια, presence of beings that are beings able to be there, indeed insofar as they can be there" (Phys. Γ1, 201a10 sq.). Movement is the presence of the ability-to-be-there as such.**

Section 26g (pp. 211--212) delivers the definition and illustrates it concretely. Aristotle "has recourse to the first determination, that a being as being-there is there in such a way that it *can* be something. A piece of wood *can* also be a chest" (p. 211). The key formula: "διηρημένου δὲ καθ᾽ ἕκαστον γένος τοῦ μὲν ἐντελεχείᾳ, τοῦ δὲ δυνάμει, ἡ τοῦ δυνάμει ὄντος ἐντελέχεια, ᾗ τοιοῦτον, κίνησίς ἐστιν." Heidegger renders this: "Movement is ἐντελέχεια, presence of beings that are beings able to be there, indeed insofar as they can be there" (p. 212). The τέλειον comprises both aspects of being: (1) as *presently there*, in the sense of *being-from-out-of*...; (2) the determination of the τέλειον comprises both *there* and *being-from-out-of*. In this being-there itself, as *presence*, there lies an aspect about which the analysis has kept silent: being-there means being-there-*now* (praesens), which means both "spatial presence" and "now" (αἴσθησις is always in the now).

The illustration: wood lies in a workshop; it lies there with the determination of usability for a chest. Movement = the presence of the ability-to-be-there as such. Whenever the cabinet-maker is at work on it, the wood is in its ability-to-be, is present in the being-at-work (ἐνέργεια) insofar as the cabinet-maker has it in hand. "Accordingly, Aristotle can also define movement in what follows as ἐνέργεια": ἐνέργεια as "a mode of being-there means nothing other than the being-at-work of something" (p. 212).

**U-SP2a-P15. Rest (ἠρεμία) is not the opposite of movement but an "extreme case" of movement: only something that has the being-determination of being in movement or being able to be in movement can rest.**

The final move of the unit (p. 212): "Rest is only possible for something that in itself has the being-determination of being in movement or being able to be in movement. We encounter many things of the world -- most of those with which we have to do -- for the most part as resting." This reversal -- rest as a *mode* of κίνησις rather than its privation -- completes the ontological revaluation: κίνησις is the fundamental determination, and even apparent stasis presupposes it.

## 4b. BT Anticipations

Heidegger uses BT-proximate language in at least three places that warrant explicit note:

**BT-1. δύναμις as Bedeutsamkeit → BT §18 (worldhood and significance).**

At p. 203: "For a long time I have been designating this being-character of being-there as *meaningfulness*." This is a direct self-citation of the concept that becomes central to the worldhood analysis of BT §18. The identification of δύναμις with meaningfulness is one of the most explicit 1924-to-1927 bridges in the entire lecture course. The tree trunk as δυνάμει available-for-shipbuilding is structurally identical to the hammer as *zuhanden* in its in-order-to (*Um-zu*). [INTERP-low: Heidegger makes the connection himself.]

**BT-2. Categories as modes of being-in-the-world → BT §§12--13, §44.**

The discussion of categories (p. 205) uses the formulation: λόγος as "the mode of being-in-the-world, such that this mode constitutes discoveredness, uncoveredness, the being-present of the world." The categories are not imposed on a subject-object relation but belong to being-in-the-world as such. In BT, this becomes the existential-hermeneutic derivation of categories from existentialia (BT §§9, 12). [INTERP-low]

**BT-3. φόβος as Grundstimmung → BT §§29--30, §40 (Angst).**

The discussion of φόβος in §26b (p. 196) uses the structure that will become the analysis of Angst: fear directed not at a particular intraworldly being but at the possibility that being-there itself (the familiar world) might vanish. The "fear that it would, at some point, no longer be" is closer to Angst (fear for being-there as a whole) than to Furcht (fear of a determinate threat), though Heidegger does not yet draw this distinction terminologically. [INTERP-high: the 1924 text does not distinguish Angst from Furcht; the structural parallel is genuine but the terminological specification is a BT innovation.]

**BT-4. στέρησις as constitutive nullity → BT §58 (Schuld/being-guilty).**

στέρησις as constitutive absence (p. 210: "I miss him very much" = a determinate way of his being-there for me) anticipates the analysis of Schuld in BT §58: being-there as essentially determined by a "not" that is not mere deficiency but constitutive of its being. The "nullity" (Nichtigkeit) pervading Dasein's being-guilty is structurally descended from Aristotelian στέρησις. [INTERP-high: Heidegger does not make this connection explicit in GA 18; it is a structural extrapolation warranted by the parallel logic.]

**BT-5. Rest as extreme case of movement → BT §§12, 25 (de-severance, spatiality).**

The claim that ἠρεμία is an extreme case of κίνησις (p. 212) structurally anticipates BT's treatment of spatial presence-at-hand as a *deficient mode* of readiness-to-hand: things encountered "as resting" are not in a default state of non-movement but are *privatively* in movement. [INTERP-high]

## 5. Tensions and Open Questions

**T1. ἐντελέχεια names both the *definition* of κίνησις and what κίνησις is *not*.**

The formula says κίνησις *is* ἐντελέχεια τοῦ δυνάμει ὄντος, yet §26d-α establishes that ἐντελέχεια and ἐνέργεια are distinct: ἐντελέχεια = completedness (what excludes every δύναμις), ἐνέργεια = becoming-completed. κίνησις is a mode of ἐνέργεια, not ἐντελέχεια -- yet the definition itself uses the word "ἐντελέχεια." Heidegger's resolution: in the definition, ἐντελέχεια means "presence" in a general sense (the being-present of the δυνάμει ὄν *as* δυνάμει ὄν), not the specific sense of being-completed. But this equivocation haunts the text and drives much of the subsequent discussion in §§27--28.

**T2. δύναμις as "already there" versus δύναμις as "not yet."**

Heidegger insists that δύναμις is not modal possibility but the being-character of a being that is *already there* (the tree trunk is already present as available for shipbuilding). Yet the definition of κίνησις requires that the δυνάμει ὄν be understood as "not yet" completed -- the wood is not yet a chest. The tension: δύναμις simultaneously names a positive mode of presence (meaningfulness) and a relation to what is not yet (the being-able-to-be that is actualized in movement). Heidegger navigates this by distinguishing the δυνάμει ὄν's being-there (its availability) from its being-completed (which belongs to ἐντελέχεια), but the distinction remains delicate.

**T3. Categories as modes of public speaking versus categories as ontological structure.**

§26d-δ grounds categories in ἀγορεύειν (marketplace speech), making them functions of λόγος and publicness. Yet §26f asserts that the διχῶς of each category is an ontological condition of movement itself -- not merely a way we talk about beings but the way beings *are*. The tension between the linguistic-pragmatic grounding and the ontological claim is not fully resolved in U-SP2a and points forward to the deeper question of the relationship between λόγος and being.

**T4. φόβος as Grundstimmung: existential or anthropological?**

§26b treats φόβος as the affective ground of the ancients' inquiry, anticipating the analysis of Angst in BT §40. But the account here remains tied to a specific historical community (the ancient Greek thinkers fearing cosmic disruption) rather than articulated as a universal existential structure. The tension between a historically specific phenomenology and a transcendental claim about being-there is palpable.

**T5. The anti-Platonic critique and the residual Platonism.**

§26e rejects the ἀγαθὸν καθόλου and κίνησις παρὰ τὰ πράγματα (κίνησις as alongside beings). Yet Heidegger's own method of "retrieving" Aristotle's concepts -- reading them back into their existential ground -- itself operates with a conception of the "genuine" meaning that transcends any particular articulation. The destructive retrieval simultaneously rejects Platonic universality and claims access to the "genuine" (eigentlich) sense of the phenomena, a tension that recurs throughout GA 18 and into BT.

**T6. The wood/chest illustration and the limits of the craftwork model.**

The cabinet-maker/wood/chest example (§26g) makes κίνησις vivid through τέχνη-based production. But this raises a question the unit does not address: is the craftwork model adequate to all forms of κίνησις, including φύσις-based change (growth, generation, corruption)? The four types of κίνησις (κατ᾽ οὐσίαν, ποιόν, ποσόν, τόπον) are established through ἐπαγωγή, but the illustration privileges τέχνη over φύσις. This limitation is not accidental -- it reflects the priority of πρᾶξις and the surrounding world in Heidegger's reading -- but it leaves the question of natural movement (e.g., a plant growing) under-determined. [INTERP-low: Heidegger himself notes that the consideration "continues" in §27.]

## Assessment: The Ontological Core

U-SP2a is the ontological pivot of GA 18. Everything in the First Part -- the analysis of λόγος, πάθος, ἕξις, ἡδονή, φόβος, δόξα, φρόνησις -- was preparation for this: the demonstration that κίνησις is not a regional phenomenon (natural science) but the fundamental mode of being of worldly beings. The definition κίνησις = ἐντελέχεια τοῦ δυνάμει ὄντος is Heidegger's Aristotle-interpretation in concentrated form: it says that movement is the *presence* of a being in its *ability-to-be* precisely *insofar as* it is an ability-to-be. This is not a formula to be mechanically applied but a phenomenological description: the wood in the workshop is in movement insofar as it is there as usable-for-making-a-chest, and the cabinet-maker's being-at-work (ἐνέργεια) is the actualization of this availability.

The unit accomplishes three things simultaneously:

1. It provides the Aristotelian ground for what BT will call *Zuhandenheit* (readiness-to-hand): δύναμις as meaningfulness is the 1924 version of the being of equipment.

2. It establishes στέρησις as a positive ontological concept -- absence as constitutive of being -- which will reappear in BT as the "not" in being-towards-death and as the nullity (Nichtigkeit) of Dasein's being-guilty.

3. It demonstrates that the categories are not logical forms but existential modes of worldly address, grounded in the publicness of speaking -- a thesis that BT will reformulate as the existential-hermeneutic derivation of the categories from existentialia.

The tensions identified (T1--T6) are not weaknesses but productive ambiguities that drive the argument forward into §§27--28 and, ultimately, into the fundamental ontology of BT.
