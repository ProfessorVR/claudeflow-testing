## Section 2: Hierarchical Outline

### A. §11: The τέλειον (Metaphysics Δ16) [pp. 55–62]

- **A1. Translation of Met. Δ16 (§11a, pp. 55–57)**: Heidegger provides a working translation of Met. Δ16 (1021b 12 sqq.), identifying three senses of τέλειον: (1) temporal completeness — that beyond which no further time constitutes the being (χρόνος τέλειος); (2) consummate possession of ἀρετή — having one's being-possibility fully at one's disposal with no ὑπερβολή relative to the γένος (the consummate flute-player or doctor); (3) things that have attained their τέλος, i.e., "what is good." The translation also introduces the derivative use via μεταφέρειν (the "consummate thief") and the formula that τέλειον obtains when "nothing is left out from that which relates to the extent of the possible ability-to-be of the being in question" (p. 56).
- **A2. Arrangement of the chapter and the method of carrying-over (§11b, pp. 57–60)**:
  - **α. First two points of arrangement (pp. 57–58)**: (1) χρόνος τέλειος — beyond which nothing is there; (2) τὸ κατ' ἀρετήν — having one's most genuine being-possibility at one's disposal. Heidegger insists that τέλος does not mean "aim" or "purpose" but ἔσχατον, "what is outermost," whose primary determination is *being-an-end* (p. 57).
  - **β. Context of the treatment (pp. 58–59)**: The τέλειον is a determination of the ἀγαθόν and has, with ἀρετή, "a peculiar relation to being-completed" (p. 58). Having a being-possibility at one's disposal means "this being is already held in its end, and I have my genuine being-possibility already in hand as my possession" (p. 58). This phenomenon is τελείωσις (ἔχειν τὸ τέλος). Aristotle's explicit use of μεταφέρειν is not a linguistic deficiency but a positive hermeneutic method: carrying-over makes visible "what was already meant in the basic meaning from which we carried it away" (p. 58).
  - **γ. Revised eight-point arrangement (pp. 59–60)**: (1) beyond-which-nothing-is-there; (2) a beyond-which-nothing that genuinely determines being; (3) the carrying-over — the "good thief"; (4) relation to ἀρετή as having a being-possibility that need not explicitly reach its τέλος; (5) τελείωσις as σπουδαῖον — having τέλος "in a serious manner"; (6) τελευτή (death) as being-gone-from-being-there; (7) τέλος assigned to οὗ ἕνεκα, the ultimate in ὄρεξις; (8) categorial division — τέλειον as a fundamental being-character and "a distinctive character of being in the sense of being-there" (1021b 30–1022a 3).
- **A3. The τέλειον as limit in the sense of the genuine there (§11c, pp. 61–62)**: Τέλος has the determination of *limit* (πέρας), but this is not spatial limit (the garden fence). Τέλειον is "not a being, or a piece of a being, whose end it constitutes. Rather, τέλειον is a *way of being*, a *mode of being itself*" (p. 61). The limit constitutes the genuine there — "this end constitutes the genuine there, determining, in a genuine way, a being in its presence" (p. 62). The connection to ἐντελέχεια: "A being determined by ἐντελέχεια means fundamentally the type of being that maintains itself in its genuine being-possibility so that the possibility is consummated" (p. 62). The soul is "the ἐντελέχεια ἡ πρώτη of a body which carries in itself the possibility of living" (De an. B1, 412a 27 sq.).

### B. §12: Continuing the Consideration of the ἀγαθόν (NE A5–6) [pp. 63–70]

- **B1. The ἀνθρώπινον ἀγαθόν as the ἁπλῶς τέλειον (§12a, pp. 63–67)**: The manifoldness of concerns presents a manifoldness of τέλη. But not every τέλος is τέλειον: work-tools are τέλη alongside a πρᾶξις — the ἔργον is παρά, and the τέλος of the shoemaker has its own existence in the world as something other (ἕτερα). These have only the character of "conduciveness," pointing away from the being (p. 63). The ἁπλῶς τέλειον is that which is always καθ' αὑτό and never δι' ἕτερον (p. 65). Among the τέλη καθ' αὑτά (ἡδονή, τιμή, ἀρετή), there must be a τελειότατον — that on which being "ultimately depends" and which can only be "its way of being" (p. 65). Εὐδαιμονία is this ἁπλῶς τέλειον, given a specifically philosophical determination (p. 66). The αὔταρκες (self-sufficient) determination shows that the τέλειον cannot apply to isolated individuals but includes being-with-one-another in the πόλις (p. 66). Εὐδαιμονία is not a summative 'what' (μὴ συναριθμουμένη) but "a how of the εὖ, εὐζωΐα, that which constitutes the genuine τέλειον of being-there itself" (p. 67). Results: (1) οἰκεῖον and (2) δυσαφαίρετον are basic determinations of the ἀγαθόν; (3) being-there as ἁπλῶς τέλειον; (4) the ἀγαθόν as αὔταρκες (p. 67).
- **B2. The ψυχῆς ἐνέργεια κατ' ἀρετήν as being-possibility sufficient for the ἀνθρώπινον ἀγαθόν (§12b, pp. 67–70)**: Aristotle provides general guidance: "I discover the ἀγαθόν of a being when I see it in its ἔργον" (p. 67). The investigation narrows by ἀφορίζεσθαι (exclusion): θρεπτική and αὐξητική ζωή are shared with plants; αἴσθησις with animals. What remains as ἴδιον is πρακτική τοῦ λόγον ἔχοντος — "the concern of a being that speaks" (p. 68). The ἔργον of human beings is "living itself, apprehended in the sense of being-in-the-world μετὰ λόγου" (p. 69). The κατ' ἀρετήν further determines the ἐνέργεια: the σπουδαῖος (the "serious" one) "has taken his being-possibility seriously, who has genuinely put into work his having" of it (p. 69). The ἀνθρώπινον ἀγαθόν is thus ψυχῆς ἐνέργεια κατ' ἀρετήν. Ψυχή is "the ontologically basic determination of living, ψυχή as πρακτικὴ ἐνέργεια" (p. 69). "Psychology has nothing to do with 'consciousness' or 'experiences,' but is rather only the *doctrine of the being of living things*" (pp. 69–70). The πολιτικός carries out the focused orientation toward ψυχή "by way of the average meanings that living has about itself. Such meaning is the determination of human beings as ζῷον λόγον ἔχον" (p. 70).

## Section 4a: Key Interlocutors

### Plato
Heidegger notes that the consideration of τέλος as τέλειον "precedes the discussion with Plato, to which we will return later" (p. 59). Aristotle's critical engagement with Plato on the ἀγαθόν (NE A4–6) is acknowledged but deferred. The manifoldness of ἀγαθά δι' αὑτά and the problem of whether there is an ἀκρότατον ἀγαθόν are framed as Aristotle's response to the Platonic Idea of the Good.

### Kant
Heidegger draws an explicit parallel between the Aristotelian τέλος-determination and Kant's formula of humanity (pp. 65–66). Kant "apprehends the concept of the law of nature in the further, almost Aristotelian, sense" (p. 66). The maxim of the categorical imperative ("Handle so, als ob die Maxime deiner Handlung durch deinen Willen zum allgemeinen Naturgesetze werden sollte") is read as echoing the Aristotelian structure of being-there as end-in-itself. Heidegger reads Kant not as surpassing Aristotle but as *echoing* a determination Aristotle had already secured.

### 19th-century "teleological" interpreters (unnamed)
Heidegger's warning against "teleology" (p. 57) targets unnamed interpreters who impose a modern purposive framework on Aristotle: "being purposeful or having an aim is an utter misinterpretation, and leads to the impression that Aristotle too was one of those primitive people who lived in the nineteenth century" (p. 57).

## Section 4b: BT Anticipations

**BT-ANT-1: Death as a mode of being-there (→ BT §§46–53, Being-toward-death)**
Heidegger's insistence that "being-completed is *being-gone-from-being-there*" and that "not-being or not-being-there can be interpreted only when one has positively explicated being-there itself in a genuine way" (pp. 60–62) directly anticipates BT's existential analysis of death. The methodological claim — that death can only be understood on the basis of a prior explication of being-there's being — is the core of BT Division II's approach to Sein-zum-Tode.

**BT-ANT-2: τέλειον as Ganzheit / wholeness of Dasein (→ BT §§45–46)**
The problem of how being-there can be "complete" while still existing — how τέλειον applies to a being whose being is in each case still underway — anticipates BT's problem of the wholeness (Ganzheit) of Dasein. The emphasis that τέλειον is not a summative aggregate but a "how of being" (p. 67) foreshadows BT's resolution that Dasein's wholeness must be understood existentially.

**BT-ANT-3: εὐδαιμονία as Eigentlichkeit / authenticity (→ BT §§54–60)**
The ἁπλῶς τέλειον as that which "is constantly and always καθ' αὑτό" — the being-possibility that depends on nothing outside being-there itself — anticipates the structure of Eigentlichkeit (authenticity). The σπουδαῖος as one for whom "everything rides on it" (p. 60) echoes the earnestness (Entschlossenheit) of resolute Dasein. [INTERP-low: Heidegger does not use the term Eigentlichkeit in this passage, but the structural parallel is strong.]

**BT-ANT-4: ψυχή as ontological determination of living, not consciousness (→ BT §10)**
The insistence that "psychology has nothing to do with 'consciousness' or 'experiences' but is rather only the *doctrine of the being of living things*" (pp. 69–70) directly anticipates BT §10's critique of Dilthey's philosophy of life and the traditional psychology of the subject. The de-psychologizing of ψυχή is a condition for the existential analytic.

**BT-ANT-5: Being-with-one-another as constitutive of αὔταρκες (→ BT §§25–27, Mitsein)**
The claim that self-sufficiency necessarily includes "being with one's parents, with children, with one's wife, friends, and those who are in the πόλις with one" (p. 66) foreshadows BT's analysis of Mitsein as an existential structure of Dasein, not an addendum to an isolated subject.

**BT-ANT-6: Being-in-the-world as basic constitution (→ BT §§12–13)**
Heidegger explicitly uses the phrase "being-in-a-world" (In-einer-Welt-sein) at p. 62 in connection with the being for whom the possibility of being explicit of its genuine being remains: "for a being that we characterize as living, being-in-a-world." This is a direct terminological anticipation, not merely structural.

## Section 5: Tensions & Open Questions

### T-1: Tension between τέλειον as mode of being and τέλειον applied to death [linked to P1, P4]
If τέλειον is a way of being, and death is "being-gone-from-being-there," does τέλειον apply to death in the same sense as to the consummate flute-player? Heidegger calls the death-application a "carrying-over" (μεταφέρειν), implying derivative status. Yet he also calls death "a distinctive mode of being-there" (p. 61), suggesting it is not merely derivative. [INTERP-low: This tension anticipates BT's problem of whether Dasein can "have" its death as its ownmost possibility.]

### T-2: The relation between ἐντελέχεια and ἐνέργεια [linked to P3, §12b]
Heidegger distinguishes ἐντελέχεια (maintaining-itself-in-its-end) from ἐνέργεια (being-at-work) at p. 62, but the precise relation is left undeveloped. The formula ψυχῆς ἐνέργεια κατ' ἀρετήν (p. 69) uses ἐνέργεια rather than ἐντελέχεια. Whether ἐνέργεια is a more general term of which ἐντελέχεια is a specific determination, or whether they name distinct structures, awaits the Second Part's analysis of κίνησις. [INTERP-high: Heidegger does not explicitly address this gap in this unit.]

### T-3: The ontological status of non-human τέλη [linked to P1, B1]
Instruments (shoes, hammers) have τέλη that "are not themselves τέλεια" but have only the character of "conduciveness" (p. 63). Whether these instrumental τέλη derive from the τέλειον of being-there or have independent ontological standing is not resolved. [INTERP-low: This connects to BT's analysis of Zuhandenheit and the reference-structure of equipment.]

### T-4: Scope of the Kantian parallel [linked to P11]
Heidegger draws a connection between Aristotle's τέλειον-determination and Kant's categorical imperative (pp. 65–66), but its scope is unclear. Is it a structural analogy, a historical dependence, or a claim that Kant's moral philosophy is grounded in Aristotelian ontology? The passage is suggestive but undeveloped. [INTERP-high]

### T-5: Whether the exclusion method (ἀφορίζεσθαι) is genuinely Aristotelian or Heideggerian [linked to P8]
Heidegger presents the exclusion of shared capacities as Aristotle's own procedure (NE A6, 1097b–1098a). But the phenomenological framing — "it is a matter of *seeing* the ἴδιον" (p. 68) — imports a Husserlian-phenomenological idiom. The tension between Aristotle's method and Heidegger's redescription of it is characteristic of the entire course. [INTERP-low]
