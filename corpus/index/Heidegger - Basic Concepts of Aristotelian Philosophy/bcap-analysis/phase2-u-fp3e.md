## 1. Unit Metadata

**One-sentence thesis**: Heidegger's analysis of φόβος (Rhetoric B5) completes the rhetorical treatment of the πάθη by demonstrating that fear -- as a disposition constituted by φαντασία of the impending, the dispositional ground of οἴεσθαι, and the condition of the possibility of courage -- is the master case for showing that the πάθη are not psychical annexes but "the ground out of which speaking arises," i.e., the basic possibilities in which being-there is primarily oriented toward itself; §22 supplements this with ἀληθεύειν as a ἕξις of self-disclosure in κοινωνία and a brief determination of the world as world of nature.

## 2. Hierarchical Outline

### §21. Φόβος (Rhetoric B5), pp. 167-175

Two directions announced at the outset (p. 167):
1. φόβος as πάθος -- "being-afraid" as a fully determinate concretion of "being-out-of-composure"
2. φόβος as πίστις -- a clue to how "being-afraid" is a basic determination of the being-there of the hearer who co-speaks in deliberation

#### §21a. Schematic Outline (p. 167)
- Three-part structure of Aristotle's treatment: (1) topic, (2) definition, (3) basic determinations (πῶς ἔχοντες)
- φοβερά characterized, φοβεροί (frightening human beings), σημεῖα (encounter-characters)

#### §21b. Topic, First Definition, First Determinations (pp. 168-169)
- **Topic**: ποῖα δὲ φοβοῦνται καὶ τίνας καὶ πῶς ἔχοντες (Rhet. B5, 1382a20) -- what is feared, which persons are feared, in what disposition
- **Formal definition**: φόβος = λύπη τις ἢ ταραχὴ ἐκ φαντασίας μέλλοντος κακοῦ φθαρτικοῦ ἢ λυπηροῦ (Rhet. B5, 1382a21)
  - λύπη: distress, a being-toned-down
  - ταραχή: confusion, being-led-by-another, "being-through-another"
  - φαντασία: not αἴσθησις (perception of present) but what "shows itself" as not-yet-there; ἐκ φαντασίας = "from something that shows itself"
  - μέλλοντος: coming-toward-me; the futural character
  - κακόν φθαρτικόν/λυπηρόν: what comes toward me "in the mode of harmfulness"
- **Heidegger's reformulation** (p. 168, italicized): "a *disposition that is set before an approaching possibility that pertains to me, comes toward me, and as such announces itself, specifically through the announcement*"
- Key finding: φόβος discloses the **futural** character of being-there

#### §21c. The Threatening (φοβερά) and the Encounter-Characters (σημεῖα) (pp. 169-171)
- Three aspects of threateningness:
  1. **Possibility/indeterminacy**: φαίνεται -- must "show itself" as not yet genuinely there but approaching; characterized by the *indeterminate* (Rhet. B5, 1382a29)
  2. **Powerfulness** (δύναμιν ἔχειν μεγάλην): what comes toward me can do me harm; I find myself in a definite weakness relative to it (Rhet. B5, 1382a28)
  3. **Proximity** (πλησιασμός): "being brought into proximity" turns threat into *danger*; "Something threatening in the greater distance is not danger. Threat becomes danger when it *draws nearer to me*" (p. 170, citing Rhet. B5, 1382a25 sqq.)
- The φοβερόν is *announced* by σημεῖα that take over the peculiar there-character of the φοβερόν (not-being-there-in-approaching):
  - (1) ἔχθρα, ὀργή -- "hostile attitude," "rage"
  - (2) ἀδικία -- "unjust attitude"
  - (3) ἀρετὴ ὑβριζομένη -- "seriousness that ridicules is provoked"
- These σημεῖα enhance the *indeterminacy* of the possible; they cultivate possibility "in its very indeterminacy" (p. 170)
- **Crucial principle** (p. 170): "there is *no parallel* between the *frightening* and *being-afraid*" -- not all aspects of the φοβερόν are contained in fearing, and vice versa. Fear is "precisely, a definite finding-oneself, a behaving with respect to oneself in being-threatened"
- The φοβερόν is *possible*; what is possible is enhanced by being *there and not there* -- it *announces* itself. The manner of presence is φαντασία, not αἴσθησις. Proximity = πλησιασμός; finding-oneself in danger through the πλησιασμός of the threatening (pp. 170-171)

#### §21d. Frightening Human Beings (φοβεροί) (pp. 171-173)
- φοβεροί = φοβερά seen according to the orientation of being-with-one-another
- Characterized by ζωὴ πρακτική and προαιρέσεις (practical resolve)
- Aristotle enumerates **nine characters** of frightening persons (Rhet. B5, 1382b4-18):
  1. Those to whom I am compromised (they know my crimes) -- possibility of betrayal
  2. "The powerful" -- the dual "can" (proairesis) already characterized
  3. Those injured or believing themselves injured -- seeking revenge
  4. Those who have injured another and now fear revenge in return
  5. "Competitors in one and the same matter" -- capability carries threat
  6. Those who are a threat to whoever can obtain more (detour through others)
  7. "Those who are superior to us, and have already ruined us"
  8. Those who are weaker -- they may attack out of desperation
  9. The πρᾶοι, εἴρωνες, πανοῦργοι -- the reserved, the ironic, the slick: most frightening through the *indeterminacy of proximity* (p. 173)
- All nine are modes of being-there characterized by προαιρέσεις
- The φοβεροί are the φοβερά "seen according to this orientation of *being-with-one-another*" within the πόλις (p. 173)

#### §21e. Genuineness of the Frightening (φοβερόν) (p. 173)
- The genuinely frightening: what is *unavoidable* -- "not in an absolute sense, but *only for me*; the unavoidability is there with the opponent" (p. 173)
- Being-threatened determined through ἐλπίς of the one threatened
- What threatens to the highest degree must hold out the *prospect of remaining absent* -- the frightening becomes more genuine as the prospect of help disappears
- Constitutive tension: fear requires both the unavoidability of the threat *and* a receding ἐλπὶς σωτηρίας (hope of being saved)

#### §21f. Disposition in Being Afraid (pp. 173-174)
- Being-afraid = an οἴεσθαι: the one afraid must "believe" that the definite thing threatens *him*, *now* (Rhet. B5, 1382b28 sqq.)
- Where οἴεσθαι is absent (those who regard themselves safe from every threat -- the rich, the powerful, the arrogant), fear *cannot arise* (pp. 174, citing Rhet. B5, 1382b34-35)
- A different οἴεσθαι: those who believe nothing more can happen to them ("hardened") also stand outside genuine fear (p. 174)
- "This type of appropriating genuine being-there into one's beliefs about it must be shifted; it must come into a definite οἴεσθαι, so that what is frightening in general can come into my proximity" (p. 174)
- The ταραχή is the *opposedness of οἴεσθαι and ἐλπίς*: believing oneself in danger while at the same time hoping to escape (p. 174)
- Two aspects of ταραχή: διῶξις and φυγή -- both "basic determinations of the genuine being-moved of being-there" (p. 174)

#### §21g. Fear as πίστις; Courage; The πάθη as Ground of λόγος (pp. 175-176)
- Insofar as οἴεσθαι and ἐλπίς bring human beings into disquiet, they become ready to *deliberate* (βουλεύειν; Rhet. B5, 1383a6)
- Fear cultivated in the hearer makes them run to one another, confer, seek counsel -- this is the connection to πίστις
- Fear as disposition that *brings to speaking*: "What appears here in the circle of everydayness is a phenomenon that has a much more originary foundation" (p. 175)
- Fear in its uncanny mode = *anxiety* (ἄγνοια of what one fears): "an indication of how the *γένεσις of speaking is measured by being-there*" (p. 175)
- **Fear → courage** (ἀνδρεία): "Fear has, as a determinate πάθος, the possibility of a ἕξις. Such a possibility is *courage*. ... *Fear is the condition of the possibility of courage*" (p. 175)
  - One can only be courageous if genuinely afraid
  - "It is a question of *taking hold of* courage ... of *being afraid in the right manner*, and thereby coming to resoluteness" (p. 175)
  - Augustine cited: "initium sapientiae timor Domini" (p. 175)
- ἐλπὶς σωτηρίας indicates that fearing stands in connection with being-there itself; ἕξις = σῴζει μεσότητα, "preserves the mean" (Rhet. B5, 1383a13; NE Γ9-10)
- σῴζειν used metaphorically: "God preserves the heavens"; σωτηρία as counter-concept to φθορά, "disappearing-out-of-being-there" (p. 176)
- **Macro-conclusion** of the entire Chapter 3 rhetoric analysis (p. 176): "the πάθη are not merely an annex of psychical processes, but are rather *the ground out of which speaking arises, and which what is expressed grows back into*"; they are "*the basic possibilities in which being-there itself is primarily oriented toward itself*, finds itself"
- The primary being-oriented is "not a *knowing*, but rather a *finding-oneself*" (p. 176)

### §22. Supplements to the Explication of Being-There as Being-in-the-World (pp. 176-181)

#### §22a. The ἕξις of ἀληθεύειν (NE Δ12-13), pp. 176-178
- πάθη as πίστεις = πρότασεις: "that from which and on the basis of which ... what is known is taken" (p. 176)
- The δόξα in whose cultivation the πάθη participate characterizes the interpretedness of being-there in everydayness
- κοινωνία = "being-with-one-another" as "having-the-world-there-with-one-another" (p. 176)
- Special possibility of κοινωνία: ὁμιλία / συζῆν ("living-with-one-another"), characterized by δόξα in the "more or less" of everydayness (NE Δ12-13, 1126b11 sqq.)
- A human being is *non-concrete* with respect to himself -- yet has the possibility of deciding something genuine through προαίρεσις
- There is a ἕξις of ἀληθεύειν in relation to ὁμιλία/συζῆν: "having the legitimacy of comportment toward others and toward oneself at one's disposal" (p. 177)
- Defined by Aristotle as ἀληθευτικός (NE Δ13, 1127a24): having being-there with discoveredness, presenting oneself as one is and as one thinks -- "not a self-concealing, feigning, *presenting oneself as one is and as one thinks*" (p. 177)
- The συζῆν characterized by ζωὴ πρακτικὴ μετὰ λόγου (NE Δ13, 1127a24)
- This manifests in προσποίησις: asserting oneself into what one claims about oneself
  - **ἀλαζών**: the boaster, who makes something up about himself, claims more (NE Δ13, 1127a21 sq.)
  - **εἴρων**: the self-deprecator, who denies what he is, makes himself less important -- Socrates as example (NE Δ13, 1127a23)
  - **ἀληθευτικός**: the mean -- "truthful," "undisguised" -- speaks and behaves as he is (NE Δ13, 1127a23 sq.)
- The εἴρων has good and bad possibilities; the ἀληθευτικός is the μέσος: "each speaks and behaves in the way that he is" (p. 178)
- **Architectonic conclusion**: ἀληθεύειν as ἕξις is accomplished in being-in-the-world; the disclosive being-oriented in being-toward-oneself and being-toward-others is characterized by ἀλήθεια
- More precisely: ἀληθεύειν in the mode of being-able-to-be-unconcealed
- Aristotle treats the manifold possibilities of ἀληθεύειν in NE Book 6: (1) σοφία, (2) φρόνησις -- "looking-around" in the moment -- and the highest: θεωρεῖν as βίος θεωρητικός, "the genuine and highest possibility of Greek existence" (p. 178)

#### §22b. The World as World of Nature (pp. 178-179)
- The environing world is determined as ἐνδεχόμενον ἄλλως: "it is more or less the way that change assigns it" (p. 178)
- Nature (φύσις) is not a being-region alongside the world but "the world itself such as it shows itself in the environing world" (p. 178)
- Nature as "being-there-always-already": sailing the sea, fish in the stream, producing from the mine, the forest
- Orientation in the world: centered on the earth (μέσον); three basic movements:
  - (1) ἄνω -- away from middle
  - (2) κάτω -- toward middle
  - (3) κύκλος -- around middle
- All beings in the κόσμος characterized by παρουσία (presence of what is always already there) and πέρας (having-become-complete, the limit)
- **Key claim** (p. 179): "Limit is not somehow determined by the relation of one being to another, but rather the limit is itself a *being-aspect in beings*; πέρας is its *site*, its *place*, its *being-produced*, *being-in-its-place*"
- Site (τόπος) = a *positive determination of being*
- Things of nature must be seen *through the environing world* -- only then does one grasp their mode of being-there

## 3. Key Concepts

### 3.1 φόβος (phobos) -- Fear / Being-Afraid
- **greek_form**: φόβος
- **definition**: A λύπη τις ἢ ταραχή arising from φαντασία of an impending destructive or painful evil; Heidegger reformulates: "a disposition set before an approaching possibility that pertains to me, comes toward me, and announces itself through the announcement" (p. 168)
- **type**: πάθος (fundamental disposition of being-there)
- **dependencies**: φαντασία (the showing-itself of what is not yet there), λύπη (distress), ταραχή (confusion/disquiet), μέλλον (the futural), κακόν (evil/harm)
- **function**: Discloses the futural character of being-there; grounds λόγος (speaking arises from the πάθη); is the condition of the possibility of ἀνδρεία (courage); serves as πίστις in rhetorical deliberation
- **pages**: 167-176

### 3.2 φοβερά (phobera) -- The Threatening / The Fearsome
- **greek_form**: φοβερά
- **definition**: Objects, circumstances, and persons that send one into fear upon encountering them; constituted by three structural moments: possibility/indeterminacy, powerfulness (δύναμις), and proximity (πλησιασμός)
- **type**: encounter-determination (what is encountered as threatening)
- **dependencies**: φόβος (to which they stand in non-parallel relation), σημεῖα (which announce them), πλησιασμός (which converts threat into danger)
- **function**: Articulates the world-side of the fear-phenomenon; demonstrates that the threatening cannot be defined from the object-side alone (no parallel principle)
- **pages**: 169-173

### 3.3 σημεῖα (semeia) -- Encounter-Characters / Announcing Signs
- **greek_form**: σημεῖα
- **definition**: The encounter-characters that take over the there-character of the φοβερόν (the function of not-being-there-in-approaching); three types: ἔχθρα/ὀργή, ἀδικία, ἀρετὴ ὑβριζομένη
- **type**: structural moment of threateningness
- **dependencies**: φοβερά (whose proximity they announce), πλησιασμός (the proximity they constitute)
- **function**: Enhance the indeterminacy of the possible; make the threatening more threatening precisely by leaving it open; through the announcement the threatening becomes the dangerous
- **pages**: 170-171

### 3.4 οἴεσθαι (oiesthai) -- Believing / Dispositional Self-Relation
- **greek_form**: οἴεσθαι
- **definition**: The "believing" that the definite threatening thing threatens *him*, this definite human being, *now*; the dispositional manner in which the φοβερά are there "for me"
- **type**: dispositional ground (the "how" of being-afraid)
- **dependencies**: φόβος (which requires it as precondition), ἐλπίς (with which it stands in constitutive opposition as ταραχή)
- **function**: Determines what can count as threatening for a given being-there; its absence explains why the powerful, rich, and arrogant cannot be brought into fear; constitutes the dispositional ground of what will become Befindlichkeit
- **pages**: 173-174

### 3.5 ἐλπίς (elpis) -- Hope (of Being Saved)
- **greek_form**: ἐλπίς
- **definition**: ἐλπὶς σωτηρίας -- the hope of salvation that is constitutive of being-afraid as such; being-afraid believes oneself in danger *while at the same time hoping to escape*
- **type**: co-constitutive moment of fear (with οἴεσθαι)
- **dependencies**: οἴεσθαι (the opposed moment), ταραχή (their opposedness), σωτηρία (the correlate of hope)
- **function**: Makes fear's constitutive tension visible: genuine fear requires that the prospect of help be *receding but not absent*; what threatens to the highest degree holds out the prospect of remaining absent while help disappears
- **pages**: 173-175

### 3.6 πλησιασμός (plesiasmos) -- Proximity / Being-Brought-into-Nearness
- **greek_form**: πλησιασμός
- **definition**: The being-brought-into-proximity that turns threat into danger; "Something threatening in the greater distance is not danger. Threat becomes danger when it draws nearer to me" (p. 170)
- **type**: structural moment of the φοβερά
- **dependencies**: φοβερά (whose third aspect it constitutes), σημεῖα (which effect proximity through announcement)
- **function**: Converts the merely possible-threatening into actual danger; constitutes finding-oneself-in-a-situation-of-danger; the σημεῖα take over this function of approaching
- **pages**: 170-171

### 3.7 ἀνδρεία (andreia) -- Courage
- **greek_form**: ἀνδρεία
- **definition**: The ἕξις of being-afraid in the right manner; "Fear is the condition of the possibility of courage" (p. 175)
- **type**: ἕξις (disposition as settled capacity)
- **dependencies**: φόβος (of which it is the ἕξις), ἕξις (the general concept), φρόνησις [INTERP-low: implied via "being afraid in the right manner"]
- **function**: Demonstrates that fear is ontologically productive, not deficient; shows the πάθος-ἕξις structure as the ground of resoluteness; connects to the fundamental relevance of fear for being-there
- **pages**: 175

### 3.8 ἀληθεύειν (aletheuein) -- Truth-Disclosing / Being-Unconcealed
- **greek_form**: ἀληθεύειν
- **definition**: The ἕξις of being-able-to-be-unconcealed in the mode of practical life with λόγος; presenting oneself as one is and as one thinks (NE Δ12-13)
- **type**: ἕξις (settled capacity of self-disclosure)
- **dependencies**: κοινωνία / συζῆν (the social context), ἀλήθεια (as the character of disclosedness), λόγος (speech), προσποίησις (the field of self-presentation in which it operates)
- **function**: Supplements the πάθη-analysis by showing a second mode of being-there's self-disclosure; grounds the five ἕξεις of NE Book 6 (σοφία, φρόνησις, etc.); reveals truth as embedded in practical-social life
- **pages**: 176-178

### 3.9 πέρας (peras) -- Limit / Boundary as Being-Aspect
- **greek_form**: πέρας
- **definition**: The having-become-complete of a being; "a *being-aspect in beings*; πέρας is its site, its place, its being-produced, being-in-its-place" (p. 179) -- not a relational property but a positive determination of being
- **type**: ontological determination
- **dependencies**: κόσμος (the totality in which πέρας operates), τόπος (site, which πέρας constitutes), παρουσία (the presence that πέρας delimits)
- **function**: Recovers the Aristotelian sense of limit against modern relational/mathematical conceptions; grounds the determination of nature-beings through the environing world
- **pages**: 179

## 4. Main Positions

### U-FP3e-P1: Fear discloses the futural character of being-there
Φόβος arises from φαντασία μέλλοντος -- an imagination of what is *coming toward me*, not from perception (αἴσθησις) of what is present. The threatening is characterized by not-yet-being-there, by approaching possibility. Fear thus reveals that being-there is constitutively oriented toward its future.
- **Source**: pp. 168-169, citing Rhet. B5, 1382a21
- **Confidence**: High (directly stated)

### U-FP3e-P2: The threatening is constituted by possibility, powerfulness, and proximity
The φοβερά are characterized by three structural moments: (1) possibility/indeterminacy -- it is not yet actual; (2) δύναμις -- it has the power to overcome my definite weakness; (3) πλησιασμός -- it is being brought into proximity. "Something threatening in the greater distance is not danger. Threat becomes danger when it *draws nearer to me*" (p. 170).
- **Source**: pp. 169-170, citing Rhet. B5, 1382a25 sqq.
- **Confidence**: High (directly stated)

### U-FP3e-P3: There is no parallel between the frightening and being-afraid
"There is *no parallel* between the *frightening* and *being-afraid*" (p. 170). Not all aspects of the φοβερόν are contained in fearing, and vice versa. Fear is a finding-oneself with respect to being-threatened, not a reaction to a pre-given frightening thing. The attempt to define fear from the object-side alone fails.
- **Source**: p. 170
- **Confidence**: High (explicitly italicized as a phenomenological principle)

### U-FP3e-P4: The σημεῖα enhance threat through indeterminacy
The three σημεῖα -- ἔχθρα/ὀργή, ἀδικία, ἀρετὴ ὑβριζομένη -- are not indicators of determinate harm but modes of proximity that intensify the *indeterminacy* of the possible. "We will have to consider how these σημεῖα are precisely σημεῖα by virtue of the fact that they cultivate this possibility in its very indeterminacy" (p. 170). The announcement does not make the danger determinate but makes it *more threatening* by leaving it open.
- **Source**: pp. 170-171, citing Rhet. B5, 1382a30-35
- **Confidence**: High (directly stated)

### U-FP3e-P5: Being-afraid requires the dispositional ground of οἴεσθαι
Fear does not arise automatically from an objective threat but requires that one "believe" (οἴεσθαι) that the threatening threatens *him*, *now*, *from this definite person*. Where this οἴεσθαι is absent, fear cannot arise. The disposition determines what can count as threatening: "it must come into a definite οἴεσθαι, so that what is frightening in general can come into my proximity" (p. 174).
- **Source**: pp. 173-174, citing Rhet. B5, 1382b28 sqq.
- **Confidence**: High (directly stated)

### U-FP3e-P6: The genuinely frightening is constituted by unavoidability-for-me and receding ἐλπίς
"The *unavoidable*, not in an absolute sense, but *only for me*" (p. 173). The genuineness of the φοβερόν lies in the conjunction of unavoidability, being-with-another who possesses the threat, and the receding prospect of help. Fear requires a constitutive tension: οἴεσθαι (believing oneself in danger) opposed to ἐλπίς (hoping to escape). The ταραχή is nothing other than this opposedness.
- **Source**: pp. 173-174, citing Rhet. B5, 1382b22 sqq.
- **Confidence**: High (directly stated)

### U-FP3e-P7: Fear is the condition of the possibility of courage
"Fear has, as a determinate πάθος, the possibility of a ἕξις. Such a possibility is *courage*. ... *Fear is the condition of the possibility of courage*" (p. 175). Courage is not the absence of fear but a ἕξις of being-afraid *in the right manner* -- "taking hold of courage," "being afraid in the right manner and thereby coming to resoluteness" (p. 175).
- **Source**: p. 175, citing Rhet. B5, 1383a6 sqq.
- **Confidence**: High (explicitly stated and italicized)

### U-FP3e-P8: Fear brings to speaking -- the πάθη are the ground of λόγος
Insofar as οἴεσθαι and ἐλπίς bring humans into disquiet, they become ready to *deliberate* (βουλεύειν; Rhet. B5, 1383a6). The πάθη "are not merely an annex of psychical processes, but are rather *the ground out of which speaking arises, and which what is expressed grows back into*" (p. 176). This is the master claim of the entire Chapter 3 rhetoric analysis.
- **Source**: pp. 175-176
- **Confidence**: High (explicitly italicized as macro-conclusion)

### U-FP3e-P9: The πάθη are the basic possibilities in which being-there is primarily oriented toward itself
"The primary being-oriented, the illumination of its being-in-the-world is not a *knowing*, but rather a *finding-oneself*" (p. 176). The πάθη are not secondary colorings of cognition but the primary way being-there is disclosed to itself.
- **Source**: p. 176
- **Confidence**: High (directly stated as the existential thesis toward which the entire analysis builds)

### U-FP3e-P10: The genesis of speaking is measured by being-there and characterized by uncanniness
Fear in its uncanny mode -- "where we do not know what we are afraid of" -- is anxiety or dread. This indicates "how the *γένεσις of speaking is measured by being-there*, as speaking is connected with the basic determination of being-there itself, which is characterized by *uncanniness*" (p. 175). The origin of λόγος is not rational deliberation but the uncanny (Unheimlichkeit) of being-there.
- **Source**: p. 175
- **Confidence**: High (directly stated)

### U-FP3e-P11: ἀληθεύειν is a ἕξις accomplished in being-in-the-world
Truth-disclosing (ἀληθεύειν) is not a theoretical capacity but a ἕξις -- a way of having oneself at one's disposal in relation to others. The ἀλαζών (boaster) and εἴρων (self-deprecator) represent deficient modes; the ἀληθευτικός (the mean) presents "oneself as one is and as one thinks" (pp. 177-178). Truth is thus embedded in the practical-social life of being-with-one-another.
- **Source**: pp. 177-178, citing NE Δ12-13, 1127a-b
- **Confidence**: High (directly stated)

### U-FP3e-P12: Nature is the always-already-being-there of the world
Nature is not a region alongside the world but "the world itself such as it shows itself" -- characterized by ἐνδεχόμενον ἄλλως (change) and the always-already (p. 178). Things of nature must be grasped *through* the environing world, not abstracted from it.
- **Source**: pp. 178-179
- **Confidence**: High (directly stated)

### U-FP3e-P13: πέρας is a positive determination of being, not a relational property
"Limit is not somehow determined by the relation of one being to another, but rather the limit is itself a *being-aspect in beings*; πέρας is its site, its place, its being-produced, being-in-its-place" (p. 179). Site (τόπος) is a positive ontological determination.
- **Source**: p. 179
- **Confidence**: High (directly stated)

## 5. Tensions & Open Questions

### T1. The no-parallel principle and its limits
Heidegger asserts that "there is no parallel between the frightening and being-afraid" (p. 170), yet the entire §21c-e analysis proceeds by characterizing fear *through* the structural moments of the frightening (possibility, powerfulness, proximity). If the two sides are genuinely non-parallel, what authorizes deriving the dispositional structure from the object-side analysis? [INTERP-high: Heidegger may intend that the non-parallelism is precisely what makes the analysis phenomenologically productive -- the *gap* between the two sides is what reveals fear as a finding-oneself rather than a reaction.]

### T2. The ἐλπίς-structure as constitutive tension
Heidegger's claim that genuine fear requires both the unavoidability of the threat *and* the ἐλπὶς σωτηρίας introduces an internal tension: fear requires hope. The ταραχή is "nothing other than the opposedness of οἴεσθαι and ἐλπίς" (p. 174). But if hope is entirely extinguished, is the result still fear -- or despair? If hope remains robust, does fear dissolve into mere concern? The boundaries of the phenomenon are left undrawn.

### T3. The nine φοβεροί and the undeveloped social ontology
Heidegger's detailed catalogue of nine types of frightening persons (§21d) is unique in the 1924 lectures. This material has no direct analogue in BT and represents a distinctively rhetorical-political contribution that is lost in the later existential analytic. The tension: Heidegger treats the φοβεροί as illustrations of being-with-one-another, but does not draw out the implications for a systematic social ontology of threat, power, and vulnerability within the πόλις.

### T4. The compressed treatment of §22b
The brief treatment of nature, κόσμος, πέρας, and τόπος in under two pages (pp. 178-179) feels compressed relative to the elaborate φόβος analysis. It reads as a placeholder for the Second Part's physics interpretation (§§25-28). The question: does Heidegger intend this as a genuine supplement or merely as an anticipatory signpost?

### T5. The transition from πάθη as ground of λόγος (§21g) to ἀληθεύειν as ἕξις (§22a)
The juxtaposition is architectonically significant: Heidegger moves directly from the culmination of the rhetoric analysis (πάθη ground λόγος) to the NE analysis of truth-disclosing in social life. [INTERP-low: This suggests the rhetoric analysis was always aimed at recovering ἀλήθεια from within practical being-with-one-another, not merely analyzing persuasion techniques.]

### T6. Fear as uncanny (p. 175) and the boundary of the text
Heidegger briefly gestures toward fear in its uncanny mode -- "where we do not know what we are afraid of" -- and calls this anxiety or dread. But this uncanny fear exceeds Aristotle's analysis in Rhet. B5, which deals exclusively with determinate φόβος (fear of specific things). The tension: is Heidegger reading Aristotle or reading *beyond* Aristotle at this point? [INTERP-high: the reference to uncanniness as the basic determination of being-there goes beyond what the Aristotelian text warrants and is Heidegger's own existential extrapolation.]
