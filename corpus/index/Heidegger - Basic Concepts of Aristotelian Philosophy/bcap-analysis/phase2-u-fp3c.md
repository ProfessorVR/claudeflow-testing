## 1. Unit Metadata

**One-sentence thesis**: Heidegger interprets hexis and pathos as co-originary ontological structures of Dasein's being-in-the-world -- hexis as the determinate how of being-composed toward the moment (kairos), arete as its mesotes-structured cultivation through temporally characterized habituation, and pathos as the full bodily being-taken that discloses the material-formal double constitution (eidos/hule) of human being-there.

## 2. Hierarchical Outline

### SS17: Hexis (Metaphysics Delta 23 and 20, NE B 1-5) [pp. 116-128]

#### SS17a: Echein and hexis [pp. 116-119]

Heidegger opens from the transition at the end of SS16: pathos already has within itself the relation to hexis, and both concepts lend themselves to being characterized as *fundamental concepts of being*. He turns to Metaphysics Delta 23, where Aristotle treats echein ("having") in four senses:

1. **Leading according to one's own nature or impulse** -- having as directing according to physis or horme (e.g., fever "has" the person, tyrants "have" towns, the dressed "have" clothes) (p. 117, Met. Delta 23, 1023a 8 sqq.).
2. **Being-in as dektikon** -- "in itself something is present, for which being-present the being in question itself has the readiness." The metal "has" the look of a statue; hule here is not indeterminate "material" but a positive mode of being-there, a "being-in-preparedness" (p. 117, Met. Delta 23, 1023a 11 sqq.).
3. **Containing/enclosing (periechon)** -- having all around; the basin has water, the town has people, the ship has sailors. Being-part is always the determinate belongingness of a part (p. 117, Met. Delta 23, 1023a 13 sqq.).
4. **Holding off, restraining (koluein)** -- "holding off another being, hindering it from being as it would like to be according to its genuine horme." Atlas holds the vault of heaven; sunechon, "holding-together" (p. 117, Met. Delta 23, 1023a 17 sqq.).

All four kinds mark beings with the being-character of *being after a definite being-possibility*, or its negation as *holding off something from being genuinely as it would like to be* (p. 118). Echein is said "in the same way as being-in-something" (epomenos); having and being share the same character (Met. Delta 23, 1023a 21 sqq.). Hexis then appears alongside the categories: it is a mode of being-there (Chapter 20), "the being-present of the having and of what is had," the energeia of having as such (p. 118, Met. Delta 20, 1022b 4 sq.).

The metaxu: "when the one does something, the other is done; thus the doing as such is the metaxu, the between" (p. 118, Met. Delta 20, 1022b 5 sqq.). Having-on (as in putting on clothes) is telos -- the clothes are what they genuinely are only when put on and worn. Hexis is further characterized as diathesis, "allotment" (taxis) with the character of thesis, a posited (not merely accidental) being-thrown-together (p. 119, Met. Delta 19, 1022b 1).

**Conclusion of SS17a**: "Hexis is *the determination of the genuineness of being-there in a moment of being-composed as to something*" (p. 119). The various hexeis are the various modes of being able to be composed. Praxis is characterized through arete, and arete through the proairetic hexis. Praxis as the how of being-in-the-world appears as the being-context that we can also designate as *existence* (p. 119). Being-composed is not optional or indeterminate; in hexis lies the primary orientation toward the kairos: "I am there, come what may!" (p. 119). Hexis is "related in itself to another possibility, to the possibility of my being, *that within my being something comes over me, which brings me out of composure*" (p. 119).

#### SS17b: Hexis and arete [pp. 119-121]

Context: hexis plays a fundamental role in the Aristotelian analysis of being, and also becomes important for another basic determination. Aristotle discusses stereseis in opposition to hexis and in detail with kinesis. The task is to understand the pathe as those determinations that characterize the audience in a definite situation. The pathe are co-decisive for the manner and mode of legein; the logos has its basis in the pathe themselves (p. 120). To take hold of pathos in the right way, "we are choosing a roundabout path toward hexis, by way of a general hermeneutic guide" (p. 120).

Three questions for every pathos (Rhet. B 1, 1378a 24-25):
1. Pos diakeimenoi eisin -- "how do we find ourselves genuinely?"
2. Poiai -- "about what do we get angry, lose composure?"
3. Epi poiois -- "in relation to whom, in encountering which sort of human beings, are we there in this way?"

These three questions disclose the basic structure of pathos: the how of finding-oneself, the what of the situation, and the with-whom of being-with-one-another.

**Historical excursus** (pp. 120-121): The Aristotelian doctrine of the pathe had effects on Thomas's doctrine of affects, on medieval theology generally (the pathe are a basic question of theology), on Luther (fear and the counter-concept to faith, *Sermo de poenitentia*, timor servilis vs. timor castus, connected to attritio and intritio), on Augustine (*De diversis quaetionibus octoginta tribus*, quaestio 33; *De civitate Dei* XIV.5 on Pelagianism), on Johannes Damascenus (*De fide orthodoxa* II), Gregory of Nyssa, Nemesius (*Peri physeos anthropou*), Dionysius the Areopagite (*De divinis nominibus*). Only Dilthey has given thorough philosophical treatment to the pathe since the Renaissance. Heidegger's point: "the whole development of the doctrine of the affects, from Aristotle through the medieval era to the present, has not been analyzed philosophically" (p. 120).

From the connection between hexis and arete, Heidegger derives *four basic aspects of being-there* (pp. 121-122):
1. Praxis is in itself the concern of being-there which is concerned. Being-there as concern is *care about itself* (hidden in the concept of hexis as having).
2. In hexis, being-there shows itself in its *particularity* -- human being as being-there is particular, at the moment.
3. This being-composed, oriented toward the moment, is the sort of possibility that has seized being-there on the basis of its particular situation -- staying within degrees of "more or less."
4. The genesis of hexis, the manner and mode in which being-there comes to be composed, can have the opportunity and type of cultivation only *in being-there itself*.

#### SS17b-alpha: The genesis of arete [pp. 122-124]

Arete as hexis is not a property or possession brought from without; it is a *mode of being-there itself*. The peculiar category of the "how" -- how being-there is *determined by its being*, characterized by *temporality*, by the stretching across time. Arete comes to be di' ethous, "through habit" (NE B 1, 1103a 25 sq.). The possibilities of being-composed are graspable only by way of *undergoing* various situations involving risk. We appropriate hexis not as possession but as chresiamenoi eschomen -- "using, taking-opportunities" (NE B 1, 1103a 30 sq.). Through being with one another in dealing with human beings, we come to be steady and level-headed; through bringing ourselves into situations involving risks, we learn courage or leave cowardice behind (pp. 122-123).

There is no techne or paraggelia (prescription, universal military field order) for this. "Everyone must have, for himself, his eyes trained on that which is at the moment and which matters to him" (p. 123). The genesis of hexis is oriented in this way: the difficulty is that in order to become just, one must already act justly (NE B 2, 1104a 7 sqq.; B 3). Aristotle resolves this through the analogy with techne (shoemaking), but the essential difference is that for praxis, the ergon of human beings is praxis itself -- the telos comes to its end through itself (pp. 123-124).

Three aspects of the "how" of prattein (NE B 3, 1105a-b):
1. **Eidos / phronesis**: knowing, being in the right "condition of looking around," oriented toward the kairos.
2. **Proairoumenos**: acting from out of himself, "on the basis of an actual being-resolved to..."
3. **Bebaios kai ametakinetos echon**: "stable and not to be brought out of composure" -- recalling the determination of pathos through metaballein (pp. 123-124).

These determinations are "not ascribed to a techne" (NE B 3, 1105b 1). Heidegger reads Aristotle's polemic against the sophists and moralizers as "a sharp contrast between legein about ethics-related problems and real philosophizing -- an advance against the misuse of Socratic method" (p. 124).

#### SS17b-beta: Arete as mesotes [pp. 124-127]

The relation of hexis and arete is sharpened through mesotes. "Hexis is nothing other than a how of pathos, being-out-of-composure, in relation to being-composed-as-to" (p. 125). Hexis is itself a basic determination of arete: "according to its being-origin, arete is a hexis, a being-composed as to..., in terms of the moment" (NE B 5, 1106a 13). Hexis also has the further meaning of dunamis -- the definite orientation toward the being of human beings. Hexis is related to the zoe praktike meta logou, the sort of being-in-the-world in which the world is encountered in the character of sumpheron, blaberon, hedu, and luperon (p. 125).

Aristotle sharpens the being-determination of arete by taking the ousia of arete as mesotes. The term comes from medicine: the healthy condition of human beings as a meson. Aristotle transposes this medical concept to ethics with a concrete glance toward the specific difference in the basic sense of being dealt with (p. 125). Two kinds of meson: (1) kat' auto to pragma (with regard to the matter itself, arithmetical/geometrical) vs. (2) pros hemas (in relation to us) (NE B 5, 1106a 26-31). For the being of human beings, there is no meson kat' auto: "*for our being, characterized by particularity, no unique and absolute norm can be given*" (p. 126). The mean is not a property of a pragma but the aptitude for *maintaining the mean*, which means nothing other than *seizing the moment*.

The mean is now taken as hexis in the sense of taxis, the "being-apportioned" of that which comes up for resolution. It is not a fixed property but a way of comporting oneself, aiming at the mesou stochastike -- the "being-composed that sees and is open to the situation" (NE B 5, 1106b 9; 1106b 28). In De Anima B 11, Aristotle characterizes aisthesis itself as a mesotes -- perception as the kritikon, the "ability-to-separate" one thing from another. Perception is a dunamis in the sense of kritike; it is a definite being-positioned toward possible objects (De an. B 11, 424a 4 sqq.) (pp. 126-127).

Taken purely ontologically, the arete mesotes is not "a pinnacle," but rather, "with regard to the possibility itself that carries it in itself, with regard to the eu, it is akrotes" (p. 130).

#### SS17b-gamma: The orientation of arete toward the moment, kairos [pp. 127-129]

Arete is defined as hexis proairetike, en mesoteti ousa te pros hemas, horismene logo kai hos an ho phronimos horiseie (NE B 6, 1106b 36 sq.) -- "a being-composed in the ability-to-resolve-oneself, in terms of the moment, as the phronimos would define it." Action itself has its telos in the kairos. Action must proceed by way of deliberating and as such be fulfilled. With training (ethizein), the possibility of action is put out of play -- training reduces deliberation to the completeness of attaining a result (pp. 127-128).

Crucial distinction: for poiesis, the result (ergon) is decisive, and techne carries the concern for this ergon. For praxis, proairesis is decisive; praxis does not take up definite material but cultivates *the how of dealing itself*. The how is only appropriated in such a way that one is composed *at each moment* -- not routine but holding-oneself-open, dunamis in the mesotes (p. 128).

"Repetition does not mean the bringing-into-play of a settled completedness, but rather *acting anew in every moment on the basis of the corresponding resolution*" (p. 128). "Cultivating hexis never depends on an operation, a routine. In an operation, the moment is destroyed" (p. 128).

Aristotle distinguishes arete and action from techne (NE B 3): to appropriation through ethos belongs empeiria and chronos (experience and time). Episteme (from didaskalias) requires a fully determinate duration; praxis must be cultivated in praxis and techne. The distinction: praxis depends on the how, and the how must be composed *at each moment*. "The always of being-there is the *frequently of repetition*" (p. 129), determined by historicality -- "entirely different time-contexts in relation to which the remaining time-determinations break down" (p. 129).

### SS18: Pathos, Its General Meanings and Its Role in Human Being-There (Metaphysics Delta 21, De Anima A 1) [pp. 129-139]

#### SS18a: Hexis as clue to the being-structure of pathos [pp. 129-131]

Hexis cannot be understood as completeness in the sense of routine. From hexis we see clearly that the pathe "in their way, more proximately determine being-in-the-world, being-in-the-moment" (p. 129). The pathe do not concern "spiritual states" or "bodily symptoms"; they characterize the entire human being in its *disposition in the world* (p. 129). The entire human being is the primary object of the Aristotelian psychology of De Anima, Book 1. The entire human being must be understood with regard to its being as zoe, as being-in-a-world -- "a genuine topic not of psychology but of the *discussion of the being of this being*" (p. 129).

A philosophically explosive remark: "For the Greeks, fear as anxiety is co-constitutive of the manner and mode of grasping what is and what is not. Thus Aristotle views the phenomenon of fear so broadly that he also becomes attentive to the fact that there is fear even if nothing is there that would be the direct inducement of a fear -- *fear in the face of the nothing*" (p. 129).

The definition of arete (NE B 6): hexis proairetike, en mesoteti ousa te pros hemas, horismene logo kai hos an ho phronimos horiseie (p. 130). Arete is "being-composed in the ability-to-resolve-oneself." Legein as being-composed corresponds with phronesis; if one considers arete like this, then one characterizes it as ousia insofar as its being constitutes praxis. The arete mesotes is not a pinnacle (akrotes); rather, with regard to the eu, it is akrotes (p. 130).

Arete ethike goes toward ethos; its genesis is habituating-oneself. Arete dianoetike requires empeiria and chronos. Duration (chronos) is co-constitutive of the cultivation of being-composed as knowing. The time-character of ethike lies in pollakis; ethos expresses genesis at the same time. "That time, not as duration, is precisely constitutive of arete as ethike is shown by Aristotle's emphasizing that genuine being-composed within being-there is gained by the human being *as a man*, and so not during youth and not during old age" (pp. 130-131).

#### SS18b: The four general meanings of pathos (Met. Delta 21) [pp. 131-132]

1. **Poiotes kath' hen alloiousthai endechetai** -- "being-constituted, regarding which something underlies alteration." Pathos is a determination of beings with the character of alterability; something can *happen* to such a being. "To happen" touches upon paschein and pathos in the genuine sense: not the passive, but that which *occurs for me*. "Pathos is a determination of beings with the character of becoming-otherwise" (Met. Delta 21, 1022b 15 sqq.) (p. 131).
2. **Energeiai kai alloioseis ede** -- beings that carry in themselves the possibility of something occurring to them in relation to their constitution. Energeia: the "being-there" of such a shifting occurring-to-one (Met. Delta 21, 1022b 18) (p. 131).
3. **Blaberai alloioseis kai kineseis, kai malista ai luprai** -- pathos as the occurring-to-one that has the character of the unpleasant, the blaberon. Pathos is defined still more precisely: harmfulness is related mostly to lupe -- "my attunement to this occurring affects me," a becoming-otherwise in the sense of becoming-depressed (Met. Delta 21, 1022b 18 sqq.) (pp. 131-132).
4. **Ta megethe ton sumpheron kai luperon pathe** -- pathos designates "size," the measure of that which occurs to me in a harmful way: "that is a blow to me" (Met. Delta 21, 1022b 20 sq.) (p. 132).

From these four meanings, the *genuine relatedness* of pathos becomes visible: it is related to the *being of living things*, characterized by the occurring-again-and-again. This occurring has in itself the character of the *harmful*. Aristotle recognizes that pathos includes metabole, kinesis, alloiosis, in which paschein has the character of phthora and soteria. Heidegger highlights the connection to Hegel: "Hegel took the phenomenon of sozein from Aristotle in the expression *Aufhebung* (sublation)" (p. 132). As opposed to the metabole through which the hexis is saved, there is a kind of paschein that has the character of steretikon -- "something happens to me, by which I lose the hexis" (p. 132).

#### SS18c: Pathos as the being-taken of human being-there in its full bodily being-in-the-world [pp. 132-137]

Pathos, in relation to the zoe praktike meta logou, is a *being-taken* of being-there. Being-there is taken with that which is there in the world -- *from without*, but from without *in the sense of the world as the wherein of my being* (p. 133). This being-taken does not involve anything "spiritual" that would invite the conception of pathos as "affect." It is always a being-taken of beings as living things as such (p. 133).

Aristotle's critical remark (De an. A 1, 403a 3 sqq.): "to say that the soul gets angry is the same as to say that the soul builds a house. It would be better to say not that the soul has pity or learns or believes, but that the human being does the psyche" (p. 133). Soul is here conceived as ousia, insofar as the being-taken of beings as living things is expressed in the pathe.

The pathe are not "psychic experiences" and not "in consciousness"; they are a being-taken of human beings *in their full being-in-the-world* (pp. 133-134). In De Anima A 1, Aristotle discusses the aporia: whether the pathe are all common to the anthropos or whether there are pathe that are cut off from the soul (De an. A 1, 403a 3 sqq.). All pathe are meta somatos: the body somehow takes part in all having-courage, and in all perceiving. Aisthanesthai -- having-courage toward, being-inclined -- is not perception in the narrow sense but awareness in the sense of having-there of the world.

Even noein (thorough deliberating of a matter) is something like phantasia or cannot be without phantasia (De an. A 4, 408b 11 sqq.). **Phantasia**: not a "brain process" but the "making-present-to-itself" of the world, in which what is made present is not actually there but instead is in memory or in a merely faint making-present. Phantasia is the ground for noein. "Insofar as noesis is the highest possibility for the being of human beings, the entire being of human beings is determined so that it must be apprehended as the *bodily being-in-the-world* of human beings" (p. 134).

Methodological conclusion: only in *phenomenology* has the division between "psychic" and "bodily" begun to be dissolved. "*The primary being-there-function of bodiliness secures the ground for the full being of human beings*" (p. 134). The erroneous orientation toward the biological (Descartes's res cogitans -- res extensa) is also found in Aristotle.

Aristotle proceeds from four general meanings of pathos to ask in what sense the pathe are ginomena tes psyches. **Psyche** means all that constitutes the being of a living thing; the manifoldness of being-contexts is subordinated to determinate object-categories. The question: are there determinations of the ability-to-occur to living things that befit a way of being of living things themselves -- psyche and anthropos? (p. 134).

**Nous** (De an. Gamma 4-5): Aristotle likens nous to phos (light) -- being-there as the characteristic illumination requires a *fundamental illumination in order to be there* (p. 135). The basic determinations: dunaton ("possibility"), apathos ("that which nothing can touch"), and the *condition of the possibility for something in general to be encountered by living things*. Nous goes toward ta panta; it is dianoesthai (p. 135). "Nous is the *basic condition of the possibility of being-in-the-world*" (p. 135), standing beyond the particular concrete being of individual human beings. Nous is defined as dektikon tou eidous -- "being-able-to-take-up the eidos at the moment, the look of a being" (De an. Gamma 4, 429a 15 sq.) (pp. 135-136).

Aristotle poses the question: whether nous is an idion pathos tes psyches or enters from outside (thurathen). He asks whether nous constitutes the being of living things as proper to such a being, or whether it arises in the being of human beings, or enters into human beings from outside (pp. 135-136). The evidence shows that a living thing, insofar as encountered by the world, *is also encountered with a view to its corporeality* -- everything aims at the living thing *in its full being-there* (p. 136). The noein of human beings is not pure; it is grounded in phantasia.

The central question: the peculiar interlacing of the being of the human being, in its full being-there, with the soma. This determines the type of treatment to which the pathe are subjected. The Rhetoric makes the eidos of the pathe visible: "that as kineseis tou somatos they look the way that they do, they are a kind of occurring to a living thing, and so an occurring that also lays claim to corporeality" (p. 136). The genesis of pathe is also given by corporeality: fear rises up in ourselves, co-given in our being; the pathe are logoi ennuloi (p. 137).

#### SS18d: The double-type of consideration of pathos according to eidos and hule, and the question concerning the task of the phusikos [pp. 137-139]

The addressing of the phenomenon of pathos "must proceed toward that on the basis of which the pathe are, that wherein they are found." Their hule is nothing other than soma, the corporeality of the human being (p. 137). The horoi that circumscribe the phenomenon must be of the orge: "Being-angry is something like a being-in-motion of the body constituted thus and so" -- a fully determinate motion from definite circumstances (De an. A 1, 403a 26 sq.) (p. 137).

The hule is at once in the being-so of toioudi somatos; the eidos is in the hupo toude heneka toude (the being-of-concern). Logos is simultaneously addressed (De an. A 1, 403a 27-29). A *fundamental epistemological definition* follows: "it is already a matter of the phusikos to take into view that which lies within the thematic circle of the being of living things" (p. 137).

The **double consideration**:
- The **dialektikos** (who deals with rhetoric) considers anger as orexis antilupeseos -- a certain implacability, being after pay-back, being-toward-others.
- The **phusikos** defines anger as a determinate boiling up of blood in the heart and of the temperature (De an. A 1, 403a 29 sqq.) (p. 137).
- The first logos yields the genuine **eidos**; the second is the **hule**. But as determination of the being of human beings in the world, it is necessarily co-determined by being a zesis, a "boiling up" of the blood.

**The house analogy** (De an. A 1, 403b 4 sq.): logos oikias -- one answer is shelter from wind, rain, heat; another is stones, brick, wood; a third is the eidos of this house in these materials -- the production of the skepasma (shelter). The real phusikos addresses the house in terms of the look that it itself is related to, what it consists of, how it is constituted in itself. The *being of nature* is determined in its look not simply by hule but *primarily by being-moved* (p. 138).

The hierarchy of considerations: the **technites** considers wood as a rudder (aptitude for purpose); the **doctor** considers somata in accordance with techne; the **mathematikos** considers things solely insofar as extended; the **protos philosophos** takes all possible beings together in the fundamental question concerning being as such (p. 138).

For the being-determination of the pathe: they are understood in themselves only when taken as the *pathe of soma*; their eidos is primarily determined as *determination of living things in relation to being-in the world* (p. 139). Thumos and phobos are suited to an altogether determinately constituted body; they are not "pure fear in the sense of an abstract comporting-oneself toward something" (p. 139). The pathe cannot be identified with mathematical objectivities -- the Greek does not see a line primarily in itself but gramme is always the limit of a surface, and surface the limit of a body. The eidos of fearing has the primary relatedness to a finding-oneself of the body. Both eidos and hule accounts are **logoi ennuloi** -- "material" in an entirely different sense (p. 139).

## 3. Key Concepts

### 3.1 Hexis (hexis)
- **Greek form**: hexis
- **Definition**: "The determination of the genuineness of being-there in a moment of being-composed as to something" (p. 119); the being-present of the having and of what is had; energeia of having as such (Met. Delta 20); a mode of being-there related to echein and standing alongside the categories.
- **Type**: Ontological category / mode of being
- **Dependencies**: echein, energeia, diathesis, taxis, metaxu
- **Function**: Fundamental clue for understanding both arete (as hexis proairetike) and pathos (pathos already stands in relation to hexis); the being-structure through which Dasein's composure and decomposure become intelligible. "Hexis is nothing other than a how of pathos" (p. 125).
- **Pages**: 116-119, 125, 129-131

### 3.2 Echein (echein)
- **Greek form**: echein
- **Definition**: "Having" in four senses: (1) leading/directing according to physis or horme, (2) being-in as dektikon (readiness), (3) containing/enclosing (periechon), (4) holding off/restraining (koluein). All four mark beings with the character of being after a definite being-possibility.
- **Type**: Basic concept / category of being
- **Dependencies**: physis, horme, hule, dektikon
- **Function**: Ground-concept from which hexis is derived; demonstrates that "having" and "being-in-something" share the same character (epomenos).
- **Pages**: 116-118

### 3.3 Arete (arete)
- **Greek form**: arete
- **Definition**: Hexis proairetike, en mesoteti ousa te pros hemas, horismene logo kai hos an ho phronimos horiseie (NE B 6, 1106b 36 sq.) -- "a being-composed in the ability-to-resolve-oneself, in terms of the moment, as the phronimos would define it." Not completeness or property but a mode of being-there determined by temporality.
- **Type**: Fundamental determination of being-there
- **Dependencies**: hexis, proairesis, mesotes, phronesis, kairos, ethos
- **Function**: The genuine way in which hexis expresses the being of human beings in its seriousness (spoudaios); simultaneously mesotes (with regard to the how of being-composed) and akrotes (with regard to the eu, the best possibility).
- **Pages**: 119, 121-128, 130-131

### 3.4 Mesotes (mesotes)
- **Greek form**: mesotes
- **Definition**: Not mediocrity, bourgeois morality, or ranking values; "rather a basic relation to hexis" (p. 121). The meson pros hemas: the way the world stands to us, or how we are in it -- being able to maintain the mean in resolving-oneself, having-there-present of the decisive moment. Distinguished from meson kat' auto (arithmetical/geometrical mean).
- **Type**: Being-determination of arete (its ousia)
- **Dependencies**: arete, hexis, kairos, phronesis, taxis, stochastike
- **Function**: Sharpens the ousia of arete; transposes the medical concept of health as meson to ethics; shows that for human beings "no unique and absolute norm can be given" (p. 126); extended to aisthesis as mesotes (De an. B 11).
- **Pages**: 121, 124-127, 130

### 3.5 Kairos (kairos)
- **Greek form**: kairos
- **Definition**: The decisive moment; the concrete situation in which being-there must resolve itself. Not a "right time" in the abstract but the situation's own demand for apportioning. Action has its telos in the kairos.
- **Type**: Temporal-situational determination
- **Dependencies**: mesotes, phronesis, proairesis, praxis
- **Function**: The orientation-point of arete as hexis; hexis lies in primary orientation toward the kairos ("I am there, come what may!"); the phenomenon through which temporality enters the analysis of arete. "In an operation, the moment is destroyed" (p. 128).
- **Pages**: 119, 123, 126-128

### 3.6 Pathos (pathos)
- **Greek form**: pathos
- **Definition**: Four general meanings (Met. Delta 21): (1) quality regarding which alteration is possible (alterability), (2) the energeiai of such alterations, (3) harmful alterations and motions especially the painful, (4) the magnitude of harmful occurrences. Fundamentally: the being-taken of human being-there in its full bodily being-in-the-world; not "psychic state" or "bodily symptom" but the mode in which living things are affected in their being.
- **Type**: Fundamental concept of being / disposition
- **Dependencies**: hexis, metabole, kinesis, alloiosis, phthora, soteria, soma, lupe
- **Function**: Co-originary with hexis as a fundamental concept of being; the being-structure through which the full bodily character of Dasein's being-in-the-world becomes visible; the ground out of which the Rhetoric B analysis of affects proceeds.
- **Pages**: 116, 120-121, 129-139

### 3.7 Eidos (eidos)
- **Greek form**: eidos
- **Definition**: The "look" of a being; in the context of pathos, the formal-disclosive aspect: anger as orexis antilupeseos (a comporting-oneself to other human beings in the mode of being-toward-others). The first logos that yields the "genuine" determination.
- **Type**: Formal-ontological determination
- **Dependencies**: logos, hule, ousia
- **Function**: One pole of the double-type of consideration of pathos; the dialektikos/rhetorician considers the pathe according to their eidos. The eidos of fearing has the primary relatedness to a finding-oneself of the body (p. 139).
- **Pages**: 117, 137-139

### 3.8 Hule (hule)
- **Greek form**: hule
- **Definition**: Two senses in this unit: (1) In Met. Delta 23 (SS17a): not indeterminate "material" but a positive mode of being-there, a "being-in-preparedness" (dektikon). (2) In De an. A 1 (SS18d): soma, the corporeality of the human being; the phusikos's perspective on the pathe as kineseis tou somatos.
- **Type**: Material-ontological determination
- **Dependencies**: soma, dektikon, physis
- **Function**: The other pole of the double consideration; shows that matter is never merely "stuff" but always already a positive determination of being.
- **Pages**: 117, 137-139

### 3.9 Phusikos (phusikos)
- **Greek form**: phusikos
- **Definition**: He who examines nature in the widest sense; interrogates somata in view of their erga and pathe, taking them as constituted thus and so, looking primarily at how the being is moved. Distinguished from dialektikos, technites, mathematikos, and protos philosophos.
- **Type**: Epistemic role / mode of investigation
- **Dependencies**: soma, physis, kinesis, eidos, hule
- **Function**: Heidegger uses the question of the task of the phusikos to establish the priority of the being-of-nature as *primarily being-moved* (p. 138), grounding the investigation of pathos in kinesis.
- **Pages**: 137-139

### 3.10 Phantasia (phantasia)
- **Greek form**: phantasia
- **Definition**: The "making-present-to-itself" of the world; not a brain process but the mode in which what is not actually present is made present (in memory, faint making-present). The ground for noein.
- **Type**: Cognitive-ontological capacity
- **Dependencies**: noein, aisthesis, soma
- **Function**: Establishes that even the highest capacity (noesis) is grounded in the bodily being-in-the-world of human beings; secures the link between nous and soma.
- **Pages**: 134, 136

### 3.11 Nous (nous)
- **Greek form**: nous
- **Definition**: Likened to phos (light); "the basic condition of the possibility of being-in-the-world" (p. 135). Characterized as dunaton (possibility), apathos (that which nothing can touch), dektikon tou eidous (being-able-to-take-up the eidos at the moment). Goes toward ta panta. Stands beyond particular concrete being.
- **Type**: Fundamental ontological capacity
- **Dependencies**: phantasia, phos, eidos, dunamis
- **Function**: Limit-case in the discussion of pathe; the question of whether nous is idion pathos tes psyches or enters thurathen remains open; establishes the scope of the investigation of being-in-the-world.
- **Pages**: 134-136, 139

### 3.12 Praxis (praxis)
- **Greek form**: praxis
- **Definition**: Action in which the telos comes to its end through itself; distinguished from poiesis (where the ergon is external). Praxis depends on proairesis -- the how of dealing itself.
- **Type**: Mode of being-there
- **Dependencies**: proairesis, hexis, kairos, techne (as contrast)
- **Function**: The being-context designated as *existence* (p. 119); shows why the genesis of hexis cannot follow the techne model.
- **Pages**: 119, 121, 122, 125, 127, 128, 130

## 4. Main Positions

### U-FP3c-P1: Hexis and pathos are fundamental concepts of being, not merely ethical or psychological categories
Heidegger opens by insisting that pathos "already has within itself the relation to hexis" and that "these two concepts lend themselves to being characterized by Aristotle as *fundamental concepts of being*" (p. 116). The analysis of hexis is not a detour through ethics but a direct confrontation with the being-structure of Dasein. The four senses of echein (Met. Delta 23) all converge on a single ontological point: having is always being after a definite being-possibility, or its negation.
- **Source**: p. 116; Met. Delta 23, 1023a 8 sqq.
- **Confidence**: High -- directly stated

### U-FP3c-P2: The four senses of echein share a common ontological structure: being-in-relation-to a definite being-possibility
The four senses -- (1) leading according to nature/impulse, (2) having-in-itself as dektikon, (3) containing/enclosing, (4) holding off from genuine horme -- are "not an arbitrary jumble, but rather relates to a basic meaning" (p. 116). Each articulates a way in which a being stands in relation to what it can be. The negation case (koluein) is especially revealing: Atlas holds the vault of heaven by preventing it from falling. Having, in all four senses, marks beings with "the being-character of being after a definite being-possibility, or its negation" (p. 118).
- **Source**: pp. 116-118; Met. Delta 23, 1023a 8-21
- **Confidence**: High

### U-FP3c-P3: Hexis is the determination of the genuineness of being-there in a moment of being-composed-as-to-something
The culminating formulation of SS17a: "Hexis is *the determination of the genuineness of being-there in a moment of being-composed as to something*" (p. 119). Hexis is "related in itself to another possibility, to the possibility of my being, *that within my being something comes over me, which brings me out of composure*" (p. 119). This connects hexis directly to the possibility of pathos.
- **Source**: p. 119
- **Confidence**: High -- direct quotation

### U-FP3c-P4: Arete is not a property but a mode of being-there itself, determined by temporality through ethos
"Arete as hexis is not a property, not a possession brought to being-there from without, but is rather a *mode of being-there itself*" (p. 122). Its genesis is through ethos -- but this is not mechanical repetition. It is "the peculiar category of the *how*" (p. 122), "a how of being-there *determined by its being*, characterized by *temporality*, by the stretching across time" (p. 122). There is no techne or universal paraggelia for this; each person must train on what matters at the moment.
- **Source**: pp. 122-124; NE B 1, 1103a 25 sqq.; B 2-3
- **Confidence**: High

### U-FP3c-P5: The three aspects of the pos echon (eidos/phronesis, proairoumenos, bebaios) define genuine ethical action in opposition to mere legein
The one acting must: (1) be eidos -- knowing, oriented toward the kairos with phronesis; (2) be proairoumenos -- acting from out of himself; (3) be bebaios kai ametakinetos echon -- "stable and not to be brought out of composure" (p. 123). This triad opposes the sophists and moralizers. Heidegger reads Aristotle's polemic as "a sharp contrast between legein about ethics-related problems and real philosophizing" (p. 124).
- **Source**: pp. 123-124; NE B 3, 1105a-b
- **Confidence**: High

### U-FP3c-P6: Mesotes is not mediocrity but the ontological structure of being-in-the-world pros hemas, characterized by particularity
Mesotes "is not some kind of 'mediocrity,' not a determination of human actions in which it would amount to mediocrity, not a so-called 'bourgeois morality,' not a principle of 'ranking values'; but rather a basic relation to hexis" (p. 121). For the being of human beings there is no fixed mean: "*for our being, characterized by particularity, no unique and absolute norm can be given*" (p. 126). The mean is not a property of a pragma but a way of being-related to us.
- **Source**: pp. 121, 124-127; NE B 5, 1106a-b
- **Confidence**: High

### U-FP3c-P7: Arete as mesotes is simultaneously akrotes with regard to the eu
"Taken purely ontologically, the arete mesotes is not a pinnacle, the highest, akrotes, but is rather akrotes with regard to the possibility itself that carries it in itself, with regard to the eu, it is akrotes" (p. 130). The mean is the highest because it is the mode of being most genuinely related to the concrete situation.
- **Source**: p. 130; NE B 6, 1107a 6 sqq.
- **Confidence**: High

### U-FP3c-P8: Ethical action is acting anew in every moment, not routine repetition; the always of being-there is the frequently of repetition
"Repetition does not mean the bringing-into-play of a settled completedness, but rather *acting anew in every moment on the basis of the corresponding resolution*" (p. 128). "Cultivating hexis never depends on an operation, a routine. In an operation, the moment is destroyed" (p. 128). The temporal character of arete is that of the *frequently of repetition* characterized by historicality -- "entirely different time-contexts in relation to which the remaining time-determinations break down" (p. 129).
- **Source**: pp. 128-129; NE B 1, 1103a 15 sqq.
- **Confidence**: High

### U-FP3c-P9: Pathos expresses the being of human beings, not "bodily symptoms" or psychological states
The pathe "determine being-in-the-world, being-in-the-moment" (p. 129) and are not "spiritual states" or "bodily symptoms" but the *disposition of the entire human being in the world*. The entire human being is the primary object of De Anima, treated "with regard to its being as zoe, as being-in-a-world" (p. 129). "It does not concern 'spiritual states' with 'bodily symptoms'; instead, the pathe characterize the entire human being in its *disposition in the world*" (p. 129).
- **Source**: pp. 129, 133-134; De an. A 1
- **Confidence**: High

### U-FP3c-P10: Fear (phobos) discloses the nothing: fear is co-constitutive of grasping what is and what is not
"For the Greeks, fear as anxiety is co-constitutive of the manner and mode of grasping what is and what is not. Thus Aristotle views the phenomenon of fear so broadly that he also becomes attentive to the fact that there is fear even if nothing is there -- *fear in the face of the nothing*" (p. 129). This is a 1924 anticipation of *Being and Time* SS40 (Angst) and "What Is Metaphysics?" (1929).
- **Source**: p. 129
- **Confidence**: High -- direct quotation

### U-FP3c-P11: The four general meanings of pathos articulate a progressive narrowing from alterability to the specifically harmful
The four meanings form a sequence: (1) alterability in general, (2) energeiai of alteration, (3) harmful and painful alterations, (4) magnitude of harmful occurrences. The progression moves from the ontological (alterability as such) to the existential (what strikes and harms the living being in its concrete being). The connection to lupe is essential.
- **Source**: pp. 131-132; Met. Delta 21, 1022b 15 sqq.
- **Confidence**: High

### U-FP3c-P12: The genuine relatedness of pathos is to the being of living things: the occurring-to-oneself-again-and-again with the character of phthora
Heidegger extracts the essential: "the *genuine relatedness* of pathos becomes visible; it is related to the *being of living things*" (p. 132). The occurring has the character of the harmful and connects to phthora (destruction) but also to soteria (preservation). "Hegel took the phenomenon of sozein from Aristotle in the expression 'sublation (Aufhebung)'" (p. 132).
- **Source**: p. 132; Met. Delta 21
- **Confidence**: High

### U-FP3c-P13: The pathe are logoi ennuloi: inseparable from the natural hule of living beings
All pathe are meta somatos (De an. A 1): "all being-angry about..., being-kind to..., fear for..., and so on, in a certain sense also concerns the body" (p. 136). The psyche is not an autonomous subject but the being-taken of beings as living things, conceived as ousia. The pathe cannot be identified with mathematical lines and surfaces: "the Greek does not see a line primarily in itself, but instead gramme is always the limit of a surface, and surface the limit of a body" (p. 139).
- **Source**: pp. 136, 139; De an. A 1, 403a 16 sqq.
- **Confidence**: High

### U-FP3c-P14: The double consideration of pathos (eidos vs. hule) defines the task of the phusikos and establishes a hierarchy of modes of investigation
The dialektikos considers anger as orexis antilupeseos (eidos); the phusikos considers it as zesis haimatos (hule). The real phusikos addresses beings in terms of how they are constituted in themselves (the house analogy). "The being of nature is determined not simply by hule, but *primarily by being-moved*" (p. 138). The hierarchy: technites, doctor, phusikos, mathematikos, protos philosophos -- corresponding to increasingly fundamental modes of questioning.
- **Source**: pp. 137-139; De an. A 1, 403a 24 sqq.; 403b 1-16
- **Confidence**: High

### U-FP3c-P15: Nous is the basic condition of the possibility of being-in-the-world
Nous, likened to phos, is "the *basic condition of the possibility of being-in-the-world*" (p. 135). It is dunaton, apathos, and goes toward ta panta. Yet noein requires phantasia as bodily making-present -- "the primary being-there-function of bodiliness secures the ground for the full being of human beings" (p. 134). The question of whether nous enters from outside (thurathen) remains open.
- **Source**: pp. 134-136; De an. Gamma 4-5, 429a 15 sqq.; 430a 14 sqq.
- **Confidence**: High

### U-FP3c-P16: The poiesis/praxis distinction is constitutive: poiesis ends in a result (ergon), praxis depends on the how of dealing itself (proairesis)
For techne, the ergon is decisive. For praxis, proairesis is decisive -- "the manner and mode of 'resolving oneself'" (p. 127). This distinction is crucial because the genesis of ethical hexis cannot follow the techne model. "They do not take up a definite material, but rather *cultivate the how of dealing itself*" (p. 128).
- **Source**: pp. 127-128; NE B 6, 1106b 36 sq.
- **Confidence**: High

### U-FP3c-P17: Phantasia grounds noein and secures the bodily being-in-the-world of human beings
Phantasia is "the 'making-present-to-itself' of the world, in which what is made present is not actually there" (p. 134). It is the ground of noein; "insofar as noesis is the highest possibility for the being of human beings, the entire being of human beings is determined so that it must be apprehended as the *bodily being-in-the-world*" (p. 134). This is not materialism but the insight that thinking is always already embodied.
- **Source**: pp. 134, 136; De an. A 4, 408b 11 sqq.
- **Confidence**: High

## 5. Tensions & Open Questions

### T1: Hule as positive being-determination vs. hule as indeterminate substrate
In SS17a (Met. Delta 23), Heidegger insists that hule "does not mean an indeterminate 'material' but rather a positive character of a mode of being-there" -- being-in-preparedness, the readiness (dektikon) of metal to become a statue (p. 117). Yet in SS18d (De an. A 1), hule is the soma, the corporeal substrate that the phusikos investigates. The question is whether these two senses are reconcilable or whether there is a genuine tension between the ontological-positive reading (SS17a) and the material-substrate reading (SS18d). [INTERP-low: Heidegger may intend the De an. A 1 passage to be read through Met. Delta 23 -- soma as hule is itself a positive mode of being-there, a "being-in-preparedness" for pathos.]

### T2: Circularity in the genesis of arete
Aristotle raises the difficulty: to become just, one must already act justly (NE B 2, 1104a 7 sqq.). The resolution through the techne analogy is explicitly inadequate, since the basic conditions for praxis are "entirely different from those in the case of a techne" (p. 123). Heidegger redirects attention to the three aspects of prattein but does not fully resolve the circularity. How does the initial hexis become possible if hexis requires prior hexis?

### T3: The status of nous -- inside or outside the human being?
Heidegger presents Aristotle's aporia about whether nous is idion pathos of psyche or enters from outside (thurathen). He notes that Aristotle "does not fully answer" this in De an. A 1 but defers to Books 3-5 (pp. 135-136). The question bears on the scope of pathos: if nous enters from outside, the full bodily being-in-the-world interpretation may have a limit that Heidegger does not address. The characterization of nous as apathos ("that which nothing can touch") stands in tension with the claim that all pathe are meta somatos.

### T4: The Hegel connection -- is Aufhebung genuinely Aristotelian?
Heidegger claims that "Hegel took the phenomenon of sozein from Aristotle in the expression Aufhebung" (p. 132). The question is whether Aristotle's use of sozein (preserving through the metabole of pathos, raising to a higher energeia) genuinely supports the triple sense of Aufhebung (negating, preserving, elevating), or whether Heidegger is retrojecting Hegelian categories. [INTERP-high]

### T5: Repetition vs. routine -- where is the boundary?
Heidegger insists that the genesis of arete is not practice but *repetition* -- "acting anew in every moment" (p. 128). Yet Aristotle's own term ethizein and the analogy with learning instruments seem to involve the kind of routine-building that Heidegger rejects. The tension between Heidegger's existential reading and Aristotle's practical pedagogy remains unresolved.

### T6: The body in hexis
Heidegger insists on the bodily character of pathe (logoi ennuloi, meta somatos) but does not systematically integrate embodiment into hexis. The "body" disappears from the hexis analysis (SS17) and reappears suddenly in SS18c-d. Whether hexis itself has a bodily dimension -- whether being-composed is always also a somatic state -- remains unaddressed.

### T7: Mesotes and the protos philosophos
The claim that there is no meson kat' auto for human beings sits uneasily with the closing reference to the protos philosophos who "takes all possible beings together in the fundamental question concerning being as such" (p. 138). Does fundamental ontology reinstate a universal perspective that mesotes pros hemas denies? This tension between the particularity of Dasein and the universality of the question of being resurfaces throughout BT.

## 8. Structural Observations

**The hexis-pathos chiasm**: SS17 moves from echein -> hexis -> arete -> mesotes -> kairos (outward toward the concrete moment); SS18 moves from pathos in general -> four meanings -> bodily being-in-the-world -> eidos/hule -> phusikos -> nous (inward toward the condition of possibility). The two sections meet at the claim that hexis is "nothing other than a how of pathos" (p. 125) -- the chiastic point where the ethical-practical analysis of composure intersects with the ontological analysis of being-affected.

**The techne/praxis distinction as structural hinge**: Heidegger uses the techne analogy to clarify the genesis of hexis, but the analogy breaks down where it matters: techne has a separable ergon, praxis does not. This breakdown is philosophically productive -- it shows that ethical being-there cannot be modeled on production.

**The progressive narrowing of pathos**: The four meanings of Met. Delta 21 form a funnel from the broadest ontological determination (alterability) to the most concrete existential one (great misfortune). This mirrors the overall movement of SS17-18 from formal ontological structures (echein, hexis) to concrete existential phenomena (fear, anger, composure lost).

## 9. Key Quotations

> "Hexis is *the determination of the genuineness of being-there in a moment of being-composed as to something*: the various hexeis as the various modes of being able to be composed." (p. 119)

> "Arete as hexis is not a property, not a possession brought to being-there from without, but is rather a *mode of being-there itself*." (p. 122)

> "Repetition does not mean the bringing-into-play of a settled completedness, but rather *acting anew in every moment on the basis of the corresponding resolution*." (p. 128)

> "For our being, characterized by particularity, *no unique and absolute norm can be given*." (p. 126)

> "For the Greeks, fear as anxiety is co-constitutive of the manner and mode of grasping what is and what is not. Thus Aristotle views the phenomenon of fear so broadly that he also becomes attentive to the fact that there is fear even if nothing is there that would be the direct inducement of a fear -- *fear in the face of the nothing*." (p. 129)

> "The pathe are also characters that, in their way, more proximately determine being-in-the-world, being-in-the-moment. It does not concern 'spiritual states' with 'bodily symptoms'; instead, the pathe characterize the entire human being in its *disposition in the world*." (p. 129)

> "The primary being-there-function of bodiliness secures the ground for the full being of human beings." (p. 134)

> "Nous is the *basic condition of the possibility of being-in-the-world*." (p. 135)

> "The being of nature is determined not simply by hule, but *primarily by being-moved*." (p. 138)

> "Cultivating hexis never depends on an operation, a routine. In an operation, the moment is destroyed." (p. 128)
