| Density | Very high (transitional pivot; introduces fore-structures and double sense of conceptuality) |

**One-sentence thesis**: Heidegger argues that the possibility of conceptuality is indigenous to being-there itself -- already present inexplicitly in its fore-having, fore-sight, and fore-grasp (the negative sense: the interpretedness *against which* conceptuality is cultivated) and accessible through *nous* as *dianoein* (the positive sense: the *for-what* and *upon-what* of cultivation), where the *as*-structure (*kata* and *apo*) simultaneously grounds the possibility of error (*pseudos*) and of genuine conceptual determination, such that the cultivation of the concept *kinesis* will be the radical grasping of the interpretedness of being-there in accordance with these three fore-structures.

## 4. Main Positions

### U-SP1-P1: Conceptuality is indigenous to being-there, already present inexplicitly

"Possibility" must be understood not as empty logical possibility maintained beforehand but as being-possible in the sense of the being-character of beings that have this character. If conceptuality is indigenous to being-there itself, "being-there itself must be conceptuality in a certain way" -- conceptuality has already come forward in its aspects inexplicitly (p. 184). The task is to show that conceptuality lies in being-there itself, not that it is brought to being-there from outside.

### U-SP1-P2: The hermeneutical principle -- every interpretation is only genuine in retrieval

"The genuine interpretation occurs in the right way, then, only if it is fulfilled on the ground of explicit conceptuality, if the interpretation is retrieved, is understood in accordance with the ground" (p. 184). A "general hermeneutical principle" appears: every interpretation is only genuine in retrieval. Only then is it a putting-forward of that which no longer stands there. This principle grounds the entire method of the Second Part: interpreting Aristotle's concepts requires retrieving them from the explicit conceptual ground that has been covered over. [INTERP-low: The extension of this principle to the method of the Second Part as a whole is strongly implied by the text's placement.]

### U-SP1-P3: Conceptuality has a double sense as ἕξις πρός ἄλλον

If science is something to which being-there can resolve itself, this ἕξις is characterized by its being as πρὸς ἄλλον -- "against," "in relation to" something from which it extricates itself. The being-possible of conceptuality can therefore be characterized as *possibility in a double sense*: (1) the arrangement against which ἕξις is cultivated (negative); (2) the for-what and upon-what of cultivation (positive) (p. 186). This double sense structures the entire unit.

### U-SP1-P4: Being-there is always already interpreted -- the fore-structures

Being-there as being-in-the-world, governed by λόγος, "operates in word-thinking, hearing-saying, reading-learning" with respect to three aspects. Being-there is *always already* a being in what is familiar, already interpreted, already apprehended (p. 186). Three structures constitute this prior interpretedness: (a) **Fore-having** (Vorhabe): "the world into which I enter, in which I awaken, is there for me in a determinate interpretedness" -- the world is already thus and so with my being-there in it (p. 186); (b) **Fore-sight** (Vorsicht): the fore-having is "already posited, at the outset, in a definite fore-sight" -- the respects in which the world is cared for are already there (p. 186); (c) **Fore-grasp** (Vorgriff): "the governing intelligibility, which includes expressing as articulation" -- all seeing, all taking-in-some-respect, is determined in the concrete sense (p. 187). Together these three constitute "the interpretation of being-there, being-transparent" (Ausgelegtheit des Daseins) (p. 187).

### U-SP1-P5: Λόγος is the genuine bearer and mastery of interpretedness

"Λόγος possesses the *mastery* of interpretedness. Λόγος is the genuine bearer of interpretedness" (p. 187). Insofar as λόγος is that in which all that is conceptual occurs, it is *also* that which constitutes the possibility of *error* in being-there. This double role -- mastery of interpretedness and source of error -- is essential to the dialectical structure of the unit.

### U-SP1-P6: Idle chatter yields dissimulation and the dominance of the false

What is experienced and seen is "for the most part" what is expressed. In expression it is communicated, and through communication comes into circulation -- "what is repeated." "In this speaking-around-us, idle chatter, what is expressed increasingly loses its ground" (p. 187). Through idle chatter the expressed comes to cover up and dissimulate what is genuinely meant. What is expressed carries the possibility of dissimulation in the literal sense; communicating is "a leading astray, even if it does so implicitly and not deliberately." This yields the possibility of deception and of being-deceived -- the dominance of the *false*, of the ψεῦδος (p. 187).

### U-SP1-P7: Dominant λόγοι can render beings inaccessible -- heritage as risk

Definite λόγοι, "precisely at times when research activities are young and vital, can assume such a dominance, that for a long time they render the beings that they refer to inaccessible" (p. 188). Heidegger cites two examples: (a) the λόγος of Parmenides, "beings are one," which possessed a dominance within the interpretation of being-there; (b) the inherited meaning of ἡδονή (κληρονομία ὀνόματος), which was "taken over early on from a definite interpretation of being-there" -- "the feeling that lies closest is sensory pleasure, enjoyment; this feeling-oneself, interpreted in the horizon of the average feeling of the crowd, took over the heritage of the word ἡδονή" (p. 188). Heritage is not neutral but carries the risk of concealment.

### U-SP1-P8: Genuine conceptuality is the explicit appropriation of fore-structures against everydayness

"Since the everyday can seize hold of heritage, it follows that being-there has the possibility of tearing heritage away from the everyday, and bringing it to an original interpretedness" -- and in opposition to it, in the ἕξις, "to appropriate the conceptual in the genuine sense" (p. 188). The fore-structures are simultaneously the arrangement *against which* conceptuality is cultivated and *possibilities of something genuine*: "to explicitly appropriate the fore-having, to cultivate the fore-sight, and to carry through the fore-grasp, following this that is secured" (p. 188). The conceptual "is not something that comes forth from out of being-there and is somehow discovered in addition to it, but rather the proper possibility of the conceptual is just the conceptual as apprehended interpretation of being-there itself" (p. 188).

### U-SP1-P9: Independent λόγος arises when πρᾶξις is given up

When λόγος no longer operates μετὰ πρᾶξις but achieves independence, it operates as ἀποφαίνεσθαι -- "no longer an acting, a managing in the sense of practical concern, but rather debating, that which itself becomes visible in the expressed, and to clarify this apart from any application" (p. 189). When πρᾶξις is given up, λόγος becomes independent; the accomplishment of this independent λόγος is related to "bringing-to-seeing as mode of fulfillment of looking-out" (p. 189). We now possess "διανοεῖσθαι, ἐπιστήμη μετὰ λόγου" (p. 189). [INTERP-low: Heidegger here traces a genetic account of theoretical λόγος emerging from practical λόγος, anticipating the BT analysis of the genesis of the theoretical attitude from circumspective concern.]

### U-SP1-P10: Νοῦς as διανοεῖσθαι is the positive possibility of conceptuality

Aristotle, *De Anima* Γ 4: νοῦς is "that by which the soul knows (γινώσκει) and thinks (φρονεῖ)" -- "'Supposing' is the genuine being-possibility of being-in-the-world, of 'being-familiar-with...' as well as of φρονεῖν, 'looking-around-oneself'" (p. 189). Two possibilities of being-oriented: (1) mere taking-into-acquaintance without any practical aim (looking-around-oneself); (2) being-oriented *toward* something, being-oriented *for* something (p. 189). Aristotle speaks of the "so-called" (καλούμενος) νοῦς -- not simply νοῦς but νοῦς "in the way that it is familiar in everydayness." This καλούμενος νοῦς is characterized as διανοεῖσθαι (p. 189). The context of *De Anima* Book 3: the νοῦς of human beings is a δια-νοεῖσθαι -- perceiving and supposing "are equally the simple calling of something" (p. 189--190).

### U-SP1-P11: The as-structure (κατά/ἀπό) grounds the possibility of error and truth

Perceiving, as a mode of disposition, is the perceiving of something *as* something; "addressing is not a simple naming or calling, but addressing something, κατά and ἀπό" (p. 190). Every λόγος is characterized by κατά and ἀπό: "every λόγος is σύνθεσις or διαίρεσις; every λέγειν is λέγειν τι κατά τινος" (p. 190). Therefore λόγος is the positive possibility of error. "Only because speaking is addressing something *as* something, is there the possibility of seeing the addressed as other than it is. The 'as something,' σύνθεσις and διαίρεσις, is the possibility of the ψεῦδος" (p. 190). Crucially: "If orienting-oneself were a simple having-and-giving-back-in-the-same-way, there would be no ψεῦδος in the being-there of human beings" (p. 190).

### U-SP1-P12: Error is the fall; from error springs the possibility of genuine determination

"Since this possibility of error exists, the fall from the genuine possibility of exhibiting and having-there of beings, and insofar as living is in turn determined by προαίρεσις, living can positively grasp the possibility of determining the being that is there in the way that it is" (p. 190). This διανοεῖσθαι as λέγειν τι κατά τινος can be fulfilled "in adapting to that which is genuinely present, to that which is posited in the right regard, and so that the λόγος that unconcealedly yields beings in their being, the *concept*, springs from it" (p. 190--191).

### U-SP1-P13: The concept κίνησις is the radical grasping of being-there's interpretedness

The closing programmatic statement: "The structure in which the consideration moves is that the conceptual, λόγος, is set up in being-there itself as possibilities for and against. We want to see how the *cultivation of the concept κίνησις* is fulfilled as the *radical grasping of the interpretedness of being-there, in accordance with these three aspects*" (p. 191). This announcement connects the entire fore-structure analysis to the Physics investigation that follows, identifying κίνησις not as one concept among others but as the concept in which being-there's self-interpretation is most radically accomplished.

## 5. Tensions & Open Questions

### T1: The dialectical ambiguity of the fore-structures

The same fore-structures that produce dissimulation and error (the "negative sense") are the very structures that must be explicitly appropriated for genuine conceptuality. Heidegger presents this as a productive counter-movement (Gegenbewegung) rather than a contradiction, but the question remains: what precisely converts the fore-structures from obstacles into resources? The text says "in opposition to everydayness" and "in the ἕξις," but the mechanism of this transformation -- how one *explicitly* appropriates what is by definition *already* operative -- is not fully articulated. BT will address this through the concept of authentic resoluteness, but in 1924 the account remains programmatic.

### T2: Νοῦς as everyday versus νοῦς as genuine

Heidegger emphasizes Aristotle's qualification "so-called" (καλούμενος) νοῦς, suggesting that Aristotle himself distinguishes between νοῦς as it shows up in everydayness (= διανοεῖσθαι) and "genuine" νοῦς. This maps onto the negative/positive double sense. But it is unclear whether Heidegger takes Aristotle to hold that there is a "genuine νοῦς" beyond or behind the everyday διανοεῖσθαι, or whether the "genuine" is precisely the *explicit working-through* of the same διανοεῖσθαι. The text seems to favor the latter reading (consistent with the fore-structure argument), but the ambiguity is not resolved.

### T3: The relationship between the two senses of ψεῦδος

In §24a-beta, ψεῦδος emerges from the *communicative circulation* of the expressed (idle chatter, dissimulation, deception) -- a social-pragmatic phenomenon. In §24b, ψεῦδος emerges from the *as-structure* of λόγος itself (σύνθεσις/διαίρεσις, addressing something *as* something) -- a logical-ontological structure. Are these two accounts of the same phenomenon? Or does Heidegger intend two distinct dimensions of error? The text does not explicitly reconcile them. [INTERP-low: The most charitable reading is that the social-pragmatic account (§24a-beta) is the ontic manifestation of what the logical-ontological account (§24b) articulates at the structural level.]

### T4: Κίνησις as "radical grasping" -- concept or method?

The closing announcement (p. 191) identifies the cultivation of the concept κίνησις as "the radical grasping of the interpretedness of being-there." This ambiguously positions κίνησις as both (a) a concept *about* beings (movement as a determination of phusei onta) and (b) a *method* of radically grasping Dasein's own interpretedness. If (b), then the Physics analysis is not merely ontological but existential-hermeneutical -- the concept of movement is simultaneously a self-interpretation of being-there. This double status is announced but not yet demonstrated.

### T5: The hermeneutical circle

The "general hermeneutical principle" of §23 -- that every interpretation is only genuine in retrieval on the ground of explicit conceptuality -- implies a circle: understanding conceptuality requires retrieval, but retrieval requires the very conceptuality it seeks to uncover. Heidegger presents this as a principle rather than a problem, but the circle is structurally identical to what BT §32 will call the hermeneutical circle. In 1924 the circularity is acknowledged but not thematized as such.
