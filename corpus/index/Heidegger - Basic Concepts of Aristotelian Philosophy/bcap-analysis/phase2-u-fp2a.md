## Section 2: Hierarchical Outline

### §9: The Being-There of Human Beings as ψυχή: Speaking-Being (λόγον ἔχειν) and Being-with-One-Another (κοινωνία) [pp. 32--45]

#### §9a: Determination of Human Beings as ζῷον λόγον ἔχον: The Task of Setting λόγος Apart from φωνή [pp. 32--33]
- Heidegger takes up λόγον ἔχειν from *Politics* A 2, where Aristotle demonstrates that the πόλις is a being-possibility (φύσει) of human life
- The traditional rendering of ζῷον λόγον ἔχον as "rational animal" is rejected; λόγος is not a faculty but a mode of being-in-the-world
- Implicit in this determination: κοινωνία as "being-with-one-another" constituted through speaking
- The task: to separate λόγος from φωνή not by degree but by ontological function

#### §9b: The λόγος of Human Beings and the φωνή of Animals as Peculiar Modes of Being-in-the-World and Being-with-One-Another [pp. 33--44]

##### §9b-α: Orientation toward Phenomena at the Basis of the λόγος/φωνή Separation [pp. 36--37]
- Both animals and humans are living beings in the mode of being-in-the-world
- The world is encountered in the character of **mattering** (not as object for subject)
- Mattering has a specific deficient mode: **inconsequentiality** (Gleichgültigkeit) -- the character of everydayness
- Disclosedness varies with the basic mode: φωνή for animals, λόγος for humans
- The subject/object distinction must be set aside entirely for Greek philosophy (p. 40)

##### §9b-β: Encounter-Characters of the Animal World: ἡδύ and λυπηρόν [pp. 38--39]
- Aristotle provides a definition of ἡδονή in *Rhetoric* A 11: κατάστασις ἁθρόα -- a "transposing-of-oneself-all-at-once" into one's natural disposition (εἰς τὴν ὑπάρχουσαν φύσιν)
- ἡδύ has a **dual character**: (1) transposing itself into a disposition, and (2) the disposition into which one is brought
- ἡδύ and λυπηρόν are not subjective feelings but modes of being-in-the-world constituting disposition (διάθεσις)
- φωνή is σημεῖον (indicating, sign): it indicates beings that are there with the character of ἡδύ
- Animal being-with-one-another operates as **enticing** (bringing into the same disposition) and **warning** (repelling from it)
- Animals do not report or exhibit; they **indicate** within the orbit of their animalistic having-to-do

##### §9b-γ: Encounter-Characters of the Human World: συμφέρον, βλαβερόν, ἀγαθόν. Λόγος as Self-Expression [pp. 39--44]
- λόγος is not σημαίνειν (indicating) but δηλοῦν (making-manifest)
- φωνή as dual function: (1) indication of ἡδύ/λυπηρόν, (2) constituting being-with-one-another of animals; λόγος has a corresponding dual function
- The three determinations of συμφέρον (*Rhetoric* A 6): (1) τὰ πρὸς τὸ τέλος -- toward the end; (2) κατὰ τὰς πράξεις -- within the purview of πρᾶξις; (3) σκοπὸς πρόκειται τῷ συμβουλεύοντι -- target for the one who reckons
- πρᾶξις = "concern," bringing-something-to-its-end; the primary mode of λόγος is λογίζεσθαι (deliberating)
- Deliberation has the structure of συλλογισμός: "if such and such is the end, then such and such must be undertaken" (p. 42)
- λόγος as λέγειν = having-there-of-the-world in the concrete situation (καιρός)
- Speaking is in itself **communicating**: "Speaking is, in itself, communicating; and, as communication, it is nothing other than κοινωνία" (p. 43)
- The four determinations of ἀγαθόν (*Rhetoric* A 6): (1) αὐτὸ ἑαυτοῦ ἕνεκα αἱρετόν -- graspable for its own sake; (2) οὗ ἕνεκα ἄλλο -- for-the-sake-of-which; (3) οὗ ἐφίεται πάντα -- toward which everything maintains itself; (4) οὗ παρόντος, εὖ διάκειται -- when present, one is well-disposed

#### §9c: The One (Das Man) as the How of the Everydayness of Being-with-One-Another [pp. 44--45]
- Being-with-one-another is not a factual claim about sociality but a **how of being**
- "I am one" (ich bin man) -- the One is "the genuine how of everydayness, of average, concrete being-with-one-another" (p. 45)
- The One maintains itself in language; the εἶδος (universal look) and δόξα (average meanings) are its characteristic modes
- The basic determination ζῷον πολιτικόν is the result of the *Politics* A 2 interpretation
- **Equiprimordiality thesis**: speaking-being and being-with-one-another are equiprimordial -- neither derivable from the other

### §10: The Being-There of Human Beings as ἐνέργεια: The ἀγαθόν (Nicomachean Ethics A 1--4) [pp. 46--54]

#### Transition and recapitulation (p. 47)
- Five preliminary determinations: (1) ζωή is being-in-a-world; (2) characterized by λόγος; (3) speaking is concern; (4) concern has a τέλος; (5) this being is being-with-one-another in the πόλις
- Four guiding questions about the ἀγαθόν: (1) where is it explicitly visible? (2) where is the ἀνθρώπινον ἀγαθόν? (3) what are its basic determinations? (4) which being-possibility suffices for the τέλειον ἀκρότατον?

#### §10a-α: The Explicitness of the ἀγαθόν as Such in τέχνη [pp. 47--48]
- Opening sentence of NE: every τέχνη, μέθοδος, πρᾶξις, and προαίρεσις has an ἀγαθόν -- a "being-after" (ἐφίεσθαι) that belongs to its being
- τέχνη = "knowing-one's-way-around in a concern"; it makes the τέλος explicitly visible
- This ἐφίεσθαι is not accidental but belongs to being itself; concern is "being-after" some good

#### §10a-β: The Explicitness of the ἀνθρώπινον ἀγαθόν in πολιτική [p. 48]
- πολιτική is the comprehensive μέθοδος that makes the specifically human good explicit
- Ethics is not a *part* of politics; politics is the knowing-the-way-around of living itself as being-with-one-another
- The ἦθος (comportment) in the world is τὸ ἧθος -- hence ἠθικὴ πολιτική

#### §10b-α: Manifoldness and Guiding Connectedness of the τέλη and Necessity of a τέλος δι' αὐτό [pp. 49--51]
- Some τέλη are ἐνέργειαι (beings-at-work: the τέλος lies in the doing itself); others are ἔργα (works separate from the activity)
- ἐνέργεια = "being-at-work," not "actuality" (Wirklichkeit): "If our expression 'actuality' were not so worn out, it would be an excellent translation" (p. 50)
- There is a guiding connectedness among concerns: one concern is authoritative for another (e.g., στρατηγική as δύναμις reaching through a manifoldness of concerns)
- Within this manifoldness there must be a τέλος δι' αὐτό (end for its own sake)
- πέρας (limit/finitude) determines being-there: "The completion of concern is only possible in that what is concerned is there, that the concern is not grasping at straws" (p. 51)
- Limitlessness (ἄπειρον) = κενὴ καὶ ματαία (empty and vain striving)

#### §10b-β: The βίοι as τέλη δι' αὐτά: Criteria for the τέλος δι' αὐτό [pp. 52--54]
- βίος distinguished from ζωή: "a new concept of 'life,' and not the same as ζωή... βίος is the 'tending of life,' 'course of life'" (p. 52) -- what life makes of itself from its concerns (περὶ τῶν πράξεων)
- Being-there always already carries an interpretation of itself (grounded in δόξα)
- Three βίοι: (1) βίος ἀπολαυστικός (pleasure/enjoyment); (2) βίος πολιτικός (political engagement); (3) βίος θεωρητικός (contemplation -- explicitly postponed)
- **Fourfold criteria** for the τέλος δι' αὐτό: (1) οἰκεῖον -- at home in being-there; (2) δυσαφαίρετον -- undetachable; (3) τέλειον -- complete; (4) αὔταρκες -- self-sufficient
- **ἡδονή fails**: steers being-there away from itself toward the world; life fully dependent on the world, not living its own being (p. 54)
- **τιμή fails**: belongs to others (fails οἰκεῖον), can be refused (fails δυσαφαίρετον) (p. 54)
- ἀρετή introduced as "competence" (being-awake-and-succeeding), but requires εὐτυχία (turning-out-well), which requires ἐνέργεια -- that ἀρετή be **in deeds**

## Section 3: Key Concepts

### 3.1 ψυχή (psyche)
- **greek_form**: ψυχή
- **definition**: Not "soul" in any metaphysical sense but the being of the living, the being-there of human beings in its full worldly character. Heidegger notes that ψυχή is an οὐσία whose basic aspects are κρίνειν and κινεῖν (p. 32)
- **type**: ontological-structural
- **dependencies**: presupposes the phenomenological destruction of the traditional "soul" concept
- **function**: Provides the ontological framework within which λόγον ἔχειν and κοινωνία become intelligible as modes of being
- **pages**: 32

### 3.2 λόγον ἔχειν (logon echein)
- **greek_form**: λόγον ἔχειν
- **definition**: "Having-logos" -- the constitutive mode of human being-in-the-world. Not a faculty or property but the way human beings have their world, through speaking, deliberating, communicating
- **type**: being-determination
- **dependencies**: ψυχή, λόγος
- **function**: The central determination that separates human being-in-the-world from animal being-in-the-world; grounds κοινωνία
- **pages**: 32--33, 40, 43, 45

### 3.3 ζῷον λόγον ἔχον (zoon logon echon)
- **greek_form**: ζῷον λόγον ἔχον
- **definition**: The standard Aristotelian definition of the human being, read ontologically rather than as "rational animal." The definition appears in *Politics* A 2 in the context of demonstrating that the πόλις is φύσει (p. 33)
- **type**: being-definition
- **dependencies**: ζῷον, λόγον ἔχειν
- **function**: Starting point for the entire analysis; contains within itself the determination ζῷον πολιτικόν
- **pages**: 33, 36, 40

### 3.4 φωνή (phone)
- **greek_form**: φωνή
- **definition**: Voice / vocal announcing. A σημεῖον (indicating, sign) that indicates the ἡδύ and λυπηρόν. Not merely a sound but the basic mode by which animals are in their world and with one another
- **type**: being-determination (animal)
- **dependencies**: σημεῖον, ἡδύ, λυπηρόν
- **function**: The contrast term to λόγος; establishes the ontological difference between animal and human being-in-the-world
- **pages**: 33--34, 36, 38--39

### 3.5 κοινωνία (koinonia)
- **greek_form**: κοινωνία
- **definition**: Being-with-one-another, constituted through speaking. Not being-situated-alongside but being-as-speaking-with-one-another: "through communicating, refuting, confronting" (p. 33)
- **type**: being-determination
- **dependencies**: λόγον ἔχειν (equiprimordial)
- **function**: Constitutes the πόλις; ground of the determination ζῷον πολιτικόν
- **pages**: 33, 35--36, 39--40, 43, 45

### 3.6 ἡδύ / λυπηρόν (hedu / luperon)
- **greek_form**: ἡδύ, λυπηρόν
- **definition**: The pleasing and the distressing -- encounter-characters of the animal world. Not subjective feelings but aspects of the encounter with the world that address themselves to being-in-the-world. The world in the character of ἡδύ/λυπηρόν is nonobjective (p. 34)
- **type**: encounter-character
- **dependencies**: φωνή, being-in-the-world
- **function**: Constitutes the animal mode of world-disclosure; contrasted with the human encounter-characters (συμφέρον, βλαβερόν, ἀγαθόν)
- **pages**: 33--35, 37--39

### 3.7 ἡδονή (hedone)
- **greek_form**: ἡδονή
- **definition**: Pleasure/joy as κατάστασις ἁθρόα -- "a transposing-of-oneself-all-at-once into the genuinely available possibility of the being-there in question" (*Rhetoric* A 11). Has a dual character: (1) transposing itself into a disposition; (2) the disposition into which it is brought (p. 38). Identified as ποιητικόν of διάθεσις (p. 35)
- **type**: ontological-affective
- **dependencies**: κατάστασις, διάθεσις, ἡδύ
- **function**: Demonstrates that affectivity is a mode of being-in-the-world, not a subjective state; later tested and rejected as ἀγαθόν (p. 54)
- **pages**: 35, 38, 53--54

### 3.8 συμφέρον (sumpheron)
- **greek_form**: συμφέρον
- **definition**: "The conducive" / "the beneficial." The manner and mode in which the world matters to human beings. Three constitutive characters: (1) τὰ πρὸς τὸ τέλος; (2) κατὰ τὰς πράξεις; (3) σκοπός (pp. 41)
- **type**: encounter-character (human)
- **dependencies**: τέλος, πρᾶξις, σκοπός
- **function**: Mediates between the world-as-encountered and the ἀγαθόν; grounds the analysis of λόγος as deliberation
- **pages**: 41--44

### 3.9 λογίζεσθαι (logizesthai)
- **greek_form**: λογίζεσθαι
- **definition**: Deliberating; bringing-to-language that which is conducive to the end. The genuine mode of fulfillment of λόγος. Characterized as συμβουλεύεσθαι (taking counsel) with the structure of συλλογισμός (the "if-then")
- **type**: modal-specification of λόγος
- **dependencies**: λόγος, συμφέρον, τέλος, συλλογισμός
- **function**: Shows that λόγος is not theoretical apprehension but practical deliberation; connects speaking to concern
- **pages**: 41--42

### 3.10 Das Man (The One)
- **greek_form**: (German: das Man)
- **definition**: "The genuine how of everydayness, of average, concrete being-with-one-another" (p. 45). Not a degradation but the phenomenal structure of average being. The One maintains itself in language; sees the world through εἶδος and δόξα
- **type**: existential-structural
- **dependencies**: κοινωνία, λόγος, δόξα, εἶδος
- **function**: Names the mode of being in which human beings initially and for the most part encounter the world; establishes the equiprimordiality of speaking-being and being-with-one-another
- **pages**: 44--45

### 3.11 ἐνέργεια (energeia)
- **greek_form**: ἐνέργεια
- **definition**: "Being-at-work" -- not "actuality" (Wirklichkeit), though that term would be excellent if not worn out (p. 50). A how of being with the being-character of πρᾶξις. Distinguished from ἔργα: some τέλη are ἐνέργειαι (the τέλος lies in the doing itself), others produce separate works
- **type**: ontological-structural
- **dependencies**: πρᾶξις, τέλος
- **function**: Names the being-character of human concern; the mode in which ἀρετή must be actualized ("in deeds")
- **pages**: 49--50, 54

### 3.12 ἀγαθόν (agathon)
- **greek_form**: ἀγαθόν
- **definition**: "The good" -- four determinations from *Rhetoric* A 6: (1) graspable for its own sake; (2) for-the-sake-of-which; (3) that toward which everything maintains itself; (4) when present, one is well-disposed. These converge in the ἀγαθόν as primarily τέλος/πέρας -- a fundamental determination of being (p. 44)
- **type**: ontological-teleological
- **dependencies**: τέλος, πέρας, συμφέρον
- **function**: The toward-which of all concern; investigated in §10 as the genuine being-character of human being-there
- **pages**: 33, 41, 43--44, 46--54

### 3.13 τέχνη (techne)
- **greek_form**: τέχνη
- **definition**: "Knowing-one's-way-around in a concern." The mode of being-in-the-world in which the ἀγαθόν first becomes explicitly visible. Every τέχνη has an ἀγαθόν within it as its ἐφίεσθαι (being-after)
- **type**: mode of knowing
- **dependencies**: ἀγαθόν, τέλος, πρᾶξις
- **function**: Provides the initial access to the ἀγαθόν; serves as the basis for the move to πολιτική
- **pages**: 47--48

### 3.14 πολιτική (politike)
- **greek_form**: πολιτική
- **definition**: The knowing-the-way-around of living itself, as being-with-one-another. The comprehensive μέθοδος that makes the ἀνθρώπινον ἀγαθόν explicit. Not "politics" as a discipline but the mode of being in which one knows one's way around living in the πόλις
- **type**: mode of knowing (comprehensive)
- **dependencies**: τέχνη, κοινωνία, πόλις, ἀνθρώπινον ἀγαθόν
- **function**: Architectonic knowledge that encompasses all particular τέχναι; ground of the investigation of the βίοι
- **pages**: 48

### 3.15 τέλος / πέρας (telos / peras)
- **greek_form**: τέλος, πέρας
- **definition**: End/limit. τέλος: that toward which concern moves. πέρας: the finitude that determines being-there. Heidegger equates them: "the ἀγαθόν is primarily end, τέλος, or more precisely, πέρας as a fundamental determination of being" (p. 44). Limitlessness (ἄπειρον) = empty and vain striving (p. 51)
- **type**: ontological-structural
- **dependencies**: πρᾶξις, ἀγαθόν
- **function**: Establishes finitude as constitutive of being-there; "the completion of concern is only possible in that what is concerned is there" (p. 51)
- **pages**: 44, 49--51

### 3.16 βίος (bios)
- **greek_form**: βίος
- **definition**: "Course of life" / "life-account" -- distinguished from ζωή. "Modern biology does not have the Greek βίος in mind. βίος is the 'tending of life,' 'course of life'" (p. 52). What life makes of itself from its concerns (περὶ τῶν πράξεων)
- **type**: ontological-structural
- **dependencies**: ζωή (distinguished from), πρᾶξις, τέλος
- **function**: The three βίοι provide the candidates for the τέλος δι' αὐτό
- **pages**: 52--54

### 3.17 Equiprimordiality (Gleichursprünglichkeit)
- **greek_form**: (German term; no single Greek equivalent)
- **definition**: The structural relation between speaking-being and being-with-one-another: "the being-there of human beings as such possesses equiprimordially speaking-being and being-with-one-another" (p. 45). Neither is derivable from the other
- **type**: structural-relation
- **dependencies**: λόγον ἔχειν, κοινωνία
- **function**: Prevents reductive accounts that ground one in the other; establishes the phenomenological method of co-original determination
- **pages**: 45

## Section 4: Main Positions & Arguments

### U-FP2a-P1: Logos as Ontological Constitution, Not Faculty
- **claim**: λόγος in ζῷον λόγον ἔχον is not "reason" as a faculty possessed by a subject but the constitutive mode of human being-in-the-world
- **premises**: (1) Aristotle introduces ζῷον λόγον ἔχον in the context of the πόλις as φύσει (*Politics* A 2); (2) the πόλις is a being-possibility, not a political institution; (3) λόγος constitutes the κοινωνία of συμφέρον and ἀγαθόν → **conclusion**: λόγος names how the human being has its world, not a property it possesses
- **aristotelian_texts**: *Politics* A 2, 1253a 9--18
- **status**: Textually explicit (pp. 33, 40, 43)

### U-FP2a-P2: Phone as Semeion vs. Logos as Deloun
- **claim**: The separation of φωνή from λόγος is not a difference in complexity but in ontological function
- **premises**: (1) φωνή is σημαίνειν (indicating): it indicates the ἡδύ and λυπηρόν; (2) λόγος is δηλοῦν (making-manifest): it makes manifest the συμφέρον, βλαβερόν, and ἀγαθόν; (3) indicating and making-manifest are different modes of being-in-the-world → **conclusion**: The φωνή/λόγος distinction is ontological, not one of degree
- **aristotelian_texts**: *Politics* A 2, 1253a 10--18
- **status**: Textually explicit (pp. 33--34, 39)

### U-FP2a-P3: World as Mattering, Not Object
- **claim**: The world is not an object for a subject but is encountered in the mode of "mattering" to the living being
- **premises**: (1) Both animals and humans are living beings in the mode of being-in-the-world; (2) the world is always already there, mattering to the living being; (3) the subject/object distinction is meaningless for Greek philosophy → **conclusion**: The world is a "definite mode of the world as encountered in living" (p. 36)
- **aristotelian_texts**: *Politics* A 2 (context); *De Anima* Γ 2 (background)
- **status**: Textually explicit (pp. 36--37, 40)

### U-FP2a-P4: Inconsequentiality as Everydayness
- **claim**: The specific character of everydayness is inconsequentiality -- most things do not matter to me, are "without consequence"
- **premises**: (1) Mattering has degrees; (2) in everyday living, most of the surrounding world does not matter; (3) this "not mattering" is not absence but a positive character → **conclusion**: Inconsequentiality is the default mode of the world's being-there in everydayness
- **aristotelian_texts**: (Heidegger's phenomenological elaboration, not tied to a specific Aristotelian passage)
- **status**: Textually explicit (pp. 36--37)

### U-FP2a-P5: Hedone as Katastasis, Not Subjective Feeling
- **claim**: ἡδονή is κατάστασις ἁθρόα -- a transposing-of-oneself-all-at-once into one's natural disposition, not a subjective feeling
- **premises**: (1) *Rhetoric* A 11 defines ἡδονή as κατάστασις ἁθρόα εἰς τὴν ὑπάρχουσαν φύσιν; (2) the ἡδύ has a dual character: transposing itself into a disposition and the disposition into which one is brought; (3) ἡδονή is ποιητικόν of διάθεσις → **conclusion**: Affectivity is a mode of being-in-the-world, not an inner psychological state
- **aristotelian_texts**: *Rhetoric* A 11, 1369b 33--1370a 2
- **status**: Textually explicit (pp. 34--35, 38)

### U-FP2a-P6: Animal Being-with-One-Another as Enticing/Warning
- **claim**: Animal κοινωνία operates through φωνή as enticing and warning
- **premises**: (1) φωνή is σημεῖον of ἡδύ/λυπηρόν; (2) this indicating has the character of addressing itself to other animals; (3) enticing = bringing another into the same disposition; warning = repelling from it → **conclusion**: Animals have genuine being-with-one-another at the level of φωνή, constituted as enticing and warning
- **aristotelian_texts**: *Politics* A 2, 1253a 10--14
- **status**: Textually explicit (pp. 38--39)

### U-FP2a-P7: Sumpheron as Triple-Structured Worldly Character
- **claim**: The συμφέρον has three constitutive characters that reveal the world in its character as conducive
- **premises**: (1) τὰ πρὸς τὸ τέλος -- toward the end; (2) κατὰ τὰς πράξεις -- within the purview of πρᾶξις; (3) σκοπὸς πρόκειται τῷ συμβουλεύοντι -- target for the deliberator → **conclusion**: The συμφέρον is the manner in which the world matters to beings in the mode of πρᾶξις μετὰ λόγου
- **aristotelian_texts**: *Rhetoric* A 6, 1362a 17--22
- **status**: Textually explicit (p. 41)

### U-FP2a-P8: Logos as Logizesthai / Deliberation
- **claim**: The primary mode of λόγος is λογίζεσθαι (deliberating), which has the if-then structure of συλλογισμός
- **premises**: (1) λόγος brings the συμφέρον to language; (2) this bringing-to-language is deliberating; (3) deliberation has the structure "if such and such is the end, then such and such must be undertaken"; (4) λόγος as λέγειν = having-there-of-the-world in a position determined by here and now (καιρός) → **conclusion**: λόγος is not theoretical apprehension but the practical mode of deliberating about what is conducive to the end
- **aristotelian_texts**: *NE* Z 10, 1142a 31--b 1; *Rhetoric* A 6, 1362a 17 sqq.
- **status**: Textually explicit (pp. 41--43)

### U-FP2a-P9: Speaking as Communicating = Koinonia
- **claim**: Speaking is in itself communicating, and communication is nothing other than κοινωνία
- **premises**: (1) Speaking is not expression of inner states but self-expressing; (2) speaking is discussing the συμφέρον with others; (3) λόγος exhibits, communicates, makes the world there with the other → **conclusion**: "Speaking is, in itself, communicating; and, as communication, it is nothing other than κοινωνία" (p. 43)
- **aristotelian_texts**: *Politics* A 2, 1253a 14 sq.
- **status**: Textually explicit (p. 43)

### U-FP2a-P10: Agathon as Fourfold Determination
- **claim**: The ἀγαθόν has four determinations that converge in τέλος/πέρας as a fundamental determination of being
- **premises**: (1) αὐτὸ ἑαυτοῦ ἕνεκα αἱρετόν; (2) οὗ ἕνεκα ἄλλο; (3) οὗ ἐφίεται πάντα; (4) οὗ παρόντος, εὖ διάκειται → **conclusion**: "These various determinations of ἀγαθόν all run together in that the ἀγαθόν is primarily end, τέλος, or more precisely, πέρας as a fundamental determination of being" (p. 44)
- **aristotelian_texts**: *Rhetoric* A 6, 1362a 22--29
- **status**: Textually explicit (pp. 43--44)

### U-FP2a-P11: Das Man as Genuine How of Everydayness
- **claim**: The One (das Man) is the genuine phenomenal structure of average being-with-one-another, not a degradation
- **premises**: (1) "I am one" is more genuine than "I am"; (2) the One arises from the manner in which human beings initially see the world; (3) the One has its dominion in language; (4) εἶδος as the "look" of the world carries a claim to universal validity → **conclusion**: The One is "the genuine how of everydayness, of average, concrete being-with-one-another" (p. 45)
- **aristotelian_texts**: *Politics* A 2 (conclusion of the analysis)
- **status**: Textually explicit (pp. 44--45)

### U-FP2a-P12: Equiprimordiality of Speaking-Being and Being-with-One-Another
- **claim**: These two determinations are equiprimordial: neither is derivable from the other
- **premises**: (1) λόγος constitutes κοινωνία; (2) but κοινωνία is also the ground of λόγος (speaking is always speaking-with); (3) "it would be altogether wrong to deduce one from the other" → **conclusion**: "The being-there of human beings as such possesses equiprimordially speaking-being and being-with-one-another" (p. 45)
- **aristotelian_texts**: *Politics* A 2
- **status**: Textually explicit (p. 45)

### U-FP2a-P13: Energeia as Being-at-Work
- **claim**: ἐνέργεια is "being-at-work," the how of πρᾶξις, not "actuality" in the worn-out modern sense
- **premises**: (1) Some τέλη are ἐνέργειαι (telos in the doing itself, e.g., walking); (2) others produce separate ἔργα (e.g., shoe from shoemaking); (3) ἐνέργεια names the being-character of πρᾶξις → **conclusion**: ἐνέργεια is a "how of being, such a way of being that has the being-character of πρᾶξις" (p. 50)
- **aristotelian_texts**: *NE* A 1, 1094a 3 sqq.
- **status**: Textually explicit (pp. 49--50)

### U-FP2a-P14: Peras as Ontological Finitude
- **claim**: πέρας is not a negative constraint but the condition of possibility for concern to reach its being
- **premises**: (1) Concern about something is already implicit in that it concerns something; (2) limitlessness makes concern empty and vain (ἄπειρον → κενὴ καὶ ματαία ὄρεξις); (3) πέρας constitutes the τέλος δι' αὐτό through guiding connectedness → **conclusion**: "The completion of concern is only possible in that what is concerned is there, that the concern is not grasping at straws, that concern has the character of the πέρας" (p. 51)
- **aristotelian_texts**: *NE* A 1, 1094a 19 sqq.
- **status**: Textually explicit (p. 51)

### U-FP2a-P15: Bios as Life-Account, Not Biological Life
- **claim**: βίος is distinguished from ζωή as "course of life" / "life-account"
- **premises**: (1) βίος is what life makes of itself from the vicissitudes of its concerns; (2) it is περὶ τῶν πράξεων -- interpretation of concerns; (3) Aristotle's method: seeing on the basis of βίοι what life has grasped as τέλος καθ' αὐτό → **conclusion**: The three βίοι are ways human being-there interprets its own being (p. 52)
- **aristotelian_texts**: *NE* A 3, 1095b 14 sqq.
- **status**: Textually explicit (p. 52)

### U-FP2a-P16: Fourfold Criteria for Telos di' Auto
- **claim**: The τέλος δι' αὐτό must satisfy four criteria: οἰκεῖον, δυσαφαίρετον, τέλειον, αὔταρκες
- **premises**: (1) οἰκεῖον: at home in being-there (*NE* A 3, 1095a 3 sq.); (2) δυσαφαίρετον: undetachable (*NE* A 3, 1095b 17 sqq.); (3) τέλειον: complete (*NE* A 5, 1097a 33); (4) αὔταρκες: self-sufficient (*NE* A 5, 1097b 8) → **conclusion**: These are existential tests for whether a τέλος genuinely determines being-there as being-with-one-another
- **aristotelian_texts**: *NE* A 3--5
- **status**: Textually explicit (pp. 52--53)

### U-FP2a-P17: Rejection of Hedone and Time as Agathon
- **claim**: ἡδονή and τιμή both fail the criteria for the genuine ἀγαθόν
- **premises**: (1) ἡδονή steers being-there away from itself toward the world (fails οἰκεῖον); (2) τιμή belongs to others, not to my being as such (fails οἰκεῖον); (3) τιμή can be refused by others (fails δυσαφαίρετον) → **conclusion**: Neither is a genuine ἀγαθόν; ἀρετή remains but requires ἐνέργεια
- **aristotelian_texts**: *NE* A 3, 1095b--1096a
- **status**: Textually explicit (pp. 53--54)

### U-FP2a-P18: Being-There as Self-Interpreting
- **claim**: Being-there always already carries an interpretation of itself, grounded in δόξα
- **premises**: (1) "There itself to have an interpretation of itself that, to a certain degree, is always already carried along with it" (p. 52); (2) Aristotle's method draws on what life says about itself; (3) "when I am oriented to that, no reference is needed to the διότι" (p. 54) → **conclusion**: Aristotle's starting from the βίοι is grounded in the self-interpreting character of being-there
- **aristotelian_texts**: *NE* A 1--3
- **status**: Textually explicit, with interpretive framing [INTERP-low: Heidegger here attributes hermeneutic self-interpretation to Aristotle]

## Section 4a: Key Interlocutors

### Aristotle
- **role**: Primary interlocutor -- Heidegger is reconstructing Aristotle's own determinations, not arguing against them
- **borrowings**: The entire conceptual apparatus: ζῷον λόγον ἔχον, φωνή/λόγος distinction, ἡδύ/λυπηρόν vs. συμφέρον/βλαβερόν/ἀγαθόν, τέχνη, πολιτική, ἐνέργεια, βίοι, criteria for τέλος δι' αὐτό
- **departures**: Heidegger consistently translates Aristotle's categories into existential-ontological terms: πρᾶξις becomes "concern," ἡδονή becomes a mode of being-in-the-world, the βίοι become self-interpretations of being-there. The ontological register is Heidegger's, not Aristotle's own vocabulary
- **texts cited**: *Politics* A 2 (1252b 30--1253a 20); *Rhetoric* A 6 (1362a 17 sqq.) and A 11 (1369b 33--1370a 2); *NE* A 1--4 (1094a--1096a); *NE* Z 10 (1142a--b); *De Anima* Γ 2 (427a 17 sq.)

### (No other named interlocutors in this unit)
Heidegger does not engage any modern philosopher by name in §§9--10. The polemic against the subject/object distinction (p. 40) and the rejection of "rational animal" as a translation are directed at an unnamed modern philosophical tradition, not at specific thinkers.

## Section 5: Tensions & Open Questions

### T1: Phone/Logos Boundary Problem [INTERP-high]
Heidegger insists the φωνή/λόγος distinction is ontological, not one of degree. Yet the phenomena he describes -- enticing/warning in animals, deliberating in humans -- suggest a spectrum of communicative capacity. Heidegger acknowledges that animals already have genuine being-with-one-another through φωνή (p. 39), raising the question of whether the distinction holds as a sharp ontological break or admits of gradation. *Linked to*: U-FP2a-P2, U-FP2a-P6.

### T2: Das Man -- Positive Structure or Deficient Mode? [INTERP-low]
Heidegger presents das Man as "the genuine how of everydayness" (p. 45) without the overtly negative valence it carries in BT. Yet the description of δόξα as "average meanings" and εἶδος as carrying "a claim to a definite averageness" (p. 45) already implies a leveling-down. The tension between das Man as genuine phenomenal structure and das Man as concealment is present but unresolved. *Linked to*: U-FP2a-P11.

### T3: Agathon as Telos vs. Agathon as Peras [INTERP-high]
Heidegger identifies ἀγαθόν with both τέλος and πέρας (p. 44). But τέλος as the toward-which of concern and πέρας as the finitude of being-there are not obviously the same concept. The former is teleological (direction of concern), the latter existential-ontological (finitude as condition of being). Heidegger's equation performs a translation that is asserted rather than fully argued. *Linked to*: U-FP2a-P10, U-FP2a-P14.

### T4: Equiprimordiality vs. Analytic Priority of Logos [INTERP-high]
Heidegger asserts equiprimordiality (p. 45), but the entire analysis of §9 arguably establishes a priority of λόγος: it is through λόγος that the κοινωνία of the πόλις is constituted; the συμφέρον and ἀγαθόν are made manifest only through λόγος. If λόγος is the constitutive ground, the relation is foundational rather than equiprimordial. *Linked to*: U-FP2a-P1, U-FP2a-P9, U-FP2a-P12.

### T5: Energeia -- Praxis/Poiesis Ambiguity [INTERP-low]
In §10b-α, Heidegger distinguishes τέλη that are ἐνέργειαι from those producing separate ἔργα (p. 50). This maps onto the πρᾶξις/ποίησις distinction, but Heidegger does not invoke it explicitly. The ambiguity matters because the analysis of ἀγαθόν proceeds under the heading of πρᾶξις (concern), yet many examples (shoemaking, saddlery, shipbuilding) are poietic. *Linked to*: U-FP2a-P13.

### T6: Bioi and the Postponed Theoretikos [INTERP-low]
Aristotle explicitly postpones βίος θεωρητικός (p. 54), and Heidegger follows this postponement. Yet this βίος was introduced earlier (p. 32) as "the life whiled away in pure contemplation." The postponement is methodologically significant: practical βίοι are examined first, suggesting θεωρία can only be understood after πρᾶξις is secured. The investigation of ἀγαθόν in §10 is therefore explicitly incomplete. *Linked to*: U-FP2a-P15, U-FP2a-P16.

### T7: Self-Interpretation and the Hermeneutic Circle [INTERP-low]
Heidegger claims that being-there always carries an interpretation of itself (p. 52) and that Aristotle's method draws on this self-interpretation ("what life says about itself"). But this attributes to Aristotle a hermeneutic-phenomenological methodology that Heidegger himself is developing. The question is whether Aristotle's appeal to ἔνδοξα and the βίοι constitutes genuine hermeneutic self-interpretation or something else. *Linked to*: U-FP2a-P18.
