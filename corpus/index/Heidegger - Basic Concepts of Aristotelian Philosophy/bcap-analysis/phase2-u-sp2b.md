**One-sentence thesis**: Heidegger argues that the earlier philosophical determinations of movement through ἑτερότης, ἀνισότης, and μὴ ὄν fail because they capture only what *can* be otherwise without touching the ontological dimension of presence and disappearing-from-presence, and that the genuine definition of κίνησις -- ἐνέργεια τοῦ δυνάμει ποιητικοῦ καὶ παθητικοῦ -- is reached only through the πρός τι (being-in-relation-to-another), which Heidegger interprets as the fundamental character of being-in-the-world, thereby grounding Greek ontology not in nature but in ποίησις and πρᾶξις as primary modes of being-there.

## 4. Main Positions

### U-SP2b-P1: Movement as genuinely ἀόριστον

**Claim**: κίνησις genuinely shows itself as ἀόριστον -- indeterminate, not capturable by any single category of presence -- and this is not a deficiency but the phenomenon's own character.

**Textual basis**: "For the Greeks, movement shows itself as a ἀόριστον. From the peculiar character of the indeterminateness of movement, it is seen that one cannot..." (p. 216); "Movement itself shows itself as something that is 'not determinable, delimitable,' ἀόριστον" (p. 214).

**Type**: Ontological characterization (negative).

**Interpretive status**: Direct textual claim.

### U-SP2b-P2: Failure of ἑτερότης/ἀνισότης/μὴ ὄν as determinations of movement

**Claim**: The earlier categorial determinations -- ἑτερότης, ἀνισότης, μὴ ὄν -- fail because they determine what *can* be otherwise but not what is *necessarily* in movement; they miss the ontological dimension of presence, being-present, and disappearing-from-presence.

**Textual basis**: "this determination of the ἑτερότης does not include the fundamental ontological respect of *presence*, of *being-present*, and of *disappearing-from-presence*" (p. 215); "the predicates ἕτερον, ἄνισον, μὴ ὄν, as such do not determine beings with respect to their being in movement" (p. 214).

**Type**: Critique (of Pythagorean/Platonic predecessors via Aristotle).

**Interpretive status**: Direct textual claim. Heidegger follows Aristotle's own critique closely.

### U-SP2b-P3: μὴ ὄν as determinate not-being, not sheer absence

**Claim**: μὴ ὄν, with respect to movement, is not "not being-there-at-all" but a *determinate* not-being -- Plato in the *Sophist* advanced to the insight that μὴ ὄν is "in a certain sense, a way of being." Yet this advance remains insufficient for defining κίνησις, since every being *is not* something determinate without thereby being in movement.

**Textual basis**: "rather μὴ ὄν apprehended in the sense of a *determinate* not-being. In his *Sophist*, *Plato* advanced to this character of beings..." (p. 216); cf. Plato, *Sophist* 256d sqq.

**Type**: Historical-philosophical acknowledgment (Plato's advance, yet still insufficient).

**Interpretive status**: Direct textual claim.

### U-SP2b-P4: The Pythagorean συστοιχία reveals Greek ontology in nuce

**Claim**: The table of opposites (ἕν/πολλά, φῶς/σκότος, etc.) is not accidental but reveals the whole Greek ontology -- the fundamental discovery of being as being-present, ἐντελέχεια, being-produced.

**Textual basis**: "The entire listing of the categories is in itself transparent, a Greek ontology in nuce, on the basis of which Aristotle worked, and which becomes vital on the whole through the fundamental discovery of being as being present, of ἐντελέχεια, of being-produced" (p. 216).

**Type**: Meta-ontological claim (about the structure of Greek thought as such).

**Interpretive status**: Direct textual claim with [INTERP-low] element: Heidegger reads the Pythagorean table *through* his own concept of being-as-presence, which the Pythagoreans themselves would not have articulated in these terms.

### U-SP2b-P5: κίνησις as ἀτελής -- the "not at the end"

**Claim**: Κίνησις has the character of ἀτελής: the being-present that is "not at the end," not completed in relation to the ἔργον. The wood in the workshop is able-to-be when taken up into work, but κίνησις is not the completion -- it is the δυνατόν as such, designated through ἐνέργεια.

**Textual basis**: "Κίνησις is defined as a being-present that has the character of the ἀτελής, of the 'not at the end'" (p. 217); "Ἀτελής is the δυνατόν as such, not at the end, but it is right there through ἐνέργεια" (p. 217).

**Type**: Ontological definition (transitional -- links §27 to §28).

**Interpretive status**: Direct textual claim.

### U-SP2b-P6: μία ἐνέργεια -- movement is one and the same for mover and moved

**Claim**: Movement is not in the mover or the moved separately but is μία ἀμφοῖν -- "one and the same mode of being-present for both" -- a unitary ἐνέργεια.

**Textual basis**: "μία ἐνέργεια, 'one and the same mode of being-present for both'" (p. 217); cf. Physics Γ2, 202a13-18.

**Type**: Ontological thesis (unity of movement).

**Interpretive status**: Direct textual claim; closely follows Aristotle's own formulation.

### U-SP2b-P7: πρός τι as the character of every being in movement (not a fifth type of movement)

**Claim**: The πρός τι does not introduce a new type of κίνησις alongside the four (γένεσις/φθορά, φορά, αὔξησις/φθίσις, ἀλλοίωσις) but names *the character of every being that is in movement* -- being-in-relation-to-another as the ontological structure of movement as such.

**Textual basis**: "the explicit statement of the πρός τι must have another sense, not a fore-indication of the type of encountering the world in a determinate movement, but rather *the character of every being that is in movement*" (p. 219).

**Type**: Ontological reinterpretation of a categorial determination.

**Interpretive status**: Direct textual claim. Aristotle's own distinction is clear: πρός τι is not a type of change but the relational character of all change.

### U-SP2b-P8: πρός τι as being-in-the-world (Heidegger's existential transposition)

**Claim**: The Aristotelian πρός τι -- relational being, being-in-relation-to-another -- is ontologically equivalent to being-in-the-world. Beings characterized as πρός τι are "the beings that are there of the world as being in relations."

**Textual basis**: "what we have come to know as a determination of the world, is ontologically characterized as πρός τι -- the beings that are there of the world as being in relations" (p. 219); the Categories analysis of πρός τι mapped onto the structure of living-in-the-world (pp. 219-220).

**Type**: Existential-ontological transposition. [INTERP-high]: This is Heidegger's strongest interpretive intervention in U-SP2b. The πρός τι in Aristotle's Categories is one category among ten, not a structural determination of being-as-such. Heidegger's move from a categorial determination to an existential structure constitutes a genuine philosophical transformation of the Aristotelian material.

### U-SP2b-P9: The genuine definition -- κίνησις as ἐνέργεια τοῦ δυνάμει ποιητικοῦ καὶ παθητικοῦ

**Claim**: The genuine (eigentlich) definition of movement is ἐνέργεια of the δυνάμει ποιητικόν and παθητικόν as such. This definition avoids circularity because ποίησις and πάθησις are not themselves movement but determinations of a unitary being that is in the mode of being-in-movement.

**Textual basis**: "κίνησις is ἐνέργεια [...] τοῦ δυνάμει ποιητικοῦ καὶ παθητικοῦ, ᾗ τοιοῦτον" (p. 221, citing Physics Γ3, 202b25 sqq.); "ποίησις is not yet movement, just as πάθησις is not. Ποίησις and πάθησις are determinations of a unitary being that is there in the mode of being-in-movement" (p. 221).

**Type**: The culminating ontological definition of the Second Part.

**Interpretive status**: Direct textual claim. This is Aristotle's own formula as Heidegger reads it. The non-circularity argument is Heidegger's explicit defense.

### U-SP2b-P10: νοῦς as openness to everything, νοεῖν as πάθος

**Claim**: Human openness to the world is grounded in νοῦς, which unlike αἴσθησις can suppose *everything*. Νοεῖν is "in a certain sense, a πάθος, a *being-approached* by the world." The νοῦς παθητικός is possible only through the νοῦς ποιητικός.

**Textual basis**: "Νοεῖν is, in a certain sense, a πάθος, a *being-approached* by the world" (p. 220); "The νοῦς παθητικός is possible only through the νοῦς ποιητικός, through a νοεῖν that uncovers the world" (p. 220); cf. De Anima Γ5, 430a10 sqq.

**Type**: Psychological-ontological (νοῦς as mode of being-in-the-world).

**Interpretive status**: [INTERP-low]: Heidegger's reading of De Anima through the lens of being-in-the-world goes beyond what Aristotle's text explicitly states but is a defensible phenomenological interpretation.

### U-SP2b-P11: Greek ontology derives from ποίησις/πρᾶξις, not from nature

**Claim**: The question τί τὸ ὄν is derived from the determinateness of ποίησις and being-there-present -- "ποίησις as primary being-in-the-world, πρᾶξις." Greek ontology is an ontology of making and action, not an ontology of nature. The later history of philosophy "neglects to look toward being-in-the-world."

**Textual basis**: "The question concerning the τί τὸ ὄν is derived from the determinateness of ποίησις and *being-there-present* -- ποίησις as primary being-in-the-world, πρᾶξις. It gives rise to, as well as the closest view of, Greek ontology -- not the ontology of nature!" (p. 222).

**Type**: Meta-ontological thesis (the origin-point of Greek ontological questioning).

**Interpretive status**: [INTERP-high]: This is one of Heidegger's most provocative claims in GA 18. He reads the Physics -- Aristotle's treatise on nature -- as evidence that Greek ontology is *not* an ontology of nature. The thesis requires showing that φύσις is itself understood through ποίησις, which Heidegger asserts but does not fully demonstrate here.

### U-SP2b-P12: ἐνέργεια/ἐντελέχεια/δύναμις as fundamental significance for the whole ontology

**Claim**: The Aristotelian investigation into κίνησις has "a fundamental significance for the whole ontology" -- it yields the basic determination of beings as ἐνέργεια, ἐντελέχεια, and δύναμις, which constitute the substance of Greek ontological thinking.

**Textual basis**: "This Aristotelian investigation into movement has a *fundamental significance for the whole ontology*: basic determination of beings as ἐνέργεια, ἐντελέχεια, and δύναμις" (p. 222).

## 4b. BT Anticipations (ALLOWED)

### BT-1: Being-in-the-world (In-der-Welt-sein) via πρός τι

The explicit identification of πρός τι with "being-in-the-world" (pp. 219-220) directly anticipates BT §12's analysis of In-der-Welt-sein as the basic constitution of Dasein. In the 1924 lectures, this structure is grounded in Aristotle's Categories analysis of πρός τι and the phenomenology of animal/human world-relatedness, rather than being derived from a phenomenological analytic of everydayness. The πρός τι as "the character of every being that is in movement" (p. 219) maps onto BT's claim that Dasein is always already in a world, always already related to beings. [INTERP-low]: The structural parallel is strong; Heidegger explicitly uses the phrase "being-in-the-world" and connects it to the relational character of all being-in-movement.

### BT-2: Befindlichkeit / Stimmung via νοεῖν as πάθος

The characterization of νοεῖν as "a πάθος, a *being-approached* by the world" (p. 220) and the analysis of the living thing's relation to its world through ἁφή and ἀντικείμενον anticipate BT §29's analysis of Befindlichkeit (disposition/attunement). The πάθος-structure of νοῦς -- being *already affected* by the world as condition of disclosing it -- maps onto the BT thesis that Dasein is always already in a mood, always already disposed toward the world before any thematic cognition. [INTERP-low]: The structural connection is explicit; Heidegger presents the πάθος character of νοῦς as the condition of possibility for openness.

### BT-3: Seinsvergessenheit (the forgetting of being-in-the-world)

Heidegger's closing claim that "the later history of philosophy neglects to look toward being-in-the-world" (p. 222) anticipates the BT thesis of Seinsvergessenheit. The 1924 lectures locate this neglect specifically in the tradition's treatment of Greek ontology as an ontology of nature rather than of ποίησις/πρᾶξις. This is a more determinate version of the BT claim: the forgetting is not just of being in general but of being-in-the-world as disclosed through the relational (πρός τι) character of movement. [INTERP-low]: The parallel is explicit; the language is continuous between 1924 and 1927.

### BT-4: Productive concept-formation and formal indication

The concluding thesis on concept-formation as "opening up the concrete character of the subject-matter" (p. 222) anticipates BT §63's discussion of the formal indication (formale Anzeige) and the methodological demand that ontological concepts be drawn from the phenomena themselves rather than imposed from without. The emphasis on making visible not only the *matter* but also the *how* parallels BT's insistence that the method of ontology must be determined by its subject-matter. [INTERP-low]: The methodological parallel is strong; the language of "opening up" the subject-matter recurs throughout BT.

### BT-5: The relational (πρός τι) character of equipment (Zeug) and world

The analysis of πρός τι as the character of all beings in movement, combined with the teaching/learning example (p. 221) and the analysis of the animal's encounter with its environment (p. 220), anticipates BT §15-18's analysis of the relational character of equipment (Zeug): every piece of equipment is *something-in-order-to* (Um-zu), always in a referential totality. The 1924 lectures ground this in Aristotle's insight that every ἕξις is ἕξις τινός, every being is a being-toward. [INTERP-high]: While the structural parallel is clear, the explicit connection to BT's Zeug-analysis requires interpretive extrapolation from the 1924 material.

## 5. Tensions & Open Questions

### T1: Circularity in the genuine definition

Heidegger claims that the genuine definition through ποιητικόν/παθητικόν avoids circularity because ποίησις and πάθησις are "not yet movement." But are they not already determined *by reference to* movement? The teaching/learning example (p. 221) seems to presuppose that we already understand what it means for these to be modes of κίνησις. Does Heidegger regard this as a productive hermeneutic circle rather than a vicious logical one? The text does not explicitly address this, but the appeal to ἐπαγωγή (p. 217) may suggest that the definition is meant to make explicit what is always already given in experience, not to derive κίνησις from more primitive terms.

### T2: πρός τι as category vs. existential structure

The identification of πρός τι with being-in-the-world (P8) is Heidegger's strongest interpretive intervention. In Aristotle's Categories (Cat. 7), πρός τι is one category among ten -- a way of characterizing the being of a particular class of entities (those whose being consists in being related to something else). Heidegger elevates this to a structural determination of *all* being-in-movement. An Aristotelian reading would resist this transposition: not all beings are πρός τι, and the πρός τι category does not have the privileged ontological status Heidegger assigns it. The question is whether Heidegger is *discovering* something implicit in Aristotle or *importing* an existential structure foreign to the text.

### T3: Physics as ontology "not of nature"

The closing claim that Greek ontology derives from ποίησις/πρᾶξις "not the ontology of nature" (P11) stands in tension with the fact that Heidegger has been interpreting the *Physics* -- Aristotle's treatise on φύσις. How does Heidegger reconcile the claim that Greek ontology is not an ontology of nature with his own reliance on natural-philosophical texts as the vehicle for the culminating ontological analysis? The implicit argument seems to be that the Physics discloses the ontological structures (ἐνέργεια, δύναμις, ἐντελέχεια) that are *presupposed by* the study of nature but that themselves derive from ποίησις (the encounter with beings in making/producing). This reading is available but requires reconstruction beyond what the text explicitly provides.

### T4: νοῦς excursus and the scope of Physics Γ3

The νοῦς passage (P10) introduces De Anima material that exceeds the scope of Physics Γ3. What work is this excursus doing? Is it strictly necessary for the Physics interpretation, or does it serve to prepare the ground for a broader existential-ontological reading that Heidegger needs but Aristotle's text does not provide? The passage appears to function as a bridge: the πρός τι as character of being-in-the-world needs a concrete phenomenological illustration of how relational being works for *human* beings (not just animals), and the νοῦς ποιητικός/παθητικός distinction provides this. But it also reveals that Heidegger is reading the Physics through a broader Aristotelian corpus than the text itself warrants.

### T5: ποίησις vs. πρᾶξις in the final sentence

The closing identification "ποίησις as primary being-in-the-world, πρᾶξις" (p. 222) collapses two concepts that Aristotle carefully distinguishes (NE VI 4-5: ποίησις has its end outside itself; πρᾶξις has its end in itself). Is Heidegger claiming that the distinction is not yet operative at the level of Physics Γ3? Or that both are modes of being-in-the-world that share the πρός τι structure? The text does not resolve this, but the earlier analysis of the ἀγαθόν as πέρας of πρᾶξις (U-SP2a-P11) suggests that Heidegger is aware of the distinction but is making a genetic claim: at the most fundamental ontological level, both ποίησις and πρᾶξις are modes of being-in-the-world from which the question of being arises.
