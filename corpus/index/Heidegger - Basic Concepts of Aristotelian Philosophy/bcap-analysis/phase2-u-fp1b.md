## Section 1: Unit Metadata

- **Sections covered**: §6 (Preliminary Clarification of λόγος, pp. 15-16), §7 (Οὐσία as the Basic Concept of Aristotelian Philosophy, pp. 17-25), §8 (Ὁρισμός as Determinate Mode of Being-in-the-World, pp. 26-31)
- **Page range**: 15-31 (PDF pages 30-46)

**Unit thesis**: Heidegger establishes the three-term nexus λόγος-οὐσία-ὁρισμός by showing that definition (ὁρισμός) is a mode of speaking (λόγος) that addresses beings in their being (οὐσία), and that this entire nexus is rooted not in formal logic but in Dasein's factical being-in-the-world -- specifically, in the concrete existential comportment of speaking that encounters beings in their limitedness (πέρας) and completeness (τέλος).

## Section 2: Hierarchical Outline

### I. λόγος as the basic determination of human being (§6, pp. 15-16)
- Concepts in their basic sense are "a concretely giving basic experience, not a theoretical grasping of the matter" (p. 15); Heidegger recovers the pre-scholastic sense of ὁρισμός as λόγος οὐσίας.
- λόγος is characterized through ἀποφαίνεσθαι and δηλοῦν as "a bringing-a-matter-to-self-showing" in speaking about something (p. 15).
- Speaking is not occasional but constitutive: it is self-expressing (*sich aussprechen*) and speaking-with-others; it is the "basic mode of the being of life" (p. 16), and where speaking ceases, we speak of death.
- The expressed (τὸ κείμενον) becomes fixed (κοινά), available to everyone, constituting a common intelligibility and averageness of understanding in which language operates.
- The human being is determined as ζῷον λόγον ἔχον, a "living being" as a category of being, not a biological concept; life is being-in-a-world.
- ἔχειν (*Met.* Δ23) is glossed as ἄγειν ("to conduct"): language belongs to the genuine drive (ὁρμή) of being of the human being (p. 16).

### II. Οὐσία as the ambiguous basic concept of Aristotelian philosophy (§7, pp. 17-25)
- The basic function of λόγος is "the bringing-to-self-showing of beings in their being, of οὐσία" (p. 17); but οὐσία is itself ambiguous, and this ambiguity is an index of the matter itself (type 3 ambiguity: legitimate familiarity).
- Three types of ambiguity are distinguished: (1) confusion, (2) insensitivity to difference, (3) the matter itself demanding multiple meanings from out of itself (pp. 17-18).
- The customary meaning of οὐσία (property, possession, estate) already contains the terminological meaning in germ: both refer to "a being in the how of its being" (pp. 18-19); from the outset, "being" for the Greeks means "being there."
- Five being-characters from *Metaphysics* Δ8 are enumerated: (1) ὑποκείμενον (being-at-hand), (2) αἴτιον ἐνυπάρχον (e.g., ψυχή as constituting being-in-the-world), (3) μόριον ἐνυπάρχον (limit/circumscription), (4) τὸ τί ἦν εἶναι (the being of a particular, containing the ἦν -- descent, history), (5) εἶδος (the "look," appearing).
- These being-characters are reframed as characters of the "there": οὐσία abbreviates παρουσία (being-present); its opposite ἀπουσία grounds στέρησις. Being-there means (1) presence and (2) being-complete/completedness (p. 26).

### III. Ὁρισμός as a determinate mode of being-in-the-world (§8, pp. 26-31)
- Ὁρισμός is λόγος οὐσίας in the sense that it addresses beings with regard to their completeness as present; it designates τὸ τί ἦν εἶναι (p. 26).
- The world is primarily given as συγκεχυμένον ("obscured," "unarticulated"); ὁρισμός requires "a particular opening of the eyes" (p. 26, citing *Phys.* A1, 184 a 22).
- The methodological principle from *Met.* Z3: learning proceeds from the customary (καθόλου) to the particular (καθ᾽ ἕκαστα) -- from average orientation to genuine knowing. This is "the genuine counter-thrust to Platonic philosophy" (p. 27).
- Πέρας (*Met.* Δ17) is the ἔσχατον, linked to εἶδος, τέλος, and the οὗ ἕνεκα; limit is the "having-of-limits of the matter, the πρᾶγμα determined in its limits" (p. 28). The maxim εἰς ἄπειρον ἰέναι is to be avoided.
- Climax: ὁρισμός is "a λόγος, a definite being-in-the-world" (p. 29); conceptuality is "an issue of being-there in a decisive sense, insofar as it has resolved radically to speak to the world -- to question and to research" (p. 29).
- The regression to customary meaning is "the overhearing of the speaking of natural being-there to its world" (p. 30); understanding basic concepts requires understanding Aristotle's experience of being-there.

### IV. Methodological framing: from customary to terminological (across §§6-8)
- Heidegger's procedure throughout is to show that terminological philosophical meaning arises from and remains tied to pre-theoretical, customary meaning in natural speaking.
- The four scholastic senses of "concept" (notio, intentio, conceptus, species) are enumerated as a foil (p. 15); the target is the pre-scholastic Greek sense.
- Two routes to terminological meaning: (1) a word is coined together with a newly discovered matter; (2) a word already at hand is fixed to a meaning previously only co-intended (p. 18).
- The child's undifferentiated address (calling all men "father") exemplifies how genuine orientation requires proceeding from the indeterminate to the determinate (*Phys.* A1, 184 b 12; p. 28).

## Section 3: Key Concepts (Heidegger on Aristotle)

### C1. λόγος
- **Greek form**: λόγος
- **Definition (as Heidegger uses it)**: The basic determination of the being of the human being; "speaking" in the sense of ἀποφαίνεσθαι (bringing-a-matter-to-self-showing) and δηλοῦν (making manifest); self-expressing as speaking-with-others about something. The "basic mode of the being of life, namely, of being-in-a-world" (p. 16).
- **Type**: ontological / existential
- **Dependencies**: none within this unit (foundational)
- **Function**: setup -- establishes the ground from which ὁρισμός and οὐσία will be investigated
- **Pages**: 15-17, 20, 26

### C2. οὐσία
- **Greek form**: οὐσία
- **Definition (as Heidegger uses it)**: The "basic concept of Aristotelian philosophy" with three interlocking senses: (1) beings themselves (what shows itself in αἴσθησις as σώματα), (2) the being of beings (the five being-characters of *Met.* Δ8), (3) being-there (the characters of the "there": presence and completeness). Customarily: property, possession, estate -- "a being that is there for me in an emphatic way" (p. 19). Terminologically: "a being in the how of its being." Abbreviation for παρουσία.
- **Type**: ontological
- **Dependencies**: λόγος (as that which addresses οὐσία)
- **Function**: positive_proposal -- the central concept around which the entire unit revolves
- **Pages**: 17-26, 29-30

### C3. ὁρισμός
- **Greek form**: ὁρισμός
- **Definition (as Heidegger uses it)**: "Circumscription," "delimitation"; a determinate mode of λέγειν (a completely distinctive possibility within speaking) that addresses beings with regard to their completeness as present. λόγος οὐσίας in the sense that it designates τὸ τί ἦν εἶναι. "A definite being-in-the-world, which meets the world that is there in its genuine there-character" (p. 29). The genuine mode of entry into beings insofar as πέρας is the basic character of the there.
- **Type**: ontological / methodological
- **Dependencies**: λόγος, οὐσία, τὸ τί ἦν εἶναι, πέρας
- **Function**: positive_proposal -- the culminating concept of Chapter 1
- **Pages**: 15, 20, 26-29

### C4. ὑποκείμενον
- **Greek form**: ὑποκείμενον
- **Definition (as Heidegger uses it)**: "Being-at-hand," the "at-hand-ness" of something; that which already lies there in advance, about which I speak. The first being-character enumerated in *Met.* Δ8. Connected to the customary sense of beings as what is there for dealing with.
- **Type**: ontological
- **Dependencies**: οὐσία (as one of its being-characters)
- **Function**: setup -- establishes the ground-level being-character from which others are developed
- **Pages**: 22, 25

### C5. ψυχή
- **Greek form**: ψυχή
- **Definition (as Heidegger uses it)**: A being-character (αἴτιον ἐνυπάρχον) that constitutes the specific being of a living thing as being-in-the-world. Not "substance" in the Cartesian sense, not the "famous 'substantiality' of the soul" (p. 23), but a mode of being: the soul is that which is responsible for the specific being of the living thing. Its two basic aspects are κρίνειν (separating/orienting) and κινεῖν (moving).
- **Type**: ontological / existential
- **Dependencies**: οὐσία, ὑποκείμενον (ψυχή comprises being as ὑποκείμενον within itself)
- **Function**: bridge -- connects the analysis of being-characters to being-in-the-world
- **Pages**: 22-23, 25

### C6. τὸ τί ἦν εἶναι
- **Greek form**: τὸ τί ἦν εἶναι
- **Definition (as Heidegger uses it)**: "The being of a particular" -- the fourth being-character of *Met.* Δ8. Not "what is individual" or "essence" in the scholastic sense but what is *particular* as accessed at a distance from the familiar. Contains the determination of the ἦν ("was"): a being seen with respect to "what it was already, from which it stems in its being, with respect to its descent, its having come into being there" (pp. 23-24). The genuine topic of λόγος as ὁρισμός. Constitutes "an entire complex of being-determinations" (p. 25).
- **Type**: ontological
- **Dependencies**: οὐσία, ὁρισμός (as the being-character that ὁρισμός addresses)
- **Function**: positive_proposal
- **Pages**: 23-26

### C7. εἶδος
- **Greek form**: εἶδος
- **Definition (as Heidegger uses it)**: "The look," "the appearing," "species"; that which is seen, sighted independently -- what I see and identify as this thing. The being that is there in its appearing. For the Greeks, a chair is a chair because it *appears* as such. In its connection with τὸ τί ἦν εἶναι: the εἶδος of the house is the way a master-builder builds it (p. 26). Also functions as the character of πέρας: the genuine "look" of a being that has a range (*Met.* Δ17, p. 28).
- **Type**: ontological
- **Dependencies**: οὐσία, πέρας
- **Function**: setup / bridge
- **Pages**: 24, 26, 28

### C8. πέρας
- **Greek form**: πέρας
- **Definition (as Heidegger uses it)**: Limit; the ἔσχατον ("the outermost aspect, outside of which, at first, nothing more of the matter is to be found," p. 28, citing *Met.* Δ17). A fundamental character of the being-there of beings. Linked to εἶδος, τέλος, and the οὗ ἕνεκα. "Limit is the having-of-limits only because it is the having-of-limits of the matter, the πρᾶγμα determined in its limits" (p. 28). The basic character of the "there" that ὁρισμός addresses.
- **Type**: ontological
- **Dependencies**: οὐσία, εἶδος, τέλος
- **Function**: positive_proposal -- the concept that ties ὁρισμός to the structure of being-there
- **Pages**: 23, 28-29

### C9. τέλος
- **Greek form**: τέλος
- **Definition (as Heidegger uses it)**: "End" in the sense of completeness, not aim or purpose. Connected to κίνησις and πρᾶξις as a πέρας: "completeness is a πέρας such that 'movement and action go toward it'" (p. 28, citing *Met.* Δ17). Linked to the οὗ ἕνεκα ("for-the-sake-of-which"). One of the two fundamental characters of the "there" for the Greeks (the other being presence).
- **Type**: ontological
- **Dependencies**: πέρας, κίνησις
- **Function**: positive_proposal
- **Pages**: 26, 28

### C10. αἴσθησις
- **Greek form**: αἴσθησις
- **Definition (as Heidegger uses it)**: The natural mode of perceiving beings -- not "sensation" or "sense-data" in the modern epistemological sense but "the 'perceiving' of beings in the natural mode, a perceiving distinguished by the fact that the senses are implicated in it by providing its access" (p. 21). The mode through which beings that have the character of οὐσία are accessed. It is "the natural mode of seeing and speaking about things such as trees and the moon" (p. 21).
- **Type**: ontological / existential
- **Dependencies**: οὐσία (as beings)
- **Function**: setup -- establishes the access-mode for beings in their primary sense
- **Pages**: 21-22

### C11. παρουσία / ἀπουσία
- **Greek form**: παρουσία / ἀπουσία
- **Definition (as Heidegger uses it)**: παρουσία = "being-present"; οὐσία is its abbreviation. ἀπουσία = "absence," which is "not simply nothing but something there, although there as a lack" (p. 25). ἀπουσία is the ontological basis for the basic category of στέρησις (privation). Together with completeness (Fertigsein), presence constitutes the two fundamental characters of the "there" for the Greeks.
- **Type**: ontological
- **Dependencies**: οὐσία
- **Function**: positive_proposal
- **Pages**: 25

### C12. σῶμα
- **Greek form**: σῶμα
- **Definition (as Heidegger uses it)**: Corporeality understood not as materiality but as "obtrusiveness" -- the characteristic of a being that is there, at my disposal, present to me. Later: "your σῶμα" = "you yourself"; σῶμα = "slave," "prisoner" -- a being that belongs to me (p. 21). Beings that show themselves openly as σώματα include bodily things, animals, trees, earth, water, air, the heavens.
- **Type**: ontological
- **Dependencies**: οὐσία (as beings)
- **Function**: example / illustrative
- **Pages**: 21

### C13. μόριον ἐνυπάρχον
- **Greek form**: μόριον ἐνυπάρχον
- **Definition (as Heidegger uses it)**: "The circumscribed"; the being-character designated by ὁρίζον. The surface of a body, the line of a surface -- limit as constituting being-there. "Body is circumscribed by the being-character of μόριον ἐνυπάρχον, that is, beings are determined in their being" (p. 23). Because circumscription determines beings in their being, some (Pythagoreans, Platonists) identified number as the genuine οὐσία.
- **Type**: ontological
- **Dependencies**: οὐσία, πέρας
- **Function**: setup
- **Pages**: 23, 25

### C14. ζῷον λόγον ἔχον
- **Greek form**: ζῷον λόγον ἔχον
- **Definition (as Heidegger uses it)**: The determination of the human being as a "living being" that has speech -- where "living being" is a category of being (not a modern biological concept) and ἔχειν means ἄγειν ("to conduct"), a drive (ὁρμή) that belongs to the genuine drive of being. Life is being-in-a-world; speaking is its basic mode.
- **Type**: ontological / existential
- **Dependencies**: λόγος, ψυχή
- **Function**: setup
- **Pages**: 16

### C15. συγκεχυμένον
- **Greek form**: συγκεχυμένον
- **Definition (as Heidegger uses it)**: "Obscured," "covered over," "unarticulated" -- the way the world is primarily given in its immediacy (*Phys.* A1, 184 a 22). Ὁρισμός as "a particular opening of the eyes" overcomes this initial obscuredness.
- **Type**: methodological / ontological
- **Dependencies**: none (characterizes the initial state that ὁρισμός addresses)
- **Function**: setup -- establishes the problem that ὁρισμός solves
- **Pages**: 26

### C16. κοινά / κείμενον
- **Greek form**: κοινά / κείμενον
- **Definition (as Heidegger uses it)**: The expressed (κείμενον) "lies fixed," is available to others (κοινά), belongs to the common intelligibility. Once a word is expressed, it "no longer belongs to me" but becomes something that "belongs to everyone" (p. 16). Constitutes the averageness of understanding in which one grows up and lives.
- **Type**: existential
- **Dependencies**: λόγος
- **Function**: setup -- establishes the public, average character of language
- **Pages**: 16

## Section 4: Main Positions & Arguments

### U-FP1b-P1: λόγος as the basic mode of the being of life
- **Claim**: Speaking (λόγος) is the basic determination of the being of the human being; it is the "basic mode of the being of life, namely, of being-in-a-world" (p. 16), and where speaking stops, we speak of death.
- **Premises**: (1) The human being is determined as ζῷον λόγον ἔχον. (2) Speaking is not an occasional occurrence but constitutive self-expressing and speaking-with-others. (3) Life is a "how," a category of being, not a biological concept. (4) Animals and humans express themselves reciprocally; where expression ceases, life ceases.
- **Conclusion**: λόγος determines the being of human being as being-in-a-world.
- **Aristotelian texts**: *Met.* Z 15, 1040 a 11 (κεῖμενα κοινά); *Met.* Δ 23, 1023 a 8 sq. (ἔχειν as ἄγειν).
- **Status**: core_course_thesis

### U-FP1b-P2: Οὐσία's ambiguity as index of the matter
- **Claim**: The ambiguity of οὐσία is not a defect of Aristotle's thought but an index of the matter itself: it is the third type of ambiguity (legitimate familiarity), where the matter "demands, from out of itself, the same expression but with various meanings" (p. 17).
- **Premises**: (1) Not all ambiguity is confusion or insensitivity. (2) Some matters genuinely demand multifarious meaning. (3) Aristotle shows his "instinct for the matter" by not systematizing away this ambiguity. (4) Multifariousness of meaning is an "index of variation" in the phenomenon.
- **Conclusion**: The multiple senses of οὐσία reflect the structure of being itself, not conceptual sloppiness.
- **Aristotelian texts**: *Met.* Δ (Book Delta generally, on ambiguity of basic concepts).
- **Status**: core_course_thesis

### U-FP1b-P3: Customary meaning contains terminological meaning in germ
- **Claim**: The customary meaning of οὐσία (property, possessions, estate) already contains the terminological meaning; both refer to "a being in the how of its being" (p. 19). The customary meaning exists "constantly and simultaneously alongside the terminological meaning" (p. 18).
- **Premises**: (1) Customary οὐσία designates definite beings (possessions, household goods) that are there "for me in an emphatic way." (2) This already intends the how of being as "being there in the manner of being-available." (3) The terminological meaning makes thematic what was only co-intended.
- **Conclusion**: From the outset, "being" for the Greeks means "being there" (Da-sein); terminological philosophy arises from and remains rooted in pre-theoretical experience.
- **Aristotelian texts**: implicit (Heidegger's etymological-phenomenological reconstruction).
- **Status**: core_course_thesis

### U-FP1b-P4: The three senses of οὐσία
- **Claim**: Οὐσία has three fundamental, interlocking senses: (1) beings themselves (σώματα, accessed through αἴσθησις), (2) the being of beings (five being-characters from *Met.* Δ8), (3) being-there (the characters of the "there": presence and completeness).
- **Premises**: (1) Οὐσία is said of various beings since they have various being-characters (*Met.* Z 2). (2) The being-characters enumerated in *Met.* Δ8 articulate the how of being. (3) These being-characters are themselves characters of the "there" -- they determine what it means for something to be there.
- **Conclusion**: Every concept of being has a "specific double-character to its meaning" (p. 20): it refers to both beings and the being of beings, and this duality is grounded in the there.
- **Aristotelian texts**: *Met.* Z 2, 1028 b 8 sq.; *Met.* Δ 8, 1017 b 10 sqq.; *Met.* H 1, 1042 a 6; *Met.* Z 3, 1029 a 33 sq.
- **Status**: core_course_thesis

### U-FP1b-P5: Ψυχή as being-in-the-world
- **Claim**: Ψυχή is a being-character (αἴτιον ἐνυπάρχον) that constitutes the specific being of a living thing as being-in-the-world, not a substance in any Cartesian or scholastic sense.
- **Premises**: (1) The soul is αἴτιον ἐνυπάρχον: "that which is also at hand therein" in a being (*Met.* Δ 8, 1017 b 15-16). (2) A living thing's being-there is characterized by being-in-the-world: "It is by having an orientation. The human being is there in such a way that he is in the world" (p. 25). (3) Ψυχή comprises being as ὑποκείμενον within itself.
- **Conclusion**: The soul is a mode of being, not a thing; it constitutes the living thing's specific way of being-in-the-world.
- **Aristotelian texts**: *Met.* Δ 8, 1017 b 15-16; *De Anima* III (Γ 2, 427 a 17 sq.).
- **Status**: core_course_thesis

### U-FP1b-P6: Presence and completeness as the characters of the there
- **Claim**: The two fundamental characters of the "there" for the Greeks are presence (παρουσία/Anwesenheit) and being-complete/completedness (Fertigsein/τέλος).
- **Premises**: (1) Οὐσία abbreviates παρουσία (being-present). (2) The five being-characters are "characters of the there" -- they articulate how something is *there*. (3) Being-there is synthesized as primarily (1) presence, (2) completedness.
- **Conclusion**: The Greek understanding of being is fundamentally structured by presence and completeness.
- **Aristotelian texts**: *Met.* Δ 8 (the enumeration as a whole).
- **Status**: core_course_thesis

### U-FP1b-P7: Ὁρισμός as definite being-in-the-world
- **Claim**: Ὁρισμός is not a formal-logical operation but "a definite being-in-the-world, which meets the world that is there in its genuine there-character, that addresses it in its genuine being" (p. 29). It is the genuine mode of entry into beings.
- **Premises**: (1) Ὁρισμός is a specific mode of λόγος that addresses beings with regard to their completeness as present. (2) It addresses τὸ τί ἦν εἶναι. (3) It encounters beings in the character of their πέρας (limit). (4) Conceptuality is "an issue of being-there in a decisive sense."
- **Conclusion**: Definition is an existential comportment rooted in Dasein's resolute speaking to the world, not a merely technical or logical procedure.
- **Aristotelian texts**: *Met.* Z 3; *Phys.* A 1, 184 a 22 sqq.; *Met.* Δ 17, 1022 a 4 sqq.
- **Status**: core_course_thesis

### U-FP1b-P8: The world as initially συγκεχυμένον
- **Claim**: The world is primarily given in its immediacy as συγκεχυμένον ("obscured," "covered over," "unarticulated"), and ὁρισμός requires "a particular organizing, a particular opening of the eyes" to see what is there in its being (p. 26).
- **Premises**: (1) *Physics* A1 (184 a 22): the word συγκεχυμένον characterizes the initial givenness of the world. (2) The everyday mode is average, undifferentiated. (3) Genuine articulation requires effort -- a move from the indeterminate to the determinate.
- **Conclusion**: Ὁρισμός overcomes the initial unarticulated state of the world through a distinctive mode of speaking.
- **Aristotelian texts**: *Phys.* A 1, 184 a 22.
- **Status**: methodological

### U-FP1b-P9: Πέρας as the fundamental character of the there
- **Claim**: Πέρας (limit) is the fundamental character of the there; ὁρισμός addresses beings in their limitedness, and the maxim εἰς ἄπειρον ἰέναι is to be avoided because it exhibits "a completely different sense of being-there" (p. 29).
- **Premises**: (1) *Met.* Δ 17: πέρας is the ἔσχατον, linked to εἶδος, τέλος, and οὗ ἕνεκα. (2) Limit is the having-of-limits of the matter itself (πρᾶγμα). (3) Being is interpreted through being-there, and being-there is characterized by finitude/limitedness. (4) Going toward the unlimited (ἄπειρον) is "a going toward something that no longer is" in the sense relevant to Greek ontology.
- **Conclusion**: The Greek understanding of being as presence and completeness requires finitude; the unlimited falls outside being properly understood.
- **Aristotelian texts**: *Met.* Δ 17, 1022 a 4 sqq.; *Phys.* Θ 5, 256 a 29.
- **Status**: core_course_thesis

### U-FP1b-P10: Conceptuality as issue of being-there
- **Claim**: "Conceptuality is no arbitrary matter, but rather an issue of being-there in a decisive sense, insofar as it has resolved radically to speak to the world -- to question and to research" (p. 29).
- **Premises**: (1) Ὁρισμός is a definite being-in-the-world. (2) It requires resolute address of the world in its genuine there-character. (3) The "there" is not merely given but must be explicitly encountered through a distinctive mode of λόγος.
- **Conclusion**: The ground of conceptuality is existential, not logical or epistemological.
- **Aristotelian texts**: implicit (Heidegger's synthesis of the foregoing).
- **Status**: core_course_thesis

### U-FP1b-P11: Regression to customary meaning as overhearing natural being-there
- **Claim**: The return to the customary meaning of οὐσία is "nothing other than the overhearing of the speaking of natural being-there to its world" (p. 30) -- a methodological principle of retrieving philosophical terminology from its pre-theoretical ground.
- **Premises**: (1) Terminological meaning arises from customary meaning. (2) The customary meaning preserves the natural intelligibility of being-there. (3) Philosophical investigation must listen to this natural speaking.
- **Conclusion**: Genuine philosophical method consists in overhearing the pre-theoretical self-communication of Dasein about the beings it encounters.
- **Aristotelian texts**: implicit.
- **Status**: methodological

### U-FP1b-P12: Aristotle's counter-thrust to Platonic philosophy
- **Claim**: Aristotle's methodological stance of proceeding from the immediately given is "programmatically the genuine counter-thrust to Platonic philosophy" (p. 27): one must have "ground under one's feet" rather than speculating from a concept of being held in fantasy.
- **Premises**: (1) *Met.* Z 3: learning proceeds from what is less familiar to what is more familiar. (2) *Phys.* A 1: proceed from the καθόλου to the καθ᾽ ἕκαστα. (3) Plato's method, by contrast, begins with Ideas/Forms as if they were immediately available.
- **Conclusion**: Aristotle's methodology is grounded in immediate self-evidence; Plato's is not.
- **Aristotelian texts**: *Met.* Z 3, 1029 b 3 sqq.; *Phys.* A 1, 184 a 23 sqq.
- **Status**: methodological

### U-FP1b-P13: The ἦν in τὸ τί ἦν εἶναι as descent/history
- **Claim**: The imperfect ἦν in τὸ τί ἦν εἶναι indicates that a being is genuinely seen when seen with respect to "what it was already, from which it stems in its being, with respect to its descent, its having come into being there" (pp. 23-24). A being is seen genuinely "in its history, coming from out of its history into being" (p. 23).
- **Premises**: (1) τὸ τί ἦν εἶναι contains the determination of the ἦν. (2) This points to descent and origin. (3) The εἶδος of the house, for instance, is the way a master-builder builds it -- the house in its coming-to-be.
- **Conclusion**: The what-being of a thing includes its genesis, its having-come-into-being.
- **Aristotelian texts**: *Met.* Δ 8, 1017 b 21 sq.
- **Status**: local_support [INTERP-high: Heidegger's emphasis on "historicity" here may be more proto-BT than straightforwardly Aristotelian; standard readings treat the ἦν as a grammatical idiom ("philosophic imperfect") rather than a substantive reference to temporal descent.]

### U-FP1b-P14: The expressed as common intelligibility
- **Claim**: The expressed (τὸ κείμενον) belongs to the common intelligibility (κοινά), constituting an averageness of understanding; once a word is expressed, it "no longer belongs to me" and "harbors the possibility of being used up, of being shoved into the common intelligibility" (p. 16).
- **Premises**: (1) Speaking is essentially self-expressing and speaking-with-others. (2) What is expressed becomes fixed, available to all. (3) Language operates in the averageness of common understanding.
- **Conclusion**: Language has an inherent tendency toward leveling and averageness.
- **Aristotelian texts**: *Met.* Z 15, 1040 a 11 (κεῖμενα κοινά).
- **Status**: local_support

## Section 4a: Key Interlocutors

### Aristotle
- **Role**: Primary source -- Heidegger is interpreting Aristotle's basic concepts throughout.
- **Key borrowings**: The entire conceptual apparatus: οὐσία, λόγος, ὁρισμός, the five being-characters of *Met.* Δ8, the methodological principle of proceeding from the customary to the terminological (*Phys.* A1, *Met.* Z3), the determination of the human being as ζῷον λόγον ἔχον, the concept of πέρας (*Met.* Δ17).
- **Key departures**: Heidegger reads οὐσία as "being-there" (Da-sein) rather than "substance"; reads the five being-characters as "characters of the there"; interprets the ἦν in τὸ τί ἦν εἶναι as indicating historicity/descent; reads Aristotle's methodology as an existential counter-thrust to Plato rather than merely a logical or dialectical procedure.
- **Aristotelian texts cited**: *Met.* Z 2, Z 3, Z 15; *Met.* Δ 8, Δ 17, Δ 23; *Met.* H 1; *Phys.* A 1; *Phys.* Θ 5; *De Anima* III (Γ 2); *Eth. Nic.* (only at the very end, p. 31, transitioning to Chapter 2).

### Plato / Platonists
- **Role**: Negative foil.
- **Key borrowings**: None explicit in this unit.
- **Key departures**: Aristotle's methodological stance is "the genuine counter-thrust to Platonic philosophy" (p. 27); Plato speculates from a concept of being "held in fantasy." The Pythagoreans and Platonists erroneously identified number as the genuine οὐσία based on the being-character of μόριον ἐνυπάρχον (limit/circumscription) (p. 23).
- **Aristotelian texts cited**: None by Plato directly; the anti-Platonic claim is Heidegger's framing of *Met.* Z 3 and *Phys.* A 1.

### Scholastic tradition
- **Role**: Negative foil (briefly).
- **Key borrowings**: None.
- **Key departures**: The four scholastic senses of "concept" (notio, intentio, conceptus, species) and the translation of οὐσία as "essence" (essentia) are presented as obscuring the indigenous Greek meaning. Heidegger criticizes the scholastic "customary meaning" of these designations (p. 15) and the later translation of οὐσία as "essence" (p. 29).
- **Aristotelian texts cited**: N/A.

## Section 5: Tensions & Open Questions

### T1. Existentialization of Aristotle's categories
The most pervasive tension in U-FP1b is whether translating Aristotelian terms into the idiom of "being-there," "being-in-the-world," and "the there" genuinely recovers Aristotle's meaning or retrojects Heidegger's emerging existential analytic onto the Greek text. When Heidegger claims that οὐσία in its customary sense already means "being-there" and that the five being-characters of *Met.* Δ8 are "characters of the there," he performs a reading that goes beyond anything explicit in Aristotle. [INTERP-high] (Relates to: U-FP1b-P3, U-FP1b-P4, U-FP1b-P6, C2)

### T2. Ordering of the three senses of οὐσία
Heidegger presents the three senses (beings, being, being-there) as ordered: the investigation of beings leads to being-characters, which leads to characters of the there. But the direction of this ordering is ambiguous: is it (a) an analytic progression within Aristotle's text, (b) a phenomenological reduction from the ontic to the ontological, or (c) a genealogical claim about the genesis of the concept from everyday experience? Heidegger's language shifts between these registers without marking the transitions. [INTERP-low: The ambiguity may be deliberate, reflecting Heidegger's conviction that the three senses are co-originary.] (Relates to: U-FP1b-P4, C2)

### T3. The "counter-thrust to Platonic philosophy"
Heidegger frames Aristotle's methodology as programmatically anti-Platonic (p. 27). But Aristotle does not formulate *Physics* A1 as an explicit attack on Plato, and Heidegger's characterization of Platonic philosophy as "fantasy" and "speculation" is tendentious. [INTERP-high: This reflects Heidegger's larger project of retrieving Aristotle against the Platonic tradition, not a neutral reading of either thinker.] (Relates to: U-FP1b-P12)

### T4. The ἦν as "historicity"
Heidegger interprets the imperfect tense (ἦν) in τὸ τί ἦν εἶναι as indicating that a being is genuinely seen in its "history," its "descent" (pp. 23-24). This is philologically creative: standard readings treat the ἦν as a grammatical idiom (the "philosophic imperfect") rather than a substantive reference to temporal descent. [INTERP-high: This is a signature Heideggerian interpretive move that anticipates the existential analytic of historicity and strains what the Aristotelian text warrants.] (Relates to: U-FP1b-P13, C6)

### T5. Πέρας and the avoidance of the infinite
Heidegger treats the maxim εἰς ἄπειρον ἰέναι as exhibiting "a completely different sense of being-there" (p. 29) -- reading the avoidance of infinite regress as an *ontological* commitment to finitude rather than merely a methodological or logical desideratum. [INTERP-low: This is defensible as an interpretation of the function the maxim plays in Aristotle's physics, even if it exceeds Aristotle's explicit formulation.] (Relates to: U-FP1b-P9, C8)

### T6. Ψυχή and being-in-the-world
Heidegger's claim that ψυχή constitutes the specific being of a living thing "as being-in-the-world" (p. 22) effectively reads Aristotelian hylomorphic psychology through the lens of Heidegger's own concept of In-der-Welt-sein. Aristotle speaks of the soul as the form/actuality of the living body, not as "being-in-the-world" in any Heideggerian sense. The question is whether Heidegger's translation captures a genuine structural parallel or imports a foreign framework. [INTERP-low: The parallel is not arbitrary -- Aristotle's ψυχή does determine the living thing's mode of being, and Heidegger is drawing out this implication.] (Relates to: U-FP1b-P5, C5)
