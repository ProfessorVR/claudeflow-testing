## 2. Hierarchical Outline

### §15. Doxa (NE Z10, Gamma 4)

**a) Demarcation of doxa in Contrast with Seeking (zetesis), Knowing (episteme), and Presenting-Itself (phantasia)** (pp. 93-94)
- 1. doxa vs. zetesis (bouleuesthai): doxa is "not a seeking" but rather a phasis, a yes-saying, a coming-first-to-a-view; in zetesis, one is on the way to the telos of a doxa, but doxa itself stands at the end of seeing (p. 93)
- 2. doxa vs. episteme: episteme is orthotes about what cannot be otherwise; doxa "must possess orthotes, being-directed toward aletheia" but can also be false -- it has the possibility of pseudos (p. 93)
- 3. doxa vs. phantasia: phantasia is simple "having-present" without perceiving directly; doxa has the possibility of truth or falsity *in itself* (De Anima Gamma 3), while phantasia is only from without; animals possess phantasia too, but doxa only belongs to beings with logos (pp. 93-94)
- The aim of logoi rhetorikon is cultivation of pisteuein, which belongs to doxa; a pistis, a phasis, accompanies doxa itself; animals have no doxa since they have no logos (p. 94)

**b) Making-Present the Context for the Treatment of doxa** (pp. 94-95)
- doxa as the mode of being-in-a-world in which one is oriented toward beings *that can also be otherwise* (endechomenon allos) -- the possibility of *revisability* (p. 94)
- episteme: no revision; with doxa: revision is assumed of itself (p. 94)
- doxa as the basic possibility of *being-against-one-another* in being-with-one-another (p. 94)
- doxa is the mode of everydayness, "living there in its everydayness" -- not the way of science (p. 94)
- Being-in-the-world as the basic structure: koinonia, logos as deloun ("making-manifest"), the world as uncovered in a definite familiarity (pp. 94-95)
- Rhetoric as "the interpretation of being-there with regard to the basic possibility of speaking-with-one-another" (p. 95)
- Three pisteis analogous to three aspects of speaking: (1) speaking *about* something (peri tinos deloun, deiknunai); (2) speaking *to* someone (pros tina -- akouon, pathos); (3) speaking-being itself (legon, ethos) (p. 95)
- The being under discussion is endechomenon kai allos echein -- that which can be otherwise; the telos of praxis as telos kata ton kairon, "in the moment" (p. 95)

**c) Repetition and Continuation of the Demarcation of doxa: doxa and Being-Resolved (proairesis)** (pp. 96-101)
- Restatement of three contrasts: (1) bouleuesthai (=zetesis), (2) episteme, (3) phantasia (pp. 96-97)
- 4. doxa vs. proairesis (NE Gamma 4): "surprising" juxtaposition -- Plato had already treated proairesis as a kind of doxa (p. 97)
- Five phenomena share the character of *being-after something* (having-in-advance): proairesis, epithumia, thumos, boulesis, doxa (pp. 97-98)
- Grouping principle: all five have concrete content of having-in-advance; they are "being-for" something (p. 98)
- epithumia and thumos belong to aloga (non-speaking beings); only proairesis involves genuine resolution through deliberation (p. 98)
- akrates acts on epithumon without resolute acting; enkrates acts on proairesis resolutely (p. 98)
- Seven differences between proairesis and doxa (pp. 99-101):
  1. Directed toward different regions: proairesis toward what *I can accomplish* (sympheron); doxa also toward beings *that are always such as they are* (aei) (pp. 99-100)
  2. doxa goes after alethes and pseudes; proairesis depends on *how it should be done* (prakton agathon) (p. 100)
  3. doxa not otherwise determined by ethos ("having-a-definite-view about a matter is not an 'ethical' determination"); in proairesis the manner of being-resolved *is decisive for my being* (p. 100)
  4. Corresponding to their being-character: doxa toward alethes; proairesis toward prakton (the manner and mode in which beings *are there*) (p. 100)
  5. Genuineness: doxa depends on orthotes (approaching the alethes); proairesis depends on being *suitably reckoned* (p. 100)
  6. Standing toward knowing: doxa relates to what one does *not know precisely* (concealed); proairesis relates to "what we know most of all" (pp. 100-101)
  7. One can have the best view and still be resolved toward a kakon; doxa and proairesis are not conflated (p. 101)

**d) The Character of doxa as the Orientedness of Average Being-with-One-Another-in-the-World** (pp. 101-103)
- doxa as "the genuine discoveredness of being-with-one-another-in-the-world" -- the world is there for us as what-is-with-one-another in discoveredness (p. 101)
- The realm of doxa is panta: not only prakta but the entire world -- the moon, the stars, what is aei (p. 101)
- doxa has the character of *going-along with the way the world initially shows itself*, the moment of trust in the immediate aspect (p. 102)
- Thales' opinion that hudor is the proton illustrates this trust: "that which initially shows itself is taken as what the world initially is" (p. 102)
- *Who* possesses doxa is co-decisive: the matter cannot speak purely for itself; it speaks for the one who has the view, the phasis (p. 102)
- doxa reaches *characteristic authority and stubbornness*: repeating opinions, not investigating *what* but *who* said it; behind doxa stand "peculiarly indefinite" others (p. 102)
- doxa is the genuine orientedness of *average* being-with-one-another: one does not investigate the world but takes it as others say (p. 102)
- doxa is simultaneously *basis, source, and motive* for discoursing-with-one-another, negotiating-with-one-another; it has *the authority and guidance of being-with-one-another in the world* (p. 102)
- doxa is also the basis for the mode of grasping beings that we designate as episteme, as theorein (p. 103)

**e) Doxa as the Basis of Theoretical Negotiating** (pp. 103-108)

**alpha. Pre-given (protasis) and Project (problema) as From-Which and About-Which of Theoretical Negotiating** (pp. 103-107)
- doxa controls theoretical legein -- logos in the sense of dialegesthai (treating, explicating) (p. 103)
- Topics A4: logoi and sullogismoi arise from doxa; the protasis (basis of discourse) and the problema (thematic of discourse) are endoxa, expressions of doxa (p. 103)
- aletheia as how the individual possibilities within doxa are negotiated: that over which the view has authority is apt for discussion (p. 104)
- protasis is "pre-given," what is put forward in advance; problema is from proballein, "to project," the genuine thematic (p. 104)
- protasis example: "Is the definition of the human being a bipedal living thing, the definition of the human being?" -- a claim for agreement (p. 104)
- problema example: "Is the determination ... the definition of the human being or not?" -- opened for investigation; requires a fully determinate, clear definition (p. 104)
- thesis: a characteristic problema, a doxa paradoxos -- not an arbitrary doxa but one belonging to those "entrusted with philosophia" (p. 106)
- thesis vs. problema: thesis speaks explicitly against the dominant opinion (e.g., Heraclitus, Antisthenes, Melissus); many problemata do not oppose dominant opinion in a pointed way (p. 106)

**beta. Inability-to-Get-Through (aporia) as the Topic of Theoretical Negotiating** (pp. 107-108)
- Topic of dialegesthai: "that which can, in general, be a possible *topic* for negotiation" (p. 107)
- logou deomenon: that which requires logos -- not settled by reprimand (kolaseos) or direct perception (aistheseos); the topic must have an aporia in itself (p. 107)
- aporia in Metaphysics B1: "inability-to-get-through" in the course of exhibiting; a series of expressions -- aporein, euporein, diaporein, proaporein (p. 108)
- porein meant "running, going" in the sense of exhibiting; aporein: "to not get through" in the course of exhibiting; the alpha-privative shows that one must generally porein (p. 108)
- aporein is fulfilled by calling upon prevalent opinions (doxai) about a matter and disclosing characteristic characteristics (p. 108)
- The telos of aporein is euporein: getting-through-well; aporia is not a telos in itself but at the service of a determinate getting-through (p. 108)
- aporia as the positive sense of investigation: one must go through a preliminary inability-to-get-through to exhibit the telos of the investigation (p. 108)
- aporia has the sense of cultivation of an interrogatory stance of scientific research (p. 108)
- Classic examples: Physics I (Ch. 2-9), Metaphysics B (Ch. 2, 1-6): aporiai of being qua being (p. 108)

### §16. Ethos and Pathos as Pisteis (Rhetoric B1, NE B4)

**a) Theoretical and Practical Negotiating** (pp. 109-110)
- Practical deliberation: topic of everyday discourse (assembly, court) is bouleuesthai about things that are always already objects of deliberation in the polis; concerns prakton, endoxon (p. 109)
- In contrast to dialegesthai: the topic here is not a theoretical fact but something that "differs according to circumstances of being-there, change in mood, change of view" (p. 110)
- lambanein tas protaseis is something different here: alongside the premise, the mood of those spoken to, the situation at each moment, the manner and mode in which one stands toward the matter must be placed in view (p. 110)
- Three factors: (1) ethos, the "comportment" of the speaker; (2) pathos, the "disposition" of the hearers; (3) the matter itself (p. 110)

**b) Ethos as pistis** (pp. 111-113)
- ethos and pathe are constitutive of legein itself (p. 111)
- ethos: "the manner the speaker offers himself to his hearers in discourse" -- how ethos acquires the possibility of co-speaking, of co-mattering (p. 111)
- Three aspects of cultivating ethos: (1) phronesis ("looking around"); (2) arete ("seriousness"); (3) eunoia ("good attitude," "good will") (p. 111)
- Aristotle examines via the *opposite course* -- how a speaker *misleads* -- to exhibit the three aspects (p. 112):
  1. Missing orthotes: the speaker does not form views "in the right manner" (ouk orthos doxazon) -- no right perspective on the matter; hearer notices the flaw, pistis is lost (p. 112)
  2. Missing phronesis proper: the speaker *can* look around, but is *not willing to say* what he thinks; screens his position; hearer notices "he is not properly serious" and withdraws trust (p. 112)
  3. Missing eunoia: the speaker is serious and looks around, but *is deficient in the requisite good will*; recommends what he believes is sympheron but withdraws the best counsel for lack of good will (p. 112)
- A speaker who shows himself out of good will, with seriousness, and looking around "will thus have real trust -- he will himself be a pistis in his logos" (p. 113)
- phronesis and arete already treated in Rhetoric A9; the third aspect, eunoia, is treated in the context of analyzing the pathe (p. 113)

**c) Pathos as pistis** (pp. 113-115)
- pathos has three basic meanings: (1) average, immediate: "variable condition"; (2) specifically ontological: pathos in connection with paschein ("suffering") -- important for understanding kinesis; (3) resulting: "variable condition in relation to a definite concrete context, a definite being-region of life: *passion*" (p. 113)
- Sense (3) is the topic of the Rhetoric and the Poetics (p. 113)
- NE B4: three things "come to be in the soul" (ginomena en te psyche): pathe, dunameis, hexeis (p. 113)
- pathe are co-given with hedone and lupe (p. 113)
- hexis: "the manner and mode in which we are in such a pathos" -- the pos echomen pros ta pathe, "how we carry ourselves with such a pathos"; pathos as a determinate losing-one's-composure (p. 114)
- dunameis: Aristotle characterizes as phusei on -- co-given possibilities of factical being-there (being enraged, being sad, being happy, hating); these are also ginomena en te psyche (p. 114)
- The being-co-given of pathe as ginomena is important: pathe as *modes of being*, as *modes of becoming*, relating to being-in-a-world (p. 114)
- hexis and the possible connection of pathe to hexis: the analysis of pathe will show "how what is designated as pathos *defines being-in-the-world in a fundamental sense*" (p. 114)
- ethos of the speaker must satisfy arete, phronesis, and eunoia; in the Poetics: ethos "makes manifest, at the moment, the being-resolved of the speaker" (p. 114)
- The right frame of mind (dianoia) is necessary for discourse; setting down these conditions is not exhausted "up to now" in scientific and philosophical accounts (pp. 114-115)
- Three constitutive aspects of pathe from Rhetoric's first determination (B1): (1) metaballontes -- "we change" from one disposition to another, shifting pros tas kriseis; (2) "we differentiate ourselves" before the change -- hearer's task is "to take a position, to form a view"; (3) the pathe are co-given (hois hepetai) with lupe and hedone -- not "following" but a "being-disposed-as-higher-or-lower" of the being-there in question (p. 115)
- The manner and mode of being in a frame of mind constitutes how we stand with respect to matters, "how we see them, how extensively and in what respects" (p. 115)
- Coming-out-of-one-definite-frame-of-mind-into-another relates primarily to the mode of taking-a-position toward the world, of being-in-the-world (p. 115)
- The world is there as endechomenon allos with determinations of "more or less"; the kairos is "the entirety of circumstances, the how, when, whither, and about which" (p. 115)

## 4. Main Positions

### U-FP3b-P1: Doxa is the genuine discoveredness of average being-with-one-another-in-the-world
Heidegger's central claim in §15d is that doxa is not mere "opinion" in a deficient sense but the *genuine* mode in which the world is disclosed for average, everyday being-with-one-another. "The world is there for us as what-is-with-one-another in discoveredness, insofar as we live in doxa" (p. 101). Doxa is the basis, source, and motive for discoursing-with-one-another; it has "the authority and guidance of being-with-one-another in the world" (p. 102). It is simultaneously oriented toward prakta and toward the entire world (panta), including the moon, the stars, what is aei (p. 101).
- **Source**: pp. 101-103
- **Significance**: Rehabilitates doxa from its Platonic degradation; shows it as constitutive of Dasein's worldly orientation rather than a deficient mode of episteme

### U-FP3b-P2: Doxa is demarcated through four contrasts that reveal its ontological specificity
The four-fold demarcation (zetesis, episteme, phantasia, proairesis) is not merely classificatory but reveals the ontological place of doxa. Key structural features: (a) doxa is a phasis (yes-saying), not a seeking; (b) it has the possibility of pseudos, unlike episteme; (c) it requires logos, unlike phantasia; (d) it is not determined by ethos in the way proairesis is -- "having-a-definite-view about a matter is not an 'ethical' determination" (p. 100). This last contrast yields the crucial distinction: doxa points to a certain *indifference with relation to being*, whereas proairesis is *decisive for my being* (p. 100).
- **Source**: pp. 93-94, 96-101 (NE Z10, Gamma 4, De Anima Gamma 3)
- **Significance**: Shows that doxa occupies a unique ontological position -- oriented toward alethes but open to pseudos, requiring logos but not ethos-determined

### U-FP3b-P3: Doxa is characterized by revisability (endechomenon allos) and the possibility of being-against-one-another
"Endoxon is the manner of being-oriented in which one is oriented toward beings *that can also be otherwise*" (p. 94). This revisability is constitutive of doxa itself, not an accidental feature. From this follows the basic possibility of *speaking-against-one-another* (being-against-one-another) in being-with-one-another, "and that one has this view and another has that view, since beings can be otherwise" (p. 94). [INTERP-low]: This structure anticipates the analysis of das Man and idle talk in BT, though Heidegger does not say so here.
- **Source**: pp. 94, 101
- **Significance**: The "can be otherwise" character grounds both the discursive character of the polis and the possibility of dialectic

### U-FP3b-P4: Doxa grounds theoretical negotiating through protasis and problema
Both protasis (the "pre-given" basis of discourse, requesting agreement) and problema (the "project" that opens something for investigation) are endoxa -- "expressings that stand in some sort of connection with doxa and maintain themselves in relation to it" (p. 103). Topics A4 establishes that logoi and sullogismoi arise from doxa: "the wherefrom, on the basis of which discourse is, and that about which there is discourse, are numerically equal and the same" (p. 103). The aporia -- the inability-to-get-through that constitutes the genuine topic of theoretical negotiation -- is itself a mode of porein (exhibiting) in the service of euporein (getting-through-well) (pp. 107-108).
- **Source**: pp. 103-108 (Topics A4, A10-11; Metaphysics B1)
- **Significance**: Demonstrates that logic (sullogismos) is not accidental but rooted in basic phenomena of being-there

### U-FP3b-P5: The characteristic authority and stubbornness of doxa
Behind the authority of doxa stand "peculiarly indefinite" other people: "repeating the opinions to others. What is said is not decisive, but rather that it is *he* who said it" (p. 102). Heidegger identifies a structure of stubbornness in doxa: the view gains force not from investigation of the matter but from the indefinite authority of the one who holds it. This is the "characteristic force" found in doxa itself. [INTERP-low]: This analysis maps onto the BT account of das Man (the "they") and Gerede (idle talk), though Heidegger does not explicitly make the connection here.
- **Source**: p. 102
- **Significance**: The "who" dimension of doxa (rather than the "what") anticipates the analysis of publicness and the dictatorship of the "they"

### U-FP3b-P6: Rhetoric's three pisteis correspond to three aspects of speaking-about-something
Heidegger identifies a structural parallel: (1) speaking *about* something (peri tinos deloun) corresponds to the matter-pistis; (2) speaking *to* someone (pros tina -- the akouon) corresponds to pathos; (3) speaking-being itself (legon) corresponds to ethos (p. 95). All three are directed toward that which "could be otherwise" (endechomenon allos echein), and the telos is kata ton kairon -- "in the moment" (p. 95).
- **Source**: p. 95, citing Rhet. A1, 1355b26; A2

### U-FP3b-P7: Ethos is constitutive of legein and is cultivated through three aspects (phronesis, arete, eunoia)
The ethos of the speaker is not an external supplement to argumentation but is constitutive of speaking itself (p. 111). The speaker acquires pistis by appearing as one who "looks around" (phronesis), is "serious" (arete), and has "good will" (eunoia). Heidegger follows Aristotle's strategy of analyzing ethos through the *opposite course* -- showing what is missing when a speaker deceives (pp. 112-113). A speaker who offers himself with all three "will thus have real trust -- he will himself be a pistis in his logos" (p. 113).
- **Source**: pp. 111-113, citing Rhet. B1, 1377b21 sqq., 1378a1 sqq.
- **Significance**: The speaker's being -- not just argumentation -- is constitutive of persuasion

### U-FP3b-P8: Pathos defines being-in-the-world in a fundamental sense; the three modes of the soul (pathe, dunameis, hexeis) characterize being-there
Heidegger reads NE B4's three ginomena en te psyche as three modes of being-there as being-in-the-world: pathe as "modes of being, modes of becoming" co-given with hedone and lupe; dunameis as co-given possibilities of factical being; hexeis as the settled manner in which we carry ourselves with respect to pathe (pp. 113-114). The analysis of pathe will show "how what is designated as pathos *defines being-in-the-world in a fundamental sense*" (p. 114). The constitutive aspects from Rhet. B1 are: (1) metaballontes (change of disposition), (2) differentiation pros tas kriseis (change in position-taking), (3) co-givenness with lupe and hedone as "being-disposed-as-higher-or-lower" (p. 115).
- **Source**: pp. 113-115, citing NE B4, 1105b19 sqq.; Rhet. B1, 1378a20 sqq.
- **Significance**: Grounds the entire subsequent analysis of pathe (§§17-21) by establishing their ontological status as modes of being-in-the-world, not mere "psychical processes"

## 5. Tensions & Open Questions

**T1: The ontological status of doxa between episteme and phantasia.** Heidegger stresses that doxa is not a deficient mode of knowing but the genuine discoveredness of everydayness. Yet the four-fold demarcation also reveals that doxa *lacks* the stability of episteme and the ethos-determination of proairesis. Is doxa genuinely positive or structurally deficient? Heidegger seems to hold both: doxa is the basis even for episteme and theorein (p. 103), yet its "authority and stubbornness" (p. 102) point toward inauthenticity.

**T2: The relationship between doxa and aletheia.** Heidegger states that "being-directed toward aletheia is constitutive of doxa, and therefore the possibility of pseudos belongs to it" (p. 93). But he also claims that doxa is characterized by going-along with "the way the world initially shows itself" (p. 102) and by trust in the immediate aspect. How can something constitutively directed toward aletheia also be characterized by the possibility of concealment? This tension is productive: it reveals that truth and untruth belong together in doxa. [INTERP-high]: This structure anticipates the BT analysis of truth (SZ §44).

**T3: The unity of theoretical and practical negotiating.** §15 treats doxa as grounding theoretical legein (dialectic, through protasis/problema); §16 treats ethos and pathos as grounding practical negotiating (rhetoric). Heidegger claims doxa underlies both, but the transition from §15e to §16a is abrupt. The question: is the unity of theoretical and practical negotiating in doxa a unity of origin, or merely a shared domain (endechomenon allos)?

**T4: Plato and Aristotle on doxa.** Heidegger notes that "the perspective is opened up for that which Plato did not see" (p. 95) through Aristotle's treatment, specifically the revisability structure. But the relationship is not fully worked out. To what extent does Aristotle *radicalize* the Platonic analysis, and to what extent does he *break* with it?

**T5: The threefold pistis and the speaker's being.** The claim that ethos and pathe are "constitutive of legein itself" (p. 111) is strong. Yet Heidegger also acknowledges that "setting down these conditions of discourse at each moment is not something that has been exhausted up to now" in scientific and philosophical accounts (pp. 114-115). This marks an open question about whether the rhetorical analysis can be fully translated into ontological terms.

**T6: Pathos as ontological vs. rhetorical.** The three meanings of pathos (variable condition, ontological paschein/kinesis, passion) are listed but not fully integrated. Meaning (2), connected to kinesis, foreshadows the Second Part's treatment of movement but is left hanging in this unit. The relationship between the rhetorical analysis of pathe and the ontological analysis of kinesis remains an open seam.
