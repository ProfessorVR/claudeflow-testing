# ped-sec-05 — Fowler, "Virtual reality and learning: Where is the pedagogy?" (2015; online-first 2014)

**Type:** journal-article-length secondary reading (educational technology / instructional design) · **Discipline:** educational technology (VR pedagogy) · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** Strand B's pedagogy-first counterweight — a conceptual, non-empirical paper that names and tries to repair the very deficit this cluster's Purpose statement targets: VR-learning literature that runs technology-first and only afterward asks what pedagogy the technology serves.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Fowler's stated aim is to build upon and extend Dalgarno and Lee's influential model of learning in three-dimensional virtual learning environments (VLEs), offering "a more pedagogical description" of that model via the concept of pedagogical immersion, itself derived from Mayes and Fowler's (1999) staged framework for mapping learning onto types of learning environment (FE, p.412). The paper explicitly adopts a "design for learning" perspective, meaning its payoff is meant to be practical — useful to practitioners designing learning activities in 3-D VLEs, not merely to theorists (FE, p.412). Fowler's governing complaint, voiced through Mikropoulos and Natsis's (2011) ten-year review of educational-virtual-environment research, is that the overwhelming majority of published VR-in-education studies proceed without an explicit theoretical or pedagogical model, and that where a model is invoked it is nearly always an unexamined variant of constructivism (FAITHFUL-Fowler, p.412, quoting Mikropoulos & Natsis 2011, p.775). Dalgarno and Lee's own model, Fowler argues, while the field's most developed attempt at systematicity, is itself technology-led: it identifies learning benefits by working outward from the technical affordances built into 3-D environments, rather than working inward from learning outcomes and objectives toward the technology needed to support them (FE, p.412–413). The paper's aim is therefore corrective rather than oppositional — to keep Dalgarno and Lee's affordance apparatus intact while adding a pedagogical layer that was, on Fowler's reading, largely absent from it.

## 2. Structure

The paper runs continuously across five identifiable movements, with a Practitioner Notes box after the introduction summarizing prior knowledge, contribution, and implications (p.413).

1. **Abstract / framing** (p.412) — states the extension aim and the "pedagogical immersion" / "design for learning" vocabulary that will carry it.
2. **Introduction** (pp.412–413) — opens from Mikropoulos and Natsis's (2011) finding of a field-wide pedagogical-theory gap; positions Dalgarno and Lee (2010) as the paper's primary object of extension rather than of rejection.
3. **Dalgarno and Lee's (2010) model of learning in 3-D VWs** (pp.413–416) — restates the source model's defining characteristics (representational fidelity, learner interaction), its emergent properties (presence, co-presence, identity construction), and its five learning affordances; then works two critical seams into that restatement — an inverted-U challenge to the fidelity/interaction-to-learning assumption (invoking Yerkes & Dodson, 1908) and an observation that presence/co-presence/identity describe antecedents of a learning experience rather than the experience itself.
4. **Extending the model** (pp.416–420) — introduces Mayes and Fowler's (1999) three-stage framework (conceptualisation, construction, dialogue), proposes "immersion" as the bridge concept linking technology, psychology, and pedagogy, distinguishes conceptual/task/social immersion (renamed empathy/reification/identification), and assembles the combined model anchored on Intended Learning Outcomes (ILOs) and a "design for learning" process running from learning stage through Bloom's revised taxonomy (Anderson et al., 2001) to Conole et al.'s (2004) mini-learning activities to a learning specification (Table 1, Figures 2–3).
5. **Further research** (pp.420–422) — calls for empirical validation of both the original and enhanced frameworks, more applied requirements-elicitation research, comparative designs that hold pedagogy or technology constant (invoking Clark's 1994 media-vs-method position), and new design guidelines for educational VLEs.

## 3. Constructs

- **representational fidelity** — restated directly from Dalgarno and Lee as the display-quality/realism half of the source model's two unique characteristics, spanning visual realism, object-behavior consistency, communication realism, and user-representation quality (FAITHFUL-Fowler, p.413–414).
- **agency / interactivity** — restated as "learner interaction," the second unique characteristic, glossed as the richness of interaction resulting mainly from the degree of embodiment the user experiences (FAITHFUL-Fowler, p.413–414).
- **embodiment / self-representation** — treated via the avatar: the user's representation, able to communicate, show emotion, and control or create objects as if actually present in the VLE (FAITHFUL-Fowler, p.414).
- **presence (spatial/physical)** and **social presence / co-presence** — both restated as emergent psychological properties arising from the combination of representational fidelity and learner interaction; Fowler's own critical contribution is to note that these constructs describe antecedents of the learning experience rather than the experience itself, and do not by themselves explain whether or how learning occurs (FE, p.415).
- **immersion (technological)** — reworked as the paper's central bridge concept. Fowler follows Dalgarno and Lee's agreement with Hedberg and Alexander (1994) that immersion arises from the interaction of fidelity and interaction, then proposes a second, pedagogical sense of immersion — a state arising from the interaction of pedagogical variables — that is not reducible to the technical sense (FAITHFUL-Fowler, p.416). This double use of "immersion" (technological vs. pedagogical) is this paper's most consequential conceptual move and is exactly the row this cluster's construct axis normalizes under "immersion (technological)," with the pedagogical sense flagged separately in concordance notes.
- **engagement / flow** — appears as one of Dalgarno and Lee's five learning affordances, and resurfaces in Fowler's design-for-learning apparatus as a "generic requirement" that should characterize all learning activities regardless of specific content (FAITHFUL-Fowler, p.418).
- **learning outcomes: retention & transfer** — addressed through Dalgarno and Lee's "contextual learning" affordance and, more centrally, through Fowler's own Intended Learning Outcomes (ILO) apparatus (citing Biggs, 2003) and the mapping of learning stages to Bloom's revised taxonomy's knowledge dimensions (metacognitive, procedural, conceptual, factual) (FAITHFUL-Fowler, p.417, 419).
- **learning outcomes: procedural / skills** — most explicitly operationalized in Table 1's "dialogue" row, which lists skill-acquisition and real-world-performance objectives (rehearsing, applying knowledge in real-world settings) mapped to Conole et al.'s (2004) mini-learning activities (FAITHFUL-Fowler, p.418–419).

Not directly treated as their own rows: affect/enjoyment as a measured construct (engagement is discussed only as a design requirement, never operationalized affectively), cybersickness/discomfort, and cognitive load (extraneous vs. germane) — none of these appear in Fowler's vocabulary.

## 4. Conditions & design

This is a conceptual/theoretical paper with no original empirical study of its own; Fowler explicitly calls, in his "Further research" section, for the empirical validation that the paper itself does not attempt (FE, p.420–421). Its own object throughout is Dalgarno and Lee's desktop-focused 3-D VLE definition — environments explorable "using a standard personal computer... commonly available in schools and homes," explicitly distinguished from fully immersive systems such as CAVE-type environments (FAITHFUL-Fowler, quoting Dalgarno & Lee 2010, p.11, at p.413). The paper's sole piece of secondary empirical evidence is Lee, Wong and Fung's (2010) structural-equation-modeling study, which Fowler cites approvingly as adopting a "process" approach to the learning experience (distinguishing input, mediating, moderating and output variables) but flags as limited: its VR programme (a virtual frog simulation) "was not immersive and involved no avatars," so it cannot itself test embodiment or immersion-dependent claims (FAITHFUL-Fowler, p.415). Accordingly this unit's condition-axis cell is `SEM / process model (no media contrast)` for the Lee et al. citation, and `desktop-3D / non-immersive VR` for Fowler's own scope-defining object throughout — Fowler never contrasts desktop-3D against IVR or 2D empirically; the media-comparison question is raised only prescriptively, in his call for designs that "hold pedagogy or technology constant" while varying the other (citing Clark's 1994 media-vs-method debate) (FAITHFUL-Fowler, p.421–422).

## 5. Findings/Claims

- Dalgarno and Lee's model, despite its influence, is technology-led: it derives learning benefits from technical affordances rather than starting from pedagogical requirements and working outward to the technology that would support them (FE, p.412–413).
- The assumption that higher representational fidelity and richer learner interaction straightforwardly produce deeper learning is questionable; Fowler proposes an inverted-U relationship (invoking the Yerkes–Dodson law) in which exceeding an optimum increases cost for flat or negative learning return, and argues some learning contexts may not need the fidelity/interaction continuum considered at all (FE, p.415).
- Presence, co-presence, and identity construction — Dalgarno and Lee's three emergent experiential properties — describe antecedents of a learning experience, not the experience itself; a learner may have a strong sense of presence without that fact alone describing what or how they learned (FE, p.415).
- "Immersion" can bridge technology, psychology, and pedagogy because a pedagogical sense of immersion (arising from the interaction of pedagogical variables) parallels the technical sense (arising from fidelity × interaction); Mayes and Fowler's (1999) three-stage framework — conceptualisation, construction, dialogue — supplies the pedagogical vocabulary this bridge needs (FAITHFUL-Fowler, p.416).
- The three learning stages correspond to three renamed immersion-types — empathy (conceptual immersion), reification (task immersion), and identification (social immersion) — chosen to sit terminologically alongside Dalgarno and Lee's construction-of-identity/presence/co-presence triad rather than overload the single word "immersion" across three registers (FAITHFUL-Fowler, p.419–420).
- The combined model anchors both frameworks on Intended Learning Outcomes and yields a "design for learning" process: learning stage → learning objective (via Bloom's revised taxonomy) → learning activity (via Conole et al.'s mini-learning-activities) → learning specification, further modulated by contextual variables (locus of control, group dynamics, task authenticity, interactivity level, information source) (FAITHFUL-Fowler, p.418–420).
- "Design for learning" is explicitly distinguished from "learning design" (the IMS-LD/EML systematic-specification tradition): the former is a holistic planning activity oriented to learning outcomes, the latter a formal specification language (FE, p.419).
- Future comparative research should isolate pedagogy's and technology's separate contributions by holding one constant while varying the other — e.g., replacing whiteboards with smart boards under an unchanged "talk and chalk" pedagogy, versus shifting to a constructivist paradigm via 3-D VLEs under unchanged hardware — while controlling for novelty and placebo effects (FAITHFUL-Fowler, p.421–422).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* This unit is the cluster's clearest statement of the pedagogy-deficit problem Part III's own VLE analysis keeps running into empirically. Fowler's diagnosis — that VR-learning research and design proceed technology-first, deriving benefits from affordances rather than deriving requirements from intended outcomes — reads directly onto `vle-03-exemplar-bank`'s "Monotony / passivity / length" and "Withdrawal / flight" exemplar clusters, where VLE respondents describe long, structurally passive video content and active disengagement from it *(anticipatory-application)*: those exemplars are exactly the symptom Fowler's design-for-learning apparatus (ILOs → learning stage → learning activity → specification) is built to prevent, and the bridge is offered as a diagnostic lens, not as evidence that the VLE's designers skipped these steps, which this unit cannot verify *(anticipatory-application)*. Fowler's insistence that comparative studies must hold pedagogy or technology constant while varying the other (citing Clark's media-vs-method debate) is a standing caution for Part III's own lab=VR / deployment=2D-PC poles: a finding that the VR pole outperforms or underperforms the 2D pole is not automatically a finding about the *medium*, unless the underlying pedagogical design was held constant across both, a condition Part III's own survey instrument does not by itself establish *(anticipatory-application)*. This unit stands in a load-bearing intra-cluster relationship to `ped-sec-04` (Dalgarno & Lee): Fowler's own framing is explicitly one of extension rather than rejection — he retains the fidelity/interaction apparatus and the five affordances wholesale — but two of his moves genuinely contest rather than merely extend that source: the inverted-U challenge to the assumption that more fidelity/interaction yields more learning, and the claim that presence/co-presence/identity describe antecedents rather than the learning experience itself. Both are treated below as `contests-reading-of` edges, distinct from the general `extends-reading-of` edge covering the paper's stated aim.

Not treated: cybersickness/physical discomfort, cognitive load (extraneous vs. germane), any IVR-vs-desktop-vs-2D empirical contrast, affect/enjoyment as a measured (rather than design-requirement) construct, and social-presence-theory's CMC tradition (Strand F) — Fowler's "co-presence" stays within Dalgarno and Lee's VLE-specific usage and does not engage Short, Gunawardena, or Garrison/Anderson/Archer.
