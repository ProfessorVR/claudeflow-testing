# ped-sec-14 — Barrett, Poe, O'Camb, Woodruff, Harrison, Dolguikh, Chuong, Klassen, Zhang, Joseph & Blair, "Comparing virtual reality, desktop-based 3D, and 2D versions of a category learning experiment" (2022)

**Type:** empirical journal article (cognitive psychology) · **Discipline:** cognitive psychology (category learning / attention) · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** Strand C's most direct three-way media-comparison experiment — the only unit in this cluster to run IVR, desktop-3D, and 2D conditions of the *same* task in a single design, making it the closest empirical analogue this cluster holds to Part III's own poles (lab=VR ↔ deployment=2D-PC) and strata (desktop-3D vs video-only).

## 1. Thesis/Aim

The study's guiding question is whether cognitive processes and behaviors established in laboratory category-learning research transfer cleanly to immersive VR, or whether VR's distinct physical/perceptual properties (near-eye fixed screen distance, decoupled accommodation/convergence, restricted field of view, motor-tracked interaction) and its associated presence/immersion literature predict a genuinely different learning experience (FE, p.1). The authors frame the comparison through an information-access-cost lens: VR requires arm/hand movement to reveal stimulus features, desktop-3D requires gamepad-directed rotation, and 2D presents all features simultaneously to a passive, eye-tracked viewer — an implicit cost ordering (2D < desktop-3D < VR) that the paper explicitly sets out to test against learning-outcome and information-access measures (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., p.7). Their overarching finding, previewed in the abstract, is that learning accuracy is statistically equivalent across all three media despite real differences in response time, fixation behavior, and attentional-optimization trajectory (FE, p.1) — an equivalence result the paper reads as license for researchers and designers to carry desktop-based findings and design principles into VR without re-deriving them from scratch (FE, p.17).

## 2. Structure

Introduction → "Same tasks, different experiences" (lit review: physical/perceptual VR-specific factors, presence/engagement, Makransky's cognitive-load findings, motor-cost framing) → "New interface, same cognitive processes" (lit review: VR-desktop equivalence evidence, VR cognitive-assessment validity, medium-agnostic information-access patterns) → "Category learning" (paradigm background, information-access-cost literature, the study's own predictions) → Methods (participants; conditions and outcome variables; stimuli; procedures; dependent-measure calculation; fixation-detection method) → Exclusions → Results (learning outcomes; information access behaviors) → Discussion → Conclusion.

## 3. Constructs

- **Immersion (technological)** — operationalized architecturally as the condition ladder itself rather than measured by a separate presence scale: VR (HTC Vive HMD) is the high-immersion/high-access-cost condition, desktop-3D the medium condition, 2D the low-cost baseline (FE, pp.7-8). The lit review separately cites the presence literature's own definition — a participant's subjective sense of "being there" within a simulation — but this study does not administer a presence instrument (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., p.2).
- **Cognitive load (extraneous vs germane)** — invoked in the lit review via Makransky's recurring finding that immersive VR groups often show elevated cognitive load relative to other instruction, sometimes offset by generative-learning, enactment, or pre-training pairings (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., pp.2-3); the present study treats this as a candidate explanatory mechanism for VR/desktop-3D response-time and fixation-duration patterns rather than measuring load directly.
- **Agency / interactivity** — VR and desktop-3D require active motor manipulation (hand-controller collision/gamepad D-pad) to rotate the stimulus cube and reveal features one at a time; the 2D condition instead presents all three features simultaneously to a viewer whose gaze alone is tracked (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., pp.7-9).
- **Learning outcomes: procedural / skills** — the paper's central dependent variable: accuracy (proportion correct per trial block, restricted to participants who reached a 24-consecutive-correct criterion) and response time jointly index category-learning skill acquisition (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., p.10).
- **Cybersickness & physical discomfort** — treated as a practical cost specific to the VR condition: 6 of 32 VR non-learner exclusions withdrew citing physical discomfort (motion sickness, dizziness, fatigue, or headache), against one 2D dropout citing dizziness (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., p.12, 16).
- **Affect: enjoyment & intrinsic motivation** — invoked post hoc, not measured directly, to explain VR's persistently elevated fixation count: the authors speculate that the novelty of arm-controller manipulation made rotating the cube intrinsically pleasing, leading participants not to rush through trials (FE, p.16).
- **Engagement / flow** — closely tied to the enjoyment explanation above; the paper does not operationalize flow but gestures toward Jensen & Konradsen and Farra, Smith & Ulrich's suggestion that VR enthusiasm may partly reflect single-session novelty rather than a durable engagement gain (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., p.16).
- **Representational fidelity** — deliberately held constant as a design control rather than manipulated: cube geometry, category structure, and feature location/relevance combinations were matched across VR and desktop-3D, with the 2D condition using a flat triangular analogue of the same three-feature structure (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., pp.8-9).

Not treated in this construct set: social/co-presence (no collaborative or multi-user element in the design), self-efficacy, and situational interest as named psychological constructs — the paper's affect/engagement claims are speculative asides in the Discussion, not measured constructs.

## 4. Conditions & design

Three between-subjects conditions, all instantiating the identical four-category, three-feature (two relevant, one irrelevant) classification task, run to a 24-consecutive-correct learning criterion or 240 trials / 30 minutes, whichever came first (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., pp.7, 12).

- **IVR (HMD)** — HTC Vive headset + HTC Vive hand controllers; participants stood in an open testing area and rotated a virtual stimulus cube by physically colliding a hand controller with it, selecting categories by reaching to an on-screen button and pulling the trigger. **Recruited n=58; final analytic n=26** after excluding 32 non-learners (of whom 6 withdrew citing physical discomfort) (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., pp.7-8, 12).
- **Desktop-3D / non-immersive VR** — standard Windows 10 desktop, 22-inch color monitor, Logitech F310 gamepad; the identical stimulus cube was rotated via the gamepad's directional pad, with bumpers/triggers used to select categories. **Recruited n=56; final analytic n=26** after excluding 30 non-learners (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., pp.7-8, 12).
- **2D video / slideshow / text** (the paper's own "eye tracking" condition) — same 22-inch monitor and gamepad for category responses, but a flat triangular stimulus displayed all three features simultaneously; a Tobii X120 eye tracker (60Hz sampling, 0.5° spatial resolution, participant seated 60cm from the device, 75ms temporal / 1.9° spatial fixation thresholds) recorded gaze. **Recruited n=65; final analytic n=8** — one participant dropped out citing dizziness, and an equipment failure left only 8 of the remaining participants with complete gaze data sets meeting the learning criterion; the authors report a supplementary check against a prior category-learning study using the same structure found no evidence the resulting 2D sample came from a different population distribution, treating the missingness as at-random (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., p.12).

Total recruited N = 179 Simon Fraser University undergraduates (course-credit compensation; SFU Research Ethics approval, Study Number 2018-s0444; VR participants specifically informed of motion-sickness risk and withdrawal procedure) (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., p.7). Dependent measures: accuracy, response time, attentional optimization (a -1 to 1 index of relative fixation time on relevant vs irrelevant features), feedback-viewing duration, fixation count, and fixation duration, each analyzed via linear mixed-effects models with trial bin, condition, and their interaction as fixed effects and subject as a random intercept, significance assessed by likelihood-ratio tests (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., pp.11-12). The conditions are explicitly framed, per the paper's own information-access-cost hypothesis, as high (VR), medium (desktop-3D), and low (2D) access-cost conditions (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., p.7).

## 5. Findings/Claims

- **No accuracy difference across media.** Baseline improvement to accuracy did not differ by condition (χ²=4.043, p=.133), nor did the rate of improvement (interaction χ²=9.012, p=.173): all three groups who reached criterion learned the category structure in roughly the same time and at the same rate (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., p.12).
- **2D was faster; VR and desktop-3D did not differ from each other.** Response time showed a significant condition effect (χ²=12.339, p=.002) and interaction (χ²=40.32, p<.001), but only 2D stood out — faster overall, with a lower peak; VR and desktop-3D response times were statistically indistinguishable (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., p.12-13).
- **Attentional optimization showed a crossover, not a main effect.** No overall condition difference (χ²=0.734, p=.693) but a significant bin-by-condition interaction (χ²=33.489, p<.001): VR and desktop-3D optimized information sampling faster early on, while the 2D condition — despite starting lower — achieved *greater* optimization by the experiment's end (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., p.13).
- **Feedback-viewing duration showed no condition effects at all** (main χ²=3.818, p=.148; interaction χ²=5.048, p=.282) (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., p.13-14).
- **VR had the highest fixation count throughout**, never converging to the lower asymptote reached by desktop-3D and 2D (condition χ²=13.938, p<.001; interaction χ²=15.954, p=.003) (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., p.13-14).
- **2D had markedly shorter, flatter fixation durations** than VR and desktop-3D, which resembled each other closely (condition χ²=39.148, p<.001; interaction χ²=33.827, p<.001) (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., p.14).
- **The predicted access-cost ordering (2D < 3D < VR) held for only one measure — fixation count — and in the opposite of the predicted direction**: the authors conclude their cost manipulation was largely ineffective, possibly because VR/desktop-3D delays were a natural byproduct of self-paced motor movement rather than an obtrusive, fixed, externally imposed delay of the kind used in prior access-cost studies (FE, p.16).
- **Individual differences (not measured here) are flagged as a likely moderator** the present null main effects cannot rule out — citing Sun, Wu & Cai's finding that low-spatial-ability learners benefit most from VR while high-spatial-ability learners do no better in VR than with paper-based learning (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., p.16).
- **VR-specific attrition from physical discomfort is named as a real cost to weigh against any VR benefit**, occurring within a sub-hour session (FAITHFUL-Barrett et al., p.16).
- **Conclusion:** because general learning-outcome and information-access patterns replicate across VR, desktop-3D, and 2D, prior desktop-based cognitive/pedagogical findings and design principles should be presumed to transfer to VR contexts (and vice versa) without requiring re-validation in each new medium (FE, p.17).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* This unit is Part III's single closest experimental analogue: its three-way IVR/desktop-3D/2D ladder maps almost directly onto Part III's own lab=VR ↔ deployment=2D-PC poles and its APP (desktop-3D) vs VID (video-only) survey strata (`vle-01-deployment-survey`). Where `vle-01`'s F6 "value-skepticism arc" documents VLE survey respondents drifting toward "why not just YouTube?" as a self-report judgment about the 3D app's *worth*, this experiment supplies an independent, behavioral counterweight: learning accuracy did not differ across media at all, so any dissertation claim that media choice determines *learning outcome* should be qualified by this null result even where media choice clearly does predict engagement, attrition, and information-access behavior *(anticipatory-application)*. The VR-specific attrition finding — discomfort-driven withdrawal concentrated in the HMD condition, none in desktop-3D — is a direct, citable caution for any future move of the King–Salvo VLE toward IVR deployment: comfort-driven dropout is not a uniform tax across media, it is an IVR-specific one *(anticipatory-application)*. The paper's own speculative reading of VR's elevated, non-converging fixation count as novelty/enjoyment rather than a stable engagement gain bears on `vle-04-mark-attention-span`'s engagement×challenge framework: high fixation count without a corresponding optimization or accuracy advantage more closely resembles Mark's "rote" quadrant (high engagement, low challenge payoff) than her "focus" quadrant, though this mapping is this cluster's own projection, not a claim either source makes *(anticipatory-application)*. More generally, the paper's central methodological claim — that desktop-3D findings and design principles should be presumed to transfer to VR without medium-specific re-validation — is directly usable as evidence-based license for treating Part III's desktop-3D findings as informative about a hypothetical IVR deployment, subject to the explicit caveat (also this paper's own) that individual differences such as spatial ability can moderate any VR-specific benefit or cost that a between-subjects average would mask *(anticipatory-application)*.

Not treated: social/co-presence, self-efficacy, situational interest, delayed retention or transfer testing (the task ends at criterion or 30 minutes with no follow-up assessment), and any direct presence-questionnaire measurement — the paper's "immersion" is architectural (which condition a participant was assigned to), never separately instrumented.
