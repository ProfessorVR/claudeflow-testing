# ped-sec-16 — Merchant, Goetz, Keeney-Kennicutt, Kwok, Cifuentes & Davis, "The Learner Characteristics, Features of Desktop 3D Virtual Reality Environments, and College Chemistry Instruction: A Structural Equation Modeling Analysis" (2012)

**Type:** empirical journal article (educational psychology / instructional design) · **Discipline:** quantitative educational psychology, structural equation modeling · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** Strand D's chemistry-education anchor — a single-condition (desktop-3D only), SEM-based mediation study of *how* a non-immersive VR environment's features translate into science-learning gains, and the cluster's clearest empirical case that presence, though generated, need not predict learning.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Merchant et al. propose and test a structural equation model of how the features of a desktop-3D virtual reality environment (Second Life) affect college chemistry achievement, mediated by a chain of perceptual and psychological learner variables — usability, spatial orientation, self-efficacy, and presence (FE, p.551). Their motivating premise is that many prior VR-chemistry studies had already found positive achievement effects (Barnea & Dori, 1999; Pribyl & Bodner, 1987) but had not examined the mediating psychological/perceptual machinery through which those effects arise (FE, p.552-553). The paper explicitly frames itself as the first SEM-based account of chemistry learning in a 3D virtual environment and the first to examine self-efficacy's role in that process (FE, p.553, 559). Its guiding hypothesis, following Salzman, Dede, Loftin & Chen's (1999) general VR-learning model, is that 3D-VR features do not act on achievement directly but only via learners' perceptual and psychological uptake of those features (FE, p.552).

## 2. Structure

The article follows a standard empirical-report skeleton in six numbered sections.

1. **§1 Introduction (1.1-1.3)** (p.551-553) — establishes the spatial-cognition rationale for VR in chemistry (molecular geometry, VSEPR theory), links spatial-learning difficulty to lowered self-efficacy, and reviews the prior positive-effect VR-chemistry literature before arguing that mediating-variable analysis, via SEM, is the paper's needed contribution.
2. **§2 Theoretical framework (2.1-2.3)** (p.552-555) — builds the model from Salzman et al.'s (1999) general VR-learning framework and Lee, Wong & Fung's (2010) SEM adaptation for high-school biology; adopts Dalgarno & Lee's (2010) two-factor VR-feature taxonomy (representational fidelity, learners' interaction, FAITHFUL-Merchant, p.552) as the model's independent latent factor; states hypotheses H1-H7 (direct paths) and H01-H03 (indirect/mediation paths).
3. **§3 Method (3.1-3.4)** (p.555-559) — participants, three measurement instruments, the Second Life "Dr K's Chemistry Corner" treatment and its three graded activities, and procedure.
4. **§4 Results (4.1-4.2)** (p.559-563) — measurement-model (CFA) results, then the structural-model path estimates.
5. **§5 Discussion (5.1-5.5)** (p.563-566) — subsection-by-subsection interpretation of the chemistry-test, self-efficacy, spatial-orientation, usability, and presence findings.
6. **§6 Conclusions** (p.566-567) — summarizes the contribution, states the single-course generalizability limitation, and recommends embedding desktop-3D spatial training broadly in science instruction, with a call for direct experimental (not only correlational/SEM) follow-up.

An Appendix A itemizes the self-report instrument items and their sources (p.563-568), not analyzed further here as it is instrument documentation rather than argument.

## 3. Constructs

The model treats six named constructs plus one outcome measure, organized into the study's own latent-factor structure (FE, p.552-555, Fig. 1).

- **3D virtual reality features (independent latent factor)** — operationalized via Dalgarno & Lee's (2010) two-factor taxonomy: **representational fidelity** (realistic display: rich graphics, smooth temporal change, consistent object behavior — e.g., a photo-realistic rotatable molecule) and **learners' interaction** (the ability to explore, manipulate, rotate, and view objects from multiple perspectives) (FAITHFUL-Merchant, p.552). This maps directly onto the cluster's `representational fidelity` and `agency / interactivity` construct-axis rows.
- **usability (mediating latent factor)** — a composite of perceived ease of use and perceived meaningfulness, drawn from Davis's (1989) technology acceptance model; framed via cognitive-load theory (Sweller, 1994; Van Merriënboer & Sweller, 2005) as the variable through which extraneous cognitive load either is or is not imposed on learners by the VR interface itself (FAITHFUL-Merchant, p.553-554). This is this unit's operationalization of the cluster's `cognitive load (extraneous vs germane)` row, framed inversely — usability is the *absence* of extraneous-load imposition rather than a direct load measure.
- **spatial orientation** — a perceptual variable, a component of spatial ability, defined as the capacity to mentally rotate an object under simple or rigid transformations (Ekstrom, French & Harman, 1976; Lohman, 1988) and measured via the Purdue Visualization of Rotations Test (PVRT) (FAITHFUL-Merchant, p.555, 557).
- **self-efficacy** — a psychological variable grounded in Bandura's social cognitive theory, defined as a person's beliefs about their capability to perform a particular task successfully (FAITHFUL-Merchant, p.556-557, citing Bandura, 1993; Zimmerman, Bandura & Martinez-Pons, 1992); operationalized via a 15-item instrument adapted from Witt-Rose (2004).
- **presence** — defined, quoting Witmer & Singer (1998, p.225) directly, as "the subjective experience of being in one place or environment, even when one is physically situated in another" (FAITHFUL-Merchant, p.557, quoting Witmer & Singer). Operationalized with a single item, adapted from the Slater & Usoh (1994) presence measure — the paper's own most consequential methodological limitation, flagged by the authors themselves in the discussion (FE, p.566).
- **chemistry learning test (outcome)** — an 11-item, instructor-authored multiple-choice test on VSEPR (Valence-Shell Electron Pair Repulsion) theory: molecular angles, molecular geometry, and species identification (FAITHFUL-Merchant, p.558-559).

Not directly treated as a separate construct but load-bearing throughout the discussion: **extraneous vs. germane cognitive load** (Sweller, 1994) is invoked twice as an *explanatory* frame — once for why usability matters at all, and once as a candidate explanation for the null presence-learning link (FE, p.554, 565) — without the paper itself operationalizing cognitive load as a measured variable.

## 4. Conditions & design

**Design:** single-condition (desktop-3D VR only), correlational/SEM mediation analysis; not a between-groups media-comparison experiment. Data were drawn from the treatment arm of a larger quasi-experimental study, but only the desktop-3D-VR group's data were used in this paper's analyses (FE, p.555) — there is no 2D or no-treatment comparison group reported here. This places the unit squarely in the concordance's `SEM / process model (no media contrast)` condition column, not the `desktop-3D / non-immersive VR` **comparison** column that Strand-C's experiments (e.g., ped-sec-11, ped-sec-14) occupy.

**Sample:** N=204 undergraduates (final analytic sample, from an initial 238) in an introductory college Chemistry 101 course at a large US southern university, spring 2011; 67% female, 92% aged 18-21, 72% Caucasian, 17% Hispanic (FAITHFUL-Merchant, p.556, Table 1).

**Treatment/environment:** Second Life's "Dr K's Chemistry Corner," a desktop-3D virtual environment (not head-mounted, not immersive) built by a chemistry-department co-author; students completed three graded activities — the Molecule Game, Chemist as an Artist, and the Tower of VSEPR Theory — over roughly eight weeks, each requiring molecule rotation, manipulation, and viewing from multiple angles (FAITHFUL-Merchant, p.557-559).

**Instruments:** (1) an 11-item VSEPR chemistry-learning test, instructor-authored, content-validated by three chemistry professors, pilot Cronbach's alpha = 0.87 (FAITHFUL-Merchant, p.558); (2) the Purdue Visualization of Rotations Test (PVRT; Bodner & Guay, 1997), 20 items, KR-20 reliability 0.78-0.80 (FAITHFUL-Merchant, p.558); (3) a 41-item self-report battery across the six named constructs, adapted from prior validated instruments except the single-item presence measure and the self-efficacy scale (FAITHFUL-Merchant, p.558-559).

**Analysis:** confirmatory factor analysis (measurement models) followed by structural equation modeling (structural model) in Mplus 6.11, maximum-likelihood estimation, composite (unit-weighted) scores rather than full latent-variable structural paths, citing sample-size/parameter-ratio constraints (FAITHFUL-Merchant, p.559-560).

**Key statistics (structural model fit):** CFI=.953, TLI=.931, RMSEA=.06, SRMR=.04 (FAITHFUL-Merchant, p.561). Path estimates: VR features → usability, β=.956 (p<.001); usability → spatial orientation, β=.166 (p<.05); usability → self-efficacy, β=.579 (p<.001); usability → presence, β=.540 (p<.001); spatial orientation → chemistry test, β=.344 (p<.001); self-efficacy → chemistry test, β=.513 (p<.001); **presence → chemistry test, β=.069, non-significant (p=.367)** (FAITHFUL-Merchant, p.561, Fig. 4). The model explained 45% of variance in the chemistry test, 34% in self-efficacy, 29% in presence, and 3% in spatial orientation (FAITHFUL-Merchant, p.561). All three indirect (mediation) hypotheses (H01-H03) were supported — usability mediates the path from VR features to spatial orientation, self-efficacy, and presence alike (FAITHFUL-Merchant, p.561).

## 5. Findings/Claims

- All hypothesized relationships were supported except H7 (presence → chemistry learning test): usability mediates the entire causal chain from VR features through to spatial orientation, self-efficacy, and presence, but only spatial orientation and self-efficacy — not presence — significantly predict the chemistry-learning outcome (FE, p.561-562).
- Usability functions as the model's structural linchpin: nearly all of the VR features' downstream effect on the three perceptual/psychological variables passes through it (β=.956 direct path), which the authors read as evidence that VR features "cannot support the development of learners' spatial orientation ability, self-efficacy, and presence" unless the environment is perceived as meaningful and easy to use (FAITHFUL-Merchant, p.564).
- The null presence-learning result is read by the authors as consistent with a genuinely mixed literature — contrasted directly against Lee et al. (2010) and Burgess (2010), who found positive presence-learning relationships, versus Mania & Chalmers (2001) and Moreno & Mayer (2002), who like this study found no such relationship (FAITHFUL-Merchant, p.565).
- Three candidate explanations are offered for the null presence result, none adjudicated as decisive: (1) media (and by extension the *sense* of being in a mediated environment) does not by itself improve learning — only well-designed *tasks* do (citing Clark, 1989; Dalgarno & Lee, 2010; Kozma, 1994); (2) extraneous cognitive load from navigating Second Life's interface may have consumed the cognitive resources presence would otherwise have converted into learning gains (Sweller, 1994); (3) the single-item presence measure may not have adequately captured the construct (FE, p.565-566).
- The authors' own headline instructional recommendation is generalized beyond chemistry: embed spatial training via desktop-3D VR broadly across science instruction wherever three-dimensional thinking is required (FE, p.566-567).
- Explicit self-flagged limitations: single-course, single-institution generalizability; correlational/SEM (not experimental) design, meaning causal claims about the VR intervention itself are not licensed by this study alone; single-item presence measurement (FE, p.566-567).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* This unit is the cluster's cleanest single-condition demonstration that a desktop-3D environment can generate significant presence (β=.540 from usability) while that presence carries no significant weight in predicting learning (β=.069, ns) — a result that pairs directly, though only by structural parallel and not by shared authorship or citation, with ped-sec-10 (Makransky et al.'s IVR-adds-presence-but-not-learning finding) and ped-sec-11 (Makransky & Petersen's desktop-VR-SEM process model), both of which the concordance and debate-map should read alongside this unit's own presence-null when documenting the "presence → learning" causal-chain debate axis (debate axis 1 in the cluster's seeds) *(anticipatory-application)*. Because this study uses only a desktop-3D condition with no IVR or 2D comparison arm, it cannot itself adjudicate debate axis 5 ("desktop-3D as sufficient, cost-effective proxy for IVR ↔ IVR carries unique affordances") — the plan's own seed list names Merchant alongside Colin et al. and the Makransky-equivalence study as evidence for the "desktop-3D is sufficient" pole, but that framing over-reads this paper's actual scope: Merchant et al. never compare their Second Life environment's outcomes against an IVR condition, so this unit's evidentiary contribution to axis 5 is at most indirect — it establishes that desktop-3D VR *by itself* can drive meaningful spatial and self-efficacy gains, which is a necessary but not sufficient premise for the "sufficient proxy" claim, not a demonstration of equivalence to IVR *(anticipatory-application, corrective note)*. The usability-as-mediator finding bridges to `vle-01-deployment-survey`: Part III's own VLE deployment sits within a desktop-3D-adjacent media condition, and this unit's evidence that usability/ease-of-use gates *all* downstream perceptual and psychological effects (not merely a nuisance variable) is directly relevant whenever Part III's survey findings on student engagement or self-efficacy are interpreted without first checking whether usability confounds are present *(anticipatory-application)*. The cognitive-load framing of usability (extraneous load absorbed by interface navigation, potentially crowding out the germane load needed to convert presence into learning) also offers a candidate mechanism for Part III's own "WE unmet want" reading corrected elsewhere in the survey analysis — usability/interface friction is a plausible confound to rule out before treating any null or attenuated engagement finding as evidence about deployment context per se *(anticipatory-application)*.
