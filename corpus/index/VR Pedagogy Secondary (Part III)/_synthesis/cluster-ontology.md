# Cluster-Level Ontology — VR Pedagogy Secondary (Part III)

The conceptual architecture of a 34-unit secondary-literature cluster spanning presence theory, VR-learning
frameworks, media-comparison experiments, simulation-based professional education, field reviews, and — added
2026-07-15 by explicit user instruction — the CMC social-presence tradition and its VR-collaborative extensions.
Loci are the cited source's own page numbers. Provenance tags: **FE** = the source's own published claim;
**FAITHFUL-\<author\>** = close paraphrase of a specific passage; **P/anticipatory-application** = *our*
interpretive link, never the source's own claim.

Companion files: `debate-map.md` (10 documented disagreements), `vr-pedagogy-construct-condition-concordance.md`
(the construct × media-condition grid), `concept-matrix.csv` (source × canonical-node presence),
`vr-pedagogy-terminology-appendix.md` (lemmas), `citation-network.md` (intra-cluster + external hubs).

## 1. The governing problem: one word, two research programs that only partly know about each other

This cluster's structure was set by its plan (23 units: presence theory, VR-learning frameworks, media-comparison
experiments, simulation education, field reviews). Its final shape was set by a mid-build addition: eleven sources
on **social presence** — the CMC/distance-education tradition running from Short, Williams & Christie's 1976
communication theory through Gunawardena's 1997 scale, Garrison/Anderson/Archer's 1999 Community of Inquiry
framework, to Richardson et al.'s 2017 meta-analysis — plus two VR-specific pieces meant to bridge that tradition
into the cluster's existing immersive-learning literature.

The two programs share almost nothing but a construct name and a shared debt to the same 1976 theory. The
VR-pedagogy program (Strands A–E) asks: *does adding immersion to a learning task change what/how much someone
learns, and through what mechanism?* Its instruments are EEG, eye-tracking, learning-outcome tests, and SEM
path models; its unit of comparison is the medium. The CMC social-presence program (Strand F) asks: *does feeling
that other people are meaningfully "there" in a mediated communication predict a learner's satisfaction and
perceived learning?* Its instrument is the self-report scale item; its unit of comparison is different
communication-channel designs within the *same* medium (text vs. audio vs. video conferencing), not media in the
VR-pedagogy sense at all.

**The cluster's central structural finding, discovered only at Phase 2, is that the bridge between these two
programs the plan's extension anticipated mostly does not exist in the literature itself.** Neither of Strand F's
two VR-specific pieces (ped-sec-33, ped-sec-34) cites the CMC-tradition canon (Short/Williams/Christie, Garrison,
Gunawardena) or even uses the term "social presence" more than glancingly. The bridge this cluster's plan wanted is
real as a research *gap*, not as a citation network — and documenting that gap honestly, rather than papering over
it with fabricated bridge edges, is this synthesis's first obligation (see §5).

## 2. The construct-axis skeleton (Strands A–E) — presence, affect, cognition, outcome

Fourteen canonical constructs (plan §3) organize Strands A–E. Three load-bearing findings recur across strands:

**2.1 Presence is not one thing, and the cluster inherits both halves of that split.** Lombard & Ditton's
six-conceptualization taxonomy (ped-sec-01, FE) already contains the fault line the rest of the cluster argues
over: presence-as-realism/transportation/immersion (form/content-driven) versus presence-as-social-richness/medium
(user-and-context-driven). Witmer & Singer (ped-sec-02) instantiate the first reading — their own Discussion
states presence "is something the individual experiences" against Slater's objective-technology account
(FAITHFUL-Witmer&Singer) — while Chow's SEM (ped-sec-03) and, from Strand F, Gunawardena & Zittle's "presence can
be cultured" finding (ped-sec-25, FE) instantiate the second. This is debate axis 3, and it is this cluster's most
heavily populated debate (10 edges across both halves of the cluster — see `debate-map.md` DA-03).

**2.2 More immersion does not reliably mean more learning, and the cluster's own empirical core proves it
directly.** Nine units feed debate axis 1. Makransky et al.'s own title (ped-sec-10, FE) states the finding
outright: adding IVR to a science-lab simulation raised presence but lowered learning relative to desktop PC on
an *identical* task. Parong & Mayer (ped-sec-08) and Parong & Mayer (ped-sec-13) supply convergent
motivation/outcome dissociations and a cognitive-load mechanism; Merchant et al. (ped-sec-16) find the identical
presence-generated-but-learning-null pattern *within* desktop-3D alone, showing the dissociation is not
IVR-specific. Mayer et al.'s review (ped-sec-07) aggregates this into a negligible-to-negative median effect
across 13 media-comparison experiments (FE). Only Radianti et al. (ped-sec-22) and, earlier and more
conceptually, Dalgarno & Lee (ped-sec-04) and Fowler (ped-sec-05) occupy the affordance-optimism pole — and
Radianti's own optimism is explicitly about *underexploited potential* given the field's evaluation weaknesses,
not a disagreement with the null-effect data itself.

**2.3 CAMIL (ped-sec-06) is the cluster's synthesis node, not just another unit.** Makransky & Petersen's model
structurally contains both poles of debate axis 4 (affective route vs. cognitive-load route) as simultaneous,
non-exclusive paths from the same antecedents (presence, agency, embodiment), generalizing rather than choosing
between ped-sec-09's affective-route SEM and ped-sec-13's cognitive-load-route mediation finding
(P/anticipatory-application). CAMIL also makes an explicit methodological intervention into debate axis 2:
abandon media-comparison designs for interaction-focused designs (FE) — the same position Mayer et al. (ped-sec-07)
argue on different (meta-analytic) grounds. Between them, CAMIL and Mayer et al. 2022 are this cluster's two most
consequential theoretical exports to Part III.

## 3. Strand D/E: simulation and review literature — a narrower, calmer debate

Strand D's professional-education simulation literature (ped-sec-15 through ped-sec-20) largely does not dispute
*whether* simulation helps (Cook et al.'s meta-analytic pooled effects — knowledge ES=1.20, skills ES=1.09–1.18 —
are large and undisputed within the strand); its live tension is narrower: simulation-vs-no-intervention (strongly
positive, Cook et al./ped-sec-15) versus sim-design-vs-sim-design (negligible differences, Kononowicz et al./
ped-sec-19, explicitly following up on the comparison Cook et al. itself called for as future work). This is
debate axis 6, and it is this cluster's calmest disagreement — both poles are held by meta-analyses, not
contested single studies.

Strand E's reviews (Potkonjak et al./ped-sec-21, Radianti et al./ped-sec-22, Cossio et al./ped-sec-23) function as
the cluster's field-level status reports. Cossio et al. is notable for refusing the alarmist framing debate axis 7
might invite: roughly half of the 25 included cybersickness studies report genuinely low/inconsistent symptom
scores alongside a real minority of high-symptom cases — a measured, not a verdict-delivering, stance
(P/anticipatory-application).

## 4. Strand F: the social-presence tradition, on its own terms

Strand F's nine units divide cleanly into a CMC-tradition core (ped-sec-25–32, 1997–2019) and two VR-application
pieces (ped-sec-33, ped-sec-34, 2021/2023) whose relationship to that core turned out to be much weaker than the
plan anticipated (see §5).

**4.1 The core's own internal debate mirrors debate axis 3 from Strands A–E, independently arrived at.**
Gunawardena & Zittle (ped-sec-25) open within Short, Williams & Christie's original medium-determinist framing but
their own data — "presence can be cultured" — pulls toward the psychological/communicator-determined pole
(FE). Garrison, Anderson & Archer (ped-sec-26) make the rejection explicit and named: they reject media-richness
theory's medium-determines-presence claim outright, relocating social presence in communication context —
familiarity, skills, motivation, duration (FAITHFUL-Garrison,Anderson&Archer). Terry & Doolittle (ped-sec-31) side
with the psychological-construct pole most directly of all nine units while flagging the entangled middle ground
the Community of Inquiry model occupies. This is debate axis 3's Strand-F wing, arrived at through a completely
different empirical tradition (text-conferencing transcripts, not VR experiments) than Strands A–E's — a genuine
convergence, not a coincidence of vocabulary (P/anticipatory-application).

**4.2 The Community of Inquiry (CoI) framework (ped-sec-26) is Strand F's hub, and it is itself contested from
within the strand.** Terry & Doolittle's 2019 "re-examination" (ped-sec-31) both builds on CoI's own definition of
social presence ("real people" projecting themselves) and explicitly criticizes the model, in the authors' own
voice, for entangling social presence with cognitive presence, teaching presence, and (via a third source, Shea
2010) learning presence until the construct becomes diffuse (FE). This is recorded as both an `extends-reading-of`
and a `contests-reading-of` edge rather than forced into one relation, since the source itself does both.

**4.3 Measurement critique is Strand F's second live thread, and it happened twice, independently, in the same
year.** Biocca, Burgoon & Harms (ped-sec-29) and Tu (ped-sec-27) each diagnose Short et al.'s original
semantic-differential measure — and, separately, Gunawardena & Zittle's SPRES fix — as inadequate, in 2002/2003,
without citing each other (confirmed absent via full-text check). This parallel, uncited convergence is itself
evidence that the measurement problem was structurally overdue for correction, independent of any single author's
argument (P/anticipatory-application). Kreijns, Kirschner & Jochems (ped-sec-30) supply this cluster's clearest
skeptical-pole source for the new debate axis 9 (social presence's effects as inconsistent/assumed-not-demonstrated),
positioned against Gunawardena & Zittle's and Richardson & Swan's more affirmative empirical findings.

**4.4 Richardson et al.'s 2017 meta-analysis (ped-sec-32) is the strand's capstone**, quantifying across 25
accumulated studies what the tradition's individual papers (including the same lead author's own 2003 study,
ped-sec-28) had argued qualitatively — though the Phase-2 build found it does *not* cite ped-sec-29 or ped-sec-30
under those exact titles, a citation-network gap worth flagging rather than smoothing over.

## 5. The bridge that mostly wasn't there — this cluster's most important finding

Both of Strand F's VR-specific units were selected, at G0-adjacent user instruction, to connect the CMC
social-presence tradition to the cluster's existing VR/immersive literature. Verification during Phase 2 found:

- **De Back, Tinga & Louwerse (ped-sec-33)** never uses the term "social presence" and cites none of the
  CMC-tradition canon. Its actual interlocutors are the VR-pedagogy-framework literature already elsewhere in this
  cluster — Dalgarno & Lee (ped-sec-04), Fowler (ped-sec-05), Parong & Mayer (ped-sec-08), Merchant et al.
  (ped-sec-16) — plus Heeter and Steuer for general spatial presence. It is, honestly described, a VLE-design
  study (cognitive load, collaborative-learning affordances, a CAVE-based neuroanatomy environment, n=40/n=158)
  that happens to sit in Strand F by editorial placement, not by its own argument.
- **VanderMeer et al. (ped-sec-34)** is the stronger of the two candidates — it cites a companion De Back paper
  directly and surveys VR-collaborative learning broadly — but it too does not engage the CMC-tradition canon by
  name.

**This is recorded as a genuine debate axis (DA-10: social presence as medium-general vs. VR social presence as
qualitatively different) rather than concealed**, because the absence of citation traffic between two literatures
that use the same construct name and could plausibly inform each other is itself the finding Part III most needs
from this half of the cluster: *if* Part III wants to describe VR co-presence using CMC social-presence vocabulary,
that move will be this cluster's own synthesis, not a citation the field has already made (P/anticipatory-application).

## 6. The evolutionary arc, in one line

Presence-concept foundations and instrument-building (1976–1998, Strands A + F's Gunawardena) → affordance
optimism and framework-building (1999–2014, Strand B + Garrison/Anderson/Archer) → the media-comparison reckoning
and dual-path process models (2016–2021, Strand C + CAMIL) → consolidation, boundary conditions, and the
unresolved CMC-VR seam (2019–2025, Strands D/E + Strand F's capstone and bridge pieces). See
`vr-pedagogy-scholarly-evolution.md`.

- **units**: ped-sec-02, ped-sec-03
- **centrality**: supporting

#### 38. Extraneous processing / seductive details
- **definition**: Task-irrelevant cognitive load induced by immersive/high-fidelity stimuli, competing with germane processing.
- **type**: TERM
- **units**: ped-sec-07, ped-sec-08, ped-sec-13
- **centrality**: supporting

#### 39. Methods-not-media (Clark's distinction)
- **definition**: Clark's (1983) argument that instructional method, not delivery medium, drives learning outcomes; extended by ped-sec-12 to physical deployment context.
- **type**: TERM
- **units**: ped-sec-07, ped-sec-12
- **centrality**: supporting

#### 40. Simulator Sickness Questionnaire (SSQ)
- **definition**: The standard cybersickness self-report instrument, originally designed for flight-simulator contexts; its fitness for HMD-VR is contested by Cossio et al.
- **type**: TERM
- **units**: ped-sec-23
- **centrality**: supporting

#### 41. Value-added research design
- **definition**: A study design that manipulates instructional features *within* a medium rather than comparing across media — the design paradigm Mayer et al. and CAMIL both recommend over media-comparison.
- **type**: TERM
- **units**: ped-sec-06, ped-sec-07
- **centrality**: supporting

#### 42. Deployment context (home / classroom / lab)
- **definition**: The physical/institutional setting in which a VR or desktop-3D learning intervention is delivered; the direct empirical analog of Part III's own lab-vs-deployment poles.
- **type**: TERM
- **units**: ped-sec-12
- **centrality**: core

#### 43. Common-method-variance risk

- **type**: TERM
- **units**: ped-sec-11

#### 45. Intimacy / immediacy (SPRES sub-factors)

#### 46. Automatic-interaction assumption (Pitfall 1)

- **units**: ped-sec-30

#### 47. Cognitive-only restriction (Pitfall 2)

#### 48. Presence as an existential orientation / individual-differences reading

#### 49. EEG workload / overload index

- **units**: ped-sec-10
