# Contested Readings — VR Pedagogy Secondary (Part III)

18 instances where a unit explicitly contests another source's reading, measurement practice, or theoretical
claim — distinct from `debate-map.md`'s broader field-level disagreements, these are point-specific contests
recorded in individual units' `contests-reading-of`/`contests` edges. Provenance: **FE** = the contesting
source's own claim; **P/anticipatory-application** = this cluster's own interpretive construction.

---

**CR-01.** Biocca, Burgoon & Harms (ped-sec-29) contest **Short et al.'s (1976) semantic-differential
social-presence measure**: the instrument is medium-oriented rather than state-oriented, shaped more by its
funders' media-effectiveness interests than by theoretical commitment, and vulnerable to embedded assumptions
(FE).

**CR-02.** Biocca, Burgoon & Harms (ped-sec-29) and Tu (ped-sec-27) independently and uncitedly diagnose the same
measurement lineage — Short et al.'s scale and Gunawardena & Zittle's SPRES — as inadequate for CMC/social
contexts, within a year of each other (P/anticipatory-application). Neither cites the other; this is recorded as
a parallel-discovery contest, not a direct exchange.

**CR-03.** Cossio et al. (ped-sec-23) contest the fitness of the **Simulator Sickness Questionnaire (SSQ)** for
HMD-VR contexts: it was designed for flight-simulator conditions (fixed seated posture, non-stereoscopic image,
no head tracking), and its psychometric properties are argued unsuited to measuring cybersickness in a
freely-moving HMD user (FE).

**CR-04.** Dubovi et al. (ped-sec-18) contest **Cook, Erwin & Triola's negligible-difference finding** for
non-immersive VR in healthcare education: Dubovi et al.'s own strong positive result runs counter to it, aligning
instead with Consorti et al. (2012) (FE).

**CR-05.** Fowler (ped-sec-05) contests **Dalgarno & Lee's model (ped-sec-04)** on two fronts: (1) names it
technology-led, deriving learning benefits from technical affordances rather than starting from pedagogical
requirements; (2) — see also `debate-map.md` DA-01 — raises an inverted-U caution against assuming more fidelity
monotonically helps.

**CR-06.** Garrison, Anderson & Archer (ped-sec-26) explicitly reject **media-richness theory's**
medium-determines-presence position, relocating social presence in communication context — familiarity, skills,
motivation, commitment, duration (FAITHFUL-Garrison,Anderson&Archer).

**CR-07.** Kononowicz et al. (ped-sec-19) contest **media-comparative research validity** for virtual-patient
studies: joining Friedman and Cook, the review's own explicit methodological verdict is that VP-vs-traditional-
format comparisons are largely uninterpretable and should be abandoned (FAITHFUL-Kononowicz et al.).

**CR-08.** Kreijns, Kirschner & Jochems (ped-sec-30) contest **purely cognitive/functional CSCL design**: their
own closing critique holds that environments supporting only cognitive/task processes forget that participants
are social beings seeking affiliation as well as information (FAITHFUL-Kreijns et al.).

**CR-09.** Makransky & Petersen (ped-sec-11) contest **Makransky & Lilleholt's (2018) subjective-outcome
methodology**: prior desktop-VR SEM models risk common-method-variance inflation from same-administration
subjective mediator/outcome measurement — a methodological self-correction within the same research program.

**CR-10.** Makransky et al. equivalence study (ped-sec-12) contests the **social-constructivist
context-centrality view**: the authors explicitly distinguish their narrower "context = physical location" finding
from social-constructivist views holding that the context of learning is central to learning itself.

**CR-11.** Makransky et al. presence-but-less-learning (ped-sec-10) contests **high-immersion-favorable
media-comparison findings** (Alhalabi 2016; Passig et al. 2016; Webster 2016): the study's own results are
explicitly positioned as diverging from these newer studies reporting learning benefits for high-immersion VR,
while aligning with earlier lower-immersion-favoring work (FE).

**CR-12.** Merchant et al. (ped-sec-16) is itself contested by the plan's own framing: the plan's debate-axis
seed 5 names Merchant as evidence for the "desktop-3D sufficient" pole, but this study runs no IVR comparison
condition and cannot itself adjudicate that question (P/anticipatory-application) — a contest of the plan's own
characterization, not of another source.

**CR-13.** Parong & Mayer cognitive-affective (ped-sec-13) contests the **IVR-affective-route-as-straightforwardly-
facilitative** proponent reading (e.g., Bailenson): this unit's own mediation finding — positive/high-arousal
emotion significantly mediates *lower* retention in IVR — complicates the claim that IVR's affective payoff
straightforwardly helps learning.

**CR-14.** Potkonjak et al. (ped-sec-21) contests the **virtual-labs-as-full-replacement-for-hands-on-training**
reading: the authors' own hedged conclusion is that virtual labs are an initial-step complement to, not a full
replacement for, real-equipment training, given the current state of the art (FAITHFUL-Potkonjak et al.).

**CR-15.** Radianti et al. (ped-sec-22) contests the reading that **the field's low-maturity evaluation record is
a ceiling on IVR's value**: Radianti's own reading is that the evaluation gaps (theory-thin design, usability-over-
outcomes focus) are a fixable research-agenda item, not a verdict against immersive VR's potential
(P/anticipatory-application).

**CR-16.** Terry & Doolittle (ped-sec-31) contest **the Community of Inquiry model (ped-sec-26)**: in the authors'
own voice, CoI's entanglement of social presence with cognitive presence, teaching presence, and (via a third
source, Shea 2010) learning presence is judged to broaden and arguably convolute social presence's definition —
recorded alongside an `extends-reading-of` edge to the same unit, since the source both builds on and criticizes
CoI.

**CR-17.** Tu (ped-sec-27) contests **Gunawardena & Zittle's (1997) social-presence instrument (ped-sec-25)**:
judges the SPRES scale an improvement over Short et al. that nonetheless remains incomplete (FE, p.34, 39).

**CR-18.** Witmer & Singer (ped-sec-02) state explicit disagreement with **Slater's objective-technology account
of immersion**, holding instead that "immersion, like involvement and presence, is something the individual
experiences" (FAITHFUL-Witmer&Singer).

## Pattern summary

Three clusters of contest emerge:

1. **Measurement-instrument contests (CR-01, CR-02, CR-03, CR-09, CR-17)** — five of eighteen contests target an
   instrument's validity or a prior study's own methodology, concentrated in Strand F (social-presence scales) and
   Strand E (cybersickness measurement). This is the single most common contest type in the cluster.
2. **Model-scope contests (CR-05, CR-06, CR-12, CR-16, CR-18)** — contests of whether a theoretical model's own
   causal account (technology-determined vs. psychologically-determined presence; media-richness; CoI's construct
   boundaries) is correctly scoped. These feed directly into debate axis 3.
3. **Empirical-generalization contests (CR-04, CR-07, CR-10, CR-11, CR-13, CR-14, CR-15)** — contests of whether a
   specific empirical finding generalizes as broadly as another source (or the plan itself) assumed. These are the
   most Part-III-relevant contests: they model exactly the kind of overclaiming this dissertation must avoid when
   citing single studies.
