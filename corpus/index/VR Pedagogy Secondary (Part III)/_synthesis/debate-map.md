# Debate Map — VR Pedagogy Secondary (Part III)

Ten documented cross-source disagreements. Each entry states both poles, the sources on each side, the resolution
status, and a note on relevance to Part III. Provenance: **FE** = source's own claim; **FAITHFUL-\<author\>** =
close paraphrase; **P/anticipatory-application** = this cluster's own interpretive construction.

---

### DA-01. More immersion → more learning ↔ Immersion adds presence but not learning

**Pole A (immersion helps):** Dalgarno & Lee (ped-sec-04, conceptual origin), Fowler (ped-sec-05, qualified —
inverted-U caution), Radianti et al. (ped-sec-22, affordance-optimism given field's underexploited potential).

**Pole B (immersion doesn't help, or hurts):** Makransky et al. (ped-sec-10, FE — the axis's title-bearing primary
source: IVR raised presence but *lowered* learning vs. desktop PC on an identical task), Parong & Mayer
(ped-sec-08, motivation/outcome dissociation d=0.92–1.12 favoring slideshow on learning), Makransky & Lilleholt
(ped-sec-09, nuances the axis — immersion affects learning only indirectly via affect/cognition, never as a direct
effect), Makransky & Petersen (ped-sec-11, cites but does not itself test the debate), Merchant et al. (ped-sec-16,
structural parallel *within* desktop-3D alone), Mayer et al. (ped-sec-07, FE — negligible-to-negative median effect
across 13 media-comparison experiments), VanderMeer et al. (ped-sec-34, stages the HMD-arousal-advantage vs.
HMD-distraction findings against each other).

**Resolution status:** Not resolved, but heavily weighted toward Pole B by this cluster's own empirical core — six
independent units, including two meta-analytic/review-level sources, land on "immersion raises presence without
raising learning, sometimes lowering it." Pole A survives mainly as an earlier conceptual/affordance-theoretic
position (2009–2014) and a field-level optimism-about-potential reading (Radianti 2020), not as a competing
empirical result.

**Part III relevance:** Directly informs how strongly Part III should expect its own VR pole to outperform its
desktop-3D/2D strata on learning outcomes — this cluster's evidence says: don't expect it to, on learning; do
expect it on presence/affect.

### DA-02. Media-comparison designs are informative ↔ Methods-not-media / value-added is the valid paradigm

**Pole A (media-comparison informative):** Radianti et al. (ped-sec-22, implicitly, by sorting inclusion criteria
on technology type).

**Pole B (methods-not-media):** Mayer et al. (ped-sec-07, FE — the PRIMARY source: 20 years of media-comparison
work yielded no reliable IVR advantage, while value-added work reliably improved IVR's own effectiveness),
Makransky & Petersen/CAMIL (ped-sec-06, FE — CAMIL's own explicit methodological recommendation: abandon
media-comparison for interaction-focused designs), De Back et al. (ped-sec-33, resonates via its own
media-comparison-vs.-within-condition Study 1/Study 2 split, without resolving the tension itself).

**Resolution status:** Effectively resolved within this cluster toward Pole B — the two most theoretically weighty
units (CAMIL, Mayer et al. 2022) both argue for abandoning pure media-comparison, on independent (meta-analytic vs.
theoretical-modeling) grounds.

**Part III relevance:** A methodological caution for how Part III itself should interpret its own desktop-3D vs.
video-only strata contrast — this cluster's consensus view says a pure media-comparison reading risks
under-crediting *within-medium* design factors.

### DA-03. Presence as technology-determined ↔ Presence as psychologically/individually determined

**Pole A (technology-determined):** Witmer & Singer (ped-sec-02, FAITHFUL — though notably *rejects* Slater's
fully objective-technology account even while emphasizing system fidelity), early Short/Williams/Christie framing
as inherited by Gunawardena & Zittle (ped-sec-25) before their own data complicates it.

**Pole B (psychologically/individually determined):** Chow (ped-sec-03, SEM determinants), Lombard & Ditton
(ped-sec-01, FE — two-track causal model: form/content variables *and* media-user variables — willingness to
suspend disbelief, prior experience, personality), Garrison, Anderson & Archer (ped-sec-26, FE — explicit,
named rejection of media-richness theory's medium-determines-presence claim), Gunawardena & Zittle (ped-sec-25,
FE — "presence can be cultured"), Makransky & Lilleholt (ped-sec-09, presence as mediator toward affect, not a
technology-determined terminal state), Makransky & Petersen (ped-sec-11, cites Witmer & Singer's *joint*
technological-and-psychological account), Richardson & Swan (ped-sec-28, adopts Gunawardena's "culturing" revision
over the medium-determinist original), Richardson et al. meta-analysis (ped-sec-32, sides with the
communicator-determined pole via Gunawardena, while its own scale-type moderator finding shows measurement choice
itself shapes the result), Terry & Doolittle (ped-sec-31, sides with the psychological-construct pole most
directly of all nine Strand-F units).

**Resolution status:** This cluster's most heavily populated debate (10 units) and its clearest majority verdict —
only Witmer & Singer's 1998 origin position holds Pole A cleanly; every later unit that engages the axis, across
both Strands A–E and Strand F independently, moves toward Pole B. This is a genuine cross-tradition convergence:
the VR-pedagogy program and the CMC social-presence program reach the same conclusion via completely different
evidence.

**Part III relevance:** Supports treating presence/social-presence measures as sensitive to individual differences
and context (deployment setting, prior VR experience, motivation) rather than as a pure readout of system fidelity
— relevant to interpreting Part III's own lab-vs-deployment presence data.

### DA-04. Affective route ↔ Cognitive-load route

**Pole A (affective route — presence → enjoyment/motivation → learning):** Makransky & Lilleholt (ped-sec-09,
PRIMARY SOURCE, FE — quantified SEM path structure).

**Pole B (cognitive-load route — presence → distraction/extraneous load → less learning):** Parong & Mayer
(ped-sec-13, PRIMARY evidentiary anchor — positive/high-arousal emotion significantly mediates *lower* retention),
Makransky et al. (ped-sec-10, FE — EEG workload/overload results, this cluster's most direct mechanistic evidence
for the cognitive-load pole), Mayer et al. (ped-sec-07, empirical weight — 13 null/negative experiments plus
ped-sec-13's biometric evidence — favors this pole despite the paper's own dual-path framing).

**Synthesis (not a pole):** CAMIL (ped-sec-06) structurally contains *both* paths as simultaneous, non-exclusive,
from the same antecedents — generalizing rather than choosing between ped-sec-09 and ped-sec-13's findings
(P/anticipatory-application).

**Resolution status:** Not resolved as an either/or; CAMIL's dual-path architecture is this cluster's best-available
synthesis, and it is directly supported by the fact that the cognitive-load pole's evidence (EEG, mediation
analysis) is more mechanistically direct than the affective pole's (correlational SEM).

**Part III relevance:** Offers Part III two competing (and one synthesizing) accounts for *why* its own VR pole
might show high self-reported engagement without matching learning-outcome gains.

### DA-05. Desktop-3D is a sufficient, cost-effective proxy for IVR ↔ IVR carries unique affordances

**Pole A (desktop-3D sufficient):** Dalgarno & Lee (ped-sec-04, earliest source of the affordance-optimism
position later tested against), Dubovi et al. (ped-sec-18, fidelity/control account of presence supports this
pole, though with no direct IVR comparison — cannot itself adjudicate), Potkonjak et al. (ped-sec-21, entire
survey built on desktop-3D/non-HMD systems, implicitly siding with sufficiency for the current state of the art).

**Pole B (IVR unique):** No unit in this cluster cleanly occupies Pole B without qualification — Merchant et al.
(ped-sec-16) is named in the plan's own seed list for this axis but explicitly **cannot** adjudicate it (no IVR
comparison condition; recorded as a `contests-reading-of` edge against the plan's own framing rather than
fabricating a Pole-B stance the study doesn't support).

**Resolution status:** Effectively undecided within this cluster — the empirical units that could speak to it
(Colin et al./ped-sec-14, Makransky-equivalence/ped-sec-12) test IVR-vs-desktop-3D-vs-2D directly but their
findings feed DA-01 and DA-08 more than a clean sufficiency verdict. This is a genuine gap: this cluster's
sufficiency claims are mostly assertions of scope (studies that simply don't test IVR), not comparative findings.

**Part III relevance:** A caution against assuming this cluster settles the desktop-3D-as-VR-proxy question Part
III's own strata depend on — it does not, cleanly.

### DA-06. Simulation beats no-intervention strongly ↔ Sim-vs-sim differences negligible

**Pole A (strongly beats no-intervention):** Cook et al. (ped-sec-15, FE — pooled effect sizes: knowledge ES=1.20,
skills ES=1.09–1.18, behaviors ES=0.79–0.81, patient-effects moderate).

**Pole B (sim-vs-sim negligible):** Kononowicz et al. (ped-sec-19, P/anticipatory-application — explicitly the
follow-up comparison Cook et al. itself called for as future work; virtual-patient design-variant comparisons show
small/negligible differences), Roe et al. (ped-sec-20, descriptive, non-controlled evidence, contributing without
adjudicating).

**Resolution status:** This is not a live disagreement but a sequential research program — Pole A and Pole B are
answers to two *different* questions (does simulation help at all? vs. does simulation *design* matter?), both
affirmed by meta-analytic evidence, not in tension.

**Part III relevance:** Suggests Part III should expect large, robust simulation-vs-none effects but should not
expect strong effects from choosing one simulation design over another (e.g., desktop-3D vs. video-based) within
the simulation family.

### DA-07. Cybersickness/novelty as substantive moderator of IVR learning ↔ Ignorable side-effect

**Pole A / Pole B (both held, measuredly, by the same source):** Cossio et al. (ped-sec-23, FE — roughly half of
25 included studies report genuinely low/inconsistent cybersickness scores, alongside a real minority of
high-symptom cases; the review's own stance is explicitly measured, not alarmist).

**Resolution status:** Thinly documented (single source) but internally well-evidenced — Cossio et al. itself
refuses to resolve the axis toward either pole, which is the finding.

**Part III relevance:** A caution against both over- and under-weighting cybersickness as a confound in Part III's
own VR-pole data; expect it in a genuine minority of cases, not universally and not negligibly.

### DA-08. Deployment context matters (lab vs. home vs. classroom) ↔ Context-equivalence

**Pole A / Pole B:** Makransky et al. equivalence study (ped-sec-12, FE — PRIMARY empirical anchor: home/classroom
manipulation with medium held constant lands squarely on the context-equivalence pole, null d=0.02–0.14 across
three outcomes, robust to covariates).

**Resolution status:** Single-source but this cluster's most directly relevant finding to Part III's own poles —
context-equivalence, not context-matters, when medium is held constant.

**Part III relevance:** THE most consequential single finding in this cluster for Part III's lab=VR ↔
deployment=2D-PC design: it suggests that any differences Part III observes between its lab and deployment strata
are more likely attributable to the *medium change* (VR to 2D-PC) than to the *setting change* (lab to home/class)
per se — since this study found setting-alone made no difference when medium was constant.

### DA-09. Social presence reliably predicts satisfaction/learning in CMC ↔ Its effects are inconsistent, confounded, or assumed-not-demonstrated

*(Strand F, added 2026-07-15 with the cluster extension.)*

**Pole A (reliable predictor):** Gunawardena & Zittle (ped-sec-25, FE — the origin finding, R²≈.68–.71),
Richardson & Swan (ped-sec-28, FE), Richardson et al. meta-analysis (ped-sec-32, quantifies the relationship
across 25 studies).

**Pole B (inconsistent/assumed):** Biocca, Burgoon & Harms (ped-sec-29, FE — this article's own measurement
critique is the strongest single articulation of the skeptical pole: the field's instruments are medium-oriented
rather than state-oriented, shaped by funders' media-effectiveness interests, and vulnerable to embedded
assumptions), Kreijns, Kirschner & Jochems (ped-sec-30, FE — this cluster's clearest skeptical-pole source,
positioned directly against Gunawardena & Zittle and Richardson & Swan's affirmative program).

**Resolution status:** Not resolved — a genuine, live methodological disagreement within Strand F, with the
strand's own capstone meta-analysis (ped-sec-32) landing on Pole A empirically while two of the strand's most
theoretically rigorous critiques (ped-sec-29, ped-sec-30) argue the underlying measurement approach cannot support
that confidence.

**Part III relevance:** A direct caution for any Part III use of social-presence-style self-report items: the
correlational finding is real at meta-analytic scale, but its causal/measurement interpretation remains contested
by the tradition's own methodologists.

### DA-10. Social presence is medium-general (portable to VR) ↔ VR social presence is qualitatively different from CMC social presence

*(Strand F, added 2026-07-15 — this debate axis directly documents the cluster's central Phase-2 finding, §5 of
`cluster-ontology.md`.)*

**Pole A (medium-general):** Kreijns, Kirschner & Jochems (ped-sec-30, P/anticipatory-application — operationalize
social presence entirely within the text-based CMC tradition's channel-reduction model; this unit is this
cluster's clean representative of treating social presence as a portable construct).

**Pole B (VR-qualitatively-different, or at minimum: unconnected in practice):** De Back, Tinga & Louwerse
(ped-sec-33, P/anticipatory-application — full-text check confirms "social presence" never appears and no
CMC-tradition source is cited; the Strand-F "bridge" role assumed for this unit is this cluster's own construction,
not De Back et al.'s own claim), VanderMeer et al. (ped-sec-34, the stronger of the two VR bridge candidates but
still does not engage the CMC-tradition canon by name).

**Resolution status:** Not resolved, and arguably not yet a live debate *in the literature itself* — the two
"sides" are less a disagreement between engaged interlocutors than a documented absence of engagement. This
cluster's honest verdict: the bridge the plan anticipated is a research gap, not a citation network.

**Part III relevance:** The single most important methodological caution this cluster offers for any dissertation
use of "social presence" language to describe VR co-presence findings — that move is this cluster's (and Part
III's) own synthesis, not a citation the field has already validated.
