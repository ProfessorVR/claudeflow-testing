# Scholarly Evolution — VR Pedagogy Secondary (Part III)

A thematic arc, not merely a chronological one, across two research programs that share a construct name and a
distant common ancestor but developed largely independently until this cluster placed them side by side.

## I. Instrument-building and origin theory (1976–1998)

Both programs begin with the same 1976 seed — Short, Williams & Christie's social presence theory — but diverge
almost immediately. The **CMC program**'s first in-cluster move is **Gunawardena & Zittle (1997, ped-sec-25)**,
which operationalizes social presence with a new Likert scale (SPRES) as an alternative to Short et al.'s original
semantic-differential approach, and finds — against the origin theory's own medium-determinist framing — that
"presence can be cultured." The **VR-pedagogy program**'s parallel origin move is **Witmer & Singer (1998,
ped-sec-02)**, which builds the field's presence-measurement instrument (the PQ/ITQ) from the opposite theoretical
starting point: individual experience over objective system fidelity, explicitly against Slater's technology-
determinist account. Both programs, that is, begin by *rejecting* a purely medium-determined reading of presence
— independently, a year apart, in completely different literatures. **Lombard & Ditton (1997, ped-sec-01)**
supplies the field's foundational taxonomy spanning both readings at once, and is the single earliest source in
the whole cluster.

## II. Framework-building and affordance optimism (1999–2015)

The CMC program consolidates around **Garrison, Anderson & Archer's Community of Inquiry (1999, ped-sec-26)** —
still this strand's most-cited internal hub two decades later — which makes the technology-determinist rejection
explicit and named, relocating social presence in communication context rather than channel bandwidth. The
VR-pedagogy program spends this period building its own theoretical apparatus: **Dalgarno & Lee's affordances
model (2010, ped-sec-04)** and **Fowler's pedagogy-first critique of it (2015, ped-sec-05)** establish the
period's central tension — is VR-learning theory technology-led or pedagogy-led? — that debate axis 1 and 2 will
later test empirically. Both programs are, in this period, optimistic: CoI about text-based community-building,
Dalgarno & Lee about 3D-environment affordances. Neither has yet been tested against a large body of controlled
comparative data.

## III. The media-comparison reckoning and dual-path process models (2016–2021)

This is the cluster's most empirically dense period, almost entirely within the VR-pedagogy program. **Makransky
et al.'s "more presence but less learning" (2019, ped-sec-10)** is the period's landmark negative result, and it
is not isolated: **Parong & Mayer (2018, ped-sec-08)**, **Merchant et al. (2012, ped-sec-16, structural parallel
within desktop-3D alone)**, and **Parong & Mayer's cognitive-affective mediation study (2021, ped-sec-13)**
converge on the same dissociation from different angles. **Makransky & Petersen's CAMIL (2021, ped-sec-06)**
arrives at the end of this period as the synthesis this convergence demanded — a dual-path model that contains
both the affective route (**Makransky & Lilleholt, 2018, ped-sec-09**) and the cognitive-load route as
simultaneous, non-exclusive mechanisms, and that explicitly recommends abandoning pure media-comparison designs.
**Mayer et al.'s "Promise and Pitfalls" (2022, ped-sec-07)** — chronologically just past this window but
belonging to it thematically — delivers the period's verdict in review form: 20 years of media-comparison
research, no reliable advantage. The CMC program has no equivalent reckoning in this period; its own
methodological crisis (Section IV) is quieter and happens slightly later, on different terms.

## IV. Consolidation, boundary conditions, and the unresolved seam (2003–2025)

Two strands run in parallel through this final period, one within each program, and they never meet.

**Within the CMC program**, 2002–2003 sees a quiet, independent double-take on measurement: **Tu (2002,
ped-sec-27)** and **Biocca, Burgoon & Harms (2003, ped-sec-29)** each diagnose the field's instruments — Short et
al.'s original and Gunawardena's SPRES fix alike — as inadequate, without citing each other. **Kreijns, Kirschner
& Jochems (2003, ped-sec-30)** supplies the strand's clearest structural skepticism about social presence's
demonstrated effects. **Richardson & Swan (2003, ped-sec-28)** and, fourteen years later, **Richardson et al.'s
meta-analysis (2017, ped-sec-32)** anchor the affirmative empirical pole across this whole span — the same
research program, quantified at increasing scale. **Terry & Doolittle (2019, ped-sec-31)** closes the period with
an explicit re-examination that both extends and contests CoI, the strand's own foundational text.

**Within the VR-pedagogy program**, the same period is occupied by boundary-condition and constraint work:
**Makransky et al.'s home/classroom equivalence study (2019, ped-sec-12)** — this cluster's single most directly
relevant finding to Part III's own lab/deployment poles — establishes that setting alone, medium held constant,
makes no difference. **Cook et al. (2011, ped-sec-15)** and **Kononowicz et al. (2019, ped-sec-19)** run the
simulation-education program's own sequential reckoning: does simulation help at all (yes, strongly), does
simulation *design* matter (much less so). **Cossio et al. (2025, ped-sec-23)**, the cluster's most recent source,
closes this program's arc with a measured verdict on cybersickness — a real but non-universal constraint, refusing
both alarmism and dismissal.

**The seam.** Two units were selected specifically to close the gap between these two parallel tracks —
**De Back, Tinga & Louwerse (2021, ped-sec-33)** and **VanderMeer et al. (2023, ped-sec-34)**. Neither does.
Verification found neither cites the CMC-tradition canon or engages "social presence" as a named construct beyond
glancing use. The two programs' final-period consolidation happens on schedule, in parallel, on both sides — and
the bridge this cluster's own extension anticipated remains, honestly, a gap rather than a citation. That gap is
this cluster's most important finding for any dissertation use of social-presence vocabulary to describe VR
co-presence phenomena: the move from one literature's terms to the other's is Part III's own synthesis to make,
not one the field has already validated.
