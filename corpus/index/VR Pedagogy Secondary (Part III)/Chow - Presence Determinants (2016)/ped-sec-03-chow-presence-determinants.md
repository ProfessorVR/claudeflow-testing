# ped-sec-03 — Chow, "Determinants of Presence in 3D Virtual Worlds: A Structural Equation Modelling Analysis" (2016)

**Type:** empirical journal article (nursing education / educational-technology, technology-acceptance & presence research) · **Discipline:** educational technology, applied via nursing-simulation pedagogy · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** Strand A's dedicated user-characteristics study — the cluster's primary source for debate axis 3's "psychologically/individually determined" pole, standing opposite Witmer & Singer's (ped-sec-02) technology-determined Control/Sensory/Distraction/Realism framework.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Chow argues that presence research has disproportionately studied technology-side determinants — immersion, interactivity, perceptual realism — while user-characteristic determinants remain comparatively neglected, despite presence being, by definition, a psychological state of the user rather than a property of the medium alone (FE, p.1, p.4). The paper's guiding aim is to fill that gap by testing a structural-equation model in which three exogenous, individually-varying beliefs — subjective norm (SN), perceived ease of use (PEOU), and computer self-efficacy (CSE) — predict perceived usefulness (PU), and PU together with CSE directly predict self presence, among 185 nursing students using a Second Life (SL) virtual world built for personal-protective-equipment (PPE) training (FE, p.1, p.6-7). Chow frames the resulting model as "an initial theoretical model of the determinants of presence in a 3D virtual world" (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.16), intended less as a finished theory than as a first, empirically validated foothold for further research into who experiences presence strongly and why, independent of how immersive the technology itself is.

## 2. Structure

The article runs nine unlabeled but internally signposted sections.

1. **Introduction** (p.1) — frames 3D virtual worlds as an educational platform and states the user-characteristics gap the study addresses.
2. **The concept of presence** (p.2) — surveys Lombard & Ditton's (1997) six explications of presence and their "perceptual illusion of non-mediation" definition (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.2, quoting Lombard & Ditton p.9), then Lee's (2004) tripartite physical/social/self presence typology, narrowing the study's own focus to self presence; situates presence as "a human reaction to immersion" after Slater (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.2).
3. **Presence and learning in virtual 3D environments** (p.3) — reviews prior findings linking presence to learning outcomes (Mikropoulos & Natsis's ten-year review; Selverian & Hwang; Mikropoulos 2006), then motivates the PPE-in-SL design via the clinical-placement constraints nursing education faces (patient safety, limited exposure, ethical considerations).
4. **Determinants of presence** (p.3-4) — introduces the study's own two-category framework, media characteristics (form/content) versus user characteristics (cognitive, emotional, motivational), and argues the latter is understudied since Alsina-Jurnet & Gutierrez-Maldonado's (2010) five-factor study.
5. **Methodology — virtual world environment** (p.5-6) — describes the SL PPE-training simulation: a virtual dressing room, a ten-room ward, sequenced donning/doffing tasks, and an automated post-task performance log.
6. **Methodology — research model and hypotheses** (p.6-7) — derives the study's model from Dunnington's (2012) Nature and Determinants of Presence Model (NDPM) and Davis's (1989) Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), stating five hypotheses (H1-H5) linking SN, PEOU, and CSE to PU, and PU and CSE to presence.
7. **Methodology — instrument, sample and data collection** (p.7-8) — a 14-item, seven-point Likert instrument across five constructs, adapted from prior validated scales; N=185 undergraduate nursing students.
8. **Data analysis and results** (p.9-13) — a two-step SEM (AMOS 19.0): measurement model then structural model, both showing good fit; all five hypotheses supported at p<.001.
9. **Discussion and Conclusions** (p.12-14, p.16) — interprets CSE's dominant effect via Sweller's cognitive load theory, discusses PU's and SN's roles, states limitations (single institution, cross-sectional, voluntary/self-selected sample), and frames the model as a first step for future extension.

## 3. Constructs

- **embodiment / self-representation** — the study's sole dependent construct. Chow adopts Lee's (2004) "self presence": a virtual self experienced as the actual self, operationalized here via three avatar-movement items (feeling as if *you* were walking/moving/inside the ward when *your avatar* walked/moved/visited), adapted from Jin & Park's (2009) Wii avatar-interaction scale (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.2, p.7-8). PU and CSE jointly explain 52% of its variance (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.11-12). Chow's own term is "presence"/"self presence"; normalized here to embodiment/self-representation because the instrument items are avatar-motor-correspondence items rather than spatial or social-actor items.
- **self-efficacy** — computer self-efficacy (CSE): "a user's belief about his/her ability to perform a specific task using the technology" (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.7). Modeled as a direct antecedent of both PU and self presence; it is the single strongest total predictor of presence in the model (total effect 0.53, versus PU's 0.36) (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.13).
- **cognitive load (extraneous vs germane)** — not itself an SEM variable; invoked post-hoc in the Discussion via Sweller's (1988) cognitive load theory to explain why CSE dominates: users with low computer self-efficacy expend extraneous cognitive resources learning to navigate SL's unfamiliar interface, leaving fewer resources available for immersing in the PPE task itself (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.12).
- **presence (spatial/physical)** — treated only as typological backdrop. Chow reproduces Lee's physical/social/self division in the literature review, then explicitly narrows to self presence alone; physical presence itself is never modeled or measured (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.2).
- **social presence / co-presence** — likewise typological backdrop only, and explicitly excluded: Chow states the study's focus is self presence rather than social presence, motivated by "the increase in visual representations of self in avatar-based virtual worlds" (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.3). This exclusion is worth flagging for Strand F, whose CMC-tradition sources treat social presence as their own primary construct — the two strands should not be conflated when both appear in the same paragraph (P/anticipatory-application).
- **affect: enjoyment & intrinsic motivation** — appears only within the literature-review synthesis of others' findings: presence in situated virtual worlds is reported (citing deByl 2009) to nurture intrinsic motivation to learn beyond mere cognitive gain (FE, p.3, reporting a claim Chow does not test). Chow's own SEM contains no affect or motivation construct.
- **representational fidelity** — named as part of the "media characteristics" determinant category (media form: sensory fidelity, spatial-temporal match to user action, interactivity; media content: avatar representation, social realism, autonomous behaviors) in the study's own determinants-of-presence framework, then set aside as the already-well-studied half of the field so the paper can focus on the neglected user-characteristics half instead (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.3-4).
- **learning outcomes: procedural / skills** — design intent only, not an outcome finding. The PPE simulation is built as a procedural-skills trainer with an automated performance log, and the literature review cites prior work linking presence to learning outcomes generally, but Chow's own study collects no skills-assessment or performance data; presence is the study's sole dependent variable (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.4-6, p.7).

Not treated in this section: immersion as a directly modeled SEM variable (Slater is cited only for the general presence-is-a-reaction-to-immersion claim, p.2); cybersickness/physical discomfort; agency/interactivity as a distinct construct (PEOU brushes against it but measures ease of accomplishing a goal, not interactive control per se); situational interest; engagement/flow.

## 4. Conditions & design

**Platform and stimulus:** a custom-built Second Life environment — a virtual dressing room and a ten-single-patient-room ward — for training nursing students to select and don/doff personal protective equipment; desktop-accessed, avatar-mediated, no head-mounted display (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.5-6). This is the study's only condition: **desktop-3D / non-immersive VR**, with no comparison arm (no video, no in-person, no HMD condition).

**Sample:** N=185 undergraduate nursing students (67.6% female, ages 20-24; 12% first-year, 65% second-year, 23% final-year; 65% with no prior 3D-game/animation experience), recruited voluntarily following hands-on PPE workshops at a single institution (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.7-8).

**Instrument:** 14 items, seven-point Likert (1=strongly agree to 7=strongly disagree), across five constructs, each adapted from previously validated scales: SN (2 items, Taylor & Todd 1995), PEOU (3 items, Venkatesh & Davis 2000), CSE (2 items, Vijayasarathy 2004), PU (4 items, Venkatesh & Davis 2000), self presence (3 items, Jin & Park 2009) (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.7).

**Analysis:** two-step SEM in AMOS 19.0 — confirmatory-factor-analysis measurement model, then structural model (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.9). Measurement model fit: χ²/df=1.78, RMSEA=.06, NFI=.96, NNFI=.97, CFI=.98; adequate reliability (Cronbach's α .81-.94), convergent validity (AVE >.50 throughout, factor loadings .80-.97), and discriminant validity (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.9-10). Structural model fit: χ²/df=1.74, RMSEA=.06, NFI=.96, NNFI=.97, CFI=.98 (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.11).

**Effect directions:** all five hypotheses were supported at p<.001. SN (β=.32), PEOU (β=.44), and CSE (β=.21) jointly explain 65% of variance in PU; PU (β=.36) and CSE (β=.45 direct, .53 total including the CSE→PU→presence indirect path) jointly explain 52% of variance in self presence, with CSE the strongest overall predictor (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.11-13). This is the study's second condition in the concordance's own terms — **SEM / process model (no media contrast)** — a single-condition nomological network of antecedent-to-outcome paths rather than a between-media comparison.

## 5. Findings/Claims

- Computer self-efficacy has the strongest direct and total effect on self presence of any variable in the model, exceeding perceived usefulness (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.11, p.13).
- Perceived ease of use is the strongest of the three predictors of perceived usefulness, followed by subjective norm, then computer self-efficacy (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.13).
- All five hypothesized paths are statistically supported: SN→PU, PEOU→PU, CSE→PU, CSE→presence, PU→presence (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.11).
- Sweller's cognitive load theory supplies the study's own explanatory mechanism for CSE's dominant effect: low computer self-efficacy consumes cognitive resources on interface navigation that would otherwise support immersion in the task (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.12) — a claim offered as interpretation, not itself tested by the SEM.
- Practical implication drawn by Chow: because presence depends more on user confidence and perceived value than the paper's own results can attribute to technological immersion, virtual-world designers and educators should invest in longer introductory/orientation sessions and on/off-line support to build computer self-efficacy, rather than relying solely on richer technological fidelity (FAITHFUL-Chow, p.12-13).
- The model is explicitly presented as a first test, not a settled account: single-institution, cross-sectional, voluntary/self-selected sample, and needing experimental (not merely correlational/SEM) follow-up (FE, p.13).

## 6. Concordance feed

- **embodiment/self-representation × desktop-3D/non-immersive VR** — self presence measured via three avatar-motor-correspondence items in the SL PPE simulation (p.7-8, p.5-6).
- **embodiment/self-representation × SEM/process model** — PU and CSE jointly explain 52% of self-presence variance (p.11-12).
- **self-efficacy × SEM/process model** — CSE's total effect on presence (0.53) is the model's strongest path (p.11, p.13).
- **cognitive load (extraneous vs germane) × SEM/process model** — Sweller-derived post-hoc explanation of CSE's dominance; not itself an SEM path (p.12).
- **presence (spatial/physical) × SEM/process model** — typological mention only (Lee's tripartite division); not modeled (p.2).
- **social presence/co-presence × SEM/process model** — typological mention only, explicitly excluded from the study's focus (p.2-3).
- **representational fidelity × desktop-3D/non-immersive VR** — named as a media-characteristics determinant category in the lit review, then set aside unmeasured (p.3-4).
- **affect: enjoyment & intrinsic motivation × SEM/process model** — mentioned only via a cited prior finding (deByl 2009), not part of Chow's own model (p.3).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* This unit is the cluster's primary grounding for debate axis 3's individually-determined pole: where Witmer & Singer (ped-sec-02) group presence's contributing factors into Control, Sensory, Distraction, and Realism — properties of the technology — Chow's explicit two-category split (media characteristics versus user characteristics) and single-minded focus on the empirically neglected user side instantiate the opposing pole, even though Chow never cites Witmer & Singer's 1998 Presence Questionnaire paper directly (only their 2005 factor-structure follow-up, p.6) — this opposition is this cluster's own alignment of the two units, not an authorial cross-reference *(anticipatory-application)*. For Part III, Chow's finding that computer self-efficacy is the single strongest predictor of self presence offers an individual-determinant lens on the VLE deployment survey's F1 threshold-foreclosure finding (install/hardware friction gating the video-only stratum's access) and its F7 presence-and-the-WE finding (self-reported presence far higher among app-users than video-only users): low computer self-efficacy is a plausible unmeasured confound behind part of that stratification, distinct from the technology-side friction the survey documents directly, and worth naming as a limitation rather than as a resolved explanation *(anticipatory-application)*. Separately, Chow's narrow, explicit restriction to self presence (avatar-as-self) rather than social presence (co-presence with others) sharpens rather than answers Part III's F7 finding that students wanted more "watching with others": that gap is a social/co-presence phenomenon outside anything Chow's own model tests, a caution against reading Chow's self-presence determinants as if they explained social presence too *(anticipatory-application)*. Finally, Chow's own account treats presence as a variable to be predicted from user-side antecedents rather than as a fixed function of technological immersion, which is broadly compatible with — but methodologically distinct from — Strand C's later media-comparison designs (Makransky et al.); the two literatures should be cited for different claims (individual antecedents vs. media effects) rather than merged.

**Not treated:** immersion as a directly modeled variable, cybersickness/physical discomfort, agency/interactivity as its own construct, situational interest, engagement/flow, and any direct measurement of learning outcomes (retention, transfer, or procedural/skills) — the PPE simulation is designed as a skills trainer, but the study measures presence alone, not performance.
