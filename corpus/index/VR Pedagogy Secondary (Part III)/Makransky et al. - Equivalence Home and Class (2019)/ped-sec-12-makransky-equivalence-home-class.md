# ped-sec-12 — Makransky, Mayer, Veitch, Hood, Christensen & Gadegaard, "Equivalence of Using a Desktop Virtual Reality Science Simulation at Home and in Class" (2019)

**Type:** empirical journal article (between-subjects experiment) · **Discipline:** educational psychology / science education · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** Strand C's dedicated deployment-context study — the cluster's single most direct empirical analogue to Part III's own lab-versus-deployment poles, and the primary source for debate axis 8.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

The article tests whether the "equivalence hypothesis" — Clark's methods-not-media thesis extended from instructional *medium* to physical *context* — holds when the same high-fidelity desktop-VR science simulation is completed unsupervised at home versus supervised in a classroom (FE, Abstract). The authors' own framing states the theory precisely: learning depends on the learner's cognitive activity as primed by the instructional method (here, the simulation itself), not on the medium or the physical setting that delivers it; if the same cognitive activity is primed in both settings, learning and motivational outcomes should not differ (FAITHFUL-Makransky, p.2). The study's aim is narrower and more targeted than the theory-building units of Strand B — it is a single controlled test of context-equivalence, with two secondary questions asking whether that equivalence holds across levels of prior knowledge and across three forms of achievement-goal orientation (FE, pp.3-4).

## 2. Structure

The article follows a standard empirical-report arc, unlabeled by numbered sections in the running text: an Introduction motivating the shift of lab activities online (citing Labster's commercial deployment and MOOC growth), a two-part theory section ("What is the equivalence hypothesis?" / "What is the educational importance...?") that states Clark's methods-not-media extension, two moderator-framing subsections on prior knowledge (expertise-reversal literature) and goal orientation (Pintrich's mastery/performance-approach/performance-avoid triad), a Materials and Methods section (design, sample, materials, procedure), a Results section organized around three research questions (basic-characteristics check, primary outcome differences, covariate-adjusted robustness), and a Discussion divided explicitly into "Empirical," "Practical," and "Theoretical contributions" subsections plus "Limitations and future directions" (FE, throughout).

## 3. Constructs

- **presence (spatial/physical)** — invoked only in the Discussion as the *proposed mediating mechanism* for the equivalence finding, not as a measured variable: the authors reason that a well-designed VR simulation induces a psychological state of presence strong enough that the virtual context itself becomes the primary learning context, making physical location "less important" (FAITHFUL-Makransky, p.10, citing Lee 2004's definition of presence as virtual objects/actors/self experienced as actual). This is explicitly theoretical, not operationalized in this study (FE).
- **affect: enjoyment & intrinsic motivation** — measured directly via a 5-item scale adapted from Deci & Ryan (FAITHFUL-Makransky, p.6).
- **self-efficacy** — measured via an 8-item scale adapted from the Motivated Strategies for Learning Questionnaire (MSLQ) (FAITHFUL-Makransky, p.6).
- **learning outcomes: retention & transfer** — measured via a 20-item multiple-choice test (10 retention, 10 transfer), Rasch-validated down to 16 fitting items (FAITHFUL-Makransky, p.6).
- **prior knowledge and goal orientation as moderators** — not row-axis constructs proper but treated as covariates/interaction terms testing whether equivalence is *robust* rather than merely additive (FE, pp.3-4).

## 4. Conditions & design

Between-subjects field experiment, N=112 University of Glasgow biology students (36 male, 76 female; home group n=62, classroom group n=50), randomly assigned to complete the same Labster "Bacterial Isolation" desktop-VR microbiology simulation either unsupervised at home on their own time or supervised in a computer lab with a teaching assistant present (FAITHFUL-Makransky, p.4). The simulation (~1 hour; five interactivity types — dialoguing, controlling, manipulating, searching, navigating — after Moreno & Mayer's multimodal-learning-environment taxonomy) is desktop-3D, non-immersive, and guided throughout by a pedagogical agent (FAITHFUL-Makransky, pp.5-6). Time-on-task was closely matched across conditions (home M=61 min, range 18-125; classroom M=59.21 min, range 18-127) (FAITHFUL-Makransky, p.7). Pre-test covariates: prior knowledge (7 items, α=.82) and three PALS goal-orientation scales (mastery α=.76, performance-approach α=.86, performance-avoid α=.86) (FAITHFUL-Makransky, p.6). Analysis combined frequentist effect sizes (Cohen's d with 95% CI) with Bayesian 95% Highest Density Intervals, then ANCOVA testing covariate main effects and group-by-covariate interactions (FAITHFUL-Makransky, p.5). Groups did not differ at baseline on prior knowledge (d=0.14) or gender composition (FAITHFUL-Makransky, p.8).

## 5. Findings/Claims

- No significant home/classroom difference on learning outcome (home M=11.57, SD=3.14; classroom M=11.12, SD=3.03; d=0.14, 95% CI -0.24 to 0.53; 95% HDI -0.78 to 1.60) (FAITHFUL-Makransky, p.8).
- No significant difference on intrinsic motivation (d=0.02, 95% CI -0.36 to 0.39) or self-efficacy (d=0.09, 95% CI -0.29 to 0.46) (FAITHFUL-Makransky, p.8-9).
- All three null differences survived ANCOVA adjustment for prior knowledge (learning outcome d=0.11; motivation d=0.10; self-efficacy d=0.01) with no significant interactions (FAITHFUL-Makransky, p.9).
- The pattern also survived adjustment for the three goal-orientation covariates, with one exception: a significant interaction (p=.0263) in which mastery orientation predicted self-efficacy more strongly in the classroom group than the home group (FAITHFUL-Makransky, p.9-10).
- The authors' theoretical interpretation: this pattern supports extending Clark's methods-not-media view to physical context — the instructional method (the simulation) drives learning, not the delivery context (home vs. classroom) — with felt presence proposed, not tested, as the mediating variable that renders physical location comparatively unimportant (FAITHFUL-Makransky, p.10).
- Limitations flagged by the authors themselves: only 112 of 289 eligible students (38.75%) consented, raising possible self-selection toward more motivated/mastery-oriented participants; adequate pedagogical-agent guidance is proposed as a likely *boundary condition* on the equivalence hypothesis rather than a settled non-factor (FAITHFUL-Makransky, p.11-12).

## 6. Concordance feed

- **presence (spatial/physical) × home vs classroom setting** — theorized mediator, not measured; p.10 (P/anticipatory-application for the causal-mediation claim itself, since the study offers no presence measure to test it — FE for the claim as the authors' own stated reasoning).
- **affect: enjoyment & intrinsic motivation × home vs classroom setting** — null difference, d=0.02; p.8-9 (FE).
- **self-efficacy × home vs classroom setting** — null difference overall (d=0.09) but a mastery-orientation × setting interaction; p.9-10 (FE).
- **learning outcomes: retention & transfer × home vs classroom setting** — null difference, d=0.14, framed as the study's central empirical contribution; p.8 (FE).
- **learning outcomes: retention & transfer × desktop-3D / non-immersive VR** — the simulation itself is desktop-3D throughout both conditions; this cell records the medium as constant, with only the setting varied — the design's own controlled variable, not a finding; p.4-6 (FE).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* This unit is the closest empirical analogue in the entire cluster to Part III's own strata and poles. Part III's survey (`vle-01-deployment-survey`) splits respondents by Q8 into APP (used the 3D application; ~51%) versus VID (video-only; 43-46% every term), with immersion/presence self-report concentrated in the APP stratum (F7: 4.3 vs. 1.2 per-100) and threshold-foreclosure frictions concentrated in the VID stratum (F1). Makransky et al.'s home/classroom manipulation is not identical to Part III's app/video-only split — it varies *where* the same desktop-VR simulation is completed while holding the medium constant, whereas Part III's poles vary *whether* the 3D application is used at all — but the two designs share a structural logic worth making explicit: both ask whether removing the classroom/lab's controlled, supervised setting degrades outcomes from a simulation-based learning artifact, and both locate presence as the theoretical variable that would explain either a null or a non-null result *(anticipatory-application; bridges-to-vle → vle-01-deployment-survey)*. Where Makransky et al. find context-equivalence (home ≈ classroom) under teacher-independent, self-directed engagement with a working, immersive-enough desktop VR simulation, Part III's VID stratum shows the *opposite* pattern when the 3D layer is dropped altogether rather than merely relocated: threshold-foreclosure (F1), friction-driven flight to YouTube-video fallback (F4), and a value-skepticism arc culminating in "why not just watch it?" (F6). Read together, this suggests the operative variable is not physical *location* (home vs. classroom) but *modal completeness* — whether the 3D/simulation layer is present at all — a distinction this cluster's own construction, not a claim either source makes about the other *(anticipatory-application)*. This unit is therefore recorded as the **primary empirical anchor for debate axis 8** ("deployment context matters vs. context-equivalence"): its own finding lands squarely on the context-equivalence pole for the home/classroom axis specifically, while Part III's F1/F4/F6 findings land on the "deployment context matters" pole for the presence/absence-of-3D axis — the two studies are not in direct contradiction because they vary different things, and that non-contradiction is itself the analytically useful point *(anticipatory-application; shifts-debate-axis)*.
