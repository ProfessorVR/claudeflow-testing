# ped-sec-11 — Makransky & Petersen, "Investigating the process of learning with desktop virtual reality: A structural equation modeling approach" (2019)

**Type:** empirical journal article (*Computers & Education* 134, pp.15–30) · **Discipline:** educational psychology / instructional technology · **Strand:** C — media-comparison & process experiments · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** the cluster's canonical desktop-VR *process* model — no media contrast is run; the paper instead isolates, via SEM on pre-to-post change scores, the affective and cognitive causal routes by which a single desktop-VR condition produces learning. It anchors the "SEM / process model (no media contrast)" column of the concordance and is the direct methodological precursor cited by CAMIL (ped-sec-06).

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Makransky and Petersen argue that studying VR's *effects* on learning outcomes alone is theoretically thin next to studying the *process* by which those effects occur — that is, which psychological variables mediate between a VR lesson's features and what a learner comes away having gained (FE, p.15–16). Their specific methodological wager is that prior desktop-VR process models — Lee, Wong & Fung's (2010) foundational SEM framework and Makransky & Lilleholt's (2018) extension of it via the Control-Value Theory of Achievement Emotions (CVTAE; Pekrun) — are contaminated by common-method variance, because they measure their mediating variables and their learning outcomes in the same post-test-only administration and rely on subjective (self-report) outcome measures (FE, p.15–16). The paper's thesis is that re-running essentially the same theoretical model, but substituting pre-to-post-test *change scores* in intrinsic motivation, self-efficacy, and objective knowledge for the earlier subjective-outcome measures, will reveal whether the previously reported affective and cognitive pathways survive this more conservative test (FE, p.16). They conclude that they do, in a modified form: two paths, an affective path keyed on presence and a cognitive path keyed on cognitive benefits, both converging on self-efficacy as the proximate predictor of learning (FE, p.24, 26).

## 2. Structure

1. **1. Background / 1.1–1.3** (p.15–20) — motivates process-over-outcome research; positions the study against Lee et al. (2010) and Makransky & Lilleholt (2018); introduces the CVTAE and Lee et al.'s desktop-VR SEM framework as the two theoretical scaffolds; defines each variable in the a priori model (VR features, usability, five affective/cognitive mediators, three outcome variables); states ten hypotheses (H1–H10, paths 1–29 in Fig.1).
2. **2. Materials and methods** (p.20–22) — 199 first-year medicine undergraduates (120 female); mandatory 3-hour session (pre-test, ~2h Labster desktop-VR genetics simulation, post-test); pre/post measures of intrinsic motivation, self-efficacy, and a 20-item knowledge test; post-test-only self-report scales (α = 0.76–0.93) for the nine mediating constructs; SEM fit via Mplus (CFI/TLI/RMSEA).
3. **3. Results** (p.22–24) — reports the trimmed final model (RMSEA=0.057, CFI=0.964, TLI=0.960) hypothesis-by-hypothesis (H1–H10); each hypothesis's support/non-support is stated explicitly with path coefficients.
4. **4. Discussion** (p.24–25) — names and interprets the affective path and the cognitive path; discusses the unexpected non-finding that usability did not predict presence; states practical implications (design for VR-feature fidelity and usability; blended-learning integration); lists limitations (single session, no collaborative-learning measure, unmeasured prior knowledge, all-self-report process measures).
5. **5. Conclusions** (p.26) — restates the two-path model as the paper's principal empirical contribution and its grounding in objectively measured (change-score) outcomes.

## 3. Constructs

- **VR features** — a latent construct comprising representational fidelity and immediacy of control, coined by Whitelock, Brna & Holland (1996) and adopted from Lee et al. (2010); the technological antecedent variable in the model (FAITHFUL-Makransky-Petersen, p.17).
- **representational fidelity** — "the resemblance between the virtual environment and the real world," one of two VR-features indicators, measured with three Lee-et-al.-derived items (α=0.86) (FAITHFUL-Makransky-Petersen, p.17, 22).
- **agency / interactivity (immediacy of control)** — the ability to change viewpoint and manipulate objects; the second VR-features indicator (α=0.76) (FAITHFUL-Makransky-Petersen, p.17, 22).
- **usability** — perceived usefulness + perceived ease of use (Davis, 1989 Technology Acceptance Model); positioned as an antecedent both to VR features' downstream effects and independently to the cognitive path (FAITHFUL-Makransky-Petersen, p.17–18).
- **presence (spatial/physical)** — glossed via Lee et al.'s "psychological sense of 'being there'" and Witmer & Singer's definition of subjectively experiencing one place while physically situated in another; measured with four items (α=0.78) including one item resembling temporal absorption ("lost track of time") (FAITHFUL-Makransky-Petersen, p.18, 22).
- **affect: enjoyment & intrinsic motivation** — perceived enjoyment (Davis, Bagozzi & Warshaw's definition, α=0.93) as a mediator, and pre-to-post change in intrinsic motivation (Deci & Ryan; measured via the Intrinsic Motivation Inventory's Interest/Enjoyment subscale, α=0.88/0.90) as an outcome (FAITHFUL-Makransky-Petersen, p.18–19, 21–22).
- **self-efficacy** — Bandura's/Schunk & DiBenedetto's perceived-capability construct, measured pre/post via an MSLQ-adapted scale (α=0.82/0.88); the model's terminal mediator immediately upstream of learning (FAITHFUL-Makransky-Petersen, p.19, 21, 24).
- **learning outcomes: retention & transfer** — operationalized narrowly as change in a 20-item multiple-choice knowledge test (facts, concepts, procedures per Mayer's knowledge taxonomy; α=0.68 both administrations) (FAITHFUL-Makransky-Petersen, p.19, 21).
- **cognitive load (extraneous vs germane)** — not directly measured in this study's own instrument battery, but explicitly invoked in the Discussion to interpret why other studies find high-fidelity VR raising presence without raising learning: "irrelevant sensory information... leads to extraneous processing which is not necessary for learning" (FAITHFUL-Makransky-Petersen, p.24). This is the paper's own citation of the extraneous-processing literature, not a construct this study operationalizes (FE, qualified).
- **engagement / flow** — touched only analogically: "control and active learning" (autonomy/pacing, α=0.88) and one presence item on losing track of time gesture toward engagement/flow without the construct being named or measured as such (P/anticipatory-application).
- **cognitive benefits** — "improved understanding, application, and positive perception of learning material" the VE affords; the cognitive path's key mediator (α=0.88) (FAITHFUL-Makransky-Petersen, p.18, 24–25).
- **reflective thinking** — Dewey's (1933) construct of productive perplexity, adapted as a four-item scale (α=0.81); included in the a priori model but ultimately non-predictive of any outcome (FAITHFUL-Makransky-Petersen, p.19, 22, 24).

## 4. Conditions & design

Single-condition, pre/post correlational-causal design — **no media contrast** is run; every participant used the same desktop-VR condition. Sample: N=199 (120 female, 79 male) first-year medicine undergraduates at a large European university, drawn from a first-year class of 300; participation and inclusion contingent on research-use consent (FAITHFUL-Makransky-Petersen, p.20). Procedure: a mandatory 3-hour computer-laboratory session (~25 students/session, three teachers on hand) — 30-minute pre-test (demographics, pre-measures of intrinsic motivation/self-efficacy/knowledge), ~2-hour desktop-VR Labster cytogenetics simulation (case-based genetic-counseling scenario; keyboard/mouse/monitor interaction), 30-minute post-test (repeated outcome measures plus nine mediator scales) (FAITHFUL-Makransky-Petersen, p.20–21). Instruments: all mediator/outcome scales five-point Likert except the 20-item knowledge test; Cronbach's alphas 0.68 (knowledge) to 0.93 (enjoyment) (FAITHFUL-Makransky-Petersen, p.21–22). Analysis: confirmatory SEM in Mplus 7.4, ordinal-variable estimation, fit judged by CFI/TLI ≥0.90 and RMSEA ≤0.06 (Hu & Bentler, 1999 criteria); a priori 29-path model trimmed iteratively to a final model of only significant paths (FAITHFUL-Makransky-Petersen, p.21–22). Final-model fit: RMSEA=0.057, CFI=0.964, TLI=0.960, all retained paths p<0.01 (FAITHFUL-Makransky-Petersen, p.22). Effect directions (final model only): VR features → usability (β=0.894); VR features → presence (β=0.871) and → perceived enjoyment (β=0.835), but NOT → control/active learning; usability → control/active learning (β=0.985), but NOT → presence or enjoyment; usability → cognitive benefits (β=0.968) and → reflective thinking (β=0.952), but VR features did NOT directly predict either cognitive-path mediator; presence → change in motivation (β=0.429), the only affective mediator with any outcome path, and NOT directly to self-efficacy or learning; cognitive benefits → change in self-efficacy (β=0.329), the only cognitive mediator with any outcome path; change in motivation → change in self-efficacy (β=0.278); change in self-efficacy → learning (β=0.579); change in motivation did NOT directly predict learning (routed instead through self-efficacy) (FAITHFUL-Makransky-Petersen, p.22–24). This paper's design instantiates the concordance's "SEM / process model (no media contrast)" and "desktop-3D / non-immersive VR" columns simultaneously — it is a single-arm process study set inside the desktop-3D medium, not a between-medium comparison.

## 5. Findings/Claims

- Two distinct paths lead from desktop-VR features to learning: an **affective path** (VR features → presence → change in intrinsic motivation → change in self-efficacy → learning) and a **cognitive path** (VR features → usability → cognitive benefits → change in self-efficacy → learning); both converge on self-efficacy as the immediate antecedent of learning gains (FE, p.24, 26).
- Presence is the only affective-family mediator with any downstream predictive power in this study, and its effect on learning is fully mediated through motivation and then self-efficacy, not direct (FE, p.23–24).
- Cognitive benefits — not reflective thinking, not VR features directly — is the cognitive path's operative mediator, and its effect on learning is similarly mediated through self-efficacy rather than direct (FE, p.23–24).
- Usability was **not** significantly related to presence in this sample, an unexpected non-finding the authors attribute to few usability breakdowns restricting variance, and/or to their reliance on subjective rather than objective (log-file, breakdown-count) usability measures (FE, p.24).
- The authors explicitly flag, from the wider literature (not their own data, since no media contrast is run here), that higher-fidelity/more-immersive VR has repeatedly been found to raise presence *without* raising learning, attributed to extraneous processing from irrelevant sensory information — and hold up their own desktop-VR affective path (where presence's effect is positively mediated through motivation and self-efficacy) as a case where presence *does* eventually reach learning, contingent on those downstream psychological variables (FE, p.24).
- Practical design implications: emphasize VR-feature fidelity (realistic display, smooth motion, 3-D audio, view/navigation control) and usability (clear navigation, entry/exit points, cues) when designing desktop-VR learning environments; consider blended-learning integration via a "replacement model" that substitutes VR sessions for costly/dangerous in-person lab activities (FE, p.25).
- Limitations explicitly named by the authors: single-session-only design (no repeated-exposure or long-term retention data); no measure of collaborative knowledge construction despite students being permitted to interact; unmeasured individual differences, especially prior knowledge; all mediating/outcome process variables are self-report, none objective (log files, EEG, eye-tracking, galvanic skin response are named as future directions) (FE, p.25).

## 6. Concordance feed

- **presence (spatial/physical) × desktop-3D / non-immersive VR** — presence operationalized and measured within a single desktop-VR arm; p.18, 22 (FE).
- **presence (spatial/physical) × SEM / process model (no media contrast)** — presence's causal position (VR-features-driven, motivation-mediated, not directly learning-predictive) established via path analysis; p.22–24 (FE).
- **representational fidelity × desktop-3D / non-immersive VR** — one of two VR-features indicators, β=0.874 to the latent VR-features factor; p.17, 22 (FAITHFUL-Makransky-Petersen).
- **agency / interactivity × desktop-3D / non-immersive VR** — immediacy of control, β=0.823 to VR features; p.17, 22 (FAITHFUL-Makransky-Petersen).
- **affect: enjoyment & intrinsic motivation × SEM / process model (no media contrast)** — perceived enjoyment as non-predictive mediator; change in intrinsic motivation as the affective path's penultimate node (β=0.429 from presence, β=0.278 to self-efficacy); p.18–19, 22–24 (FE).
- **self-efficacy × SEM / process model (no media contrast)** — the model's convergence point for both paths; sole direct predictor of learning (β=0.579); p.19, 23–24 (FE).
- **learning outcomes: retention & transfer × SEM / process model (no media contrast)** — operationalized as pre-to-post change in a 20-item knowledge test, the model's terminal outcome; p.19, 23–24 (FE).
- **cognitive load (extraneous vs germane) × desktop-3D / non-immersive VR** — invoked interpretively (not measured) to explain why other studies' immersive-VR presence gains fail to reach learning, contrasted with this study's own desktop-VR affective route; p.24 (FE, qualified — construct not operationalized in-study).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* This unit is the cluster's cleanest instance of the "process, not outcome" methodological stance that CAMIL (ped-sec-06) later formalizes, and it is the direct empirical scaffold Makransky & Lilleholt (2018, ped-sec-09) both anticipates and is retrospectively corrected against on common-method-variance grounds — any Part III argument that leans on "presence causes learning" from either paper should cite this unit's two-path, self-efficacy-mediated structure rather than a simple bivariate claim. Because the paper runs no media contrast, it cannot itself speak to Part III's desktop-3D-vs-video-only strata directly; its value to Part III is instead as a *within-desktop-VR* process account of how the deployment-survey's APP stratum (`vle-01-deployment-survey`) might translate presence into engagement and eventual learning-relevant motivation, via a specific causal chain (presence → motivation → self-efficacy) rather than a direct presence-to-outcome link *(anticipatory-application)*. The finding that usability and presence were *not* significantly related — attributed to low usability-breakdown variance in a curated, teacher-supported lab session — is a useful caution when interpreting `vle-01`'s F2 friction scorecard (install/lag/navigation complaints concentrated in the VID stratum): where usability breakdowns are common and uneven, as in the VLE's messier deployment context, a usability-presence link this study could not detect might well appear *(anticipatory-application)*. The paper's own citation of the extraneous-processing/immersion debate (more fidelity → more presence but not more learning) is this cluster's debate axis 1 in miniature, cited here as background rather than tested; Part III synthesis work should draw the actual test from ped-sec-10 (Makransky et al., immersive-VR-causes-more-presence-less-learning) rather than from this unit. Finally, the self-efficacy convergence point in both paths is a candidate construct Part III's own survey instrument does not currently measure (`vle-01`'s F-code families concern friction, presence, and value-skepticism, not self-efficacy) — flagged here as a possible instrument gap for the survey-analysis team to weigh, not a claim that the gap must be filled *(anticipatory-application)*.

**Not treated:** immersive/HMD VR (no such condition exists in this study; the desktop VR vs. HMD contrast is discussed only via citation to other authors' work), 2D/video conditions, social presence/co-presence, embodiment/self-representation, cybersickness, situational interest (touched only via a citation to interest theory in the intrinsic-motivation discussion, not measured), and any home-vs-classroom deployment contrast (the entire sample was run in a single supervised computer laboratory).
