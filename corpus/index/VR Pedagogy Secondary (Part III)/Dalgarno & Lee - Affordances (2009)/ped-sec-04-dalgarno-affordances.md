# ped-sec-04 — Dalgarno & Lee, "What are the learning affordances of 3-D virtual environments?" (2010; online-first 2009)

**Type:** journal-article-length secondary reading (educational technology / instructional design) · **Discipline:** educational technology, instructional design · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** Strand B's founding theory-model source — the affordances-of-3D-VLEs model itself, the single most-cited hub of the cluster's Strand-B/C literature, and the frame against which the media-comparison reckoning (Strand C) and the reviews (Strand E) implicitly argue.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Dalgarno and Lee set out to isolate what, specifically, is distinctive about three-dimensional virtual learning environments (3-D VLEs) as against both 2-D digital alternatives and non-3-D learning-management systems, and to derive from two decades of published applications a defensible list of the learning *affordances* — not benefits, not advantages, but task-possibilities made available by an environment's properties to an agent with a given ability (after Gibson, 1979, and Greeno, 1994) — that 3-D VLEs make available (FE, p.10–11, 21). Their explicit target is the field's own evidentiary weakness: "very few empirical studies" have documented enhanced post-test knowledge or skills for students using desktop-based 3-D environments over equivalent 2-D technologies (FE, p.10), against a backdrop of what they repeatedly call "show-and-tell" enthusiasm rather than research (FE, p.23). The paper's ultimate aim is not to settle the question of 3-D VLE benefit but to build the theoretical scaffolding — a set of distinguishing characteristics, a set of derived affordances, and a model linking the two through intermediate outcomes — that a subsequent research agenda could test (FE, p.23–25). This makes the paper deliberately a *THEORY-MODEL* source, not an empirical one: no data are collected here; the contribution is conceptual synthesis and a testable structure for others (including, fourteen years later, this cluster's own Strand C).

## 2. Structure

The article proceeds in five untitled-but-clearly-delineated movements.

1. **Introduction** (pp.10–11) — states the hype/evidence gap and the paper's aim of isolating 3-D-specific distinguishing characteristics and deriving affordances.
2. **3-D VLEs** (pp.11–17) — defines the object via Wann & Mon-Williams (1996); distinguishes desktop from immersive VEs; synthesizes prior characteristic-frameworks (Hedberg & Alexander 1994; Whitelock, Brna & Holland 1996; Brna 1999) into a two-category, ten-item taxonomy of representational fidelity and learner interaction; adopts Slater's immersion/presence distinction and argues presence, co-presence, and identity construction are *consequences* of fidelity and interaction rather than independent properties; presents an initial model (Figure 1).
3. **The learning affordances of 3-D VEs** (pp.17–23) — reviews applications across three categories (3-D simulations/microworlds; 3-D environments as interfaces to learning resources; 3-D multi-user VLEs) and derives, in sequence, five numbered affordances.
4. **A model of learning in 3-D VLEs** (p.23) — integrates characteristics and affordances into an elaborated model (Figure 2), with identity, presence, and co-presence positioned as intermediate outcomes between environment characteristics and anticipated learning benefits.
5. **Towards a research agenda: conclusion and recommendations** (pp.23–25) — characterizes existing evidence as anecdotal; explicitly rejects simple 2-D-vs-3-D media-comparison designs on Clark's (1983, 1994a, 1994b) media-vs-method grounds; proposes a roadmap of research questions (Table 2) organized around testing the model's assumed links and, eventually, deriving design principles.

## 3. Constructs

As a THEORY-MODEL source, this unit's primary contribution *is* the constructs, organized into two tiers the paper itself distinguishes: ten "distinguishing characteristics" (independent variables, in the paper's own causal picture) and five "affordances" (the learning-task possibilities those characteristics make available), linked through three "intermediate outcomes."

**Representational fidelity (construct axis: `representational fidelity`)** — six sub-characteristics: realistic display of environment; smooth display of view changes/object motion; consistency of object behavior; user representation (avatar); spatial audio; kinaesthetic/tactile force feedback (FE, p.15, Table 1). The paper explicitly folds Brna's (1999) "social fidelity" into this category for multi-user contexts (FE, p.16).

**Learner interaction** — four sub-characteristics: embodied actions (view control, navigation, object manipulation); embodied verbal/non-verbal communication; control of environment attributes/behavior; construction/scripting of objects (FE, p.16–17, Table 1). This maps onto the `agency / interactivity` axis row directly, and its "embodied actions" and "embodied verbal and non-verbal communication" items are this source's explicit treatment of `embodiment / self-representation` (FE, p.16–17).

**Presence (spatial/physical)** — treated not as an independent characteristic but as a consequence: following Slater (1999, 2003, 2004), immersion is "the objective and measurable properties of the system," presence "the subjective sense of being in a place" that results from fidelity and interactivity (FAITHFUL-Dalgarno & Lee, p.13). The paper explicitly refuses to treat immersion or presence as unique properties of 3-D VLEs in their own right (FE, p.13).

**Social presence / co-presence** — glossed as "being there together" with geographically dispersed others, an "extension of social presence" tracing to Short, Williams & Christie's 1976 CMC tradition (a direct external forerunner of this cluster's own Strand F) and to Biocca, Harms & Burgoon (2003) (FAITHFUL-Dalgarno & Lee, p.14). Co-presence is likewise treated as a consequence of fidelity/interaction, not an independent 3-D-VLE property (FE, p.14) — this is the source's clearest single grounding of the construct-axis's `social presence / co-presence` row, and it is the row Strand F's later addition (ped-sec-25…34) was built to supply dedicated primary sources for; this unit supplies the row's earliest appearance *inside* the VR-pedagogy literature itself, prior to and independent of the CMC tradition Strand F documents.

**Embodiment / self-representation** — via avatar-mediated identity construction: citing Dickey (2002), the paper names presence, representation (avatar appearance/name), and embodiment (physical actions, social positioning) as the three contributors to online identity construction, again treated as a consequence rather than a unique characteristic (FAITHFUL-Dalgarno & Lee, p.14–15).

**Affect: enjoyment & intrinsic motivation / Engagement-flow** — Affordance 3 explicitly invokes Csikszentmihalyi's (1990) flow construct: "the high degree of fidelity and the natural interface of 3-D VEs may increase the likelihood that learners will experience this feeling of flow" (FAITHFUL-Dalgarno & Lee, p.19).

**Learning outcomes: retention & transfer** — Affordance 4 (contextualization/transfer), grounded in situated-learning theory (Lave & Wenger, 1991; Brown, Collins & Duguid, 1989) and a "knowledge anchored to experience" argument (FE, p.20–21).

**Learning outcomes: procedural/skills** — Affordance 2 (experiential tasks impractical/impossible in the real world), illustrated by nuclear-plant-worker, astronaut, and forestry-operator training simulations (FE, p.18–19).

Not among the ten characteristics but load-bearing throughout: `cognitive load` is absent as a named construct here (this source predates the field's cognitive-load reckoning that Strand C's Parong & Mayer and Makransky units later supply); the paper instead frames its open questions in terms of "what changes to accepted design principles from established theories (eg, cognitive load theory, dual coding theory, cognitive theory of multimedia learning)... are needed" (FE, p.24, Table 2) — i.e., cognitive load is explicitly deferred to future research, not treated.

## 4. Conditions & design

This is a conceptual/model paper, not an empirical study — it has no participants, no instrument, no measured condition of its own. Its "conditions & design" content is instead the literature it synthesizes and the empirical designs it argues *against*. Structurally, the paper draws on roughly three decades of prior applied work across three categories (3-D simulations/microworlds; 3-D-as-interface; 3-D multi-user VLEs), citing dozens of specific implemented systems (a historic-theatre reconstruction, a driver-education simulator, a Second Life scavenger-hunt collaborative task, an architecture/communication cross-disciplinary SL project, among others) as illustrative rather than data-bearing cases (FE, p.18–22). Methodologically, the paper's most consequential move is a negative one: citing Clark (1983, 1994a, 1994b), it explicitly rejects simple 2-D-vs-3-D media-comparison designs as unable to separate learning design from media, arguing such studies would demonstrate only the "trivial fact" that unharnessed 3-D affordances yield minimal unique learning benefit (FAITHFUL-Dalgarno & Lee, p.24). This positions the paper as the origin, within this cluster, of the debate-axis pole later contested by Strand C's own media-comparison experiments (ped-sec-08 through ped-sec-14), several of which do run exactly the kind of controlled comparison this source cautions against, and by Mayer et al.'s 2022 "methods-not-media" restatement (ped-sec-07). Table 2's roadmap proposes three study types — testing basic assumptions, linking characteristics to affordances, and establishing best-practice design principles/guidelines — a research-agenda structure this cluster's Strand C/D units can be read as variously fulfilling.

## 5. Findings/Claims

- 3-D VLEs are distinguished from other VLEs (e.g., LMS platforms) by three-dimensionality, smooth temporal change, and interactivity, elaborated into ten characteristics across two categories: representational fidelity and learner interaction (FE, p.11, 15–17).
- Presence, co-presence, and identity construction are not independent, defining properties of 3-D VLEs but *consequences* of representational fidelity and learner interaction — a deliberate theoretical simplification distinguishing this model from prior six-dimensional frameworks (e.g., Brna 1999) (FE, p.13–15).
- Five learning affordances follow from the literature review: (1) enhanced spatial knowledge representation; (2) experiential learning tasks impractical/impossible in reality; (3) increased intrinsic motivation and engagement; (4) improved contextualization/transfer of learning; (5) richer/more effective collaborative learning versus 2-D alternatives (FE, p.18, 19, 20, 21, 23).
- The relationships between characteristics, intermediate outcomes, and affordances are *assumptions* requiring empirical validation, not established facts — the paper is explicit that the existing literature is "largely anecdotal" show-and-tell (FE, p.23).
- Media-comparison research designs (2-D vs. 3-D holding learning design constant) are methodologically unsound per Clark's media-vs-method argument and should be avoided in favor of research testing whether affordances are actually harnessed by learning design (FE, p.24).
- The paper closes with a call for research linking 3-D VLE characteristics to design principles and best practice, contingent on first validating the model's assumed links (FE, p.24–25).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* This unit is the cluster's earliest and most-cited theoretical hub for Strand B and, by extension, the affordance-optimism pole that Strand C's media-comparison experiments and Strand E's reviews (especially Radianti et al., ped-sec-22) will test, extend, or complicate. Its Affordance 5 (collaboration) and the "shared dimension" of co-presence supply the cluster's earliest grounding for the `social presence / co-presence` construct-axis row — a grounding this cluster's own manifest records as otherwise under-supplied until Strand F's later addition; the two should be read together rather than as competing accounts, since Strand F documents the CMC tradition this paper cites only briefly (Short, Williams & Christie 1976; Biocca, Harms & Burgoon 2003) without engaging its measurement instruments *(anticipatory-application)*. Bridging to the VLE anchor: the exemplar bank's "Action-affordance requests" and "The shared dimension (co-disclosedness — mostly optative)" sections (`vle-03-exemplar-bank`) register student desire for exactly the interactivity and collaborative-task affordances this model predicts — respondents explicitly wanting "options on which route you want to take" and describing collaborative viewing as "the main pro to the platform" — a striking real-world echo of Affordances 2 and 5 that the VLE's own students voice unprompted *(anticipatory-application)*. The exemplar bank's monotony/passivity and value-skepticism sections, conversely, register precisely the failure mode this paper's model predicts when characteristics (fidelity, interactivity) are *not* harnessed by learning design — video-only VLE content with low interactivity produces the "minimal unique learning benefits" this paper anticipates for un-harnessed affordances (FE, p.24) *(anticipatory-application)*. Mark's attentional-states framework (`vle-04-mark-attention-span`) offers a partial bridge to this unit's Affordance 3 (motivation/engagement/flow): Mark's "Focus" quadrant (high engagement × high challenge) is a plausible psychological cousin of Csikszentmihalyi's flow as this paper invokes it, though the two frameworks classify along different axes (levels vs. structures) and should not be treated as equivalent *(anticipatory-application)*. Most consequentially for this unit's place in Strand B: **ped-sec-24** (Dalgarno & Lee's own 2012 followup, "Exploring the relationship between afforded learning tasks and learning benefits in 3-D virtual learning environments") is the direct empirical test this 2009 paper calls for in its own concluding research agenda — it surveys higher-education staff (N=53 of 117 respondents) on which afforded tasks and characteristics they associate with which learning benefits, operationalizing this unit's own Figure 2 model rather than a third party's reading of it. That relationship is recorded on ped-sec-24's side as an `extends-reading-of` edge back to this unit; this unit's own claim to be "a research agenda... contingent on empirical studies" (FE, p.23–25) is the reason such a direct, same-authorial-team followup exists at all within the cluster, an unusually tight anchor-instance-of-its-own-call relationship not paralleled elsewhere in Strand B *(anticipatory-application)*. Finally, the `grounds-measure` edge from this unit to the Boredom Experiment (VR Attention Study) dataset was **retired 2026-07-16** (rescope): this cluster is Part III LIT-SLOT literature, not a boredom-*lab* interface. This unit's affordance model relates to the deployed-VLE evaluation — carried by its `bridges-to-part-iii`/`bridges-to-vle` edges — not to the lab dataset, which is upstream of the phenomenology rather than grounded by this literature.

**Not treated:** cognitive load (extraneous vs. germane) — explicitly deferred to future research rather than analyzed; cybersickness & physical discomfort — outside this paper's scope (predates the HMD-cybersickness literature Strand E's Cossio et al. covers); situational interest and self-efficacy as named constructs — absent from the ten-characteristic/five-affordance apparatus; any IVR-vs-desktop-3D empirical contrast — the paper is explicitly desktop-3D-focused and non-empirical throughout.
