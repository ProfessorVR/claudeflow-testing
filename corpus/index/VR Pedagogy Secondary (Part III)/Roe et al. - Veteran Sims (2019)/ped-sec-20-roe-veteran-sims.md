# ped-sec-20 — Roe, Galbraith, Boudiab & Johnson, "The Use of Veteran-Centric Simulations in a Nursing Program" (2019)

**Type:** research brief (empirical, single-group pre/post) · **Discipline:** nursing education / health-professions simulation · **Depth:** map · **Corpus role:** Strand D's shortest and most narrowly clinical unit — the only source in the cluster whose "condition" is an in-person, non-digital simulation lab rather than a VR/desktop-3D/2D contrast; its value to Part III is methodological (a deliberate-practice-grounded, validated-instrument pre/post design) and thematic (extending simulation's reach from psychomotor skill to affective/attitudinal readiness), not media-comparative.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Roe et al. report a descriptive-comparative study testing whether embedding Veteran-centric clinical simulations across a first-semester baccalaureate nursing curriculum measurably shifts students' self-reported confidence and comfort caring for Veterans (FE, p.44). The brief's stated contribution is corrective: prior publications on Veteran-centric simulation offered only anecdotal support, and this study supplies the first (to the authors' knowledge) empirical pre/post evaluation using a purpose-built instrument (FE, p.45).

## 2. Structure

Six sections: Background (Veterans' distinct health needs, low VA-system utilization ~29%, the VA Nursing Academy/Joining Forces institutional backdrop, and the literature gap) → Conceptual Framework (deliberate practice) → Methods (design, sample, instrument) → Results (item- and total-score change) → Limitations → Discussion (situates findings against related simulation-perception studies and the NCSBN National Simulation Study).

## 3. Constructs

- **self-efficacy (confidence/comfort caring for Veterans)** — the study's sole measured outcome, operationalized as a 12-item Likert total and per-item means on Lippman & Ozment's "Nursing Student Perceptions About Caring for Veterans" instrument (FAITHFUL-Roe, p.46). §3's canonical label is used here for what Roe et al. call "perceptions of confidence and comfort"; the source's own term is recorded in the edges.
- **learning outcomes: procedural / skills** — treated only as deliberate practice's *traditional* target, explicitly set up as a contrast: deliberate practice "has been studied primarily related to skills in nursing education," and this study's premise is extending it to an affective/perceptual outcome instead (FAITHFUL-Roe, p.45-46). Not itself measured here.

## 4. Conditions & design

Descriptive-comparative, one-group pretest/posttest, nonmatched design (no comparison or control group), run across four semesters / eight data-collection points at a ~450-student Midwestern U.S. BSN program (FAITHFUL-Roe, p.45). First-semester students (N=325 pre, N=282 post, ~13% attrition) participated in Veteran-centric simulations comprising 41% of that semester's total simulation load — six scripted scenarios (Table 1) spanning Korean-Conflict, Vietnam, and Operation Enduring/Iraqi Freedom-era Veterans and conditions including chronic pain/PTSD, respiratory distress, urinary tract infection, type-2 diabetes, interpersonal conflict, and altered mobility (FAITHFUL-Roe, p.46-47). Instrument: Lippman & Ozment's (2013, unpublished) 12-item Likert questionnaire, Cronbach's alpha = .901 excluding two VA-clinical-experience items (FAITHFUL-Roe, p.46). ## 5. Findings/Claims

Total mean score rose from 32.7 (SD 7.2) pre to 38.1 (SD 5.0) post (t=10.7, p=.00) (FAITHFUL-Roe, p.47). Ten of twelve items improved significantly; the two non-significant items were both VA-clinical-experience-specific ("VA staff support meaningful clinical rotations," p=.21; "Veterans would choose health-seeking behaviors," p=.22) (FAITHFUL-Roe, p.47-48). Debriefing and course-evaluation feedback reported increased likelihood of asking all patients about Veteran/military status and of thanking Veterans for service (FE, p.47-48). The authors read the gain as support for extending deliberate-practice theory from psychomotor skill acquisition to affective/confidence outcomes (FE, p.48), while explicitly declining to claim the design shows simulation can substitute for clinical hours (FE, p.48-49).
