# ped-sec-06 — Makransky & Petersen, "The Cognitive Affective Model of Immersive Learning (CAMIL)" (2021)

**Type:** theoretical review article (educational psychology) · **Discipline:** educational psychology / instructional technology · **Depth:** deep · **Corpus role:** the cluster's single most important THEORY-MODEL hub — a synthesizing dual-process model that pulls presence, agency, immersion, cognitive load, and affect into one causal architecture, built directly on two of this cluster's own prior Makransky studies (ped-sec-09, ped-sec-11) and citing four more of its own Strand-C/E members (ped-sec-08, ped-sec-10, ped-sec-13, ped-sec-22) as evidentiary grounding.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Makransky & Petersen propose the Cognitive Affective Model of Immersive Learning (CAMIL) as the field's first research-based theoretical synthesis of how learning happens *in* immersive virtual reality (IVR), responding directly to two recent verdicts — Radianti et al.'s (2020) systematic review (this cluster's own ped-sec-22) and Wu et al.'s (2020) meta-analysis — that the rapidly growing IVR-learning literature lacks any organizing theory (FE, p.938). The paper's central theoretical move is to stake out a third position in the long-running media-vs-methods debate (Clark 1994 vs. Kozma 1994 vs. McLuhan 1964): rather than siding with Clark's "mere vehicles" claim that media cannot affect learning, or with McLuhan's opposite claim that the medium itself is the message, CAMIL argues that *media interacts with method* — instructional methods developed for less-immersive media generalize to IVR, but certain methods become specifically more effective in IVR because they facilitate that medium's unique affordances (FE, p.939–940). Those affordances are named as exactly two: **presence** and **agency**. The paper's payoff is a full causal path model (its Figure 2, reconstructed in prose across the article) running from technological antecedents, through presence/agency, through six affective and cognitive mediating factors, to four kinds of learning outcome — offered explicitly as a synthesis "based upon previous empirical literature within the field of learning with immersive technology" (FE, p.939) rather than as a wholly new proposal.

## 2. Structure

The article proceeds in eleven named movements: (1) Introduction, motivating the theory gap; (2) Defining Immersive Virtual Reality, distinguishing IVR (HMD/CAVE) from desktop/low-immersion VR by immersion and interaction; (3) The Theoretical Perspective of CAMIL, situating the model in the media-vs-methods debate and grounding "media interacts with method" in three published 2×2 studies; (4) General Overview of the CAMIL, stating the model's four core claims and previewing its path structure; (5) What Factors Lead to Presence? and (6) What Factors Lead to Agency?, each identifying the technological antecedents of one affordance; (7) How Do Presence and Agency Influence [the] Six Affective/Cognitive Factors?, walked path-by-path through situational interest, intrinsic motivation, self-efficacy, embodiment, cognitive load, and self-regulation; (8) What Are the Different Learning Outcomes Included in the CAMIL?, defining factual, conceptual, procedural knowledge and transfer (after Anderson et al. 2001); (9) How Do the Six [...] Factors Lead to Learning Outcomes?, closing the causal loop; (10) Implications for Future Research and for Instructional Design; and (11) Important External Factors that Influence the CAMIL plus a short Conclusion. This skeleton *is* the model's own architecture — the section headings track the arrows of Figure 2 almost one-for-one, so the "structure" and "constructs" of the paper are functionally identical, a feature this unit treats explicitly in §3 below rather than re-describing the same content twice.

## 3. Constructs

CAMIL's own dual-path structure is this cluster's clearest instantiation of debate-axis 4 (the affective route to learning vs. the cognitive-load route), so it is worth stating precisely how CAMIL relates to that axis rather than simply listing constructs. CAMIL does not resolve axis 4's opposition; it *structurally contains both poles as simultaneous, non-exclusive paths* through the same two antecedent affordances. This is a synthesis move, not a concession to one side (P/anticipatory-application) — the paper's own SEM-derived lineage (Makransky & Lilleholt 2018, ped-sec-09; Makransky & Petersen 2019, ped-sec-11) had already empirically identified an affective path and a cognitive path running in parallel from the same technological antecedents, and CAMIL generalizes that dual-path finding into a theory-level claim (FE, p.939, 949).

- **Presence (spatial/physical, social, self)** — CAMIL adopts Lee's (2004) three-dimensional taxonomy wholesale: physical presence (virtual objects experienced as actual physical objects), social presence (virtual social actors experienced as actual social actors), and self-presence (a virtual self experienced as the actual self) (FAITHFUL-Makransky&Petersen, p.943). Presence itself is driven by immersion, control factors (borrowed directly from Witmer & Singer's Presence Questionnaire lineage — ped-sec-02), and representational fidelity (borrowed from Dalgarno & Lee's affordances framework — ped-sec-04, cited by CAMIL as "2010," a citation-year discrepancy against ped-sec-04's own 2009 publication year, consistent with the same discrepancy flagged elsewhere in this cluster) (FE, p.942–943).
- **Social presence / co-presence** — treated only as one of the three presence sub-dimensions and, separately, as the mechanism social agency theory (Mayer) invokes when embodied on-screen agents display human gestures and eye contact; CAMIL does not engage the CMC-tradition social-presence literature (Strand F of this cluster) at all — no Short/Williams/Christie, Gunawardena, Garrison/Anderson/Archer, or Biocca/Burgoon/Harms citation appears — so this row is only thinly grounded here (FE, p.943, 945).
- **Immersion (technological)** — defined, following Cummings & Bailenson, as an *objective* system property (sensory channel count, degree of shutting-out the physical world), explicitly distinguished from the *subjective* experience of presence it produces; this immersion/presence distinction is CAMIL's load-bearing terminological move and is used consistently throughout (FAITHFUL-Makransky&Petersen, p.939).
- **Agency / interactivity** — defined via Moore & Fletcher as the felt sense of generating and controlling one's own actions; its principal antecedent is control-factor fidelity, glossed through Kilteni et al.'s forward-modeling/CNS account (predicted vs. actual sensory feedback correlation) (FE, p.943–944).
- **Embodiment / self-representation** — glossed via Kilteni et al.'s definition ("being inside, having, and controlling a body") and linked to embodied-cognition theory generally; CAMIL posits self-presence and agency as its two direct antecedents (FE, p.946).
- **Cognitive load (extraneous vs. germane)** — grounded in Sweller's CLT and Mayer's CTML; CAMIL's most counter-intuitive path here is that *presence and agency themselves positively predict extraneous cognitive load* (paths 13–14), because a wider field of view and richer interactivity both create more content the learner must search and filter, including "seductive details" (Moreno & Mayer) that are not necessary for learning (FE, p.946–947). This is the mechanism by which CAMIL's cognitive-load route can run *counter to* its own affective route from the identical antecedent — the dual-path structure noted above is not merely additive but can be net-negative for learning even while raising every affective indicator.
- **Affect: enjoyment & intrinsic motivation** — grounded in Deci & Ryan's self-determination theory (autonomy/competence/relatedness) and Pekrun's Control-Value Theory of Achievement Emotions (perceived control + value trigger enjoyment); presence and agency both feed this factor along separate paths (7–8) (FE, p.944–945).
- **Situational interest** — following Hidi & Renninger's situational/individual distinction, defined as focused attention and affective reaction activated in-the-moment by novel, intense stimuli; presence and agency both predict it (paths 5–6) (FE, p.944).
- **Self-efficacy** — grounded in Bandura's four sources of efficacy information, with presence and agency framed as generating "performance accomplishments" (the strongest source) because IVR is experienced as real rather than merely vicarious, unlike video (FE, p.945–946).
- **Learning outcomes: retention & transfer, and procedural/skills** — CAMIL adopts Anderson et al.'s (2001) factual/conceptual/procedural taxonomy and adds transfer as a fourth outcome; it reports that IVR is disproportionately used for procedural-practical knowledge (citing Radianti et al. 2020, ped-sec-22) but shows mixed, sometimes negative, results for factual/conceptual knowledge specifically (FE, p.947–949).
- **Representational fidelity** — treated as a presence antecedent (realism of display, smoothness of view changes, consistency of object behavior), sourced to Dalgarno & Lee (ped-sec-04) (FE, p.942–943).
- **Cybersickness & physical discomfort** — treated only briefly, as one of several "external factors" (below), not as a mediating pathway; CAMIL cites Munafo et al.'s finding of a gender difference in cybersickness susceptibility but does not integrate cybersickness into the model's own path structure (FE, p.953).

## 4. Conditions & design

CAMIL is not itself an empirical study; it reports no participants, apparatus, or original data collection. Its evidentiary base is instead an explicit, enumerable set of prior studies it draws on as grounding, which this unit treats as its functional "conditions" register:

- **Foundational SEM studies** (the model's direct empirical scaffolding): **Makransky & Lilleholt 2018** (ped-sec-09; N=104, immersive-HMD vs. desktop-VR crossover, identified the affective/cognitive dual-path structure CAMIL generalizes) and **Makransky & Petersen 2019** (ped-sec-11; N=199, desktop-VR-only pre/post design, the same dual-path structure with self-efficacy as the terminal mediator) are named as the direct sources of the model's affective-and-cognitive-factors framework, alongside the earlier desktop-VR causal model of Lee, Wong & Fung (2010) that both papers themselves built on (FE, p.941–942).
- **Contrasting media-comparison evidence**: **Parong & Mayer 2018** (ped-sec-08; IVR less effective than PowerPoint for factual knowledge, no difference for conceptual knowledge) and **Makransky et al.'s "presence but less learning" study** (ped-sec-10; immersive VR less effective than the identical desktop simulation for declarative knowledge, cited by CAMIL as "2019b") are both cited as evidence for the mixed, sometimes-negative findings on factual/conceptual outcomes that motivate CAMIL's cognitive-load path (FE, p.948).
- **The cognitive-load evidentiary base**: **Parong & Mayer 2020** (ped-sec-13) is cited alongside Moreno & Mayer 2002 and Makransky et al.'s own prior work as direct support for the claim that IVR produces higher extraneous cognitive load than less-immersive media (FE, p.946).
- **The review/meta-analysis motivating the whole project**: **Radianti et al. 2020** (ped-sec-22) is cited twice — once as one of two sources (with Wu et al. 2020, not in this cluster) diagnosing the field's atheoretical state that CAMIL exists to remedy, and once as the source for the claim that IVR is disproportionately used for procedural-knowledge training (FE, p.938, 949).
- **CAMIL's own three named 2×2 grounding studies for "media interacts with method"** — none of which are units in this cluster (all post-date or lie outside the corpus): Meyer et al. (2019, N=118, pre-training principle × IVR/video, positive interaction); Makransky et al. (2020a, N=165 high-schoolers, generative enactment × IVR/video, positive interaction); Klingenberg et al. (2020, N=89 biochemistry undergraduates, generative teaching × IVR/desktop-VR, positive interaction). These three studies are CAMIL's direct empirical warrant for its signature theoretical claim and are recorded here because the dissertation may need to trace CAMIL's evidentiary chain past this cluster's own boundary (P/anticipatory-application).
- **Instruments**: as a theory paper CAMIL introduces no new instrument; it inherits the adapted-Likert presence/motivation/self-efficacy scale families used across ped-sec-09 and ped-sec-11 (Lee et al. 2010; Davis 1989; Witmer & Singer 1998 lineage) by reference rather than by re-specifying them.

## 5. Findings/Claims

- CAMIL's four core claims (stated as the paper's own numbered summary, FAITHFUL-Makransky&Petersen, p.941–942): (1) motivational/learning methods validated on less-immersive media generalize to IVR, but media interacts with method; (2) the general affordances of IVR learning are presence and agency; (3) these affordances influence six affective/cognitive factors (interest, intrinsic motivation, self-efficacy, embodiment, cognitive load, self-regulation); (4) these factors predict four kinds of learning outcome (factual, conceptual, procedural, transfer).
- Immersion, control factors, and representational fidelity are presence's three technological antecedents; control-factor fidelity is agency's primary antecedent (FE, p.942–944).
- Presence and agency both positively predict situational interest, intrinsic motivation, and self-efficacy — the affective route — while simultaneously and positively predicting extraneous cognitive load — the cognitive-load route — from the identical antecedents (FE, p.944–947).
- Self-regulation is the model's one path whose *direction* is left explicitly unresolved (paths 15–16): high presence/agency can either support self-regulated learning (via meaningful peer/agent interaction) or undermine it (via absorption that forecloses reflection), contingent entirely on whether the instructional design builds in reflection/scaffolding activities (FE, p.947).
- Cognitive load is the model's one factor with a *negative* path to learning outcomes; every other factor's path to outcomes is positive (FE, p.949–950).
- The paper's own practical recommendation is to abandon media-comparison research designs (IVR vs. video/desktop, treating media as the independent variable) in favor of interaction-focused designs that test how specific instructional methods interact with presence/agency (FE, p.950–951) — this is CAMIL's most direct methodological intervention into debate-axis 2 of this cluster (media-comparison designs are informative vs. methods-not-media is the valid paradigm).
- Seven external factors are named as influencing but not appearing inside the model proper: usability (ISO 2018), social influence/technology acceptance (Venkatesh & Davis's TAM), age, cybersickness (gender-differentiated per Munafo et al.), spatial ability (Li et al. 2020), personality (Watjatrakul), predisposition to absorption (Wirth et al. 2007), and prior IVR experience/novelty (Clark's 1983 novelty effect; Fenn & Blosch's hype-cycle framing) (FE, p.952–953).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* CAMIL is, by design, the single most citable ready-made theoretical framework this cluster contains for Part III's own analysis, and this unit's most consequential task is to state that role precisely rather than merely gesture at it. Part III's strata (desktop-3D vs. video-only) and poles (lab=VR vs. deployment=2D-PC) map directly onto CAMIL's own immersion/interaction gradient — video-only deployment sits at CAMIL's lowest-immersion, near-zero-agency end (structurally closest to CAMIL's "video/PowerPoint" comparison condition), while the lab pole sits closer to CAMIL's desktop-VR or IVR conditions depending on hardware — so when Part III needs a theory-level account of *why* its own strata should differ on presence, motivation, or cognitive load, CAMIL's path model is the direct citation, not a paraphrase requiring independent derivation *(anticipatory-application)*. CAMIL's cognitive-load path (presence/agency → extraneous load → less learning) offers Part III a theoretically motivated caution against reading its own VLE deployment survey's F7 presence finding ("Presence & the WE," `vle-01-deployment-survey.md`) as straightforwardly positive for learning outcomes: higher presence in the APP stratum could simultaneously raise engagement *and* extraneous cognitive load, and CAMIL is explicit that these are not mutually exclusive *(anticipatory-application)*. CAMIL's external-factors list (usability, novelty, cybersickness) also bears directly on `vle-01`'s F1 "Threshold foreclosure" finding (installer/hardware friction as the VLE's first gate): CAMIL's own usability discussion predicts exactly this kind of low-usability breakage in presence and agency before any of the model's causal paths can even begin to operate *(anticipatory-application)*. A further, more tentative bridge: CAMIL's extraneous-cognitive-load path (more presence/interactivity → more content to filter → higher extraneous load) is a formally distinct but suggestively parallel mechanism to Gloria Mark's rote/bored attentional-states framework in `vle-04-mark-attention-span.md` — both models predict that highly engaging, low-friction environments can paradoxically produce states of reduced productive processing, though the vocabularies (educational-psychology cognitive load vs. attention-science engagement×challenge) should be kept explicit and not silently merged *(anticipatory-application)*. Once the Boredom Experiment (VR Attention Study) dataset entry is built, CAMIL's cognitive-load and self-regulation paths are the most likely theoretical anchor for interpreting any EEG-workload or behavioral-disengagement measures that dataset contains (`****** UNVERIFIED: anchor entry not yet built`).

**Not treated:** CAMIL does not engage the CMC-tradition social-presence literature (Short et al., Gunawardena & Zittle, Garrison/Anderson/Archer, Tu, Biocca/Burgoon/Harms, Kreijns/Kirschner/Jochems, Richardson et al. — this cluster's Strand F) at all; it does not name "flow" or "engagement" as constructs despite discussing closely adjacent territory (hedonic activity, absorption); it does not treat deployment context (home vs. classroom, lab vs. field) as a modeled factor, leaving that question to ped-sec-12's separate equivalence study; it reports no primary data of its own and specifies no sample, instrument battery, or effect sizes beyond what it inherits by citation from other studies; and it does not resolve, only formally co-locates, the affective-route/cognitive-load-route tension that constitutes this cluster's debate-axis 4.
