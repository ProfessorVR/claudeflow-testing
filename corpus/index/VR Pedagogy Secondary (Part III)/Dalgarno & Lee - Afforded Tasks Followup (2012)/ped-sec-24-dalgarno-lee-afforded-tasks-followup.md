# ped-sec-24 — Dalgarno & Lee, "Exploring the Relationship Between Afforded Learning Tasks and Learning Benefits in 3D Virtual Learning Environments" (2012)

**Type:** short conference-paper secondary reading (educational technology / instructional design) · **Discipline:** educational technology, instructional design · **Depth:** map · **Corpus role:** Strand B's sole empirical follow-up unit — the same authorial team's own attempt to test the affordances-of-3D-VLEs model (ped-sec-04) against survey data, rather than a third party's reading of it.

## 1. Thesis/Aim

Dalgarno and Lee return to their own 2009/2010 *British Journal of Educational Technology* model (cited throughout this paper as "Dalgarno & Lee, 2010") to pursue the empirical gap that model's own conclusion named: the unvalidated links between 3-D VLE characteristics, the learning tasks they afford, and the five learning benefits the model predicts (FE, p.236). Drawing on a DEHub-funded Australia/New Zealand scoping-study questionnaire, they pursue two aims: (1) gauge how strongly higher-education staff perceive each of the five hypothesized benefits as occurring, and (2) test whether specific grounded-coded categories of afforded learning task predict specific benefit ratings (FE, p.236, 238).

## 2. Structure

Abstract → Introduction (recaps the 2010 model and states this paper's two aims) → Background and related work (surveys prior 3D-VLE/Second Life activity taxonomies — Ryan; Kay & FitzGerald; Jiang; Hew & Cheung — and affordance accounts — Hollins & Robbins; Warburton & Pérez García; Lim; Dickey — adopting Norman's real/perceived-affordance distinction) → Methods and data sources (DEHub questionnaire, N=117, this paper analyzes the 53 respondents describing detailed 3D-VLE use; Q116 open-ended activity description, Q120 five Likert benefit items) → Results: activity categories (ten grounded-coded, non-exclusive) → Results: reported benefits (Table 1) → Results: activities vs. benefits (MANOVA, Table 2) → Discussion and recommendations → Conclusion (pp.236–245).

## 3. Constructs — empirically testing ped-sec-04's own model

This unit operationalizes ped-sec-04's five learning affordances as five Likert outcome items (Q120). **Representational fidelity** (Affordance 1): "familiarity with a place and the objects within it" (M=5.0, N=53). **Affect: enjoyment & intrinsic motivation / engagement-flow** (Affordance 3): "motivating and engaging" (M=5.9, highest-rated, FAITHFUL-Dalgarno & Lee, p.241). **Learning outcomes: retention & transfer** (Affordance 4): "transfer of learning to real situations" (M=5.5). **Social presence / co-presence** (Affordance 5): "more effective collaborative learning" (M=5.6). **Learning outcomes: procedural/skills** (Affordance 2): "learn through experience in context" (M=5.8). All five means cluster tightly (5.0–5.9), which the paper treats as substantively significant (FE, p.241).

## 4. Conditions & design

Part of a larger DEHub-funded scoping study (Dalgarno, Lee, Carlson, Gregory & Tynan, 2011a): an online questionnaire completed by 117 HE academic staff across Australia/New Zealand; this paper analyzes the 53 who described their 3D-VLE use in detail (FE, p.238). Question 116 (open-ended activity description) was coded via Corbin & Strauss's (2008) constant-comparison method into ten non-exclusive categories: place exploration, concept exploration, task practice, role-play, gaming, communication, slide show, building/scripting, instruction, machinima (FE, p.239–240). Question 120 asked 7-point Likert agreement with the five benefit statements above. A MANOVA (11 activity categories as independent variables x 5 benefit ratings as dependent variables) tested whether activity category predicted benefit rating (FE, p.242).

## 5. Findings/Claims

Respondents strongly endorsed all five benefits essentially regardless of activity type (FE, p.241). The MANOVA found a significant main effect for only two of five benefits — place/object familiarity (F(1,40)=4.841, p=.034) and motivating/engaging (F(1,40)=4.136, p=.049) — both driven by the "instruction" category scoring higher than others; none of the authors' own a priori hypothesized links (place exploration→familiarity; task practice/role-play→transfer; communication→collaboration) reached significance (FE, p.242–243). The authors offer two rival explanations: most activities spanned multiple categories, confounding attribution, and/or respondents' uniformly high agreement may reflect enthusiast self-selection and positive-attitude bias rather than evidenced outcomes (FE, p.243–244) — a self-authored caution weakening confidence in their model's task-specificity. Recommendations: controlled 3D-VLE-vs-non-3D-VLE comparisons (flagging Clark's 1994 media-vs-method confound and access-ethics problems), student- rather than solely educator-perceived benefit data, and attending to the perceived-affordance gap (Norman, 1999) between students and educators (FE, p.243–244).

## 6. Concordance feed

`representational fidelity` × `desktop-3D / non-immersive VR` (educator-survey aggregate, p.241, M=5.0); `affect: enjoyment & intrinsic motivation`/`engagement / flow` × same condition (p.241, 243, M=5.9, only benefit besides familiarity to show a significant activity-category effect); `learning outcomes: retention & transfer` × same condition (p.241, M=5.5); `social presence / co-presence` × same condition (p.241, M=5.6); `learning outcomes: procedural / skills` × same condition (p.241, M=5.8, the single highest-rated benefit).

## 7. Analytical role

*(anticipatory-application)* This unit is Strand B's direct empirical answer to ped-sec-04's own closing call for research testing its model's assumed links, executed by the same authorial team rather than a third party — an unusually tight anchor-instance-of-its-own-call relationship (see `extends-reading-of` edge). Its central finding complicates rather than confirms the parent model: near-uniform high endorsement of all five benefits regardless of task design suggests either genuine task-benefit robustness or an enthusiast-response artifact the authors cannot rule out, a caution Part III should weigh whenever activity/condition differences are inferred from self-report alone *(anticipatory-application)*.
