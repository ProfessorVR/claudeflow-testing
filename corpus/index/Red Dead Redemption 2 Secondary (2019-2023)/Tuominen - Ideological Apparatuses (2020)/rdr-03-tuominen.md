# Tuominen, Juho (2020) — *Rockstar's Open World Video Games as Ideological Apparatuses: A Case Study of Red Dead Redemption Series*

**Type:** MA thesis (Tampere University, Internet and Game Studies; supervisors Mikko Meriläinen, Olli Sotamaa). **Discipline:** game studies / media studies, Marxist-Althusserian cultural critique. **Depth:** deep-long (~24.8K words). **Corpus role:** primary cluster anchor for the *celebratory ↔ critical-ideological* debate axis, and the most explicit Althusserian reading of RDR/RDR2 in the cluster.

## Thesis

The article argues that Rockstar's single-player open-world games function as ideological apparatuses **on two distinct levels**. First, as mass-cultural products they are part of the wider *cultural Ideological State Apparatus* (ISA) in Althusser's sense — they help produce, reflect, and renegotiate dominant ideology. Second, and more novel, each game *internally renders a new world that contains its own simulated apparatuses*: an in-game Repressive State Apparatus (RSA) and in-game ISAs that "hail" the player and interpellate them to progress, and in progressing, to absorb the game's ideology. Within the game the designers hold both State power and the State Apparatus; the player is rendered a subject who "freely" submits by choosing from premeditated options. The substantive payoff for the RDR case is a counter-celebratory but also counter-cynical reading: against critics who read RDR as endorsing neoliberalism (Humphreys, Morgan), Tuominen contends the *RDR series read as a whole* (RDR2 then RDR) is "mainstream but radically progressive," condemning the neoliberal-capitalist state as a "syndicate of cruelty, corruption, and coercion" by transplanting the radical Italian (Spaghetti) Western's politics into a AAA open world.

## Structure

1. **Introduction** — political/ideological dimensions of virtual worlds; the industry's denial of its own politics; three reasons Althusserian reading matters (games as part of new capitalism; games as cultural ISA; designer↔player as ruler↔subject / class struggle). Case scope: RDR (2010) + RDR2 (2018), single-player only; *Red Dead Revolver* and multiplayer/DLC excluded.
2. **Theoretical framework** — (2.1) key concepts: Althusser's ideology/ISA; open-world adventure game as a genre; the radical Italian Western (drawing Fisher 2014); (2.2) video-game literacy (Zimmerman, Zagal, Bourgonjon, Hayes & Gee); (2.3) games as rhetorical tools (Bogost's procedural rhetoric; Mäyrä's core/shell); (2.4) open world as experience (Mitchell, Murray, Bown, Galloway, self-determination theory via Meriläinen); (2.5) politics of the open world (frontier/"new world" critique via Gunkel & Murray).
3. **Literature review** — (3.1) Althusser and games (Garite 2003; Quijano-Cruz 2008); (3.2) other Rockstar/RDR studies (Pérez Latorre, Dyer-Witheford & de Peuter, Humphreys, Pallant, Triana, Westerside & Holopainen, Bagnoli, Morgan, Donald & Reid).
4. **Methodology** — formal analysis (Lankoski & Björk, Fernández-Vara) combined with critical analysis (Gunkel, Murray); close reading (Bizzochi & Tanenbaum); reflexive acknowledgment of the researcher's position; data = each game played through twice (RDR on Xbox 360, RDR2 on PS4), notes taken, single-player focus.
5. **Analysis** — (5.1) context: Rockstar Games and the "Rockstar method"; (5.2) game overview, rules/gameplay, and formal+critical close reading organized chronologically ("Outlaws for Life" = RDR2 / Van der Linde gang; "Outlaws to the end" = RDR / John Marston; "Long live the alternative" = Jack as the surviving "woke" hope); (5.3) RDR as ideological apparatus.
6. **Discussion** — contrasts findings against each cluster-mate and interlocutor.
7. **Conclusions** + future research (the GTA+RDR combined timeline; Finnish games).

## Concepts

- **Ideological State Apparatus (ISA) / Repressive State Apparatus (RSA).** Following Althusser (2012, in Žižek ed. *Mapping Ideology*), the State Apparatus splits into the RSA (government, police, army — functioning by violence) and the ISAs (religion, education, family — functioning by ideology). The article's signature move is to locate *both inside the diegesis*: a game's in-world police/army/law form its RSA; its in-world media, religion, culture, legal-political system, and — crucially — its **side quests, activities, and NPC cast** form its ISAs, even when they don't resemble real institutions.
- **Interpellation / "hailing."** The mechanism by which an apparatus offers the subject an identity accepted as "natural or obvious." The article repurposes it twice over: the game hails the *player* (via rewards, feedback, lucrative story missions) to invest and to "freely" submit to designer-set rules; and the game's narrative ISAs hail the player to keep progressing, so that progression *is* ideological absorption.
- **Open-world ideology / the apparatus on two levels.** The thesis's central synthesis — games are simultaneously a node of the cultural ISA *and* self-contained worlds running their own apparatuses (credited to Quijano-Cruz 2008).
- **Directed / illusory freedom.** Open-world freedom is "largely illusory" (Mitchell); the player gets "experiences of experiences" (Muriel & Crawford); the player makes no choices "the game has not already made." Žižek's free-floating-vs-total-control dyad anchors this.
- **Procedural rhetoric** (Bogost) and **core/shell** (Mäyrä) — games persuade through rule-based representation, so analysis must read gameplay *and* representation together.
- **The radical Italian Western (Fisher 2014).** Four plot variants (Foundation; Transitional; Zapata-Spaghetti; Repressive State Apparatus plot). RDR is argued to use all four, but the **RSA plot** dominates: an outwardly civilized society unmasked as predicated on violent tyranny; the law as repressive force, the state as absolute enemy. This is the bridge connecting RDR's content to Althusser.
- **Player-as-revolutionary / class struggle simulation.** From a Marxist read, mastering a game = a player's attempt to "capture State power" from designers ("your rules won't hold me"); designer↔player mirrors ruling class↔proletariat.
- **Woke gaming** (Gray & Leonard 2018) — invoked for Jack Marston as the educated, justice-alert youth who carries the surviving "alternative."

## Readings about RDR2 / Rockstar

- **The two-level apparatus applied.** Rockstar is unquestionably part of the mass-culture industry (cultural ISA); RDR/RDR2 *also* render self-contained worlds whose RSA (Pinkertons, US Army, lawmen, the encroaching state) and ISAs (the gang/cast, side activities, in-world institutions) simulate Althusser's theory in action.
- **Reading the series as one whole.** A methodological keystone: because RDR2 (the gang's rise) and RDR (its end) "narratively form a whole," the ideology only becomes legible across both, analyzed in story-chronological order. This is what separates Tuominen from cluster-mates who treat a single title.
- **RDR2 = "Long live the alternative."** RDR2 keeps the dream of a non-neoliberal society alive by depicting a (multicultural, quasi-socialist) utopia that *could* be achieved; it pushes progressive values (ridicule of racism, support for women's suffrage). The gang's collective stash is read as socialist: "life isn't about the survival of the individual but collective."
- **RDR = "Outlaws to the end" / mourning.** RDR dramatizes the *loss* of the alternative; John Marston is read **not as a cowboy but as an outlaw** (an explicit correction of Humphreys, Triana, Westerside & Holopainen) and as a reluctant revolutionary who refuses "the only option of neoliberalism." The state's betrayal — extorting then murdering John despite a freedom deal — exposes its coercive nature.
- **Jack Marston as the surviving hope.** The third, briefly playable character; the "woke," book-loving, homeschooled youth in whom the revolutionary future persists, even after the gang's demise.
- **The Rockstar contradiction.** A sustained ideology-critique turned on the studio itself: RDR/RDR2 promote progressive/communal values, yet Rockstar as a company "betrays the very ideology it promotes" — crunch culture, fear-based management, unpaid overtime (Schreier, Sinclair, Berry, Badenhorst), ~8-year/2,000-staff development, ~$1B estimated cost. The author concludes Rockstar acts in the very way it "criticize[s] and ridicule[s] in their games."
- **GTA+RDR combined timeline.** RDR lays the "building blocks and foundations" of a future society that GTA then "simulates in effect" — America developing "from the wild to the hyper capitalist theme park." RDR2 read as opposing Trump and the alt-right by valorizing community over the individual.

## Game-elements treated

- **Honor system** (rules-of-world / mechanic). Read as procedural-rhetorical and *ideologically inconsistent*: good deeds raise honor (better pay, merchant discounts); theft/murder lower it (cheaper bribes, black-market discounts); NPC reactions track the scale. Two pointed readings: (a) the player *must* commit dishonorable acts (beating hostages in train robberies) to advance "heroic," so honor is incoherent; (b) **killing Ku Klux Klan members in the woods costs no honor** — the rules thereby "imply that killing racists is okay," reinforcing the progressive read via the mechanic itself.
- **Collective stash / "check the books"** (mechanic + narrative ISA). Donations to the gang's shared fund are read as socialist redistribution; but inspecting the ledger reveals Arthur is the most generous contributor, seeding his disillusionment — and the gang stays "chronically poor" no matter how much the player banks, so in-game money is narratively near-irrelevant.
- **Main-mission vs. side-activity economy** (rules / incentive structure). Main missions (mostly crime) give the largest economic rewards; side quests/fishing/hunting/gambling/chores give small rewards or pure entertainment. Their function is to *interpellate* the player toward the story — side content "exists solely to interpellate a player to progress."
- **Side quests + NPC cast as ISAs** (system→apparatus mapping). The Van der Linde cast and the games' activities are explicitly identified as the game's ISAs — institutions that hail the player to invest, regardless of real-world resemblance.
- **Dutch's "Tahiti" utopia plan / "we rob Uncle Sam and leave"** (narrative). The motivating false promise; a Robin Hood façade that masks Dutch's personal gain — the engine of interpellation that the player (as Arthur) is hailed to believe in.
- **The bank-manager line** (scripted dialogue / representation). When John takes a loan to buy a ranch: "Congratulations! You are now a real American, indebted and owned by the bank!" — read as the game congratulating the player's submission to the neoliberal state.
- **"Season meat but can't swim underwater"** (rule-boundary / field of possibilities). The thesis's emblem of the designed "incomplete universe": what a game renders possible vs. impossible always encodes a worldview (after Mitchell/Murray/Žižek).
- **Honor-bearing slogans** (paratext/branding). "Outlaws for Life" (RDR2, hopeful) vs. "Outlaws to the End" (RDR, fatalist) read as encoding the series' arc from living alternative to its inevitable loss.
- **Completion statistic** (player-behavior datum). Only ~22% of PS4 players had finished RDR2's story by Jan 2019 (Treese 2019) — used to argue that experiences diverge by how players approach the world, and that "cowboy-simulator" play represents a *failure of interpellation* (the game doesn't reward it as progress).
- **Honor system's KKK exception** is highlighted again as the clearest case of the rules "reinforcing certain behavior" — i.e., a mechanic carrying ideological content directly.
- **Spaghetti-Western set-piece borrowings** (level/scene design). The Fort Mercer assault ≈ *Django* (1966); "Tall Trees" ≈ *Il Grande Silenzio* (1968); revolutionary-Mexico setting ≈ *Per un pugno di dollari* (1964) — formal evidence that RDR transplants the radical Western's apparatus-unmasking.
- **Lifelike-NPC behavior contrast** (world simulation). In RDR, NPCs work, chat, and persist regardless of the player ("the world exists despite the player"), versus GTA crowds that "run around aimlessly" ("the world exists for the player") — read both as technical advance and as RDR camouflaging the player's centrality (after Westerside & Holopainen's "world to exist in").

## Themes

- Open-world freedom as designed/illusory; every choice "already made."
- Games as procedural-rhetorical, ideology-bearing cultural artifacts; the need for video-game literacy and critical analysis.
- The Western as a vehicle for left critique (Spaghetti Western → AAA game) — cultural borrowing, renegotiation, and "demonic satire" of neoliberalism.
- Neoliberalism's "end of history" (Žižek: easier to imagine the end of the world than alternatives) vs. the persistence of utopian hope.
- The reflexive gap between progressive game content and exploitative game-industry labor.
- The series-as-canon method: ideology legible only across the whole arc.

**Cluster-mates cited:** **Bagnoli (2019)** — closest sibling, also an Althusserian MA thesis on RDR2; Tuominen credits the overlap but claims to push further by theorizing game-specific ISAs and player-progression interpellation, where Bagnoli mainly reads how RSA/ISAs are *represented*. **Westerside & Holopainen (2019)** ("Sites of Play") — adopted for "world to exist in" and for granting RDR2 more progressive content, but Tuominen contests their (Humphreys-derived) view that RDR endorses neoliberalism, and their cowboy framing. **Morgan (2020)** — agreed-with on the oscillating player/game control balance, but sharply contested on hegemonic masculinity/whiteness: Tuominen says Morgan "does not grasp the potential" of the gang's communal-survival logic (its socialist, alternative-bearing function). **Donald & Reid (2020)** — invoked on RDR2's educational/historical-accuracy use, but set aside because they "ignore completely RDR2's narrative elements," making comparison hard. (Humphreys 2012, Triana 2015, Pallant 2013 are also engaged but fall outside the named cluster.) **Not cited:** Crowley, Wright, Vanderhoef & Payne, Ventomäki, McEvoy, Moser, Ruffino.

## Debate-axis positioning (celebratory ↔ critical-ideological)

The article sits firmly on the **critical-ideological** pole — it is an explicit ideology critique, never a celebratory or aesthetic appreciation. But it occupies a *distinctive, third position within* the critical camp. The dominant critical reading in the cluster/literature (Humphreys; Morgan; and, for RDR, Westerside & Holopainen) is that RDR *reproduces* neoliberalism, exceptionalism, white supremacy, and hegemonic masculinity — i.e., ideologically complicit. Tuominen contests that: read as a whole and through the Spaghetti-Western/RSA-plot lens, RDR/RDR2 are a *critique of* neoliberalism that is "mainstream but radically progressive," even gesturing at a surviving alternative (Jack). So the axis here is less celebratory↔critical than **complicit-critique ↔ subversive-critique**: Tuominen shifts the debate from "is the game ideological?" (yes, all are) toward "does its ideology endorse or unmask the neoliberal state?" — answering *unmask*. He simultaneously re-anchors the discussion away from "RDR as American cultural artifact" (Humphreys, Bagnoli, Morgan) toward "RDR within the European tradition of capitalism-critique." The one counter-celebratory check he keeps is the Rockstar-labor contradiction, which prevents his progressive reading from becoming an endorsement of the studio.

## Tensions

- **Author's reflexivity vs. strong claims.** Tuominen concedes (Bownian) that his reading may be "mere opinions" shaped by his position (white North-European male, self-identified proletarian, lifelong gamer), yet asserts the analysis is "well-informed" and grounded in prior research — an unresolved hermeneutic tension he names but doesn't dissolve.
- **Progressive content vs. exploitative production.** The thesis's own apparatus is turned on Rockstar: the games preach community/anti-greed while the studio practices crunch and centralization — the "Rockstar contradiction" the author flags as ideologically revealing.
- **Player agency vs. designer control.** Honor and "freedom" are mechanically real yet narratively void (outcomes fixed; money irrelevant); the player is "revolutionary" (mastering the game = capturing State power) yet wholly interpellated.
- **Honor as moral system vs. honor's incoherence.** Honor rewards virtue but forces vice to progress, and exempts KKK-killing — so the mechanic both encodes a progressive ideology and undercuts its own moral coherence.
- **Series-as-whole method vs. cluster comparability.** Reading RDR+RDR2 as one entity yields the progressive thesis, but makes head-to-head comparison with single-title studies difficult (the author admits this re: Donald & Reid).

---

*diss-hook (anticipatory-application): The honor system's "must commit dishonor to act heroically" incoherence — plus the no-honor-cost KKK killing — is a precise site for the actualization-of-desire chain: a mechanic that interpellates desire toward a scripted "good" while withholding the agency to actualize it cleanly, i.e., a designed gap between orektic pull (rewarded virtue) and the praxis the game forces, readable against the RODA tutorial/disruption frame for RDR2's Part II analysis.*
