# Bagnoli, Nicolas (2019) — *How Video Games Reflect a Discourse About Another Era: The Case of Red Dead Redemption II*

**Type:** MA thesis (Master en Communication Multilingue), Université de Liège, Faculté de Philosophie et Lettres, Département Médias, Culture et Communication. Promoters: Fanny Barnabé and Marie Herbillon. Académic year 2018–2019. ~100 pp. of body text.
**Discipline:** Francophone game studies / media-and-communication discourse analysis (ludo-narrative method).
**Depth handled here:** deep-long (RDR2-substantive focus on Part 3; Parts 1–2 skimmed for method).

## Thesis / argument arc

Bagnoli argues that *Red Dead Redemption II*, though set at the turn of the twentieth century (he repeatedly dates the diegesis to "the very beginning of the XXth century"), conveys a **discourse about the player's own contemporary era** — that the game "manages to say something about a period of time that is not his." The premise is that the fictional Old-West world tells us about the present. The mechanism by which it does so is, centrally, Ian Bogost's **procedural rhetoric**: arguments mounted "through the authorship of rules of behavior," not only through words or images. The game embeds modern values (feminism, anti-racism, the animal/ecological cause) into a historically distant setting, and it does so by coordinating *all* of the game's elements at once — avatar, cut-scenes, interactivity, the **honor** system, environment, components (letters, pamphlets, newspapers), PNJs (the French *personnages non-joueurs* / NPCs), and dialogue. A recurring thesis-claim: "games are a whole and one aspect does not go without another." The game both **remediatizes** stereotypes (re-circulates ready-made cultural shortcuts) and, for some groups, **deconstructs** them.

The work is explicitly framed by Bogost's slogan that video games are "an expressive medium" that represents systems, invites interaction, and prompts judgment. Bagnoli layers onto Bogost a second theoretical spine — Louis Althusser's **Ideological State Apparatuses (ISA)** and **Repressive State Apparatuses (RSA)** — to read the game's institutions (police, army; church, family) as ideological machinery.

## Section structure (three parts)

**Part 1 — The specific features of RDR2.** Production context (Rockstar Games, the Houser brothers, eight years of development, "crunch" controversy, 23 million copies); "American video games" (via Gaël Seydoux's claim of US mastery of action/war/banditry games derived from Hollywood); open-world action/adventure genre. Then the conceptual toolkit: the **ludo-narrative conflict** (narratology vs. ludology — Eskelinen on games-as-simulation vs. Barnabé/Hurel on porosity); the **concept of video game** subdivided (after Hurel/Järvinen) into information, environment, components, virtual interface, characters; the **avatar** (Fanny Georges, Genvo, Frank Beau — Arthur Morgan as an "avatar-marionnette," predefined, customizable only in clothes/guns/hair); **rules & gameplay** (Salen & Zimmerman: free movement within rigid structure); the **narrative aspect** (Chevaldonné, Lits on "world view"); and **movies & video games** (Blanchet on cinema-to-game borrowing; the GTA license; the **western parallelism**; **cut scenes**).

**Part 2 — Video games, ideology and discourse.** Discourse theory (Krieg-Planque's *présupposé* and *sous-entendu* as implicit-persuasion techniques); **procedural rhetoric** (Bogost, with the *McDonald's Videogame* example); **layering values**; **interactivity** (Barnabé's *Candy Crush* vs. *Undertale* tutorials); **ideology** (Marx, Althusser; *America's Army* as recruiting ideology); **morality** (Cameron Moore, Sicart, Bogost on arithmetic/linear morality systems; comparisons to *Fable*, *Mass Effect*, *KOTOR*); **RSA and ISA** (police, army; church, family); **stereotypes** (Amossy, Blanchet — stereotype as cognitive shortcut and narrative-economy device).

**Part 3 — The means put in place to deliver a discourse in RDR2 (the analytic core).** Seven worked scene-analyses, each cross-wiring multiple game systems: (2.1) the Saint-Denis suffragette + the *Course of True Love* feminist-march mission; (2.2) the eugenics supporter Norris Forsythe; (2.3) the Ku Klux Klan encounters; (2.4) foreign-people representations — (2.4.1) the French painter Charles Châtenay, (2.4.2) the Italian mafioso Angelo Bronte; (2.5) the animal cause / Albert Mason; (2.6) a random PNJ conversation (the trial of a black man); (2.7) the conflict between the "Red Skins" (Wapiti Indians) and the US Army.

## Key concepts & interpretive readings

- **Procedural rhetoric (Bogost).** The load-bearing concept. Bagnoli reads RDR2's **honor gauge** as the game's primary procedural argument: dialogue actions ("greet") are coded positive, violent actions ("rob," "aim weapon," pulling the trigger) negative, producing a "polarized system of values." Crucially he stresses that RDR2 *permits* the "bad" actions (unlike *Horizon: Zero Dawn* or *Breath of the Wild*, which forbid killing friendly NPCs and so make non-violence a *présupposé*); what the game valorizes is therefore "not pacifism but the possibility of choice," albeit a "free will" already biased because bad actions are punished.
- **Layering of values.** During missions the player may kill police/army with **no honor penalty**, but the same act in free roam tanks honor. The procedural message is "conflicting": killing a lawman is wrong, yet "tolerated" if scenario-justified. Even self-defense in free roam is scored as a bad action. Honor is *absent* from main missions.
- **Remediatization vs. deconstruction of stereotypes.** The central interpretive axis of Part 3. National/ethnic stereotypes (British eccentricity, French romantic narcissism, Italian mafia-and-spaghetti) are *remediatized* — re-circulated as recognizable shortcuts. By contrast the **Indian "savage"** stereotype is deliberately *deconstructed* through Rains Fall and the missions done alongside the Wapiti.
- **The avatar as ideological vector.** Arthur Morgan, an "avatar-marionnette," carries a fixed progressive disposition independent of player choice: even if the player turns Arthur into a killer, the scripted Arthur is open-minded ("Women voting? Sure, why not"), anti-racist, tolerant ("good and bad in all"). Cut-scenes "put distance" between player and avatar (Genvo's being/appearing) and are precisely where un-resistable values are delivered.
- **Présupposé / sous-entendu (Krieg-Planque).** Used to parse in-game dialogue: Norris Forsythe's racism works by *sous-entendu* (implying white people are under threat); Arthur's rebuttals deploy *sous-entendus* of his own ("dumb white people").
- **Althusserian apparatuses.** The camp is read as a Family ISA; church (mocked via Reverend Swanson, the fictional cult "Chelonia") and family are ISAs; police and army are RSAs. Bagnoli notes the asymmetry: in games, **religion is typically criticized** (its abuses) while the **army is typically valorized** — so RDR2's criticism of each is "not subversive in the same way."
- **Western-cinema remediation.** Via Blanchet/Chevaldonné and Dan Houser's western binge-watching: horse shootings, cowboy duels, train robberies, bridge demolitions are inherited western codes; the **cinema mode** camera and the "cinemascope" black bars on conversation reinforce the filmic register.

## The game-elements treated (the concordance payload)

Bagnoli is unusually concrete about specific systems, missions, characters, regions, and components:

- **Honor system / honor gauge** — the spine of his procedural-rhetoric reading; honor rises for greeting/helping and for killing KKK members, falls for killing innocents, dogs, horses, over-hunting; absent in main missions.
- **Interaction wheel / four free-roam verbs** — "greet," "antagonize," "rob," "aim weapon"; NPC reactions are contingent on the chosen verb (except scripted PNJs).
- **The snake-bite roadside encounter** — the four-option dilemma (give tonic, suck venom, leave to die, mercy-kill) used as the paradigm of open-world choice-with-consequence.
- **The Course of True Love (Chapter 2 mission)** — Arthur drives a wagon of suffragists in a women's march, must protect them; no option to refuse (procedural compulsion).
- **The Saint-Denis suffragette (free-roam PNJ)** — scripted two-stage conversation; identical discourse whether greeted or antagonized; harming her costs honor.
- **Norris Forsythe, the eugenics supporter (Saint-Denis free-roam PNJ)** — racist pamphlet (a **component**); modeled to resemble Hitler; can be beaten/killed with no honor loss or police response.
- **The Ku Klux Klan encounters (×2, free-roam, Lemoyne)** — cross-burning ceremony that self-immolates; killing members *grants* honor; lootable "Meeting notes" letter ridicules the group.
- **Charles Châtenay (French painter, Saint-Denis quest line "The Artist's Way")** — bar meeting (only "greet" available), the nude art-gallery exhibition scene; stereotype of the French *remediatized*.
- **Angelo Bronte (Italian mafioso, Chapter 4 antagonist, Saint-Denis)** — "Angelo Bronte, a Man of Honor" mission #44; Italian music cue, mafia dress/manner; kidnaps Jack (the gang's youngest boy); betrays the gang.
- **Animal cause / hunting system** — over-hunting penalty (honor loss for excess kills); horse-killing and dog-killing penalties (dogs trigger sheriff/town hostility and lasting grudges); meat/pelt economy.
- **Albert Mason (photographer, "Arcadia for Amateurs" 5-mission strand)** — wolves mission (Arcadia II), alligator mission; read as a *paradoxical/hypocritical* conservationist (animals die for his photos; he ignores the boar eaten by the gator). Anchors the contemporary eco-discourse.
- **Random PNJ overheard conversation (Rhodes)** — two strangers debating the rigged trial of Sampson Black, a black man; staged as conservative vs. progressive worldviews; environment-as-discourse.
- **Red Skins vs. US Army (Chapter 6 strand)** — the **Wapiti Indians**, **Rains Fall** (peace-seeking chief, stereotype deconstructed), **Colonel Favours** (withholds vaccines/medicine, confiscates horses, allied with oil tycoon **Leviticus Cornwall**), missions **Honor, Amongst Thieves** (recovering stolen medicine) and **The Fine Art of Conversation** (#77, escorting Rains Fall to Favours); music underscores Indian peacefulness.
- **Banking, the Old American Art (Chapter 4, mission #57)** — the Saint-Denis bank robbery gone wrong; Pinkertons, rooftop escape, long pursuit; used as the exemplar of "gameplay as staging tool" + cinema mode.
- **The Van der Linde gang as Family-ISA / camp** — Dutch (anti-modern, "return to nature," increasingly violent/decadent), Hosea ("You'll damn us all, Dutch!"), John Marston (jailed after Saint-Denis, rescued behind Dutch's back), Herr Strauss (the German loan-shark accountant Arthur finally refuses to collect for and expels), Reverend Swanson (debauched clergyman). Arthur's late-game moral turn (acting on his own convictions as death nears) is read against Dutch's decadence via Bogost on "crime as decadence."
- **Agent Milton (Pinkerton, Chapter 3 camp confrontation)** — the "we didn't kill all them savages…" exchange establishing the authorities' view of Indians and the encroachment of "civilized land."
- **He Is British, Of Course (side mission)** — retrieving a "zebra" (a painted donkey) for an eccentric Englishman; mission *name* itself read as information-channel stereotype.
- **Regions / setting** — Saint-Denis (the industrialized city, hub of the suffragette, Norris, Châtenay, Bronte), Lemoyne (swamps, gators), Rhodes; the 6-chapters-plus-epilogue structure.
- **Cinema mode + cinemascope conversation framing** — the player-as-camera "movie-making" affordance; black bars on every NPC conversation.

## Themes

Feminism and women's suffrage; race, eugenics, and white supremacy (KKK, Norris Forsythe, the Sampson Black trial); national/ethnic stereotyping (British, French, Italian, Indigenous); the animal/ecological cause and conservationist hypocrisy; settler-colonial violence and the Manifest Destiny doctrine (Wounded Knee invoked); modernization vs. the outlaw's freedom; institutional ideology (church, family, police, army); satire and political correctness as Rockstar's house style; the cinema–game relationship.

## Debate-axis positioning

- **Ludology vs. narratology:** Bagnoli explicitly sets the debate aside as "belonging to the past," siding with the porosity camp (Barnabé/Hurel): narration and gameplay are "constantly articulated," and he declines to adjudicate whether players actually experience the game narratively (he brackets the speed-runner case).
- **Mechanics vs. fiction as the locus of meaning:** he refuses the either/or. His signature move is that meaning emerges only from the *co-operation* of mechanics (honor, interaction verbs, permissions), fiction (dialogue, cut-scenes, characters), and environment (PNJs, components, regions) — "games are a whole."
- **Does play transfer values to the player? (Bogost vs. Jenkins/Sicart):** he foregrounds Bogost's caution (and Sicart's "moral imperative inscribed in the character, not transferred to the player") — the honor system models morality "arithmetically/linearly," embedding values in Arthur rather than coercing the player's own ethics.
- **Authenticity / historical representation:** he treats RDR2's history as deliberately anachronistic — "realistic" period detail is the vehicle for smuggling in present-day values; the game is "historically accurate" on facts (alligator near-extinction, Wounded Knee, suffrage timeline) yet "idealizes"/satirizes the discourse layered on top. This anticipates the cluster's authenticity debate (Donald & Reid, McEvoy, Wright) without naming it.

## diss-hook (anticipatory-application)

> **diss-hook:** [anticipatory-application] Bagnoli's reading of the honor gauge as a procedural argument that *permits but penalizes* "bad" acts — valorizing "the possibility of choice" over any particular virtue — is a ready-made counterpart to the RODA / actualization-of-desire chain: the gauge externalizes, scores, and feeds back the consequences of an enacted desire, and the free-roam vs. main-mission asymmetry (honor present vs. absent) is a clean case of the game constraining which desires can be actualized and which are merely *présupposé*. His suffragette and eugenics scenes — interaction → forced cut-scene → un-resistable scripted utterance → honor consequence — map directly onto a tutorial/disruption analysis of how RDR2 shapes, blocks, and re-channels player desire at the moment of completed action.
