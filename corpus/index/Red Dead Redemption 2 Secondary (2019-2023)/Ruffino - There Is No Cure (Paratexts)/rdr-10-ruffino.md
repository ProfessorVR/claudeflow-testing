# Ruffino, "There Is No Cure: Paratexts as Remediations of Agency in *Red Dead Redemption 2*" (2022)

**Citation.** Ruffino, Paolo (2022). "There is no cure: Paratexts as remediations of agency in *Red Dead Redemption 2*." *Convergence: The International Journal of Research into New Media Technologies* 28(2): 345–358. DOI: 10.1177/13548565221081062. Part of the special issue *Politicizing Agency in Digital Play After Humanism*.

**Discipline.** Media/communication and game studies (paratext and affect theory; auto-ethnography). **Depth.** Deep.

---

## Thesis

Ruffino argues that the YouTube videos players have produced about curing Arthur Morgan's tuberculosis function as *paratexts that "remediate" a perceived loss of player agency*, not as instrumental guides to an actual in-game solution. The central claim turns on the double sense of "remediation" from Bolter and Grusin: the videos at once *reform/restore upon* the videogame medium and, etymologically (Latin *remederi*, "to restore health"), attempt to *heal* a wound — the gap RDR2 opens between the event of Arthur's illness and its invisible, unrepresented cause. Because RDR2 stages tuberculosis as "a consequence with no actions" (a symptom with no shown contagion and no cure), it withdraws an agency normally granted to the white, able-bodied male protagonist. The paratexts try to restore that agency on two registers — *narrative* (the story as a navigable database of forks) and *environment* (the "gamespace" of exploitable resources and disposable NPCs) — yet they concede their futility in the recurring annoyed comment "there is no cure." Through an auto-ethnography spanning spring 2019 to spring 2020, Ruffino then folds in COVID-19, arguing the pandemic's visual remediations (contagion simulations, prevention infographics) reproduce the *same affective structure* and the same white, able-bodied normativity: both restore an illusory subject who can see the invisible, navigate causation backwards, and control an environment of disposable others.

## Structure

The article proceeds in six movements.

1. **Introduction: "a consequence with no actions."** Sets up RDR2's premise — Arthur in the declining Van der Linde gang, with limited control over his life, contracts tuberculosis in Saint Denis and dies across all four endings. The puzzle: the game shows neither when contagion happens nor how to cure it, yet the player community has produced a corpus of "cure" paratexts that *affirm a cure cannot be found*.
2. **Methodology: auto-ethnography and the performative potential of paratexts.** Positions the method against play-session-bound auto-ethnographies (Giddings & Kennedy, Keogh, McArthur); Ruffino's is retrospective, multi-text, and self-implicating. Theorizes paratexts via Consalvo and Genette as performative and co-constitutive of reading.
3. **Agency on the narrative: the disembodied subject of interactive storytelling.** YouTube thumbnails as threshold-paratexts promising unexplored forks, producing a "disembodied" navigating subject.
4. **Agency on the environment: prevention in gamespace.** Close reading of the "Thomas Downes hypothesis" videos, where players try to mask Arthur or kill Downes to prevent infection.
5. **Remediating a global pandemic.** Maps the RDR2 affective structure onto COVID-19 visual culture (memes, WHO virus imagery, contagion curves, distancing/jogging infographics).
6. **Conclusion.** Fictional and real respiratory infections alike are "enmeshed in the virtuality of gamespace"; prevention's normativity assuages fragility while excluding those who cannot control their storyline or environment.

## Key concepts

- **Paratext (Genette → Consalvo, Gray).** Ruffino re-reads Genette's *Seuils* ("thresholds") as texts with illocutionary force and a performative function — prefaces, epigraphs that "involve a commitment" — and stresses that text and paratext are *not ontologically separate*: paratextuality is co-constitutive of interpretation, prefiguring the representationalism-versus-performativity debate (via Barad). Consalvo supplies "challenges to sedimented meanings" and the de-centering of the game-as-text; Gray, the capacity to extend a product's life. Paratexts here are *mediations*, not separate media objects.
- **Remediation (Bolter & Grusin).** The load-bearing concept. The videos "reform and restore upon" the videogame and try to "fulfill its unkept promise." Ruffino exploits the double meaning — media remediation *and* healing (*remederi*) — to argue they attempt to suture the wound between Arthur's symptom and its invisible cause, even as the attempt reopens it (hence the angry comments).
- **Agency (split into narrative and environmental).** Drawing on Jennings's meta-synthesis, Joyce's ludic/narrative distinction, and Cole & Gillies's mechanical/fictional distinction, Ruffino treats agency as the *affect of being in control* (Owen) rather than causal power, underscoring Stang's point (after Smethurst & Craps) that interactive-storytelling agency is largely an *illusion* — choices exceed actual affordances.
- **"There is no cure" / Arthur's illness as figure.** The annoyed comment names a contradiction: the simultaneous desire to save Arthur and the awareness that doing so is impossible. Tuberculosis becomes the figure of an invisible agent cutting across all narrative options independently of player performance — a "consequence with no actions."
- **Gamespace (Wark, via Anikina/Munster).** The environment imagined as "everywhere and nowhere," a network aesthetic flattening all relations into "a uniform, all-encompassing cloud of connections" tied to the user; "turn story-line into navigation" names the disembodied narrative subject.
- **Affect / structure of feeling.** Following Anable, Massumi, and Kember & Zylinska, the videos are *productive of affective states* — a "productive, rather than negative, relationship between the event and its mediation" — not descriptions; thumbnails provoke "bodily" responses and let meaning "appear retrospectively."
- **White, able-bodied normativity.** The politicizing throughline: the agency RDR2 denies is that "typically assigned to the white, able-bodied male character," and the paratexts (later, the pandemic visuals) restore that subject while dispossessing human and nonhuman others (Nakamura on racialized gold-farmers, Carr on disability, De Wildt et al. on the avatar). COVID-19 is in turn glossed as a Mortonian *hyperobject* refracted in micro-actions (hand-washing, masking, watching TB videos).

## Readings

**Thumbnails as narrative paratexts.** Ruffino reads YouTube thumbnails as threshold-paratexts between one video and the next: Arthur "NEVER Dies and LIVES After The Ending" (a grey-haired smiling Arthur "we will never see"); "TB Cure … secret outcome"; "Revisiting the Doctor that Diagnosed Arthur"; "How to Avoid Getting Tuberculosis from Thomas Downes." These promise storylines/dialogues/actions that *never actually appear in the game or videos*; they work as clickbait where "linking" and "liking" collapse (Lovink), performatively producing a *disembodied subject* able to "see the virus," know the cause, and turn story-line into navigation (Wark) — a position Ruffino confesses he came to occupy.

**The "Thomas Downes hypothesis" as environmental paratext.** The most detailed reading centers on Doak1851's ~10-minute video (30 Nov 2018; 2M+ views, 5,000+ comments). Downes is a sick farmer the gang lent money to; Arthur must beat and extort him early on, getting close to his face — the speculated infection moment. Doak1851 tries to keep a face mask on Arthur, finds the game strips it before the non-interactive fight, then exploits a quirk: RDR2 removes only *one* item, so grabbing the horse's lead leaves Arthur masked (but horseless) while beating Downes. The user admits they did not continue the game and doubts the outcome would change. The second half (3'07''–7'30'') shows attempts to *kill* Downes, producing two Downeses (one dead, one respawned), since the encounter cannot be skipped. Ruffino reads this as the imagination of *environmental control* — keeping NPCs at distance or eliminating them, "straightening" the narrative (Kagen), letting mechanical/ludic agency act on fictional/narrative agency (Joyce; Cole & Gillies; Galloway's algorithmic "nonlinear narrative"). The infective Downes becomes "an expendable NPC," disposability he links via Anikina and Nakamura to racialized network flattening.

**The pandemic coda.** Ruffino reads a bat-soup meme (causation as "consequences with no actions"), the WHO virus image, a *Guardian* contagion curve, and distancing/jogging infographics (one modeled by a *Gran Turismo* aerodynamics developer, per Magee). None cure anything, but all *heal* by imagining an able-bodied, white, middle-aged male subject of prevention who navigates causation backwards and keeps others at distance — "the Thomas Downeses" being those who cannot work from home, must take transit, or are "running while black" (Petrzela).

## Game-elements treated

- **Arthur's tuberculosis** — *central narrative/mechanical event*. Contracted in Saint Denis; not shown happening, incurable in 1899; slows Arthur and impairs healing via food/drink. The hinge of the argument ("a consequence with no actions").
- **The four endings (Arthur's death)** — *narrative structure*. All four endings kill Arthur while TB-stricken; the final act's dialogues and choices are defined by awareness of impending death. Used to argue the game withdraws agency.
- **The Thomas Downes encounter** — *scripted mission beat / NPC*. A mandatory early debt-collection scene where Arthur beats a sick farmer at close range; community-theorized infection point; unskippable (Downes respawns if killed). The core case study.
- **The Owl Feather Trinket** — *item/mechanic*. Powers up Arthur and "partially alleviates the symptoms" but does not cure TB — mitigation without cure.
- **Choice / "narrative branches"** — *interactive-storytelling design*. Promised-but-denied control over the storyline; the community's imagined "forks" read against Stang's *Walking Dead* analysis of surplus, illusory choice.
- **Open-world violence (honor-adjacent)** — *systemic behavior*. Arthur "hunts animals; he beats and robs anyone he meets"; "the environment is his playground." The honor system is not named as such; pre-illness environmental violence is treated as the fantasy of control later revoked (see *Tensions*).
- **Mask / item-removal quirk** — *exploited mechanic*. The one-equipped-item-removal rule is leveraged by Doak1851 to keep Arthur masked; a precise detail doing argumentative work.
- **NPC respawn / cutscene item-stripping** — *engine behavior*. Killing Downes yields a duplicated NPC; the fight scene strips masks before triggering — the environment's resistance to player will.

## Themes

- Agency as **affect of control** rather than causal efficacy; the **invisibility of cause** and the desire to make it visible/navigable.
- **Disposability of human and nonhuman others** (NPCs, animals, landscape) under a user-centered, networked notion of agency.
- **White, able-bodied normativity** as the implicit subject of both videogame mastery and pandemic prevention.
- **Attention economy** and clickbait as the material condition of the paratexts.
- **Mediation over representation** (the post-humanist, performative turn of the special issue).
- The continuity between **virtual and lived "gamespace"** under capitalist networks.

## Debate-axis positioning: player-agency ↔ scripted-constraint

Ruffino sits firmly toward the **scripted-constraint** pole, with a twist: he treats the *experience* of agency as an affective and ideological fantasy the game first grants and then conspicuously revokes. RDR2 "contradicts the promises of control and continuous progression" (Muriel & Crawford), and the article explains what players do *with* that revocation. The constraint is not merely narrative determinism (all four endings kill Arthur) but the *invisibility of cause*, which frustrates the wish to locate an actionable point of intervention. The paratexts are players refusing the constraint; Ruffino's contribution is that even the refusal *concedes* it ("there is no cure") while restoring the controlling subject elsewhere. The article thus reframes the agency debate as one about *affect and normativity* rather than about whether choices "matter."

> diss-hook: *(anticipatory-application)* For the RODA method and the Aristotelian–Heideggerian actualization-of-desire chain, Ruffino offers a case of *desire that cannot actualize*: Arthur's (and the player's) *orexis* toward self-preservation meets an invisible, uncaused privation, so the chain from desire to completed action is structurally broken, and the paratexts become a displaced, never-completed attempt to restore the missing efficient cause — a useful foil for the *failure* mode of the desire-to-action chain in RDR2's final act, distinct from the cluster's authenticity/ecology/ideology readings.

## Tensions

- **Auto-ethnographic generalizability.** Grounded in Ruffino's own affect as a self-identified white, able-bodied male player. He owns the positionality, but the leap from one player's "structure of feeling" to "white able-bodied normativity in contemporary digital culture" (and onto the pandemic) is a deliberate, contestable stretch — framed as a *hypothesis* of a "comparable affective structure," not a demonstrated equivalence.
- **Scale asymmetry (game ↔ pandemic).** Ruffino concedes the cases are "diverse in nature, gravity and scale"; the parallel is offered as illuminating rather than equivalent.
- **Honor system unaddressed as a system.** Despite foregrounding Arthur's environmental violence as the "fantasy of control," the article does not engage RDR2's *honor mechanic* as a formal feedback system tying violence to ending outcomes — a gap relative to cluster-mates who treat morality/choice systems more mechanically.
- **Clickbait vs. sincere play.** Ruffino notes many videos are "clickbait" yet commenters claim genuine hours of testing; he holds these together (desire and awareness "co-exist"), but leans on reading sincerity *into* an attention-economy genre partly gaming engagement metrics.
- **Performativity vs. representation.** The framework wants videos as "performances/mediations" more than "representations," yet its close readings necessarily describe representational content — a productive tension internal to its Barad-inflected stance.
