LOAD-BEARING CONCEPTUAL DECISION — THE PHANTASMA / RESONANT-KINESIS DISTINCTION
====================================================================

A central conceptual decision must be honored throughout this section: the *phantasma* proper is **not identical** with the resonant *kinēsis* / residual *aisthēma* that persists in the sensory apparatus after the perceptual object withdraws. They share a substrate (the persisting kinetic affection in the sensory apparatus) but differ in being:

- **Resonant *kinēsis* / residual *aisthēma*** = the kinetic disposition described *qua* persisting motion in the organ. This is what A₂ deposits as the dual-trace residue and what subsists as kinetic disposition even in the absence of *phantasia*'s activity (cf. dreamless sleep: residue persists, no *phantasmata* present).
- **The *phantasma* proper** = that same kinetic disposition described *qua* image-presented-to-the-mind. The *phantasma* comes into being only when *phantasia* (the activity, the \gk{κίνησις} "in virtue of which" *phantasmata* arise) takes up the residual substrate and constitutes it as a determinate, recallable image-for-the-mind.

The relation between resonant *kinēsis* and *phantasma* is governed by the **one-in-substrate-two-in-being formula** that Aristotle deploys repeatedly elsewhere (Thebes-Athens road at *Physics* III.3, 202b13–14; mover-and-moved at *Physics* III.2, 202a13–20; perception-and-appetite at *DA* III.2, 426b26–27 and III.7, 431a13–14). This same formula governs the residual-kinēsis / *phantasma* relation: one substrate (the persisting kinetic affection), two beings (kinetic disposition vs. image-presented-to-the-mind), with *phantasia* as the activity holding the substrate under the second description.

The signet-ring metaphor must be deployed carefully: the wax's retaining the ring's impression after the ring departs illustrates the residual *aisthēma* (the persisting formal trace), NOT the *phantasma* itself. Wax has no *phantasia*; wax retains passively. What distinguishes the ensouled body from the wax is precisely that the ensouled body possesses *phantasia*, which activates the residual substrate as image-for-the-mind. The metaphor's reach is limited to A₂'s substrate; A₃ requires the additional activity of *phantasia* to come about.

This decision has structural consequences: *phantasia* is the moved mover of M₂→A₃ (not a faculty separate from the substrate, but the activity that constitutes the substrate as *phantasma*). The *phantasma* inherits the dual-trace content from A₂ (resonant *aisthēma* + resonant *orexis*) but presents it now as image-with-aspect-and-valence to the mind, available for cognitive uptake. The four cognitive orientations and *doxa* operate upon this *phantasma*-as-presented; they do not operate directly upon the residual *kinēsis* qua bare kinetic substrate.

**Subsection 1 — A₂→A₃: The Constitution of the Phantasma (~500 words).**
Declare the architectural role. A₂→A₃ is the phantastic motion whose unmoved originator is A₂ (the residual dual-trace deposited by completed perception: resonant *kinēsis* carrying resonant *aisthēma* and resonant *orexis*), whose moved mover is *phantasia* as the activity that takes up the residual substrate and presents it as image-for-the-mind, and whose terminus A₃ is the *phantasma* proper together with the four cognitive orientations and the *doxa*-dimension that operate upon it. State that this section proceeds by (a) establishing the *phantasia*/*phantasma* distinction under the one-in-substrate-two-in-being formula, (b) specifying the three characteristics of the *phantasma* (stability, formal similarity, role as hinge between perception and thought), (c) developing the four cognitive orientations (intellection, memory, deliberative *phantasia*, speculative thinking) under which the *phantasma* can be addressed, (d) developing *doxa* as the orthogonal committal dimension that supervenes upon any of the four orientations, and (e) closing with the synthesis of A₃ as ground of A₄. Cross-reference: *see the prior section on A₁→A₂ for the dual-trace framework — resonant kinēsis, resonant aisthēma, resonant orexis — that this section's analysis presupposes.* Stress that the *phantasma* is not a new ontological item conjured ex nihilo but the same residual substrate now held under the description of image-presented-to-the-mind.

**Subsection 2 — Phantasia as Activity, Phantasma as Image: One in Substrate, Two in Being (~700 words).**
This is the section's load-bearing conceptual move. Develop the distinction between *phantasia* as activity (\gk{κίνησις}) and *phantasma* as the image presented through that activity. DEPLOY *De Anima* III.3, 428b10–17 EARLY as the decisive textual warrant: "imagination is held to be a movement and to be impossible without sensation, i.e. to occur in beings that are percipient and to have for its content what can be perceived, and since movement may be produced by actual sensation and that movement is necessarily similar in character to the sensation itself…" Argue that *phantasia* is the activity in virtue of which *phantasmata* come into being — the \gk{κίνησις} that takes up the residual *aisthēma* deposited by perception and presents it as a determinate image. Develop the one-in-substrate-two-in-being formula explicitly: the persisting kinetic affection in the sensory apparatus is one substrate; under the description "kinetic disposition persisting in the organ" it is the resonant *kinēsis* / residual *aisthēma*; under the description "image-presented-to-the-mind-as-image-of-X" it is the *phantasma*. *Phantasia* is the activity by which the substrate is held under the second description. Reference the parallel formulae Aristotle deploys elsewhere: Thebes-Athens road (*Physics* III.3, 202b13–14), mover-and-moved (*Physics* III.2, 202a13–20), perception-and-appetite (*DA* III.7, 431a13–14). The dreamless-sleep evidence sharpens the distinction: residual sensings persist as kinetic disposition (*On Dreams* 459a24–28) but no *phantasmata* are presented because *phantasia* is dormant; in dreams, the same residual substrate is activated by *phantasia* and presented as *phantasma*. Cite Frede's framing: "the phantasma must be produced while sense-perception is still in operation to ensure causal continuity" (Frede, "The Cognitive Role of Phantasia in Aristotle," p. 284) — meaning the phantasmatic image is produced *as image* by *phantasia*'s ongoing activity, not given ready-made by the residual motion alone. Cite White's reading: the residual motion "in the sense-power caused by the sensible object itself sets up a second movement which continues after the sensation has ceased and the object is gone" (White, "The Meaning of *Phantasia* in Aristotle's *De Anima*, III, 3–8," p. 498) — the second movement is *phantasia*'s activity, the first is the residual *kinēsis*. Conclude by stating the load-bearing payoff: this distinction allows the chain to honor Aristotle's enmattered-account commitment (no two ontological items — physical motion + mental image — only one psychophysical event under two formal aspects) while also accommodating the cases (dreamless sleep, dormant memory, distracted attention) in which residue persists without active phantasmatic presentation.

**Subsection 3 — The Three Characteristics of the Phantasma Proper (~500 words).**
Specify the three characteristics of the *phantasma* once it has been constituted by *phantasia* from the residual substrate. (i) **Stability and recallability** — the *phantasma* is available as an object for further cognitive operations that may occur at considerable temporal remove from the original perceptual encounter; it is the form under which the perceiving organism's encounter with the world becomes cognitively durable. (ii) **Formal similarity to the original perception, operative in the absence of the sensible object** — *DA* III.3, 428b10–16: "imagination is held to be a movement … necessarily similar in character to the sensation itself"; this similarity is the source both of the *phantasma*'s cognitive utility (it preserves what the world disclosed) and of its susceptibility to error (the persisting image can mislead when the originating object has changed or departed). (iii) **Role as hinge between perception and thought** — *DA* III.7, 431b2: "the soul never thinks without an image"; *DA* III.7, 431a15–17: "the thinking faculty thinks the forms in the images." This claim binds the highest operations of *nous* to the phantasmatic residue of sensory experience: thought is not free-floating but always operates upon what *phantasia* has presented. Stress that the *phantasma* inherits the dual-trace content from A₂: it carries both formal-epistemic content (the resonant *aisthēma*: what the object is) and affective valence (the resonant *orexis*: what the object means for pursuit or avoidance). The *phantasma* presents this dual content not as two separate items but as a single aspectual disclosure — object-with-aspect-and-valence — available for cognitive uptake. Note that the dual-trace inheritance is what makes the *phantasma* both cognitively informative (intellection, memory, synthetic operation can operate on it) and motivationally consequential (it can serve, mediated by A₄'s realizable good, as ground of orektikon motion).

**Subsection 4 — Intellection: The Phantasma as Bearer of the Universal (~600 words).**
Develop intellection as the first orientational mode under which the rational soul addresses the *phantasma*. DEPLOY *DA* III.7, 431b2 ("the soul never thinks without an image") and *DA* III.7, 431a15–17 ("the thinking faculty thinks the forms in the images") together. *Nous* operates upon the *phantasma*; what it grasps in the *phantasma* is not the particular image as particular but what is universal within it. The intelligible form is abstracted from the sensory image, apprehended as holding across all the instances of which this particular *phantasma* is one. The rational animal, having intellected the *phantasma* of this man as bearing the universal *human*, thereby grasps something that applies not only to the man presently imaged but to every human that ever was or will be. Argue that the temporal orientation of intellection is **atemporal**: the universal, once grasped, is not indexed to past, present, or future; it holds across all the times at which its instances might occur. Distinguish intellection structurally from memory (whose object is constitutively past) and from the synthetic mode (whose objects are projected future possibilities or contemplated unchangeable truths). Mark the structural boundary between intellection and *doxa*: to intellect the universal *human* is to grasp it as a conceptual content; to hold a *doxa* that "this man is human" is to propositionally ratify a truth-claim in which the universal figures. The grasping and the ratifying are distinct operations — *nous* abstracts; *doxa* asserts. *Nous* can operate without *doxa* (one can grasp what *human* is without forming a belief about any particular human), and *doxa* can operate on the universals that *nous* supplies. Note that the A₄ actuality produced by intellection is the universal grasped; whether a *doxa* about that universal also arises is a further question that subsection 9 (on doxa) and subsection 10 (on doxa as orthogonal dimension) take up. Optional supporting material: Caston on intellectual content as derived from sensory phantasmata.

**Subsection 5 — Memory: The Phantasma as Likeness of the Past (~700 words).**
Develop memory as the second orientational mode. Memory addresses the *phantasma* as a likeness of something past. DEPLOY *On Memory* 449b25–27 EARLY: "There is no such thing as memory of the present while present, for the present is object only of perception, and the future, of expectation, but the object of memory is past." The object of memory is, definitionally, past *qua* past. Develop the "*qua* past" condition: not every *phantasma* is a memory. An image of an apple may arise in the mind as a free-floating representation (a *phantasma* of apples in general, unassociated with any particular past encounter) and such an image is a *phantasma* but not a memory. Memory requires, in addition to the *phantasma*, the recognition that the *phantasma* is the likeness of something specifically past. Cite *On Memory* 449b22–25: "When one exercises the faculty of remembering, he must say within himself that he formerly heard or perceived or thought of that. Memory is, therefore, neither perception nor conception, but a state or affection of one of these, conditioned by lapse of time." Stress that the lapse of time is not external to memory as its condition but internal to what memory discloses: the past is marked as past and felt as past within the memorial act itself. Cite Frede's framing of the phenomenology: memory "is always the act of remembering a past experience *qua* past" (Frede, "The Cognitive Role of *Phantasia* in Aristotle," p. 285). Develop the temporal orientation of memory as **retrospective**: the *phantasma* is addressed not as bearing a universal (intellection) and not as composable into a future possibility (the synthetic mode) but as pointing backward toward the past encounter of which it is the likeness. The A₄ actuality produced by memory is the past recalled and recognized as past — a content indexed to the temporal horizon of its original occurrence and apprehended from the position of the present as belonging to that prior horizon. Conclude by noting memory's role as deposit upon which the other modes draw: a memory may provide the particular instance from which intellection abstracts a universal; a repertoire of memories may provide the phantasmatic materials that the synthetic mode combines into future projections; a settled memory may function (like a settled *doxa*) as an additional unmoved originator that shapes how a current *phantasma* is taken up in subsequent engagements.

**Subsection 6 — The Synthetic Mode I: Deliberative Phantasia (~700 words).**
Develop the synthetic mode in its practical-prospective domain: \gk{βουλευτικὴ φαντασία} (deliberative *phantasia*). The synthetic mode differs from the other two orientations in a structural respect: it operates not upon a single *phantasma* but upon several, synthesizing them into a unified object of thought that neither constituent *phantasma* alone could furnish. DEPLOY *DA* III.11, 434a5–10 as the foundational warrant: "whether this or that shall be enacted is already a task requiring calculation; and there must be a single standard to measure by, for that is pursued which is greater. It follows that what acts in this way must be able to make a unity out of several images." Develop two interlocking sub-functions: (a) the **projective-synthetic** sub-function — the phantastic faculty recombines *phantasmata* drawn from the available repertoire into images of states not yet actual: possible futures, anticipated outcomes, unrealized configurations. *Phantasia* here constructs rather than merely preserves, assembling from the materials of past experience images of what might yet come to pass. (b) The **calculative-practical** sub-function — the projected possibilities are measured against one another by a single standard (\gk{ἑνί τινι δεῖ μετρεῖν}) to determine which course of action shall be enacted. Here deliberative *phantasia* converges with practical thought (\gk{διάνοια πρακτική}): the projected image is evaluated not merely as possible but as to-be-pursued or to-be-avoided. Argue that the temporal orientation of deliberative *phantasia* is **prospective**, and that this dual structure (synthetic projection + calculative evaluation) distinguishes rational action from appetitive reaction. Non-rational animals act from sensitive *phantasia* operating upon a single present *phantasma*: DEPLOY *De Motu Animalium* 7, 701a32–33: "I want to drink, says appetite; this is drink, says sense or imagination: straightaway I drink." Rational animals, by contrast, because they possess deliberative *phantasia*, can hold back from what is immediately present by projecting what is future. DEPLOY *DA* III.10, 433b5–10: "while thought bids us hold back because of what is future, desire is influenced by what is just at hand: a pleasant object which is just at hand presents itself as both pleasant and good, without condition in either case, because of want of foresight into what is farther away in time." The capacity to calculate and the capacity to perceive time cannot, on Aristotle's account, be separated without destroying both — the sense of time is co-implicated with deliberative *phantasia*. Note Bowin's reading: deliberative *phantasia* actively manipulates images to produce determinate temporal representations, treating temporal representation as a *function* of deliberative *phantasia* rather than its mere precondition. Optional supporting material: Nussbaum on phantasia as supplier of practical-deliberative material.

**Subsection 7 — The Synthetic Mode II: Speculative Thinking (~500 words).**
Develop the synthetic mode in its theoretical-contemplative domain: speculative thinking (\gk{διάνοια θεωρητική}). The synthetic operation is the same — multiple *phantasmata* are combined into a unified object of thought, for the soul never thinks without an image — but the domain of the object differs. Where deliberative *phantasia* synthesizes toward the realizable (toward what might yet come to pass and is therefore measurable by the standard of the greater good), speculative thinking synthesizes toward the unchangeable (toward what is and cannot be otherwise and is accordingly not subject to practical measurement at all). A mathematical demonstration, a contemplation of the eternal, a grasping of necessary causes: each involves the synthetic composition of multiple *phantasmata* into a unified object of thought. DEPLOY *DA* III.10, 433a13–15 as the decisive warrant for the practical/speculative distinction: "thought which is concerned with action" differs from "thought which is speculative and not concerned with action"; the former "calculates means to an end," while the latter "moves nothing." Practical thought moves because its object is a realizable good that engages *orexis*; speculative thought does not move because its object is not a realizable good but a truth that is what it is independently of anything the soul might do. Argue that the temporal orientation of speculative thinking is **atemporal-contemplative**: its object neither lies in the past (as memory's does) nor in the projected future (as deliberative *phantasia*'s does) but stands outside the practical temporal horizon altogether, as what holds and cannot be otherwise. State the structural consequence for the chain: the A₄ actuality produced by speculative thinking is the truth contemplated — the universal, essence, or necessary connection grasped through the composed phantasmatic materials — without issuing in any realizable good. The chain therefore terminates at A₄ for the speculative case: the contemplated truth moves nothing, and no *orektikon* motion is initiated at M₄→A₅ on its basis. Practical deliberation, by contrast, does initiate such motion, for the deliberated possibility discloses, at A₄, a realizable good toward which *orexis* can be moved. Note that speculative *doxa* is a possible orthogonal commitment to a contemplated truth (one may believe that the proposition expressing a necessary truth is true), but the speculative *doxa* itself does not move because its content is not a realizable good.

**Subsection 8 — The Unified Cognitive Apparatus (~400 words).**
Argue that the four orientational modes — intellection, memory, deliberative *phantasia*, speculative thinking — should not be mistaken for four separate faculties operating independently within the rational soul. They are, rather, analytical descriptions of a single rational cognitive apparatus operating upon the *phantasma* under distinct intentional directions. They share a common structural role within M₂→A₃: in each mode, the unmoved originator is the *phantasma* (or, in the synthetic case, the repertoire of *phantasmata* available to the soul); the moved mover is the rational cognitive apparatus in its respective orientation; that which is moved is the rational animal, cognitively transformed by the engagement. They differ in the intentional direction along which the engagement proceeds — toward the universal, toward the past, toward the projected future, toward the contemplated unchangeable — and in the temporal orientation that each direction presupposes: atemporal for intellection, retrospective for memory, prospective for deliberative *phantasia*, atemporal-contemplative for speculative thinking. The modes may operate simultaneously or in succession; they are not stages arranged in a fixed sequence but concurrent aspects of the rational cognitive apparatus as it takes up the *phantasma*. The same *phantasma* can be intellected as bearing a universal while also being remembered as a likeness of something past; the same repertoire of *phantasmata* can be synthesized into an anticipated practical possibility while being simultaneously examined for its theoretical implications. Each mode terminates in a distinct sort of A₄ content: the universal grasped, the past recognized as past, the possibility constructed and evaluated, the truth contemplated. Mark the transition to subsection 9: alongside these four orientational modes operates the orthogonal committal dimension of *doxa*, which adds to any orientation the dimension of involuntary propositional ratification.

**Subsection 9 — Doxa: The Soul's Involuntary Taking-as-True (~900 words).**
Open with Aristotle's contrast between *phantasia* and *doxa* on the will. DEPLOY *DA* III.3, 427b17–21: "imagining lies within our own power whenever we wish (e.g. we can call up a picture, as in the practice of mnemonics by the use of mental images), but in forming opinions we are not free: we cannot escape the alternative of falsehood or truth." The asymmetry is total. *Phantasia* is \gk{ἐφ' ἡμῖν} — within our power, available to summoning. *Doxa* is \gk{οὐκ ἐφ' ἡμῖν} — not within our power, withheld from the will because it is answerable to something beyond the will. Develop the source of the asymmetry: *doxa* is constitutively truth-or-falsehood-bearing and is answerable to how things *are* rather than to how we might wish them to appear. Develop the **three conditions** Aristotle specifies: belief (\gk{πίστις}), conviction (\gk{πεπεῖσθαι}), and discourse of reason (\gk{λόγος}). DEPLOY *DA* III.3, 428a19–24: "Opinion involves belief (for without belief in what we opine we cannot have an opinion), and in the brutes though we often find imagination we never find belief. Further, every opinion is accompanied by belief, belief by conviction, and conviction by discourse of reason." Argue that the three conditions are not redundant glosses but a layered specification: *pistis* names the assent that distinguishes *doxa* from non-committal entertainment of an image; *pepeisthai* names the binding force of that assent; *logos* names the medium through which assent and binding force operate. Conclude that *doxa* is found only in rational animals, for only they possess the calculative faculty through which *logos* operates.

Then develop the **sun passage** as the canonical proof of *doxa*'s ontological distinctness from *phantasia*. DEPLOY *DA* III.3, 428b2–9: "we imagine the sun to be a foot in diameter though we are convinced that it is larger than the inhabited part of the earth … either while the fact has not changed and the observer has neither forgotten nor lost belief in the true opinion which he had, that opinion has disappeared, or if he retains it then his opinion is at once true and false. A true opinion, however, becomes false only when the fact alters without being noticed." The *phantasma* persists unchanged; *doxa* asserts contrary content; both are simultaneously held; the *phantasma* cannot bear truth-value (else its persistence would override the belief or its persistence would falsify the belief); therefore *doxa* must be a distinct assertoric act that takes the *phantasma* as its material yet can override or diverge from its content.

Then develop the **taking-as function** as the hinge of the entire architecture. Two distinct operations must be distinguished and their distinctness is load-bearing: (a) **aspectual taking-as** — the presenting of an object under a determinate aspect together with its affective valence; this is *phantasia*'s accomplishment, not *doxa*'s. (b) **propositional taking-as-true** — the ratification of that aspect as a truth-claim about what is the case; this is *doxa*'s accomplishment. They are related (the propositional ratification operates upon the aspectual presentation) but structurally distinct. Argue that the affective valence is constitutive of *phantasia*'s presenting (not superimposed by a subsequent operation), which is why non-rational animals can navigate without *logos*: they have *phantasia*, which presents object-with-aspect-and-valence aspectually (the wolf as predator-to-be-avoided), and resonant *orexis* responds to the aspectual presentation directly without doxastic mediation. Cite *De Motu Animalium* 7, 701a32–33 again: "I want to drink, says appetite; this is drink, says sense or imagination: straightaway I drink" — no *doxa* required. Argue further that the sun-passage phenomenology proves the structural distinctness: if *phantasia*'s aspectual presenting were itself propositional assertion, the simultaneous holding of the "one-foot" *phantasma* and the "larger than the inhabited earth" *doxa* would be impossible. State the three doxastic features: (i) aspectual taking-as belongs to *phantasia*, not *doxa*; (ii) propositional taking-as-true belongs to *doxa*, not *phantasia*; (iii) the ratification is involuntary in the sense 427b17–21 specifies — the soul does not choose whether to ratify the aspectual presentation, for the ratification arises from the way *phantasia* presents its object. Cite *DA* III.11, 434a10–11: "imagination does not involve opinion based on inference, though opinion involves imagination." *Phantasia* can occur without *doxa* (the brute has images, navigates by them, but does not opine); *doxa* cannot occur without *phantasia* (the aspectual taking-as upon which *doxa*'s propositional ratification operates is supplied by *phantasia* alone). Cite O'Gorman: "opinion's external grounding — we form opinions about the world about us, and the world about us forms our opinions…. Through *phantasia*, opinion makes judgments about the world of appearances" (O'Gorman, *Aristotle's Phantasia*, p. 25).

**Subsection 10 — Doxa as Orthogonal Committal Dimension (~600 words).**
Develop the structural consequence: *doxa* cannot be treated as a fifth cognitive mode parallel to intellection, memory, deliberative *phantasia*, and speculative thinking. The four orientational modes differ from one another in their intentional direction (universal, past, projected future, contemplated unchangeable). *Doxa*, by contrast, does not orient along any such axis; it ratifies along every axis. One may remember and simultaneously hold a belief about what is remembered; one may deliberate and form opinions about projected outcomes; one may grasp a universal through intellection and hold a *doxa* about the truth of the proposition apprehended; one may contemplate a necessary truth and hold a *doxa* that the proposition expressing it is true. *Doxa* thus supervenes upon any orientational mode, adding to each the dimension of involuntary assertoric commitment. It is, accordingly, not a fifth mode but an orthogonal committal dimension — the layer through which any of the four orientations becomes a truth-claim upon which the soul is bound.

Develop **settled doxai as standing dispositions**. *Doxa* operates not only episodically (in response to a particular *phantasma* in the moment of its disclosure) but also dispositionally (as a settled \gk{ἕξις} that shapes how subsequent *phantasmata* are taken up). DEPLOY *De Motu Animalium* 7, 701a7–25 — Aristotle's account of the practical syllogism — as the textual warrant: a universal premise ("all sweet things are to be tasted," "this kind of man is an enemy") combines with a particular perception or *phantasma* ("this is sweet," "this is that kind of man") to issue in action. The universal premise is itself a *doxa* (a settled belief held as a disposition of the rational soul) and operates alongside the present *phantasma* as an additional unmoved originator, shaping how the current *phantasma* is taken up in M₂→A₃. The repertoire of settled *doxai* thus conditions the episodic taking-as-true: what the soul already takes to be the case bears upon what it can now involuntarily come to take as the case. Cross-reference: settled memories (subsection 5) also function as additional unmoved originators in this same dispositional mode; the rational cognitive apparatus operates not merely upon the current *phantasma* but upon the current *phantasma* as inflected by the soul's repertoire of standing *hexeis* (settled *doxai*, settled memories, settled habits of practical reasoning).

Develop the **gatekeeping condition** and its failures. DEPLOY *De Insomniis* 460b3–16 (paraphrase, with brief direct citation as appropriate) on the condition under which the controlling rational faculty intercepts the *phantasma* before doxastic commitment occurs: "when the controlling sense is not disabled … we are not deceived; but when it is disabled, as happens in sleep and illness, then what appears is taken to be what is the case." Cross-reference *DA* III.3, 428b25–429a2 on *phantasia*'s special vulnerability to error when the perceptual object is far off. The gatekeeping condition explains how the sun-passage divergence is possible: the controlling faculty allows the persisting *phantasma* of the small sun while withholding doxastic commitment to it; under conditions in which the controlling faculty is disabled (sleep, fever, passion, distance), the *phantasma* passes unchallenged into *doxa* and the soul is committed to the appearance as though it were the fact. Note that the gatekeeping condition will receive deeper analysis in the downstream subsection on emotion (where doxastic commitment to evaluatively complex aspects flips generic resonant *orexis* into determinate *pathē*); here the gatekeeping is established only as the structural feature that allows aspectual presentation to remain non-ratified until conditions admit doxastic commitment.

**Subsection 11 — Synthesis: A₃ as Ground of A₄ (~400 words).**
Closing synthesis that explicitly prepares A₄. State A₃'s architectural role: A₃ is the *phantasma* proper (constituted by *phantasia* from the residual dual-trace substrate deposited by A₂) together with the rational cognitive apparatus's engagement with that *phantasma* under four orientational modes (intellection, memory, deliberative *phantasia*, speculative thinking) and the orthogonal committal dimension of *doxa*. The motion M₃→A₄ produces, in each mode, a determinate cognitive content: the universal grasped, the past recognized as past, the possibility constructed and evaluated, the truth contemplated — each capable of being ratified or not by *doxa*. Where the A₄ content discloses a realizable good (deliberative *phantasia*'s adopted course of action; intellection or memory entering a practical syllogism; *doxa* ratifying an evaluatively complex aspect), A₄ functions as the unmoved originator of M₄→A₅ (orektikon motion). Where the A₄ content is speculative (a contemplated truth that cannot be otherwise), the chain terminates at A₄: speculative thought moves nothing (*DA* III.10, 433a13–15).
