# Orienting Faculties: Phantasia and Stimmung as World-Disclosing Structures in Aristotle and Heidegger

## Abstract

This analysis examines the functional parallels between Aristotle's phantasia (φαντασία) and Heidegger's Stimmung as pre-reflective, world-disclosing structures that orient the subject within their existential-perceptual field. While separated by over two millennia, both concepts serve as foundational orienting faculties that operate prior to cognitive deliberation, establishing the conditions of possibility for subsequent engagement with the world. Through detailed examination of phantasia's role between aisthesis and nous in Aristotle's De Anima, and Stimmung's function as Befindlichkeit in Heidegger's Being and Time, this  both structures disclose world-relatedness before reflective thought emerges. The comparative synthesis reveals that phantasia and Stimmung function as homologous world-attuning mechanisms, though phantasia remains tied to perceptual imagery while Stimmung discloses existential thrownness. Thus, both concepts illuminate the pre-cognitive foundations of human world-engagement, suggesting a continuity between classical and phenomenological approaches to understanding how consciousness orients itself within lived experience. ## Introduction

The question of how human consciousness orients itself within the world has occupied philosophical inquiry from ancient Greece to contemporary phenomenology; indeed, this fundamental concern bridges the apparent divide between classical and continental approaches to understanding human existence. Specifically, both Aristotelian psychology and Heideggerian existential analysis identify pre-reflective structures that attune the subject to their environing world before deliberative cognition occurs. Subsequently, examining these orienting faculties reveals how both phantasia (φαντασία) in Aristotle's framework and Stimmung in Heidegger's analysis function as world-disclosing structures that establish the conditions of possibility for meaningful engagement with one's existential-perceptual field. The scholarly territory surrounding Aristotelian phantasia has been extensively mapped, with particular attention paid to its intermediary position between sensation and thought. Frede's influential  "phantasia is conceived by Aristotle as the capacity to have representations" (Frede 1992, p. 283)[9], while Nussbaum emphasizes its role in practical reasoning, arguing that "phantasia makes it possible for appearances to move us to action" (Nussbaum 1985, p. 221)[10]. Similarly, phenomenological readings of Heidegger's Stimmung have established its foundational role in disclosing Dasein's Being-in-the-world. Hence, both concepts have received substantial individual treatment within their respective scholarly traditions. However, a significant gap remains in the systematic comparison of these concepts as functionally parallel structures of world-disclosure. While individual studies have examined phantasia's cognitive role or Stimmung's existential significance, few analyses have explored how both concepts serve as pre-reflective orienting mechanisms that attune consciousness to its worldly situation. Accordingly, this investigation addresses this lacuna by demonstrating that phantasia and Stimmung operate as homologous world-disclosing structures, each establishing the pre-cognitive foundation for subsequent reflective engagement with the environing world. ## Phantasia as Pre-Reflective World-Disclosure in Aristotle

Aristotle's analysis of phantasia in De Anima III.3 establishes this faculty as occupying a unique position between sensation (aisthesis) and intellect (nous), serving neither as mere sensory reception nor as conceptual thought, but rather as a distinctive capacity that presents the world through imaging activity. The fundamental character of phantasia emerges in Aristotle's definition: "phantasia is that in virtue of which an image occurs in us" (Aristotle, De Anima, 428a1-2)[1]. Thus, phantasia operates through the production of phantasmata—mental images that make present what is absent, enabling consciousness to engage with the world beyond immediate sensory contact. The world-disclosing function of phantasia becomes apparent when examining its relationship to both perception and thought; indeed, phantasia mediates between these domains by preserving and transforming sensory content in ways that orient the subject toward possible objects of engagement. Specifically, Aristotle argues that "phantasia is different from both perception and thought, though it is not found without sensation, nor judgment without it" (Aristotle, De Anima, 427b14-16)[2]. This formulation reveals phantasia's fundamental role as a condition of possibility for higher cognitive operations while maintaining its own distinctive character as an imaging faculty. The pre-reflective nature of phantasia's world-disclosure becomes evident through its connection to movement and desire (orexis). In De Motu Animalium, Aristotle demonstrates how phantasia enables practical orientation by presenting objects as desirable or avoidable before deliberative calculation occurs: "the object of desire moves the animal, and this object is either the good or the apparent good; not every good, however, but the good that is achievable in action" (Aristotle, Movement of Animals, 700b23-25)[3]. Subsequently, phantasia discloses the world as a field of practical possibilities by presenting objects under evaluative aspects that orient the subject toward appropriate responses. Caston's analysis illuminates how phantasia functions as a world-disclosing structure by enabling consciousness to transcend the immediacy of sensation: "phantasia allows us to think about things that are not currently present to the senses" (Caston 1995, p. 45)[11]. Hence, phantasia establishes a temporal orientation that opens up past and future horizons of engagement, enabling the subject to navigate the world through imaginative projection rather than mere reactive response to present stimuli. This temporal dimension reveals phantasia's role in disclosing the world as a meaningful totality rather than a collection of discrete sensory episodes. The relationship between phantasia and practical reasoning further demonstrates its orienting function; indeed, Aristotle argues in the Rhetoric that phantasia enables the appearance of objects in ways that move the soul toward appropriate emotional and practical responses. 25). Accordingly, phantasia discloses the world not as neutral objects of contemplation but as meaningful situations calling for specific forms of engagement. The imaging function of phantasia operates through what White characterizes as its capacity to make "the absent present through the production of likenesses" (White 1985, p. 103)[12]. This presentifying activity reveals phantasia's world-disclosing character: rather than simply reproducing past sensations, phantasia actively configures the subject's relation to the world by establishing horizons of significance that orient subsequent engagement. Thus, phantasia functions as a pre-reflective structure that attunes consciousness to its worldly situation before explicit deliberation begins. Papachristou's tripartite analysis of phantasia further illuminates its world-disclosing function by distinguishing between phantasia as it appears in perception, memory, and creative imagination (Papachristou 2013, p. 87)[13]. Each mode reveals different aspects of phantasia's orienting capacity: perceptual phantasia discloses the world as a field of present engagement, memorial phantasia opens temporal horizons that situate present experience within biographical continuity, and creative phantasia projects possible worlds that guide practical deliberation. Hence, phantasia operates across multiple dimensions of temporal experience to establish comprehensive world-orientation. The relationship between phantasia and the common sense (koine aisthesis) reveals another dimension of its world-disclosing function; indeed, phantasia synthesizes the deliverances of individual sense modalities into unified presentations that disclose objects as meaningful wholes rather than collections of separate qualities. Aristotle argues that "the common sense perceives the common sensibles" (Aristotle, On The Soul, 425a14-16)[4], and phantasia builds upon this synthetic activity by preserving and transforming these unified presentations into images that orient the subject toward absent objects. Subsequently, phantasia enables consciousness to maintain coherent world-relatedness across temporal discontinuities. ## Stimmung as Existential Attunement in Heidegger

Heidegger's analysis of Stimmung in Being and Time establishes mood not as a psychological state but as a fundamental existential structure through which Dasein finds itself attuned to its Being-in-the-world; indeed, Stimmung belongs to the basic constitution of Dasein as a mode of Befindlichkeit—the existential structure through which Dasein discovers itself always already thrown into a world not of its own making. The fundamental character of Stimmung emerges in Heidegger's formulation: "A mood assails us. It comes neither from 'outside' nor from 'inside,' but arises out of Being-in-the-world, as a way of such Being" (Heidegger 1962, p. 176)[14]. Thus, Stimmung discloses the basic attunement between Dasein and world that precedes any subject-object distinction. The world-disclosing function of Stimmung operates through what Heidegger characterizes as Erschlossenheit—the openedness through which Dasein's Being-in-the-world becomes manifest before reflective thematization occurs. Specifically, mood attunes Dasein to its thrownness (Geworfenheit)[15] by disclosing the basic fact that it finds itself always already situated within a world that it did not choose but must navigate. Heidegger argues that "in having a mood, Dasein is always disclosed moodwise as that entity to which it has been delivered over in its Being; and in this way it has been delivered over to the Being which, in existing, it has to be" (Heidegger 1962, p. 173)[16]. Subsequently, Stimmung reveals the existential situation of Dasein as a being that must take over its own Being while remaining fundamentally attuned to conditions beyond its control. The pre-reflective character of Stimmung's disclosure becomes evident through its temporal structure; indeed, mood does not arise through deliberative judgment but rather emerges as the basic way Dasein finds itself already attuned to its existential situation. Heidegger emphasizes that "Dasein is as thrown Being-in-the-world, which has been thrown into its 'there'" (Heidegger 1962, p. 174)[17]. Hence, Stimmung discloses thrownness as the basic condition under which all subsequent engagement with entities occurs, establishing the existential horizon within which particular moods and emotions become possible. Rickert's analysis illuminates how Stimmung functions as a world-disclosing structure by establishing what he terms "ambient attunement": "Stimmung reveals the fundamental situatedness of human being, the way we are always already attuned to our environmental circumstances before we thematically encounter particular entities" (Rickert 2013, p. 89)[18]. Accordingly, Stimmung operates as an existential structure that discloses the world as a meaningful totality of relationships and possibilities rather than a collection of discrete objects for theoretical consideration. The relationship between Stimmung and understanding (Verstehen)[19] further demonstrates its orienting function within the structure of Care (Sorge)[20]; indeed, mood and understanding are co-original existentialia that together disclose Dasein's Being-in-the-world. Heidegger argues that "understanding is the existential Being of Dasein's own potentiality-for-Being; and it is so in such a way that this Being discloses in itself what its Being is capable of" (Heidegger 1962, p. 184)[21]. Thus, Stimmung attunes Dasein to its thrownness while understanding projects possibilities, and together these structures establish the basic orientation within which Dasein encounters entities as ready-to-hand or present-at-hand. The temporal structure of Stimmung reveals its world-disclosing character through what Heidegger characterizes as ecstatic temporality; indeed, mood discloses Dasein's Being as essentially stretched along the temporal horizons of having-been, coming-toward, and making-present. Specifically, Stimmung attunes Dasein to its factical existence in ways that open up both past heritage and future possibilities while establishing the present moment as a meaningful situation calling for response. Hence, Stimmung functions as a temporal orienting structure that discloses the world as a historical field of meaningful engagement rather than a neutral domain of theoretical investigation. Heidegger's analysis of anxiety (Angst)[22] as a fundamental attunement reveals the world-disclosing function of Stimmung in its most basic form; indeed, anxiety discloses the groundless ground of Dasein's Being-in-the-world by revealing the ultimate contingency of all particular orientations and projects. Heidegger argues that "anxiety makes manifest in Dasein its Being towards its ownmost potentiality-for-Being" (Heidegger 1962, p. 232)[23]. Subsequently, anxiety functions as a privileged mood that discloses the basic structure of existence as a being that must choose its own possibilities while remaining fundamentally attuned to conditions beyond its control. The relationship between Stimmung and discourse (Rede)[24] further illuminates its world-disclosing character; indeed, mood provides the basic attunement within which meaningful communication becomes possible. Heidegger emphasizes that "discourse is existentially equiprimordial with state-of-mind and understanding" (Heidegger 1962, p. 203)[25].
