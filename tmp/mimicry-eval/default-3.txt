The emergence of immersive virtual environments has compelled philosophers to revisit foundational questions concerning the nature of presence — that phenomenological sense of "being there" within a spatially coherent world. Drawing on Heidegger's notion of *Dasein* as always already situated within a meaningful environment, scholars such as Mel Slater and Thomas Metzinger have argued that presence in virtual space is not merely a perceptual illusion but a genuine ontological condition, constituted through embodied interaction and first-person perspective. The body schema, as elaborated in Merleau-Ponty's phenomenology of perception, adapts with notable plasticity to virtual proxies, suggesting that presence is less a property of physical space than of the felt coherence between action and sensory feedback. This repositioning challenges the classical Cartesian boundary between the real and the simulated, demanding a metaphysics capacious enough to accommodate multiple, overlapping registers of inhabited reality.
