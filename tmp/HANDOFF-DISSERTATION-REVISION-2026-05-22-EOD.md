## Critical Findings This Session

### Coope misattribution (L127 footnote)

The "On what I shall call the strong reading … I shall argue for the strong reading" quote attributed to Coope in the original L127 footnote is **NOT VERBATIM** in her PDF (`corpus/download/dissertation-fresh-perplexity/Q-010_Ursula_Coope_2005_Time_for_Aristotle_Physics_IV.10_14.pdf`). The strong-vs-weak reading distinction in that exact form does not appear in her text. Her actual position uses different framing: "mind-dependent in a way in which change is not" + "the nature of time itself implies that time cannot exist in the absence of ensouled beings" (Coope Ch. 10).

**Replaced** in L127 with verified Coope text from her actual Chapter 10 "Time and the Soul" (pp. 160, 162).

### Broadie 1982 not in corpus

Broadie's *Nature, Change, and Agency in Aristotle's Physics* (Oxford, 1982) is **NOT** in `corpus/rhetorical_ontology/`. The original quote attributed to her at L127 footnote cannot be PDF-verified. Replaced with `****** UNVERIFIED` placeholder per the missing-source convention. User to supply verified verbatim text manually.

### Rhetoric I.11 paraphrase issue (L115 + L133 instances)

The dissertation had a paraphrase of Rhetoric I.11 in two places:
- L115: "imagination is a feeble sort of sensation, and memory and expectation involve imagination; therefore things remembered and expected can be pleasant"
- L133: same paraphrase (shorter form)

The "memory and expectation involve imagination" portion is NOT verbatim per the Roberts ROT translation in user's corpus. Actual verbatim: "there will always be in the mind of a man who remembers or expects something the imagination of what he remembers or expects… memory and expectation also, being accompanied by sensation, may be accompanied by pleasure."

**L115 fixed**: Full verbatim block quote (Rhet I.11, 1370a27–b1) via adjustwidth.
**L133 fixed**: Short verbatim snippet "imagination is a feeble sort of sensation" (Rhet I.11, 1370a28) only.

### Burke *Grammar* p. 194 out-of-context (L97 sentence)

The Burke quote at the original L97 ("'conditions' are likewise contextual, as with the conditions of an organism's existence") IS verbatim per Burke's GoM p. 194. But the surrounding context is Burke discussing Leibnizian monads and Kantian things-in-themselves — NOT motion's reciprocal determination. The dissertation's application stretched the quote out of context.

**Removed** the Burke sentence and follow-up from L97. Aristotle quotes already establish dyadic relationality.

### Heidegger's Phenomenological Interpretations of Aristotle (L71)

The original L71 referenced "in the the 1924 Marburg lectures (BCAP) and in the *Phenomenological Interpretations of Aristotle* fragments (GA 61)". The GA 61 reference NOT in corpus and not verified.
