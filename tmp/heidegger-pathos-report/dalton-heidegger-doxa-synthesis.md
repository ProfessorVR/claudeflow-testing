# *Phantasia*, *Phantasma*, and *Doxa*: Dalton's Architecture under Heideggerian Pressure (GA18)

A comparative philosophical synthesis of Dalton, *Orientational Modes and Doxa*, and Heidegger, *Basic Concepts of Aristotelian Philosophy* (GA18, Marburg 1924), §§15–18, with supporting reference to the lecture course as a whole.

---

## 1. Dalton's Architecture of *Phantasia*, *Phantasma*, and *Doxa*

Dalton's paper proposes a structural reading of *De Anima* III.3–11, *On Memory* 1, and *Movement of Animals* 7 that recasts the Aristotelian psychology of perception, imagination, and judgment as a single *actualization chain* — a sequence of ontological stages, each being a determinate completion of a prior potency. The chain is not a flowchart of mental modules; it is, on Dalton's reading, a hylomorphic reconstruction of the ontological transitions through which the soul moves from being affected by sensible particulars to producing determinate cognitive and *orectic* (desiring) actuality. The renumbered A-chain — A₀ through A₄ — is the binding architecture, and every comparative move that follows in this report is keyed to it.

### A₀ through A₄: The Renumbered Chain

A₀ designates the underlying ontological horizon of motion and time. It is not a stage in the soul's activity but the condition of intelligibility for the entire chain: motion as *energeia* of the potential *qua* potential (*Physics* III.1, 201a10–14), time as the number of motion (*Physics* IV.11, 219a–220a). Without this horizon, none of the *kinēseis* that follow are even formulable as such.

A₁ is the moment at which the sensible object is in actuality and the corresponding sense-organ holds its first actuality (*De An.* II.5, 417a22–28; II.5, 417b14, *heterou genous alloiōseōs*). A₁ marks the readiness of the perceptual apparatus and the presence of the perceptible thing as actually presenting itself to be received. It is, in Aristotle's language, the situation in which the knower-of-grammar is actually using grammar — in this case, the perceiver actually perceiving — but not yet the residue of that perception.

A₂ is the structurally most subtle stage in Dalton's account, and it is where the canonical "one-in-substrate-two-in-being" distinction first makes itself felt. The actualization of perception, Dalton argues, terminates not in an ephemeral event but in a *residual composite*, a dual-trace consisting of (i) a *resonant aisthēma*, the formal-epistemic content of what was perceived, and (ii) a *resonant orexis*, the affective valence that accompanied the perceptual encounter. Dalton's terminology is precise: this is *resonant* *kinēsis* — a persisting motion still kinetic in character, still bearing the formal and affective imprint of the perceived particular, but now sustained without the external sensible object. This residual *kinēsis* is what Aristotle in *De Anima* III.3 (428a1 ff.; 429a1–2) and *On Memory* 1 (449b22–25) names as the *kinēsis* arising from the actual exercise of a power of sense.

A₃ is the *phantasma* proper, and it is here that Dalton's metaphysical claim becomes most distinctive. The transition from A₂ to A₃ is not the appearance of a new substance over and above the *resonant kinēsis*; it is the same persisting kinetic affection now taken up under a different ontological description. The residue at A₂ still bears the formal and affective imprint of the perceived particular, but it is at A₃, taken up by *phantasia* as image-for-the-mind, that the *phantasma* proper occurs. This is the canonical "one-in-substrate-two-in-being" formula: resonant *kinēsis* and *phantasma* proper share a substrate — the persisting kinetic affection in the sensory apparatus — but differ in being. Described *qua* persisting motion, the disposition is the residual *aisthēma*; described *qua* image-presented-to-the-mind, the same disposition is the *phantasma*. *Phantasia* is the soul's activity of holding the substrate under the second description. The textual locus is *Physics* III.3, 202a13–21 ("a single actuality of both alike") and 202b13–14 (the Thebes-Athens road). The wax-and-signet metaphor of *De An.* II.12, 424a17 ff. illustrates only the residual *aisthēma* — the substrate as imprint — not the *phantasma*; wax has no *phantasia*, and so the metaphor cannot capture the transition to A₃.

A₄ is the completed cognitive actuality: the universal grasped (in intellection), the past recognized as past (in memory), the possibility evaluated (in deliberative *phantasia*), or the truth contemplated (in speculative thinking). A₄ conditionally serves as unmoved originator of the M₄→A₅ motion, where M₄ is the *orektikon* motion that issues in action. The conditionality matters: when A₄ contemplates an unchangeable truth (the speculative case), no realizable good is disclosed and the chain terminates in contemplation; when A₄ discloses a realizable good (the practical case), it functions as the apparent good that moves *orexis* and ultimately the animal.

### *Phantasia* and *Phantasma*: The Distinction and the Three Characteristics

Within this chain, Dalton's central conceptual distinction — the one upon which everything subsequent depends — is between *phantasia* as the soul's *activity* and *phantasma* as its *product*. The two are related, in Dalton's striking metaphor, "as the act of stamping is related to the impression in wax," but with the crucial proviso that this metaphor describes the *substrate* relation only; the *phantasma* proper is the impression *taken up under the aspect of image-for-the-mind*. *Phantasia* names "the soul's capacity and activity of generating presentation images from perceptual residue"; *phantasma* names "the determinate image that results from that activity." Aristotle himself, Dalton observes, situates *phantasia* as the capacity that stands between bare perception and rational thought, thereby making the transition from A₂ to A₃ "the hinge upon which the entire cognitive architecture turns."

Following Papachristou, Dalton resists the standard English rendering of *phantasia* as "imagination," for that translation collapses the tripartite ambiguity of the Greek term — its simultaneous reference to capacity, activity, and product — and so obscures the very distinction that the chain is built to track. The *phantasma*, as constituted at A₃, exhibits three essential characteristics that distinguish it both from the *resonant kinēsis* of A₂ (raw perceptual residue) and from the conceptual content that serves as the foundation for higher-order operations.

First, the *phantasma* is *determinate*: it possesses a definite formal structure that allows it to present a particular content to the mind, rather than remaining an inchoate sensory afterimage. Second, it is *available*: it stands before the cognitive faculties as an object that can be contemplated, recalled, combined, or judged, thereby serving as the common substrate for cognitive operations. Third, it is *representational*: it bears a structural likeness to the sensible particular from which it derives, and this likeness is what allows it to function both as an object of contemplation in its own right and as a sign pointing beyond itself to the thing it resembles. These three characteristics, Dalton emphasizes, "are not extrinsic features but follow from the essential nature of *phantasia* itself as a movement resulting from actual sensation."

### The Three Orientational Modes

Once the *phantasma* is constituted, it can be taken up under several distinct intentional directions, each issuing in a different sort of completed cognition. Dalton identifies three such *orientational modes*, each operating upon the *phantasma* that A₃ supplies.

The first is *intellection* (*nous*), in which the *phantasma* is addressed as bearing a universal. *Nous* operates upon the *phantasma* by abstracting the universal form that it instantiates; the *phantasma*, in this mode, serves as the substrate from which intellect extracts intelligible content. Aristotle's well-known dictum that "the soul never thinks without a *phantasma*" (*De An.* III.7, 431a14; III.8, 432a8) is, on Dalton's reading, not merely a psychological condition but an indication that all higher cognition is grounded in the image-based mode of being-toward-the-world that *phantasia* discloses. The temporal orientation of intellection is *atemporal*: the universal, once grasped, is not indexed to past, present, or future, but holds across all the times at which its instances might occur.

The second mode is *memory*, in which the *phantasma* is addressed as a likeness of something past. As Aristotle clarifies in *On Memory* 1, 449b25–27, "there is no such thing as memory of the present while present, for the present is object only of perception, and the future of expectation, but the object of memory is past." The *phantasma* in this mode functions not merely as a spatial representation of an absent object, but as a temporally indexed residual image that carries within itself the mark of pastness. The temporal orientation of memory is, accordingly, *retrospective*; the *A*₃ actuality produced by memory is the past recalled and recognized as past.

The third mode is the *discursive* mode, which differs from the other two in operating not upon a single *phantasma* but upon several, synthesizing them into a unified object of thought (*De An.* III.11, 434a5–10). Within this synthetic mode Dalton distinguishes two domains: a *practical* sub-domain (deliberative or anticipatory *phantasia*) and a *theoretical* or *speculative* sub-domain (speculative thinking). Both operate by recombining *phantasmata* drawn from the available repertoire, and they differ in whether the synthesis issues in action (the practical case) or terminates in contemplation (the speculative case). The temporal orientation of deliberative *phantasia* is *prospective*; the temporal orientation of speculative thinking is *atemporal-contemplative* — its object neither lies in the past (as with memory) nor in the projected future (as with deliberative *phantasia*), but stands outside the practical temporal horizon altogether.

### The Two-Level *Taking-As* and *Doxa* as Orthogonal Committal Dimension

The architecturally decisive move in Dalton's paper — the move that distinguishes his account from competing readings of *De Anima* III.3 — is the precise specification of two distinct *taking-as* operations.

The first is what Dalton terms *taking-as-something* — *phantasia*'s pre-propositional, affectively charged presentation of an object under a determinate aspect together with its *resonant orexis* (the affective valence). The *phantasma* never presents its object barely; it presents the object *as* fearful, as pleasant, as a likeness of what is past, as a possibility to be pursued or avoided. This is *phantasia*'s constitutive accomplishment, and it is pre-propositional, does not require *logos*, and is accomplished by *phantasia* alone — the brute animal navigates the world entirely through this pre-linguistic *taking-as*.

The second is what Dalton terms *taking-something-as-true-or-false* — *doxa*'s *logos*-dependent ratification of what *phantasia* has presented. *Doxa* requires the three explicit conditions Aristotle specifies at *DA* III.3, 428a19–24: belief (*pistis*), conviction (*pepeisthai*), and discourse of reason (*logos*). It supervenes upon any cognitive engagement as its necessary affective-epistemic accompaniment in evaluatively complex cases, and it is the medium through which the assent and its binding force operate. Aristotle's contrast at *DA* III.3, 427b17–21 is total: *phantasia* lies *eph' hēmin* — within our power, available to summoning at the disposal of any soul that cares to practice the mnemonic art — whereas *doxa* is *ouk eph' hēmin*, not within our power, withheld from the will because answerable to something beyond it.

The architectural consequence Dalton draws is that *doxa* is not a *fourth* orientational mode parallel to intellection, memory, and discursive *phantasia*, but an *orthogonal committal dimension* that supervenes upon any of the three. One may intellect a universal and hold a *doxa* about it; one may remember and hold a *doxa* about what is remembered; one may deliberate and hold *doxai* about the possibilities deliberated; one may contemplate a truth and hold a *doxa* about the proposition that expresses it. *Doxa*, in this sense, "does not orient along any such axis; it ratifies along every axis." It is, accordingly, not a fourth mode but the *committal layer* through which any of the three orientations becomes a truth-claim upon which the soul is bound.

The orthogonal-doxa thesis carries with it a further distinction, which Dalton handles with care: *doxa* operates not only *episodically* (as a momentary *taking-something-as-true-or-false* in response to a particular *phantasma*) but also *dispositionally* (as a settled *hexis* shaping how subsequent *phantasmata* are taken up). Aristotle's account of the practical syllogism in *Movement of Animals* 7 (701a17–24) places this dispositional dimension at the center of rational action: a universal premise such as "all sweet things are to be tasted" combines with a particular *phantasma* such as "this is sweet" to issue in action. The universal premise is itself a *doxa* — a settled belief held as a disposition of the rational soul — and it operates alongside A₂ as an additional unmoved originator, shaping how the current *phantasma* is taken up in M₂ → M₃. The repertoire of settled *doxai* thus conditions the episodic *taking-something-as-true-or-false*: what the soul already takes to be the case bears upon what it can now involuntarily come to take as the case. This dispositional dimension will become important when Heidegger's analysis of *hexis* in §17 is brought into contact with Dalton's account.

## 2. Heidegger's GA18 Architecture in His Own Ordering

Heidegger's treatment of *doxa*, *phantasia*, and *pathos* in GA18 is internally structured by a sequence quite different from Dalton's actualization chain. The proper way to read §15 is on its own terms, before any thematic reorganization, because Heidegger's ordering carries phenomenological argumentative weight any thematic re-presentation tends to neutralize.

§15 stands at the systematic threshold of Chapter 3 of GA18's First Part. Heidegger has just completed the analysis of *pistis* (credence) as the basic phenomenon disclosed by the *Rhetoric*; he turns now to *doxa* as the *ontological matrix* within which *pistis* operates. His decisive move, against the entire tradition that reads *doxa* against *epistēmē* as defective knowing, is to refuse the epistemological reading. *Doxa* names the average, everyday *discoveredness* (*Entdecktheit*) of being-with-one-another in the world — "the way being-there initially has its world there in an average way" (GA18 p. 94). *Doxa* is "to have a view" (*eine Ansicht haben*), the *endoxon*: a positive ontological structure prior to and grounding both rhetorical persuasion and theoretical science.

The opening contrast (§15a) anchors *doxa* in *NE* Z 10, 1142b 11 ff. *Doxa* is *ou zētēsis* (not a seeking) but *phasis tis ēdē* — "I have a view already." The decisive feature: *doxa* "asserts, *holds* a view — as already-in-place — not as a result of inquiry but as the antecedent ground from which inquiry could even begin" (GA18 p. 93). It stands at the *end* of seeing, not at its beginning. A second contrast, drawn through *orthotēs*, distinguishes *doxa* from *epistēmē*: *doxa* is structurally capable of falsity, *epistēmē* not. The third demarcation, developed at *De Anima* Γ 3, 428a 18 ff., is the load-bearing one for everything that follows:

> "Φαντασία (*phantasia*) is the having-present (*poiein paron*) of something without perceiving it directly, the mere 'presenting-itself.' It can be true or false like δόξα (*doxa*). It has both possibilities, but it has them, in a certain sense, only 'from without,' while δόξα has the possibility *in itself*. Within the sense of opining itself lies the can-be-true-or-false." (GA18 pp. 93–94)

The phrase *poiein paron* is decisive: *phantasia* is "making-present," a determinate making-present that can also be true or false, but in a *different sense* than *doxa*. Animals possess *phantasia* but not *doxa*, which is "only there where *logos* is" (GA18 p. 96). The chain *doxa* → *pistis* → *logos* defines specifically human discoveredness.

§15b re-anchors the analysis hermeneutically: human being-there is being-with-one-another in the world; *logos* is the basic phenomenon of *koinōnia*; rhetoric is "the *interpretation of being-there with regard to the basic possibility of speaking-with-one-another*" (GA18 p. 95). §15c then works through the seven *NE* Γ 4 differences between *doxa* and *proairesis*, turning on toward-which, genuine direction, and the disinterestedness of *doxa* with respect to *ēthos*. The convergence point: "*Doxa* and *proairesis* approach each other precisely when one takes *doxa* in the narrow meaning of being-able-to-be-otherwise, the *endechomenon allōs*, insofar as it is a *sumpheron*" (GA18 p. 101).

§15d gives *doxa* its definitive existential determination:

> "*Doxa* is the *genuine discoveredness of being-with-one-another-in-the-world*. The world is there for us as what-is-with-one-another in discoveredness, insofar as we live in a *doxa* means having it *with others*. That others also have it belongs to opinion." (GA18 p. 101)

The realm of *doxa* is *panta* — all things. *Doxa* has the "going-along with the way the world initially shows itself, the moment of *trust in the immediate aspect*," with Thales' *hydōr* as exemplary case. Heidegger identifies the authority structure that anticipates *das Man*: "Behind the authority of the view stand other people, who are peculiarly indefinite, whom one cannot get a hold of — *one* has the view" (GA18 p. 102).

§15e turns to *doxa* as basis of theoretical negotiating: *protasis* and *problēma* both arise from *doxa* as *endoxa*; even *epistēmē* arises from *doxa*. "*Doxa* is the characteristic trust in that which shows itself initially. And that which shows itself initially is the basis of the investigation of the matter itself" (GA18 p. 103).

The architecture of §15 in Heidegger's own ordering, then, runs: *doxa* as *phasis* (yes-saying, not seeking); *doxa* vs. *epistēmē* via *orthotēs*; *doxa* vs. *phantasia* via the inner/outer ground of truth-and-falsity; the seven differences with *proairesis*; and the existential consolidation of *doxa* as the genuine discoveredness of average being-with-one-another, the basis from which *epistēmē* itself arises. The central thesis is already in place by the end of §15: *doxa* is the orientational matrix of *koinōnia*, not an epistemic deficiency.

## 3. Heidegger's Re-Reading of *Phantasia* and *Doxa* with BCAP Support

Heidegger's account of *phantasia*, *pathos*, and *doxa*, when expanded across §15, §17, §18, and the surrounding BCAP material, reveals a thematically organized phenomenological reading whose primary aim is to displace the Cartesian inheritance — the framing in which *phantasia* is "imagination" as private mental imagery, *pathos* is "affect" as inner state with bodily symptom, and *doxa* is "opinion" as defective epistemic stance. In each case, Heidegger refuses the inner-outer division and reframes the phenomenon as a determinate mode of bodily being-in-the-world.

### *Phantasia* as *Poiein Paron*: Making-Present and the Look of the Being

The decisive Heideggerian formula for *phantasia* is *poiein paron*, "making-present." *Phantasia* is not a faculty that produces inner copies of outer objects; it is a *mode of being-in-the-world* in which what is made present "is not actually there, but instead is, say, in memory or in a merely faint making-present" (BCAP p. 134). This formulation is decisive because it rejects, in advance, the picture of *phantasia* as image-formation in a private mental theatre. *Phantasia* is the bodily ground of *noein*: "if even *noein* (thinking), the thorough deliberating of a matter when I do not have it perceptually present, is something like *phantasia* or cannot be without *phantasia*, then thinking too cannot be without participation in the context of the entire life of a human being" (GA18 p. 134).

What *phantasia* makes present is the *eidos* — the look of a being. Heidegger argues at BCAP pp. 168–169 that *eidos* is best defined as *dektikon*, "as being-able-to-take-up the *eidos* at the moment, the look of a being." Accordingly, *phantasia* is the light in which the look of something is seen. In Heidegger's striking formulation, "every being that is there as a being requires a fundamental illumination in order to be there. This possibility is nothing other than *nous*" (GA18 p. 135), and "every concrete grasping operates and maintains itself within *nous*." The *phantasma*, on this reading, is the way in which the *eidos* of a being is taken up and held in the soul; it functions as the illuminated presence in which the thing itself can be seen and thought. It is structurally the same *eidos* that perception first received, now held in a different, non-sensory mode.

The making-present of *phantasia* grounds the three operations Heidegger names: thinking ("the matters are there in the making-present"), memory (recollection as a specific kind of making-present in which the having-once-experienced is also known as such), and deliberation. This is the textual support for Dalton's three orientational modes: each is, in Heideggerian idiom, a mode of *poiein paron*, of having-something-there without its being sensorily present.

### *Pathos* as Bodily Being-Taken-by-the-World

§18 advances the most ambitious thesis of GA18: *pathos* names not "affect" as inner state but the bodily *being-taken* of being-there in its full being-in-the-world. The ontological structure has four progressively narrowing meanings (drawn from *Met.* Δ 21, 1022b 15–21): *pathos* as alterability-as-such, as actualization of alteration, as harmful alteration, and as magnitude of misfortune. These four senses converge on a single ontological discovery: *to be a living being is to be ontologically structured as occurrable-to*; the being of *zōē* is itself the structure of *being-able-to-have-something-happen-to-one*. Heidegger's German equivalent — *Sichereignen*, "thus-finding-oneself-again-and-again" — names precisely the structure of bodily exposure to the world.

The crucial phenomenological observation, which Heidegger presses against the entire post-Cartesian tradition, is that *pathē* "are not psychic experiences, are not 'in consciousness,' but are a being-taken of human beings in their full being-in-the-world. The so-called 'bodily states' of anxiety, joy, and so forth, are not symptoms, but also belong to the characteristic being of beings, of human beings" (GA18 p. 133). The specific vocabulary is Aristotle's, drawn directly from *De An.* A 1, 403a 24: the *pathē* are *logoi enyloi*, "enmattered *logoi*" — their meaning-articulation (their *logos*-character) is inseparable from their bodily occurrence. To strip a *pathos* of its corporeality is to lose the phenomenon. Heidegger quotes Aristotle's formula for anger: the dialectician/rhetorician defines it as *orexis antilypēseōs*, an aiming-after pay-back, a being-toward-others; the *phusikos* defines it as *zesis tou peri kardian haimatos kai thermou*, "a boiling up of blood in the heart and of the temperature." Both are *logoi*, but only the *prōtos philosophos* takes both together (GA18 p. 138).

The *pathē* are accordingly characterized by three structural moments (drawn by Aristotle, *Rhet.* B 1, 1378a 24, and isolated by Heidegger): *pōs diakeimenoi eisin* (how we find ourselves disposed), *poiais* (about what we are angry, fearful, etc.), and *epi poiois* (in relation to whom we are there in this way). This threefold structure is, as Heidegger's text and later editorial commentary observe, the proto-form of the *Stimmung* and *Befindlichkeit* of *Being and Time* §29: how one finds oneself, what one is attuned to, with respect to whom.

The clearest test case is fear (*phobos*), discussed at *Rhetoric* B 5, 1382a. Heidegger's formula: fear is "a disposition that is set before an approaching possibility that pertains to me, comes toward me, and as such announces itself, specifically through the announcement. It must matter to me. It cannot be something of indifference" (GA18 §15-context, and developed in §18). What threatens is not yet there; it is presented as announced, as coming-toward-me, *in a certain look*. The "announcement" is carried by *eidos* and *phantasia* — appearances and signs — that Heidegger ties back to the function of *phantasia* as making present the not-yet-present in a way that grips desire and aversion. The same phantastic structure that presents something as such-and-such (the representational dimension) is what allows it to be taken as threatening, pleasant, advantageous, or noble. This is where *phantasia* and *pathos* intersect: *the image is not just epistemic content; it is the mode in which a possible good or bad is made present as mattering for me*.

The structural depth of Heidegger's reading is that even *noēsis*, the highest cognitive activity of human being-there, is bodily through *phantasia*. At BCAP p. 134, Heidegger pushes the thesis to its limit: "the entire being of human beings is determined so that it must be apprehended as the *bodily being-in-the-world* of human beings." *Phantasia* is the *bodily ground* of *noein*; *noein* is *meta sōmatos*, "with the body." Even thinking, on Heidegger's reading, is touched, embodied, finite. (Heidegger himself, of course, holds the (A)/(B) distinction half-open: *nous* in its structural-ontological sense as *apathēs*, "untouchable," is the medium of disclosure, not an item within disclosure; but *nous* as concrete cognitive exercise — what *De An.* Γ 5 calls the *nous* "that becomes all things" — is fully embodied. The user's paper does not need to enter this reservation; for Dalton's purposes it suffices that all *concrete* cognition is grounded in *phantasia*.)

### *Doxa* as Public Standing-at-the-End-of-Seeing

The Heideggerian re-reading of *doxa*, building on §15, foregrounds three features that resist standard epistemic readings.

First, *doxa* is the *koinōnia*-medium: it is "having it with others" (GA18 p. 101). To have a *doxa* is to participate in the average disclosedness through which the world is there for being-with-one-another. The authority of *doxa* is, in part, the authority of the indefinite "one" — "behind the authority of the view stand other people, who are peculiarly indefinite, whom one cannot get a hold of — *one* has the view" (GA18 p. 102). This is the 1924 proto-formulation of *das Man*.

Second, *doxa* stands at the *end* of seeing, not at its beginning. "*Doxa* is no longer a seeing, but stands at the end of seeing; it is a *phasis*. But although it is a yes, it is still not a knowing" (GA18 p. 96). It is a *yes-saying* — an asserting, a being-for-something-being-thus-and-so. The truth-and-falsity of *doxa* is *internal* to its mode of presentation, in contrast to *phantasia* whose truth-and-falsity is "from without."

Third, and crucially for Dalton's argument, Heidegger reads rhetoric through the lens of *krisis*: "rhetoric has its sight on *krisis*, view-construction, a definite decision in the sense of a *doxa*" (BCAP p. 124). *Krisis* — the formation of a definite view — is cultivated through discourse itself. *Doxa* is, accordingly, not merely a private psychological event but a publicly oriented stance: even when the soul takes-as-true in the privacy of inner deliberation, it does so in a manner that is structurally homologous with the public expression of opinion in rhetorical contexts. This means that the *taking-something-as-true-or-false* by which Dalton's account characterizes *doxa* has, on Heidegger's reading, an inherently social-discursive character.

### Temporality: The Ground of the Three Modes

Finally, Heidegger's analysis of temporality bears upon all three orientational modes. The three ecstases of *Zeitlichkeit* — future, having-been, present — provide a phenomenological framework for understanding the temporal indexing of memory (as a mode of *Gewesenheit*), deliberation (as a mode of projective futurality), and intellection / contemplation (as a mode of present discoveredness, though atemporal in its grasp of the universal). At GA18 §17 (especially p. 129), Heidegger identifies the closing thought-figure: "the *always* of being-there is the *frequently* of repetition" — the ontological structure of *praxis* as repeated re-appropriation rather than routine, which Heidegger will develop in *Being and Time* as historicality. Memory, in Dalton's chain, is the temporal deposit upon which the soul's cognitive life draws; in Heidegger's idiom, it is a *making-present of the having-been* that orients the soul's bodily disclosedness toward what has already structured its present concern.

The thematic concentration of Heidegger's *re-reading*, then, can be summarized as follows. *Phantasia* is bodily *poiein paron* that holds the *eidos* of a being and thereby constitutes the medium of any concrete cognition. *Pathos* is the bodily being-taken of being-there, an enmattered *logos* that does not happen *to* a soul-substance but constitutes the mode of being-in-the-world of a finite, embodied living being. *Doxa* is the public standing-at-the-end-of-seeing, the *phasis* in which the *koinōnia* takes its world up under a definite aspect, prior to and grounding both rhetorical persuasion and *epistēmē*. Each phenomenon is reconfigured against the Cartesian framework that would isolate it inside a self-standing inner subject; each is recast as a determination of bodily being-with-one-another in an already-disclosed world.

## 4. Thematic Comparisons Organized by Dalton's Architecture

The thematic comparison can now proceed pillar by pillar, in each case juxtaposing Dalton's claim, Heidegger's corresponding analysis, and the comparative consequences.

### Pillar 1: The Three Characteristics of the *Phantasma* and Heidegger's *Eidos* / Look

**Dalton's claim.** The *phantasma* possesses three essential characteristics: *determinacy* (it has a definite formal structure presenting a particular content), *availability* (it stands before the cognitive faculties as object of operation), and *representationality* (it bears structural likeness to the sensible particular, functioning both as object of contemplation and as sign pointing to the thing it resembles). These three features are not extrinsic but follow from the essential nature of *phantasia* as a movement resulting from actual sensation (paper, pp. 1–2; *DA* III.3, 428a1 ff.).

**Heidegger's correlate.** At BCAP pp. 168–169, Heidegger defines *eidos* as *dektikon*, "as being-able-to-take-up the *eidos* at the moment, the look of a being." *Phantasia* is the way the *eidos* (the look) of a being is taken up and held in the soul; the *phantasma* "bears" this look and functions as the illuminated presence in which the thing itself can be seen and thought. Aristotle's analogy of *phantasia* to light is decisive: as light makes the look of something visible, *phantasia* is the structural medium in which the look of a being is held in availability.

**Comparative commentary.** Heidegger's reading does not contradict Dalton; it reframes determinacy, availability, and representationality as *modes of disclosure* rather than as features of an inner mental image. Dalton's "determinacy" is the *eidos*-character of the look: the *phantasma* presents a being "as such-and-such" (the *toionde*) because the look it has is the look of *this* being and not another. Dalton's "availability" is the *poiein paron* that grounds remembering, thinking, and deliberation. Dalton's "representationality" is the *uncovering* (*alētheuein*) function of *phantasia*: it bears the same look as the sensible object and thereby makes the thing itself manifest in a different, non-sensory mode.

The pressure Heidegger applies is therefore *deepening*, not displacing. The three characteristics are right; what Heidegger adds is that they are not three properties of a private mental object but three structural moments of the soul's disclosedness toward the *eidos* of a being. A₃ is precisely the moment at which the soul holds the *eidos* of the perceived particular under the description "image-for-the-mind."

The single point of genuine pressure concerns the *orectic* dimension. Dalton's representationality includes a motivational component: the image "engages the *orectic* dimension of the soul, moving the desire toward or away from what the image represents." Heidegger's reading of fear at *Rhetoric* B 5 — with its emphasis on the *announcement* of what threatens *as* coming-toward-me — gives this extension a phenomenological grounding. The *phantasma*'s representationality is not just epistemic; it is the mode in which a possible good or bad is made present *as mattering for me*. Heidegger's *Rhetoric* II reading thus supports, rather than displaces, Dalton's account of the *phantasma* as carrying its valence intrinsically — exactly Dalton's own thesis at the level of *resonant orexis*.

### Pillar 2: The Three Orientational Modes — Temporality and the *Phantasia* / *Nous* Relation

**Dalton's claim.** The *phantasma* can be taken up under three distinct orientational modes — intellection (atemporal), memory (retrospective), and discursive *phantasia* (prospective for deliberation, atemporal-contemplative for speculation). Each mode shares a common structural role within M₂ → M₃: the unmoved originator is A₂ (the *phantasma* or the repertoire of *phantasmata*); the moved mover is the rational cognitive apparatus in its respective orientation; that which is moved is the rational animal, cognitively transformed by the engagement (paper, pp. 3–6).

**Heidegger's correlate.** Heidegger does not articulate Dalton's tripartite scheme as such, but his reading of *phantasia* as *poiein paron* (BCAP p. 134) provides the unifying structural ground: *phantasia* is making-present of what is not now given in sensation, serving as the ground of both *nous* and *doxa*; thinking never occurs without a *phantasma*, because this image furnishes the "there" in which thought takes up its object.

For intellection, Heidegger explicitly affirms that "the *noein* of human beings is *not pure*; it is grounded in *phantasia*" (BCAP p. 136), and that even *noēsis* is determined as bodily being-in-the-world. This corresponds to Dalton's claim that intellection abstracts the universal *from* the *phantasma*. Heidegger's gloss adds the temporal dimension implicit in Dalton's "atemporal" classification: the atemporality of intellection is the atemporality of *eidos*-grasping — the universal has no date, but its grasp is conditioned on the bodily, temporal availability of the *phantasma* through which it shows.

For memory, Heidegger's analysis of recollection in *Being and Time* §§72–77 (anticipated in GA18 §17 p. 129) provides the temporal framework: memory is a mode of *Gewesenheit*, a living dimension of *Dasein*'s being that continues to determine present understanding and future projection. This corresponds directly to Dalton's claim that the *phantasma* in memory carries the mark of pastness. The having-been is not a dead past but a living orientation that conditions the soul's current cognitive engagement.

For deliberative and speculative *phantasia*, Heidegger's reading of *praxis* in §17 and *logos* as the medium of "exhibiting beings in their having-of-limits" (BCAP p. 43) provides the broader conceptual frame: *praxis* requires a being-resolved combining a settled view with a particular grasp of the situation, while theoretical *theōrein* maintains "the question of being-uncovered as its orientating concern" (BCAP p. 159).

**Comparative commentary.** Heidegger reads the commonality of the three modes through the unifying notion of *poiein paron*: each is a different mode of making-present. The atemporal universal, the past as past, the future possibility, and the unchangeable truth are four ways in which something can be made present without being sensorily present. Where Dalton speaks of "intentional directions," Heidegger speaks of "modes of *poiein paron*"; where Dalton speaks of "temporal orientation," Heidegger speaks of "ecstases of *Zeitlichkeit*." Both terminologies pick out the same phenomenon, but Heidegger's adds an explicitly ontological-temporal frame consistent with Dalton's hylomorphic apparatus.

### Pillar 3: The Two-Level *Taking-As* — *Phantasia*'s *Taking-As-Something* vs. *Doxa*'s *Taking-Something-As-True-Or-False*

**Dalton's claim.** Two distinct *taking-as* operations must be distinguished. The first is *taking-as-something* — *phantasia*'s pre-propositional, affectively charged presentation of an object under a determinate aspect (e.g., bear-as-predator-to-be-avoided). The second is *taking-something-as-true-or-false* — *doxa*'s *logos*-dependent ratification of what is presented. These are structurally distinct: the first belongs to *phantasia* and operates through the *phantasma*; the second belongs to *doxa* and supervenes on the *phantasma* by ratifying its presentation as a truth-claim. The first does not require *logos* and is accomplished by *phantasia* alone (allowing the brute animal to navigate its world); the second requires *logos* and is found only in the rational animal (paper, pp. 6–9).

**Heidegger's correlate.** Heidegger's analysis of *doxa* as *phasis* and his analysis of *phantasia* as *poiein paron* together yield a two-tier structure that closely parallels Dalton's distinction, although Heidegger does not formulate it in Dalton's exact terms.

For *phantasia*'s *taking-as-something*, Heidegger's account of fear in *Rhetoric* II.5 is paradigmatic. Fear, on his reading, is "a disposition that is set before an approaching possibility that pertains to me, comes toward me, and as such announces itself, specifically through the announcement. It must matter to me." The "announcement" is carried by *eidos* and *phantasia*: *phantasia* is what makes present the not-yet-present *as* dangerous, *as* coming-toward-me, *as* mattering. The same phantastic structure that presents something as such-and-such (representationality) is what allows it to be taken-as threatening, pleasant, advantageous. This is Dalton's *taking-as-something* in Heideggerian register: the *phantasma* discloses its object under an aspect together with its valence, and this disclosure is *pre-propositional* — it does not yet require *logos*-articulation, even though in human beings *logos* will inevitably take it up.

For *doxa*'s *taking-something-as-true-or-false*, Heidegger's formula is decisive:

> "*Doxa* is no longer a seeing, but stands at the end of seeing; it is a *phasis*. But although it is a yes, it is still not a knowing. The being-able-to-be-true-or-false is already contained in the formation of the view. *Phantasia* is simple having-present. Therefore, animals possess *phantasia* too, while *doxa* is only there where *logos* is." (GA18 p. 96)

Aristotle's formula at *De An.* Γ 3, 428a 18 ff., which Heidegger underlines — *pasē men doxē akolouthei pistis, pistei de logos: tōn de thēriōn eniois phantasia men hyparchei, logos d' ou* ("To every *doxa* *pistis* follows; to *pistis* follows *logos*; but in some animals *phantasia* is present, but not *logos*") — is exactly the structural division Dalton's two-level *taking-as* requires. *Phantasia* is "simple having-present"; *doxa* is the "yes-saying" that adds *logos*-dependent ratification. The animal has *phantasia* but not *doxa* because it lacks *logos*; rationality is precisely what makes the *taking-something-as-true-or-false* possible.

**Comparative commentary.** This pillar is the strongest convergence between Dalton and Heidegger. The two-level structure Dalton articulates — *taking-as-something* (pre-propositional, affectively charged, accomplished by *phantasia* alone) and *taking-something-as-true-or-false* (*logos*-dependent, propositional, accomplished by *doxa*) — is exactly the structure Heidegger reconstructs from the *De Anima* III.3 / *NE* Z 10 / *Rhetoric* B chain. Heidegger's contribution is to embed this structure in the broader account of *koinōnia* and being-in-the-world: the *taking-something-as-true-or-false* is not just a private cognitive operation but the structural form of public discourse; the *phasis* that *doxa* performs is what makes *koinōnia* possible as a community of beings sharing a world under definite views.

There is, however, one Heideggerian inflection that pushes Dalton's account in a productive direction. Dalton's *taking-something-as-true-or-false* is described as "involuntary" and as the soul's stance toward propositions made available by reason. Heidegger reads this involuntariness in a specific way: *doxa* is *ouk eph' hēmin* not just because the truth is "answerable to something beyond the will," but because *doxa* is structurally *publicly grounded*. The "behind the authority of the view stand other people, who are peculiarly indefinite, whom one cannot get a hold of — *one* has the view" (GA18 p. 102). The involuntariness of *doxa*, on Heidegger's reading, has a *koinōnia*-character: even when the soul takes-as-true in private, the structure of that taking-as-true is shaped by what the indefinite "one" already takes-as-true. This is a friendly amplification of Dalton's involuntariness thesis, and it suggests that the *dispositional* dimension of *doxa* (settled *doxai* as *hexeis*) is not just a private psychological repertoire but a *socially deposited* structure — the average disclosedness through which the soul receives its world.

### Pillar 4: *Doxa* as Orthogonal Committal Dimension and Heidegger's *Doxa* / *Krisis*

**Dalton's claim.** *Doxa* is not a fourth orientational mode parallel to intellection, memory, and discursive *phantasia*, but an *orthogonal committal dimension* that supervenes upon any of the three orientational modes. It does not orient along any axis; it ratifies along every axis (paper, pp. 6, 9). Operative both episodically (momentary doxastic acts) and dispositionally (settled *doxai* as *hexeis* shaping future uptake).

**Heidegger's correlate.** Heidegger does not use the precise terminology of "orthogonal committal dimension," but the texts of §15 and his treatment of *krisis* in BCAP support the structural claim. The most direct support comes from BCAP p. 124:

> "Rhetoric has its sight on *krisis*, view-construction, a definite decision in the sense of a *doxa*."

Here *krisis* — the formation of a definite view — is presented as the *telic* function of rhetoric, and *doxa* is what *krisis* aims at. This is structurally consonant with Dalton's claim that *doxa* is a *committal dimension*: the *krisis* is precisely the act of taking-as-true, the involuntary ratification through which appearance becomes truth-claim.

Furthermore, Heidegger's own analysis of *doxa* in §15c — distinguishing *doxa* from *proairesis* — emphasizes that "having-a-view about a definite matter is not an 'ethical' determination; *it does not pertain to changing the general comportment of human beings according to their being*" (GA18 p. 100). *Doxa* "points to a certain *indifference* with relation to being." This is exactly the orthogonality that Dalton claims: *doxa* does not orient the soul along any of the orientational axes (intellectual, memorial, deliberative); it cuts across them, applying its verdict of truth or falsity to any content they produce.

The dispositional dimension of *doxa* — its operation as a settled *hexis* — finds its strongest Heideggerian support in §17. Heidegger's central thesis is that *hexis* is not a "habit" in the moral sense but "the determination of the genuineness of being-there in a moment of being-composed as to something" (GA18 p. 119). *Hexis* is "a being-possibility that is *related in itself to another possibility*, to the possibility of my being, that within my being something comes over me, which brings me out of composure" (GA18 p. 119). Here Heidegger's *hexis*-analysis directly supports Dalton's claim about settled *doxai*: the settled *doxa* is a *hexis* that conditions how subsequent *phantasmata* will be taken up, in precisely the way that an ethical *hexis* conditions how a particular situation will be addressed in the moment.

The connection between *doxa* and *hexis* is tightened by Heidegger's reading of *ēthos* and *pathos* in §17 and §18: *hexis* is "a how of *pathos*, being-out-of-composure, in relation to being-composed-as-to..." (GA18 p. 125). The settled *doxa*, in Dalton's account, functions as a *hexis* in this technical sense: it is the standing capacity through which the soul is composed-as-to a settled view, and it conditions how subsequent *pathē* (in the broad sense — *phantasmata* together with their *resonant orexis*) will be taken up. The episodic *doxa* is the *energeia* of this *hexis* in the moment; the dispositional *doxa* is the *dynamis* in the bivalent Aristotelian sense — both power-to-do and power-to-undergo. This is exactly Dalton's structural claim, and Heidegger's analysis at GA18 p. 125 provides the explicit Aristotelian-ontological support for it.

**Comparative commentary.** This is the pillar where Heideggerian support is most substantive, and where Heidegger's reading positively *consolidates* Dalton's distinctive thesis. The orthogonality of *doxa* — its non-alignment with any of the three orientational axes — is precisely what Heidegger's reading of *doxa* as *phasis* (yes-saying, not seeking) requires. *Doxa* does not investigate; it ratifies. It does not orient toward the *prakton* (as *proairesis* does), nor toward the *alēthes* qua future possibility (as deliberative *phantasia* does), nor toward the universal qua *eidos* (as intellection does). It cuts across these axes by adding the *phasis* — the yes — that any of them might receive.

The Heideggerian inflection that most enriches Dalton's account is the *koinōnia*-character of *doxa*. The settled *doxai* that condition the soul's uptake are, on Heidegger's reading, not merely a private repertoire but the *average disclosedness* through which the soul's world is shaped by being-with-others. This means the orthogonal-doxa thesis is not just a structural claim about individual psychology; it is a claim about the way the rational animal's being is conditioned by its participation in *koinōnia*. The settled *doxai* are the medium through which the indefinite "one" shapes the soul's uptake of *phantasmata*. This is a substantial Heideggerian addition to Dalton's account, and it is one that the orthogonal-doxa thesis can absorb without architectural modification: the dispositional dimension of *doxa* is publicly sourced even when it operates privately.

A second enrichment is the relation of *doxa* to *epistēmē*. Heidegger's striking thesis at GA18 p. 103 — "*Doxa* is the characteristic trust in that which shows itself initially. And that which shows itself initially is the basis of the investigation of the matter itself" — provides a reading of how *doxa*-as-orthogonal-dimension grounds even theoretical inquiry. The settled *doxai* condition not only practical action but also the inception of theoretical investigation: even *epistēmē* arises from *doxa* as basis. This is a structural amplification of Dalton's claim that *doxa* "supervenes upon any orientational mode," extending it explicitly into the speculative case (the *theōrein* of unchangeable truths).

### Synthesis: Direction of Heideggerian Pressure

Across the four pillars, the direction of Heideggerian pressure is *not uniform* but it is *not contrary* either. Pillar 1 (the three characteristics of the *phantasma*) and Pillar 2 (the three orientational modes) experience *deepening* — Heidegger reads Dalton's structural distinctions as ontological-temporal modalities of *Dasein*'s disclosedness, but the structural distinctions themselves remain intact. Pillar 3 (the two-level *taking-as*) experiences *strong convergence* — Heidegger's reading of *phantasia*-as-*poiein-paron* and *doxa*-as-*phasis* directly supports Dalton's two-level distinction, and Aristotle's *De An.* Γ 3, 428a 18 ff. formula (which Heidegger underlines) is the textual locus shared by both. Pillar 4 (orthogonal-doxa) experiences *consolidation* — Heidegger's *krisis*-analysis and *hexis*-doctrine provide direct ontological support for the orthogonality thesis and for its dispositional dimension.

The Heideggerian deepening that most affects all four pillars is the *koinōnia*-grounding: the recasting of these phenomena as modes of being-in-the-world rather than as inner mental events. This grounding does not displace Dalton's actualization-chain architecture, but it embeds the architecture within a phenomenological-ontological frame that the original paper, working primarily from Aristotelian psychological texts, leaves largely implicit. The result is that Dalton's architecture survives the Heideggerian reading intact and emerges *strengthened* — the orthogonal-doxa thesis, in particular, finds explicit Aristotelian-ontological support in §15 and §17 that the original paper had to construct primarily on internal grounds.

## 5. Refinements to Dalton's Architecture in Light of Heidegger (GA18)

Each of the four pillars admits a determinate refinement that incorporates Heideggerian phenomenological depth without substituting a Heideggerian framework for Dalton's actualization-chain. The refinements are articulated in Dalton's idiom — preserving the A-chain, the *taking-as-something* / *taking-something-as-true-or-false* distinction, and the orthogonal-doxa thesis — while incorporating the Heideggerian additions identified in Section 4.

### Refinement 1: The *Phantasma*'s Three Characteristics as Modes of *Eidos*-Disclosure

**Original claim** (paper, pp. 1–2): The *phantasma* exhibits determinacy, availability, and representationality.

**Heideggerian pressure.** Heidegger reads the three characteristics not as properties of an inner mental object but as structural moments of the soul's disclosedness toward the *eidos* of a being (BCAP pp. 168–169). To present them as inner-object-properties risks importing a Cartesian framework Aristotle does not endorse.

**Refined formulation.** Within Dalton's architecture, the three characteristics should be re-articulated as three modes of *eidos*-disclosure. Determinacy is the *phantasma*'s presentation of the being *as such-and-such* (the *toionde*) — the *eidos*-character of its disclosure, which makes possible the *taking-as-something* that *phantasia* accomplishes. Availability is the *poiein paron*-structure — the mode of being-there for the soul without sensory presence — which grounds the three orientational modes. Representationality is the *eidos*-bearing function: the *phantasma* carries the same look as the sensible object, now held in a non-sensory mode, allowing it to function both as object of contemplation and as sign pointing to the thing. The actualization-chain is unchanged: A₃ remains the *phantasma* proper, constituted as the substrate's being-taken-up under the description "image-for-the-mind." What the refinement adds is that this taking-up is itself an *eidos*-disclosing mode in which the *resonant orexis* discloses not merely *what* the thing is but *how it matters*.

### Refinement 2: The Three Orientational Modes as Modes of *Poiein Paron*

**Original claim** (paper, pp. 3–6): The *phantasma* is taken up under intellection (atemporal), memory (retrospective), and discursive *phantasia* (prospective for deliberation, atemporal-contemplative for speculation).

**Heideggerian pressure.** Heidegger reads the three modes as modes of *making-present* (*poiein paron*), with their differences ecstatic-temporal: each is a different way of having something "there" without sensory presence (BCAP p. 134; *Being and Time* §§72–77).

**Refined formulation.** Within Dalton's architecture, the three modes are characterized as three modes of *poiein paron*. Intellection makes present the universal *eidos* across all the times at which its instances might occur; memory makes present the past *as past*; deliberative *phantasia* makes present the projected future possibility; speculative thinking makes present the unchangeable structural relations between *eidē*. Each is, at root, a *temporal modality of disclosure*: the atemporality of intellection is the holding-of-the-*eidos* across time; the retrospectivity of memory is the *Gewesenheit*-mode of having-been; the prospectivity of deliberation is projective-futurality toward the *kairos*; the atemporal-contemplative orientation of speculation is the holding-open of unchangeable relations. The architectural consequence is that A₂'s unmoved-originator function within M₂ → M₃ acquires a temporal-ecstatic dimension: the *phantasma* is the bodily-temporal medium in which the soul's disclosedness is actualized.

### Refinement 3: The Two-Level *Taking-As* as Pre-Propositional Disclosure plus *Logos*-Dependent *Krisis*

**Original claim** (paper, pp. 6–9): *Taking-as-something* is *phantasia*'s pre-propositional presentation; *taking-something-as-true-or-false* is *doxa*'s *logos*-dependent ratification. They are structurally distinct.

**Heideggerian pressure.** The distinction is sound. Heidegger adds two specifications. First, the *taking-as-something* is the *poiein paron* that holds the *eidos* in its illuminated availability — not just a private presentation but a mode of bodily disclosure. Second, the *taking-something-as-true-or-false* is the *phasis* — the yes-saying at the end of seeing — whose *logos*-character is tied to *koinōnia* (GA18 p. 96; BCAP p. 124).

**Refined formulation.** *Taking-as-something* is the bodily-disclosive *poiein paron* through which the *phantasma* presents the *eidos* of the perceived particular under an aspect together with its *resonant orexis*. It is pre-propositional in not yet involving *logos*-articulation, but it is structurally *eidos*-disclosing — the *phantasma* presents the being *as* such-and-such precisely because it bears the *eidos*. *Taking-something-as-true-or-false* is the *logos*-dependent *phasis* through which *doxa* ratifies the aspectual disclosure as a truth-claim; the *phasis* is structurally homologous with public *koinōnia*-discourse. The asymmetry Aristotle marks at *De An.* III.11, 434a10–11 ("imagination does not involve opinion based on inference, though opinion involves imagination") is preserved: the brute has *phantasia* and *taking-as-something* but no *doxa*; *doxa* presupposes *phantasia* but is not entailed by it. The Heideggerian addition extends Dalton's involuntariness thesis: *doxa* is *ouk eph' hēmin* not only because answerable to truth but because its structural form is conditioned by *koinōnia*.

### Refinement 4: *Doxa* as Orthogonal Committal Dimension Operating Episodically and as *Hexis*

**Original claim** (paper, pp. 6–9, 13–14): *Doxa* is not a fourth mode but an orthogonal committal dimension supervening on any of the three orientations, operating both episodically and dispositionally as settled *doxai*-as-*hexeis*.

**Heideggerian pressure.** The orthogonality thesis receives direct support from Heidegger's *doxa*-as-disinterested-*phasis* analysis (GA18 p. 100). The dispositional-*hexis* dimension is consolidated by Heidegger's §17 reading of *hexis* as bivalent *dynamis* — the settled capacity exercised in *praxis* (*energeia* of having) and struck in *pathos* (*paschein* of having). *MA* 7, 701a17–24 places the settled *doxa* as universal premise of practical action.

**Refined formulation.** *Doxa* is the orthogonal committal dimension of the actualization-chain — the *logos*-dependent *phasis* through which any of the three orientational modes' contents becomes a truth-claim binding the soul. It operates episodically as the *energeia* of *taking-something-as-true-or-false* in response to a present *phantasma*, and dispositionally as a *hexis* conditioning subsequent uptake. The dispositional-*hexis* dimension functions in the *MA* 7 syllogism as an additional unmoved originator alongside A₂: the settled universal premise combines with the particular A₃ content to issue in M₃ → M₄ motion. Two specifications are added. First, settled *doxai* are bivalent *dynameis* that operate as standing capacities in the manner of any Aristotelian *hexis*. Second, the *koinōnia*-grounding extends the involuntariness thesis: the settled *doxai* are received from being-with-others. The orthogonal committal dimension is, in this sense, *doubly orthogonal*: synchronically across the three orientational axes, and diachronically across the deposit of past doxastic acts through *hexis*-formation.

These four refinements are clarifications, not amendments. Each preserves the actualization-chain stages, the *taking-as-something* / *taking-something-as-true-or-false* distinction, and the orthogonal-doxa thesis, while incorporating the bodily-disclosive, temporal-ecstatic, and *koinōnia*-grounded dimensions Heidegger's GA18 analysis makes explicit.

## 6. Points of Tension and Possible Misreadings of Aristotle

Heidegger's reconstruction converges with Dalton's architecture in many places, as Sections 4–5 have shown. There are, however, four points of genuine tension where Heidegger's reading either pushes against Dalton's emphasis or risks misreading Aristotle in ways Dalton's account avoids. In each case there is at least one Aristotelian counter-pressure that supports Dalton's reading.

### Tension 1: The Public Character of *Doxa* and the *De Anima* Privacy

Heidegger's reading in §15 emphasizes *doxa*'s *koinōnia*-character — *doxa* is "having it with others," and "behind the authority of the view stand other people, who are peculiarly indefinite, whom one cannot get a hold of" (GA18 pp. 101–102). This pushes Dalton's account toward construing *doxa* as essentially public-discursive.

But Aristotle's discussion at *De An.* III.3, 428a19–24 is, on its surface, individualistic: the three conditions Aristotle specifies — *pistis*, *pepeisthai*, *logos* — are conditions of the *individual* rational soul's assent. The sun-passage at *DA* III.3, 428b2–4 — the one-foot sun simultaneously held with the supra-terrestrial sun *doxa* — is a phenomenological observation about an *individual* soul holding two contents at once, not a thesis about public discourse. The structural distinction between *phantasia* and *doxa* that *DA* III.3 draws is a distinction between two soul-capacities, not between private and public modes of disclosure.

**Adjudication.** Heidegger's *koinōnia*-reading is anchored in *NE* Z 10 and *Rhetoric* B, where *doxa*'s public character is most evident; it cannot replace the *De Anima*'s individualistic analysis. The orthogonal-doxa thesis can absorb the Heideggerian *koinōnia*-inflection as a *contextual* feature of how dispositional *doxai* are formed, without conceding that *doxa qua* soul-capacity is essentially public. The episodic *taking-something-as-true-or-false* in response to a present *phantasma* remains a private soul-event in Aristotle's text.

### Tension 2: *Phantasia* as Bodily Versus *Phantasia* as Soul-Capacity

Heidegger's most ambitious thesis at GA18 p. 134 — "the entire being of human beings is determined so that it must be apprehended as the *bodily being-in-the-world* of human beings"; *phantasia* is "the bodily ground of *noein*"; even *noēsis* is *meta sōmatos* — risks pushing *phantasia* so deeply into the body that it threatens the Aristotelian distinction between *phantasia* as soul-capacity (a *dynamis* of *psychē*) and the bodily organs that subserve it.

Aristotle himself at *De An.* II.1, 412a27 ff. is careful to distinguish *psychē* from *sōma*: *psychē* is the form (*eidos*) of a natural body having life potentially within it; the soul is its first actuality, not identical with it. The *De An.* III.3, 428a1–2 description of *phantasia* as "*kinēsis* arising from the actual exercise of a power of sense" treats *phantasia* as a soul-motion, not as a bodily phenomenon as such.

**Adjudication.** Heidegger is correct that *phantasia* is *meta sōmatos* and inseparable from bodily being-in-the-world; but his reading risks collapsing *psychē* into *sōma* in a way Aristotle's hylomorphism resists. The "one-in-substrate-two-in-being" formula Dalton invokes — the resonant *kinēsis* and the *phantasma* share a substrate but differ in being — is precisely the hylomorphic structure that Heidegger's bodily-phenomenological reading risks collapsing. Heidegger's insight (*phantasia* is *meta sōmatos*) can be absorbed by Dalton without conceding the collapse.

### Tension 3: The Asymmetry Between *Phantasia* and *Doxa* in Truth-Bearing

Heidegger's formula at GA18 pp. 93–94 — "*phantasia* can be true or false like *doxa*. It has both possibilities, but it has them, in a certain sense, only 'from without,' while *doxa* has the possibility *in itself*" — captures a crucial asymmetry, but his gloss occasionally treats *phantasia* as a *deficient* form of *doxa*, as if *phantasia*'s truth-and-falsity were an external accident while *doxa* alone has internal truth-bearing capacity.

But Aristotle is explicit at *De An.* III.3, 428b30 ff. and III.11, 434a10–11 that *phantasia* and *doxa* are *categorically* distinct: "imagination is held not to involve opinion, in that it does not involve opinion based on inference, though opinion involves imagination." The asymmetry is not that *phantasia* is a defective truth-bearer; it is that *phantasia* *is not in the business of asserting truth at all*. The sun-passage demonstrates this: *phantasia* continues to present the one-foot sun even after *doxa* has corrected the appearance. The persistence of the *phantasma* against the *doxa* shows that the *phantasma* is not a truth-claim that *doxa* corrects but a presentation that *doxa* may or may not ratify.

**Adjudication.** Dalton's two-level *taking-as* — *phantasia* as *taking-as-something*, *doxa* as *taking-something-as-true-or-false* — captures the Aristotelian asymmetry more transparently than Heidegger's "from without" / "in itself" formula. Heidegger's formula is salvageable when read carefully, but Dalton's structural distinction is the more textually faithful articulation of *DA* III.3, 428b2–4.

### Tension 4: *Doxa*'s "Indifference" Versus Settled *Doxai* as *Hexeis*

An internal tension in Heidegger's own reading bears directly on Dalton's dispositional-*doxa* thesis. At GA18 §15c, p. 100, Heidegger insists that *doxa* "does not pertain to changing the general comportment of human beings according to their being.... *doxa* points to a certain *indifference* with relation to being." The disinterestedness of *doxa* differentiates it from *proairesis*: *proairesis* shapes *ēthos*, *doxa* does not.

But at GA18 §17 (p. 125) Heidegger argues that *hexis* is the bivalent settled capacity that conditions *praxis* and *pathos*, with *hexis*-formation through *ethos* the structural ground of *aretē* and *ēthos*. If settled *doxai* are *hexeis* (as Dalton's account and the *MA* 7 practical syllogism require), then settled *doxai* shape *ēthos* — contradicting the §15c indifference-thesis.

**Adjudication.** Aristotle at *MA* 7, 701a17–24 explicitly treats the universal premise of the practical syllogism — a settled *doxa* — as what combines with the particular *phantasma* to issue in action. Settled *doxa* is *not* indifferent to *ēthos*. Heidegger's §15c indifference-thesis is correct *for theoretical doxa* in the narrow *NE* Z 10 sense, but it cannot be generalized to settled *doxai* in the *MA* 7 sense. Dalton's account, by treating *doxa* as operating both episodically *and* dispositionally, captures both senses; this is a place where Dalton's reading is more textually comprehensive than Heidegger's, and it resolves a tension that the GA18 §15 commentary itself flags between the disinterested *doxa* of *NE* Γ 4 and the existentially weighty *doxa* of §15d.

### Synthesis on Tensions

The four tensions do not undo the convergence of Sections 4–5. They mark places where Dalton's reading is *more textually faithful to Aristotle* than Heidegger's reconstruction, and where the orthogonal-doxa thesis can absorb Heideggerian inflections without conceding the ground onto which Heidegger's phenomenological emphasis would push it. What is required is careful disambiguation: between *De Anima*-grounded individual soul-functions and *Rhetoric*-grounded *koinōnia*-disclosure, between hylomorphic soul/body distinction and bodily-phenomenological emphasis, between the asymmetric *taking-as* / *taking-as-true* distinction and Heidegger's "from without" / "in itself" formula, and between episodic theoretical *doxa* and dispositional practical *doxa*. In each case, Dalton's text provides the resources for the disambiguation; Heidegger's reading deepens but does not displace.

## 7. Outlook: Further Development of the Orthogonal *Doxa* Thesis

The orthogonal-doxa thesis, as refined through the four pillars of Section 5 and defended through the four tensions of Section 6, opens onto four lines of further inquiry that the present synthesis has only gestured toward.

The first concerns *pistis* and rhetoric. Dalton's account treats *pistis* as the belief-condition of *doxa* (one of the three conditions specified at *DA* III.3, 428a19–24), but the relation between *pistis* as condition-of-*doxa* and *pistis* as element-of-rhetorical-persuasion (the *pisteis entechnoi*: *ēthos*, *pathos*, *logos*) deserves further development. If settled *doxai* function as *hexeis* and rhetorical *pistis* operates by working through them, then the orthogonal-doxa thesis carries rhetorical-theoretical consequences that Heidegger's reading of rhetoric as "the interpretation of being-there with regard to the basic possibility of speaking-with-one-another" (GA18 p. 95) precisely supports.

The second concerns the interaction with *hexeis* in the *Nicomachean Ethics*. The dispositional dimension of *doxa* — settled *doxai* as *hexeis* — is structurally homologous with the *aretai* that Aristotle treats in *NE* II as *hexeis proairetikai*. If *aretē* is *hexis proairetikē* and settled *doxa* is *hexis* in the bivalent *dynamis*-sense, then the orthogonal-doxa thesis intersects with virtue ethics at a structural level: settled *doxai* may be the *cognitive* analogues of *aretai*, conditioning rational uptake as *aretai* condition rational action.

The third concerns *krisis* and *doxa* in *Rhetoric* II. Aristotle's catalog of *pathē* (anger, fear, pity, and the rest) operates by showing how each *pathos* is a settled disposition that conditions the audience's *krisis* — the formation of a definite view. Heidegger's BCAP p. 124 reading of rhetoric's "sight on *krisis*, view-construction, a definite decision in the sense of a *doxa*" implies that rhetoric works *in* the orthogonal-doxa dimension by cultivating *pathē*-related dispositions that shape *taking-something-as-true-or-false*. The mechanics of this shaping — how *pathē* function as *hexeis* conditioning *doxa* — is a productive site for further investigation.

The fourth concerns philosophy's relation to *doxa*. Heidegger's §15 tensions include the question of whether genuine philosophy ever fully escapes *doxa*, or whether it remains a *thesis*-*doxa* of a special kind. For Dalton's account, the analogue question is whether the speculative-A₄ contemplation of unchangeable truths is free of doxastic ratification, or whether speculative thinking too involves an orthogonal *taking-something-as-true-or-false*. The doxa-as-basis-of-*epistēmē* thesis suggests that even *epistēmē* carries a doxastic shadow.

These four lines — *pistis* and rhetoric, *hexeis* in *NE*, *krisis* in *Rhetoric* II, philosophy's relation to *doxa* — are the most promising Heideggerian resources for refining the orthogonal-doxa thesis without absorbing it into a Heideggerian framework. The architecture is robust enough to receive these developments while remaining recognizably Aristotelian.
