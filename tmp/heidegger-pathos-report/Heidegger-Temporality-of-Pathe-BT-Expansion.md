# The Temporal Ecstases of the Πάθη (*pathē* "passions/emotions/affects")

## A *Being and Time* Expansion of Section 7, Point 4 of the GA 18 Pathos Report

*This document expands the claim made in §7, point 4 (p. 16) of the prior report* Heidegger, GA 18 — Higher-Order Πάθη (pathē "passions") of Rhetoric II *— that "each πάθος (*pathos* "passion/emotion/being-affected") articulates being-there in its three temporal ecstases — having-been, present, projected-futural — but with a characteristic profile that distinguishes one πάθος (*pathos*) from another." The earlier report did not separate the textually warranted moves from the synthetic ones; this expansion does. It works primarily from* Being and Time *(BT) §§28–31, §40, §65, and §68(a)–(c), the principal loci where Heidegger temporalizes the πάθη (*pathē*) in his own systematic vocabulary.*

*Source primarily indexed: Heidegger,* Being and Time *(GA 2; Macquarrie & Robinson, Blackwell 1962), via the project's BT analysis at* corpus/index/Heidegger - Being and Time/. *Page references give Heidegger's standard German pagination (H.NNN) followed by Macquarrie–Robinson English (Eng. NNN). Supporting GA 18 references retain the Metcalf–Tanzer (2009) pagination used in the prior report.*

---

## 1. Statement of the Problem

The prior report assigned to each higher-order πάθος (*pathos* "passion/emotion/affect") a "characteristic temporal profile" articulated through the three ecstases — *Gewesenheit* (German: "having-been"), *Gegenwart* (German: "Present"), *Zukunft* (German: "future"). The formulation is correct in its essentials, but in GA 18 itself it is not Heidegger's stated thesis. GA 18 lacks the technical apparatus of the three ecstases (which is a *Being and Time* §65 development) and never marches systematically through the *Rhetoric* B 2–11 catalogue assigning each πάθος (*pathos*) its temporal signature. What GA 18 *does* contain are local temporal analyses — the μέλλον (*mellon* "what is coming/the impending") -structure of fear (GA 18, p. 168), the ἐν τῷ νῦν (*en tō nun* "in the now/in the moment") character of ἡδονή (*hēdonē* "pleasure") / λύπη (*lupē/lypē* "pain/distress") (GA 18, p. 164), the historicality of ἀρετή (*aretē* "excellence/virtue") (GA 18, p. 129) — and a general framework (the *Rhet*. B 1 constitutive aspects, GA 18, p. 115) within which a per-πάθος (*per-pathos*) temporal taxonomy *can* be developed.

The systematic completion of that taxonomy is a *Being and Time* achievement. BT §§28–31 thematize state-of-mind (*Befindlichkeit*, German for "the way one finds oneself") as a fundamental existentiale, with fear analyzed as a concrete mode (§30); §40 introduces anxiety in structural contrast to fear; §65 develops the three ecstases as the ontological meaning of care; and §68(a)–(c) explicitly assigns *primary* ecstases to the structural moments of disclosedness, including to specific πάθη (*pathē*). It is on the basis of these passages that the per-πάθος (*per-pathos*) temporal taxonomy of the prior report can be defended — as a Heideggerian thesis articulated in *Being and Time*, of which GA 18 supplies the philosophical preparation.

What follows lays out, with full textual anchoring, what *Being and Time* does and does not say about the temporal articulation of the πάθη (*pathē*), and how the prior report's claim should be understood in light of those texts.

## 2. The State-of-Mind / Πάθη (*pathē*) Identification (BT §29)

The first systematic move is the identification of the Aristotelian πάθη (*pathē* "passions/affects") with the existentiale of *Befindlichkeit* (German: "the way one finds oneself; state-of-mind"). Heidegger makes this identification explicit at H.138–139:

> "Publicness, as the kind of Being which belongs to the 'they', not only has its own way of having a mood, but *needs* moods and 'makes' them for itself.... It is in such a mood that the orator addresses himself. He must understand the 'possibilities' of moods in order to rouse them and guide them aright. **It has long been familiar that the affects [πάθη (*pathē*)] have been the theme of philosophical Interpretation. One must point to Aristotle's *Rhetoric*, which, contrary to the traditional orientation according to which it is read as a 'textbook' on the art of public speech, must be taken as the first systematic hermeneutic of the everydayness of Being-with-one-another. Publicness, as the kind of Being which belongs to the 'they' (cf. Section 27), not only has in general its own way of having a mood, but needs moods and 'makes' them for itself.... The Stoics had already taken over Aristotle's Interpretation, and through Patristic and Scholastic theology it has been transmitted to modern times.**" (BT §29, H.138–139; Eng. 178)

This passage is the textual hinge. Heidegger himself names the *Rhetoric*'s πάθη (*pathē*) -analysis as "the first systematic hermeneutic of the everydayness of Being-with-one-another," and identifies the πάθη (*pathē*) with what he is now calling *Stimmungen* (German plural: "moods/attunements") — the moods that *Befindlichkeit* discloses ontologically. The structural identification permits everything that follows: whatever the temporal analysis of *Befindlichkeit* (and of specific moods like fear) yields in BT, applies *eo ipso* to the πάθη (*pathē*) Aristotle catalogued.

The further structural claim of §29 — equally important for the temporal analysis to come — is that *Befindlichkeit* discloses Dasein in its *Geworfenheit* (German: "thrownness"):

> "In a state-of-mind Dasein is always brought before itself, and has always found itself, not in the sense of coming across itself by perceiving itself, but in the sense of finding itself in the mood that it has. As a state of Being which expresses how one is, and how one is faring, having a mood brings Being to its 'there'.... This characteristic of Dasein's Being — this 'that it is' — is veiled in its 'whence' and 'whither', yet disclosed in itself all the more unveiledly; we call it the 'thrownness' of this entity into its 'there'." (BT §29, H.135; Eng. 174)

Mood is *the disclosure* of Dasein's having-been-thrown — its facticity. This is the first temporal anchor: state-of-mind, considered structurally, has an essential relation to *the having-been*. Each πάθος (*pathos*), as a mode of *Befindlichkeit*, will inherit this anchoring in *Gewesenheit* (German: "having-been"), while at the same time articulating it differently in conjunction with the other ecstases.

## 3. Fear: The Three Structural Moments (BT §30)

Heidegger's analysis of fear in §30 furnishes the first concrete πάθος (*pathos* "passion") -analysis through which his temporal articulation will run. He follows the Aristotelian formulation closely — Aristotle's φόβος (*phobos* "fear") at *Rhet.* B 5, 1382a 21. Fear is structured by three vantages:

> "We can look at fear in three points of view; we shall analyze (1) that in the face of which we fear, (2) fearing, and (3) that about which we fear." (BT §30, H.140; Eng. 179)

The first moment, *das Wovor* (German: "that in the face of which") — that-in-the-face-of-which — is the threatening (*Bedrohliches*, German: "the threatening") encountered within-the-world:

> "Such an entity has the character of being detrimental [*Abträglichkeit*]... The detrimental, as that which threatens us, is not yet within striking distance, but it is coming close. In such a coming-close, the detrimental radiates out, and in this it has the character of threatening. *That which is detrimental as something that threatens, is not yet within striking distance, but it is coming close.*" (BT §30, H.140; Eng. 179–180)

The structural language is unmistakably futural: *coming close*, *not yet within striking distance*, *coming-close* (*näherkommt*, German: "comes-near"). The fearsome is determined precisely by its character of approaching-but-not-yet. This is the same μέλλον (*mellon* "what is coming/the impending") -structure GA 18, p. 168 isolated, now restated in *Being and Time*'s vocabulary of intraworldly encounter. Heidegger writes a few lines later:

> "It can come close, and yet it can pass us by. *In such a coming-close, threatening is something which 'can yet either go away' [vorbeigehen, German: "to pass by"]....*" (BT §30, H.141; Eng. 180)

The second moment, fearing as such, is then a determinate way of *being-disposed*:

> "Fearing, as a slumbering possibility of Being-in-the-world in a state-of-mind (we call this possibility 'fearfulness' [*Furchtsamkeit*, German: "fearfulness as existential possibility"]), has already disclosed the world, in that out of it something like the fearsome may come close. The capacity to come close is itself freed by the essential existential spatiality of Being-in-the-world." (BT §30, H.141; Eng. 180)

The third moment, *das Worum* (German: "that about which") — that-about-which fear fears — is Dasein itself:

> "That about which fear fears is the very entity which fears — Dasein. Only an entity for which in its Being this very Being is an issue, can be afraid. Fearing discloses this entity as endangered and abandoned to itself.... Even when fear is fear-for [*Fürchten um*, German: "fearing-about"] another, that is, when we 'fear about' another, this does not nullify the fact that the one who fears is afraid in his Being. What is feared about is not the Other; rather, we fear about ourselves, that the Other might be torn away from us. The one for whom we fear is not necessarily the entity who fears." (BT §30, H.141–142; Eng. 181)

These three moments — the threatening that approaches, the fearing as a disclosive way of being-disposed, the about-which that is one's own being — already articulate fear in a *latent* threefold temporality: a futural moment in the *coming-close* of the threat, a present moment in the *being-disposed* of the fearing itself, and a having-been moment in the *being-already-thrown* (to which the about-which returns Dasein). But §30 does not yet *thematize* this temporal articulation. The thematization comes in §68.

## 4. The Three Ecstases (BT §65)

The technical apparatus required to give the πάθη (*pathē*) their temporal articulation is supplied in §65, where temporality is exhibited as the ontological meaning of care. The three ecstases — *Zukunft* (German: "future, coming-toward"), *Gewesenheit* (German: "having-been"), *Gegenwart* (German: "Present, making-present") — are introduced as the structural moments of an essentially ecstatic unity:

> "This phenomenon has the unity of a future which makes present in the process of having been; we designate it as *temporality* [*Zeitlichkeit*, German: "temporality"].... Temporality 'is' not an entity at all. It is not, but it temporalizes itself.... Temporality temporalizes — and indeed, it temporalizes possible ways of itself. These make possible the multiplicity of Dasein's modes of Being, and especially the basic possibility of authentic or inauthentic existence." (BT §65, H.328; Eng. 377)

The three are not a serial succession of "nows" but three structural directions of being-out-toward, with the future as primary:

> "Future, having-been, and Present show the phenomenal characteristics of the 'towards-oneself', the 'back-to', and the 'letting-oneself-be-encountered-by'. The phenomena of the 'towards...', the 'to...', and the 'alongside...', make temporality manifest as the ἐκστατικόν (*ekstatikon* "ecstatic/standing-out") *pure and simple*. *Temporality is the primordial 'outside-of-itself' in and for itself*. We therefore call the phenomena of the future, the character of having been, and the Present, the *ecstases* of temporality." (BT §65, H.328–329; Eng. 377)

Two further claims of §65 are decisive for the πάθη (*pathē*) -analysis:

*(i) Priority of the future.* "The primary phenomenon of primordial and authentic temporality is the future" (H.329; Eng. 378). "Temporality temporalizes itself primordially out of the future." This priority is asymmetric, not absolute; the three ecstases are equiprimordial in their structural role within temporality, but among them the future is the leading moment from which the others "arise."

*(ii) The ecstases as differential temporalizing.* "Temporality temporalizes — and indeed, it temporalizes possible ways of itself" (H.328). Different modes of Dasein's existence (authentic/inauthentic; understanding / state-of-mind / falling) correspond to different *temporalizations* of the same three ecstases. The same three are at work in every disclosure, but with differential weighting and authenticity.

These two structural claims provide the apparatus on which §68's per-mode temporal taxonomy will rest.

## 5. The Decisive Passage: BT §68(a)–(c) on the Temporality of Disclosedness

§68 is where Heidegger applies the §65 ecstases-doctrine to the structural moments of disclosedness developed in §§28–31, with the explicit result that *each moment has a primary ecstasis*. This is the closest *Being and Time* comes to a systematic per-mode temporal taxonomy, and it is the textual ground on which the prior report's per-πάθος (*per-pathos*) claim must rest.

### 5.1 Understanding (§68a): Primary Ecstasis is the Future

Understanding (*Verstehen*, German: "understanding as projective potentiality-for-Being") — the existentiale of projective potentiality-for-Being — is exhibited as primarily futural:

> "When we said earlier that in our investigation of the structure of care we were guided by an understanding of Being which had been worked out in advance, our claim was substantiated. The kind of Being of Dasein which we have characterized as care has its first preliminary primordial unity in *the future*.... The *primary* meaning of existentiality is the future." (BT §68a, H.336; Eng. 385–386)

> "Only because Dasein, as constituted by the future, can be its own 'have-been' in the manner of resolutely disclosing itself, can it, as making-present, be an entity that 'has been'." (BT §68a, H.339; Eng. 388)

Understanding is the *projective* moment of disclosure; it is structurally the *coming-toward-itself* of Dasein from out of its possibilities. The future is its leading ecstasis.

### 5.2 State-of-Mind / Fear (§68b): Primary Ecstasis is Having-Been

The identification *for state-of-mind in general*:

> "What is the existential-temporal possibility of the fact that an entity which has been thrown can disclose itself in a state-of-mind?... A *state-of-mind* makes manifest 'how one is'. In this 'how one is', having a mood brings Being to its 'there'. *In what existential-temporal sense, however, does state-of-mind possess this temporal foundation?* That this existential phenomenon is grounded in temporality, becomes phenomenally evident if one points out that the mood of *fear*, for instance, has *already been* in advance.... *The primary existential meaning of state-of-mind is having been*. The expression 'bringing back' must not be misunderstood as if it meant that for state-of-mind a 'past' has somehow to be restored back into the present.... Rather, state-of-mind discloses Dasein in its thrownness, and indeed proximally and for the most part in the manner of an evasive turning-away. From an existential-ontological point of view, there is not the slightest justification for minimizing the 'evidential character' of states-of-mind by measuring them against the apodictic certainty of a theoretical cognition of something which is purely present-at-hand." (BT §68b, H.339–340; Eng. 389–390)

This is the master textual claim. *Befindlichkeit* — the structural genus of which the πάθη (*pathē*) are species — has its *primary existential meaning* in *Gewesenheit* (German: "having-been"). Every πάθος (*pathos*), *qua* mode of state-of-mind, is grounded primarily in having-been. This is what "anchors" the πάθη (*pathē*) in thrownness: each is a determinate way of finding-oneself-as-having-been-delivered-over to a world.

Heidegger then specifies the temporal structure of fear in particular:

> "How is the temporality of *fear* related to that to which it belongs? Fear was characterized as an inauthentic state-of-mind. In what sense does the existential-temporal foundation of fear belong to *forgetting* (*Vergessen*, German: "forgetting")? And of what kind is this forgetting? *In fearing, the structure of state-of-mind is in a quite peculiar way modified.* That in the face of which we fear, encounters us within-the-world either with the kind of Being of something ready-to-hand, or with that of something present-at-hand, or with the kind of Being of Dasein-with [*Mitdasein*, German: "Dasein-with/being-there-with-others"]. *In our existential-temporal Interpretation of the phenomenon of fear, we shall not aim at giving a strictly ontical account of the multiplicity of possible kinds of fear. We shall do so all the less, because such an Interpretation merely presupposes the fundamental existential analysis of the temporal Constitution of fear* — an analysis which we have not yet made. *Fearing as such* — what is the temporality which it has?... *In fearing, that backing-away which is grounded in forgetting, modifies the awaiting*, in such a manner that *the awaiting* of fear can be characterized as one which is *depressed and bewildered* [*niedergedrückt und benommen*, German: "depressed and bewildered/numbed"].... The peculiar temporality of fear is a *forgetting which awaits and makes present*. The 'now-here' of the bewildered making-present, for example, gets entangled in itself, so that the threatening entities can be encountered by it for the first time." (BT §68b, H.341–342; Eng. 391–392)

This passage bears very careful reading. Heidegger's claim is *not* that fear has a single ecstasis. It is that fear is a *unitary temporalizing* in which all three ecstases operate, but in a determinate and characteristic configuration: a *forgetting* (modally inauthentic having-been), an *awaiting* (modally inauthentic future, "depressed and bewildered"), and a *bewildered making-present* (modally inauthentic present). Fear is *temporally articulated* as forgetting-awaiting-bewildered-making-present, with all three ecstases operative but with the specific character that the futural awaiting is "depressed" by the threatening-coming-close, the past is "forgotten" in the recoiling from one's thrownness as endangered, and the present is "bewildered" in being seized by the threatening that has come too close.

### 5.3 Anxiety as Structural Contrast (§68b cont.; §40)

Heidegger immediately contrasts fear with anxiety (*Angst*, German: "anxiety/dread"), which has a different temporal-modal configuration:

> "Anxiety springs from Dasein itself.... How is the existential-temporal Constitution of anxiety related to that of fear? We have called this phenomenon a basic state-of-mind. It brings Dasein face to face with its ownmost thrownness and reveals the uncanniness of everyday familiar Being-in-the-world.... *Anxiety brings Dasein back to the very thrownness of its 'there', not to its actuality in any mood whatever, but to the thrownness as such.* What anxiety brings forth is the bare 'that' in the 'that one is and has to be'." (BT §40, H.187–188; Eng. 232–233; cf. §68b, H.343)

> "*Anxiety is anxious about naked Dasein as something that has been thrown into uncanniness.* It brings one back to the pure 'that-it-is' of one's ownmost individualized thrownness.... *On the temporality of anxiety:* the future of anxiety is *not* an awaiting which is held fast to *something* particular. Anxiety has *no* such object. It is anxious about Being-in-the-world as such. For this reason, anxiety can neither *forget* in the sense in which fear's *Befindlichkeit* forgets, nor *make present* in the sense in which falling concern makes present. *Anxiety brings Dasein back to its thrownness as something possible which can be repeated.*" (BT §68b, H.343–344; Eng. 393–394)

The structural contrast is now clear and is decisively temporal. Fear and anxiety are two modes of *Befindlichkeit*, both grounded in having-been, but they articulate their three ecstases differently: fear *forgets* the thrownness in being absorbed by the threatening object that approaches; anxiety brings Dasein back to its thrownness *as repeatable*. Fear is *inauthentic* state-of-mind; anxiety is the *authentic* state-of-mind that makes resoluteness possible. Both are temporally articulated, but with characteristic profiles that distinguish one from the other.

### 5.4 Falling and the Inauthentic Present (§68c)

The third structural moment of disclosedness — *Verfallen* (German: "falling/falling-prey-to-the-world"), falling — has its primary ecstasis in the present:

> "*Falling* has its existential meaning in the *Present*.... The chief mode in which Dasein is alongside, in a fallen way absorbed by what one is concerned about, is *making-present* [*Gegenwärtigen*, German: "making-present, the activity of the present-ecstasis"]." (BT §68c, H.346–347; Eng. 396–397)

This completes the §68 schema:

| Moment of disclosedness | Primary ecstasis | Modality |
|-------------------------|------------------|----------|
| Understanding (*Verstehen*, German: "understanding") | Future (*Zukunft*, German: "future") | anticipation (authentic) / awaiting (inauthentic) |
| State-of-mind (*Befindlichkeit*, German: "the way one finds oneself") | Having-been (*Gewesenheit*, German: "having-been") | repetition (authentic) / forgetting (inauthentic) |
| Falling (*Verfallen*, German: "falling-prey") | Present (*Gegenwart*, German: "Present") | moment of vision (authentic) / making-present (inauthentic) |

This is the fullest *Being and Time* statement of how the structural moments of disclosedness are *temporally individuated*. The πάθη (*pathē*) — as modes of *Befindlichkeit* — fall under the second row: their primary ecstasis is *Gewesenheit*. But each πάθος (*pathos*), as a determinate concretion of state-of-mind, will *temporalize* the three ecstases in its own characteristic way, with one or another ecstasis foregrounded in the determinate articulation of *that particular* πάθος (*pathos*).

## 6. The Per-Πάθος (*per-pathos*) Profile: Defending the Synthetic Step

With §65 and §68 in place, the per-πάθος (*per-pathos*) claim of the prior report can now be defended with proper textual anchoring. The claim is *not* that each πάθος (*pathos*) has *its own primary ecstasis* (which would contradict §68b's master claim that all *Befindlichkeit* is primarily grounded in having-been). The claim is that each πάθος (*pathos*) *temporalizes* the three ecstases — the having-been that grounds it, together with the future and present that it co-temporalizes — in a *characteristic configuration* that distinguishes it from other πάθη (*pathē*).

This is the structural pattern Heidegger himself instantiates in §68b. There, fear is given its "peculiar temporality" — a *forgetting which awaits and makes present* with specific bewildered-and-depressed characteristics — and contrasted with the different temporality of anxiety. The same procedure can in principle be carried out for any πάθος (*pathos*): identify the modal configuration of its three ecstases, isolate the characteristic way it forgets-or-repeats its thrownness, awaits-or-anticipates its futural object, and makes-present-or-encounters-as-moment-of-vision its present situation. Each πάθος (*pathos*) in *Rhetoric* B 2–11 will yield a determinate temporal-modal signature.

Heidegger does *not* perform this analysis for each πάθος (*pathos*) in BT. Only fear and anxiety are treated explicitly. But the framework BT supplies makes the analysis available, and the Aristotelian definitions in *Rhetoric* B already provide the differentiae the framework requires. The synthetic step in the prior report is therefore properly described as: a *Heidegger-warranted but Heidegger-uncompleted* application of the BT §65 / §68 apparatus to the determinate πάθη (*pathē*) of *Rhet*. B 2–11.

The following four examples — anger, shame, pity, indignation — illustrate how the per-πάθος (*per-pathos*) profile would be developed using the BT apparatus together with Aristotle's definitions.

### 6.1 Anger (ὀργή, *orgē* "anger/wrath")

Aristotle's definition: "ὄρεξις μετὰ λύπης τιμωρίας φαινομένης διὰ φαινομένην ὀλιγωρίαν εἰς αὐτὸν ἢ τῶν αὐτοῦ" (*orexis meta lupēs timōrias phainomenēs dia phainomenēn oligōrian eis auton ē tōn autou* — literally: "a desire/striving with pain for an apparent revenge/retribution on account of an apparent slight/belittlement against oneself or one's own") — "a desire, accompanied by pain, for an apparent revenge, on account of an apparent slight against oneself or one's own" (*Rhet*. B 2, 1378a 31). The key terms here: ὄρεξις (*orexis* "desire/striving/reaching-toward"); λύπη (*lupē* "pain/distress"); τιμωρία (*timōria* "revenge/retribution"); ὀλιγωρία (*oligōria* "slight/belittlement/looking-down-upon").

Temporal-modal profile in BT register:
- **Having-been**: a *retained* (rather than forgotten) thrownness — the "having-been-slighted" persists as the dispositional ground from which anger arises. The slight is precisely *not* forgotten; it is held-onto as a still-present grievance. This is the primary ecstasis (in conformity with §68b's claim about state-of-mind in general) but in a determinate mode: an *unforgetting retention* of the thrown disposition as wronged.
- **Future**: an *awaiting-of-revenge* — a determinate inauthentic future of the awaiting-type, but with specific projective content (the imagined retaliation).
- **Present**: a *making-present* of the slighter and the situation of the slight — the perpetrator and the deed are held in vivid presence as the objects of the desired retaliation.

Anger thus differs from fear precisely in *not* being a *forgetting* of thrownness; it is an *insistent retention* of the having-been-slighted, coupled with an awaiting that is not "depressed" but heated by the prospective revenge. The same three ecstases are in play, but with a characteristic profile that is *anger's* rather than *fear's*.

### 6.2 Shame (αἰσχύνη, *aischynē* "shame/disgrace")

Aristotle's definition: "λύπη τις ἢ ταραχὴ περὶ τὰ εἰς ἀδοξίαν φαινόμενα φέρειν τῶν κακῶν, ἢ παρόντων ἢ γεγονότων ἢ μελλόντων" (*lupē tis ē tarachē peri ta eis adoxian phainomena pherein tōn kakōn, ē parontōn ē gegonotōn ē mellontōn* — literally: "a kind of pain or disturbance regarding bad things appearing-to-bring disrepute, whether being-present or having-come-to-be or being-about-to-be") — "a kind of pain or disturbance in regard to bad things, present, past, or future, which seem to bring shame upon us" (*Rhet*. B 6, 1383b 14). Key terms: λύπη (*lupē* "pain/distress"); ταραχή (*tarachē* "disturbance/confusion"); ἀδοξία (*adoxia* "disrepute/ill-repute"); παρόντων (*parontōn* "of things being present"); γεγονότων (*gegonotōn* "of things having-come-to-be/past"); μελλόντων (*mellontōn* "of things being about-to-be/future").

This is the locus where Aristotle himself names *all three* temporal directions in the very definition. The temporal-modal profile in BT register:
- **Having-been**: the thrown disclosure of oneself as having-fallen-short before others — what Heidegger would call a *thrownness-into-publicness*. Shame is essentially being-thrown-as-exposed-to-the-they.
- **Present**: a *making-present* in the heightened, exposed mode — Dasein is *under the gaze* in the present moment, the present situation of being-seen.
- **Future**: an awaiting of the *continuation* of the having-been-marked — the projection of oneself as one-who-has-fallen-short into the future of being-with-others.

Shame is concentrated in the present-of-public-display, but its "now" is an exposure-now that is *thick* with the having-been (the falling-short that produced it) and the future (the persistence of the social mark). Heidegger does not develop this directly, but his BT §27 analysis of *das Man* (German: "the they/the anonymous one") and §68 analysis of falling supply the apparatus through which shame's temporal profile can be articulated as a determinate concretion of the inauthentic present grounded in a publicly-disclosed having-been.

### 6.3 Pity (ἔλεος, *eleos* "pity/compassion")

Aristotle's definition: "λύπη τις ἐπὶ φαινομένῳ κακῷ φθαρτικῷ καὶ λυπηρῷ τοῦ ἀναξίου τυγχάνειν" (*lupē tis epi phainomenō kakō phthartikō kai lupērō tou anaxiou tygchanein* — literally: "a kind of pain at an apparent evil destructive-and-painful happening to one not deserving it") — "a kind of pain at an apparent destructive or painful evil befalling one who does not deserve it" (*Rhet*. B 8, 1385b 13). Key terms: λύπη (*lupē* "pain"); φθαρτικόν (*phthartikon* "destructive/corrupting"); λυπηρόν (*lupēron* "painful"); ἀνάξιος (*anaxios* "undeserving/unworthy of").

Temporal-modal profile:
- **Having-been**: the having-been-thrown-into-vulnerability that I share with the one suffering — pity rests on the dispositional recognition that "this could befall me." The thrownness disclosed is *common* thrownness.
- **Present**: a *making-present* of another's present suffering, but in a non-absorbed mode — the witnessing of the suffering as undeserved.
- **Future**: an awaiting that is conditional and counterfactual — *what could yet happen*, both to the one suffering and to me. Heidegger does not analyze this directly, but BT's account of *Mitsein* (German: "Being-with/being-with-others", §26) supplies the structural ground: pity is a *Befindlichkeit* of being-with-the-other-as-thrown.

Pity's distinguishing feature is the *with-character* of its thrownness-disclosure: it is not just my having-been but the *having-been-with-the-other-as-vulnerable* that grounds the disposition.

### 6.4 Indignation (νέμεσις, *nemesis* "righteous indignation")

Aristotle's definition: "λύπη ἐπὶ τῷ φαινομένως ἀναξίως εὐπραγοῦντι" (*lupē epi tō phainomenōs anaxiōs eupragounti* — literally: "pain at one apparently/manifestly undeservedly faring-well") — "pain at undeserved good fortune" (*Rhet*. B 9, 1386b 11). Key terms: λύπη (*lupē* "pain"); ἀναξίως (*anaxiōs* "undeservingly"); εὐπραγέω (*eupragein* "to fare well, to prosper").

Temporal-modal profile:
- **Having-been**: a thrownness disclosed as just-order-in-the-world — indignation arises only against the background of a settled disposition that good fortune *should* track desert.
- **Present**: a making-present of the unjust contrast — the other's having-attained-without-deserving.
- **Future**: an awaiting that is structurally protest-laden — a futural orientation toward *the situation being put right*, even if no specific action is projected.

Indignation pairs with pity (Aristotle himself notes the structural pairing at *Rhet*. B 9): both rest on the same thrown disposition toward proportionate desert, but pity faces the undeserved evil while indignation faces the undeserved good.

## 7. The Structural Result

What emerges from §§29, 30, 40, 65, and 68 of *Being and Time*, together with the GA 18 framework, is the following layered claim about the temporal articulation of the πάθη (*pathē*):

**(A)** The πάθη (*pathē*) are modes of *Befindlichkeit* (BT §29, H.138–139). This identification is explicit in *Being and Time*.

**(B)** *Befindlichkeit* in general is grounded primarily in *Gewesenheit* (German: "having-been") (BT §68b, H.339–340). All πάθη (*pathē*) therefore share an essential rooting in thrownness as their primary temporal anchor.

**(C)** Within this shared anchoring, each πάθος (*pathos*) *temporalizes* the three ecstases in a characteristic configuration, articulating having-been, future, and present in a specific modal profile. Heidegger himself performs this analysis for fear (§68b, H.341–342) and anxiety (§40 and §68b, H.343–344), exhibiting the contrast between fear's "depressed-and-bewildered awaiting" / "forgetting" / "bewildered making-present" and anxiety's "bringing-back-to-thrownness-as-repeatable."

**(D)** For πάθη (*pathē*) Heidegger does not himself analyze (anger, shame, pity, indignation, kindness, envy, etc.), the BT §65 / §68 apparatus, applied together with Aristotle's own *Rhet*. B 2–11 definitions, makes the per-πάθος (*per-pathos*) temporal profile available. This step is *not* in *Being and Time* itself but is structurally supported by it — it is the sort of analysis BT would carry out if it pursued the *Rhet*. B catalogue with the same care it brings to fear.

**(E)** The priority of the future in temporality as such (BT §65, H.327–331) means that even where a πάθος (*pathos*) is "primarily" anchored in having-been (as a mode of *Befindlichkeit*), its temporalizing draws ultimately from the futural ecstasis. This is consistent with Aristotle's *Rhetoric* II treatment where many πάθη (*pathē*) are explicitly futurally-inflected (fear before what is coming; hope before what is coming; anger toward prospective revenge).

The synthetic step in the prior report — "each πάθος (*pathos*) articulates being-there in its three temporal ecstases ... but with a characteristic profile that distinguishes one πάθος (*pathos*) from another" — is, with this layered grounding, defensible. It should be presented as a Heideggerian thesis whose framework is BT §§65 and 68(a)–(c), whose paradigm cases are fear (§68b) and anxiety (§40, §68b), and whose application to the broader *Rhetoric* B 2–11 catalogue is a properly philosophical extension that BT licenses but does not itself complete.

## 8. The Asymmetry of the Future and the Status of the Per-Πάθος (*per-pathos*) Profile

A final structural point clarifies the standing of the per-πάθος (*per-pathos*) claim. BT §65 insists that primordial temporality "*temporalizes itself primarily out of the future*" (H.329; Eng. 378), and that the future is the leading ecstasis even where having-been is the primary anchor of a particular existentiale. This asymmetry has direct consequences for the πάθη (*pathē*).

In §68b, having-been is named as the *primary existential meaning* of *Befindlichkeit* — but Heidegger immediately specifies that this primary meaning is articulated *together with* an awaiting-future and a making-present. The "primary" of "primary ecstasis" does not mean exclusive; it means the moment from which the determinate temporal character of the existentiale is most prominently articulated. Fear *forgets* its thrownness, but in *forgetting* it also *awaits* and *makes present*; anxiety *brings back* its thrownness, but only because it *is anxious about* something futural (the bare possibility of being-able-not-to-be) and is *held in* a specific present (the moment of vision is *latent* in anxiety's *auf dem Sprung* — German: "on the leap/poised-to-spring").

For the per-πάθος (*per-pathos*) taxonomy, this means: each πάθος (*pathos*) is to be characterized by *its determinate threefold temporal configuration* — a specific mode of having-been (forgetting, retaining, repeating), coupled with a specific mode of futural awaiting (depressed, heated, dread-laden, hopeful), coupled with a specific mode of present making-present (bewildered, vivid, exposed, withheld). The "characteristic profile" the prior report named is, on this reading, the *triplet of modal specifications* across the three ecstases.

This is the more rigorous formulation of the prior report's claim. Each πάθος (*pathos*) is *temporally articulated by its three ecstases in a determinate mode*. No πάθος (*pathos*) occupies a single ecstasis to the exclusion of the others. What distinguishes one πάθος (*pathos*) from another is not a different *primary* ecstasis but a different *modal configuration* of the same three.

## 9. Direct Textual Anchors (Consolidated)

For citation in the dissertation, the following BT and GA 18 passages provide the textual ground for the claim that each πάθος (*pathos*) articulates being-there in its three temporal ecstases with a characteristic profile.

### Foundational identification of the πάθη (*pathē*) with state-of-mind
- BT §29, H.138–139 (Eng. 178): Aristotle's *Rhetoric* II is "the first systematic hermeneutic of the everydayness of Being-with-one-another"; the πάθη (*pathē*) are *Stimmungen* (German: "moods/attunements") / modes of *Befindlichkeit*.

### State-of-mind as primarily grounded in having-been
- BT §29, H.135 (Eng. 174): mood discloses Dasein in its thrownness — the "that-it-is" veiled in its whence and whither.
- BT §68b, H.339–340 (Eng. 389–390): "*The primary existential meaning of state-of-mind is having been*."

### The three ecstases and their priority
- BT §65, H.328 (Eng. 377): "*Temporality is the primordial 'outside-of-itself' in and for itself*. We therefore call the phenomena of the future, the character of having been, and the Present, the *ecstases* of temporality."
- BT §65, H.329 (Eng. 378): "The primary phenomenon of primordial and authentic temporality is the future."

### The temporal articulation of fear
- BT §30, H.140–142 (Eng. 179–181): the three structural moments of fear (the threatening, fearing as such, that-about-which).
- BT §68b, H.341–342 (Eng. 391–392): "*The peculiar temporality of fear is a forgetting which awaits and makes present*. The 'now-here' of the bewildered making-present, for example, gets entangled in itself, so that the threatening entities can be encountered by it for the first time."

### The temporal contrast of anxiety to fear
- BT §40, H.187–188 (Eng. 232–233): anxiety as the *fundamental* state-of-mind that brings Dasein back to its thrownness as such.
- BT §68b, H.343–344 (Eng. 393–394): "*Anxiety brings Dasein back to its thrownness as something possible which can be repeated.*"

### Per-moment temporal taxonomy of disclosedness
- BT §68a, H.336–339 (Eng. 385–389): understanding (*Verstehen*) as primarily futural.
- BT §68b, H.339–344 (Eng. 389–394): state-of-mind (*Befindlichkeit*) as primarily grounded in having-been (with the analyses of fear and anxiety).
- BT §68c, H.346–349 (Eng. 396–399): falling (*Verfallen*) as primarily grounded in the present.

### GA 18 anchors (preparation for the BT analysis)
- GA 18, p. 168 (Metcalf-Tanzer): the μέλλον (*mellon* "what is coming/the impending") -structure of fear ("not yet there in the sense of standing-before, coming-toward-me").
- GA 18, p. 164: ἡδονή (*hēdonē* "pleasure") as ἐν τῷ νῦν (*en tō nun* "in the now/in the moment"), "complete in the moment," not a κίνησις (*kinēsis* "motion/change") in a span.
- GA 18, p. 129: "The *always* of being-there is the *frequently of repetition*" — the historicality of ἀρετή (*aretē* "excellence/virtue").
- GA 18, p. 115: the three constitutive aspects of every πάθος (*pathos*) — μεταβάλλοντες (*metaballontes* "those-undergoing-change/changing-along-the-way"), διαφέρουσι πρὸς τὰς κρίσεις (*diapherousi pros tas kriseis* "we-differentiate-ourselves with respect to judgments/position-takings"), οἷς ἕπεται λύπη καὶ ἡδονή (*hois hepetai lupē kai hēdonē* "with which pain and pleasure are co-given/follow-along") — temporally articulated triple structure.

### Aristotelian definitions supplying per-πάθος (*per-pathos*) differentiae
- *Rhet*. B 2, 1378a 31 — anger (ὀργή, *orgē*).
- *Rhet*. B 5, 1382a 21 — fear (φόβος, *phobos*).
- *Rhet*. B 6, 1383b 14 — shame (αἰσχύνη, *aischynē*) — with all three temporal directions named explicitly.
- *Rhet*. B 8, 1385b 13 — pity (ἔλεος, *eleos*).
- *Rhet*. B 9, 1386b 11 — indignation (νέμεσις, *nemesis*).

## 10. Recommended Formulation for the Dissertation

A philosophically rigorous version of the prior report's §7-point-4 claim, with full textual anchoring, runs as follows:

> *"The Aristotelian πάθη (*pathē* "passions/affects"), identified by Heidegger in BT §29 (H.138–139) with the existentiale of Befindlichkeit, share the structural anchoring that BT §68b assigns to state-of-mind in general: 'the primary existential meaning of state-of-mind is having been' (H.339–340). Each πάθος (*pathos*) is therefore primarily grounded in Gewesenheit, but it co-temporalizes all three ecstases — having-been, future, and present — in a characteristic modal configuration. Heidegger explicitly performs this analysis for fear and anxiety. Fear's peculiar temporality is 'a forgetting which awaits and makes present' with specific 'depressed and bewildered' character (BT §68b, H.341–342); anxiety's contrasting temporality 'brings Dasein back to its thrownness as something possible which can be repeated' (BT §68b, H.343–344). For the broader catalogue of πάθη (*pathē*) in Rhetoric B 2–11, the per-πάθος (*per-pathos*) temporal profile can be articulated as a triplet of modal specifications across the three ecstases, drawing on Aristotle's own definitions for the differentiae and on BT §65 and §68 for the structural framework. This extension is Heidegger-warranted but not Heidegger-completed: the apparatus is supplied in Being and Time, the per-πάθος (*per-pathos*) application is a properly philosophical extension. In each case, what distinguishes one πάθος (*pathos*) from another is not a different primary ecstasis but a different modal configuration of the same three — a different way of forgetting-or-retaining the thrown disposition, a different way of awaiting-or-anticipating the futural moment, and a different way of making-present-or-encountering the present situation."*

This formulation is *defensibly Heideggerian*. It distinguishes (a) what is explicit in BT (the structural identification, the shared anchoring in *Gewesenheit*, the temporal analyses of fear and anxiety), (b) what is implicit in BT's framework (the per-πάθος (*per-pathos*) modal-configuration analysis), and (c) what is supplied by Aristotle's own definitions (the determinate differentiae of each πάθος (*pathos*)).

## 11. Closing Note on Method

The prior report stated the per-πάθος (*per-pathos*) temporal claim more confidently than GA 18 alone supports. With the BT material now in hand, the claim survives — but its standing has changed. It is no longer to be presented as Heidegger's own systematic thesis about the πάθη (*pathē*) in their entirety; it is to be presented as a *Heideggerian thesis articulated in Being and Time*, of which only fear and anxiety receive determinate temporal-modal analyses, and whose extension to the broader *Rhetoric* B 2–11 catalogue is a properly philosophical task that BT licenses by supplying the framework.

The work that needs to be done is therefore the work of *applying* BT's apparatus to the determinate πάθη (*pathē*) Aristotle catalogues, drawing the per-πάθος (*per-pathos*) modal configurations from the *Rhet*. B definitions and supplementing where Aristotle's definitions underspecify. This is a defensible original contribution to the dissertation: not a discovery in the BT text, but a structured *extension* of BT's framework to a domain BT itself does not fully cover. The prior report's claim, in the more cautious formulation given in §10 above, is exactly the kind of result this extension yields.
