# Two Articulational Concretions of σωτηρία-Paschein

## A Synthesized Defense of the Architectural Cut and the Doxa-Gate Against the [T1] and [T2] Heideggerian Tensions

*Compiled from analyses generated 2026-05-06 in response to Dalton's twofold pushback against the [T1] and [T2] tensions of the Heideggerian overlay to the chapter draft "Emotion is Motion: Phantasia, Appearance, and the Affective Architecture of Aristotelian Being-Moved." Greek terms appear in their original script with transliteration and translation in parentheses on each occurrence to preserve textual transparency.*

---

## 0. Preface: What the Synthesis Accomplishes

The Heideggerian overlay on the chapter draft flagged two structural tensions with Heidegger's GA 18 reading of πάθος (*pathos* "affect/passion"). The [T2] tension concerned the architectural cut between basic affective valence (πάθος as *resonant orexis*) and the higher-order πάθη (*pathē* "passions") of *Rhetoric* B 2–11; Heidegger does not draw the cut, treating πάθος as a single ontological structure articulated by the *Met*. Δ 21 fourfold (1022b15ff). The [T1] tension concerned the directional dependence between δόξα (*doxa* "opinion/yes-saying") and the higher-order πάθη; Heidegger's GA 18, p. 176 master thesis runs the πάθη → λόγος (*logos* "speech/reason") direction, while the chapter draft locates δόξα upstream of the higher-order πάθη on the basis of *DA* III.3, 427b21–24.

The synthesis advanced here dissolves both tensions together. It establishes:

1. **(Synthesis of [T2])** A *single* ontological structure of πάθος — articulated by the *Met*. Δ 21 fourfold and the *Rhetoric* B 1 three constitutive aspects — invariant across two **articulational concretions**: minimal articulation (basic *resonant orexis*, perceptual-level) and maximal articulation (higher-order πάθη, δόξα-mediated).
2. **(Synthesis of [T1])** *Two* directional dependencies operating at *different transitions* in the actualization chain: (i) basic πάθος grounds λόγος and δόξα (Heidegger's direction, preserved at the basic level); (ii) δόξα produces higher-order πάθη through propositional articulation (Aristotle's direction at *DA* III.3, preserved at the doxastic level). The two directions describe different transitions; they are not in conflict.
3. **(Common ground)** Both concretions are species of σωτηρία-paschein (*sōtēria-paschein* "preservation-being-affected") in the sense established by *DA* II.5, 417b2–16 and GA 18, p. 132 — being-affected that *fulfills* rather than diminishes. The unifying ontological ground is σωτηρία (*sōtēria* "preservation/fulfillment"), realized at two articulational levels along a continuous magnitude-axis.

The result is a position more rigorously Heideggerian than Heidegger's own undifferentiated treatment, because it gives the "single structure" thesis the phenomenological articulation that the variable-presence cases of *MA* 701a25–b1 require.

## Part I — The [T2] Synthesis: Single Structure, Two Articulational Hows

### 1.1 The Thesis Stated

> There is a single ontological structure of πάθος (*pathos* "affect/passion") — articulated by the four senses of *Met*. Δ 21, 1022b15ff and the three constitutive aspects of *Rhet*. B 1, 1378a20–25 — that is invariant across (a) basic *resonant orexis* at the perceptual level and (b) the higher-order πάθη (*pathē* "passions") at the δόξα (*doxa* "opinion")-articulated level. The difference between (a) and (b) is not categorical but **articulational**: (a) realizes the structure at minimal cognitive articulation (recognition without proposition); (b) realizes it at maximal cognitive articulation (propositional evaluation under the *Rhetoric* B 2–11 categories). Both satisfy all four *Met*. Δ 21 moments and all three *Rhet*. B 1 constitutive aspects. The cut drawn in the chapter is therefore not an ontological cut between two strata but an articulational concretion within a single structure.

This thesis preserves Heidegger's commitment to a single ontological structure of πάθος (GA 18, p. 132: "the genuine relatedness of πάθος becomes visible; it is related to the *being of living things*") while introducing the phenomenological distinction that the textual evidence requires. It is, in a precise sense, *more rigorously Heideggerian than Heidegger's own undifferentiated treatment*, because it gives the single-structure thesis the explanatory grip on the variable-presence cases of *MA* 701a that GA 18 leaves implicit.

### 1.2 The Decisive Heideggerian Concession: αἴσθησις (*aisthēsis*) as κριτικόν (*kritikon*)

The lynchpin of the synthesis comes from Heidegger himself, not from a polemical reading. At GA 18, p. 126–127, commenting on *DA* II.11, 424a4ff, Heidegger writes:

> "In *De Anima* B 11, Aristotle characterizes αἴσθησις (*aisthēsis* "perception") itself as a μεσότης (*mesotēs* "mean") — perception as the κριτικόν (*kritikon* "ability-to-separate, ability-to-discriminate"), the 'ability-to-separate' one thing from another. Perception is a δύναμις (*dunamis* "capacity, potency") in the sense of κριτική (*kritikē* "discriminative"); it is a *definite being-positioned toward possible objects*."

This concession is decisive. αἴσθησις (*aisthēsis* "perception") itself, *for Heidegger*, is already a κριτικόν (*kritikon* "ability-to-discriminate") — an ability-to-separate, a "definite being-positioned." The διαφέρουσι πρὸς τὰς κρίσεις (*diapherousi pros tas kriseis* "we differentiate ourselves with respect to position-takings") moment of the *Rhet*. B 1 constitutive structure is therefore *already operative at the perceptual level on Heidegger's own analysis*. Position-taking is not added by δόξα; it is constitutive of αἴσθησις (*aisthēsis* "perception") as such.

This Heideggerian concession aligns directly with Aristotle's own statement at *DA* III.7, 431a8–12 (Smith trans., Barnes ed., p. 686):

> "ἡ μὲν ψυχὴ ὥσπερ καταφᾶσα ἢ ἀποφᾶσα διώκει ἢ φεύγει" — "the soul makes a sort of affirmation or negation [κατά-φασις (*kataphasis* "affirmation"), ἀπό-φασις (*apophasis* "negation")] and pursues or avoids."

The φάσις-character (*phasis* "yes-saying / asserting") is operative at the perceptual level: the soul's basic valence is already a primitive κατά-φασις / ἀπό-φασις (*kataphasis* / *apophasis* "affirmation/negation"), a primitive φάσις (*phasis* "yes-saying") that the higher-order πάθη will articulate further but do not first introduce. Combined with Heidegger's αἴσθησις-as-κριτικόν reading, the conclusion follows necessarily: *resonant orexis* (basic affective valence) satisfies the διαφέρουσι πρὸς τὰς κρίσεις (*diapherousi pros tas kriseis* "we differentiate ourselves with respect to position-takings") moment without needing δόξα to inject it. Heidegger cannot deny perception its position-taking character without abandoning his own reading of *DA* II.11.

### 1.3 The *Met*. Δ 21 Fourfold at the Basic-Valence Level

The chapter draft asserts that all four moments of πάθος articulated at *Met*. Δ 21, 1022b15ff (Aristotle, trans. Ross, Barnes ed., p. 1612) apply to *resonant orexis* at the basic level. Heidegger's reading at GA 18, pp. 131–132 enumerates the four senses (BCAP analysis, phase2-u-fp3c.md, pp. 131–132):

1. ποιότης καθ' ἣν ἀλλοιοῦσθαι ἐνδέχεται (*poiotēs kath' hēn alloiousthai endechetai* "being-constituted regarding which alteration is possible") — "Pathos is a determination of beings with the character of becoming-otherwise."
2. ἐνέργειαι καὶ ἀλλοιώσεις ἤδη (*energeiai kai alloiōseis ēdē* "actual occurrings of alteration") — "the 'being-there' of such a shifting occurring-to-one."
3. βλαβεραὶ ἀλλοιώσεις, καὶ μάλιστα αἱ λυπηραί (*blaberai alloiōseis, kai malista hai lupērai* "harmful alterations, especially the painful") — "harmfulness is related mostly to λύπη (*lupē* "pain"), 'my attunement to this occurring affects me'."
4. τὰ μεγέθη τῶν συμφορῶν καὶ λυπηρῶν πάθη (*ta megethē tōn sumphorōn kai lupērōn pathē* "magnitudes of misfortunes and painful affects") — "πάθος designates 'size,' the measure of that which occurs to me in a harmful way: 'that is a blow to me'."

Each moment is structurally satisfied at the basic valence level:

**(1) Alterability.** Perception is *constitutionally alterable*: the perceptual δύναμις (*dunamis* "capacity") is precisely the capacity to be altered by its proper object. Aristotle establishes this at *DA* II.5, 416b33–417a20: perception begins as a δύναμις (*dunamis* "potentiality") that requires an external agent to actualize it — like combustible material requiring an external flame (417a2–6, Smith trans., Barnes ed., p. 664). *Resonant orexis* is the alterability of the perceiving being *as a being-disposed toward goods and evils*. The first Δ 21 moment is satisfied *a fortiori* at the basic level.

**(2) Actual alterations.** The αἴσθημα (*aisthēma* "perceptual content/presentation") itself is an ἐνέργεια (*energeia* "actuality, being-at-work") of the αἰσθητικόν (*aisthētikon* "perceptive faculty"). The basic valence is the ἐνέργεια (*energeia* "actuality") of the ὀρεκτικόν (*orektikon* "desiderative faculty") at its perceptual-level actualization. Heidegger's gloss — "the 'being-there' of such a shifting occurring-to-one" (GA 18, p. 131) — is exactly what the basic valence is: the being-there of the soul's being-affected by the pleasant or painful object.

**(3) Harmful alterations.** This moment looks problematic for the synthesis because Heidegger's narrowing toward λύπη (*lupē* "pain") seems to specify higher-order πάθη. But Aristotle's own treatment of perception in *DA* II.12, 424a28–32 and III.2, 426a30–b8 (the destruction-by-excess passages) explicitly includes both the βλαβερόν-as-painful (*blaberon* "harmful") within ordinary range *and* the βλαβερόν-as-destructive-excess at the limit. Excess sound deafens; excess light blinds; excess flavor destroys taste. The harmful-alteration moment is operative at the perceptual level — it just runs through the entire range of πάσχειν (*paschein* "being-affected").

**(4) Magnitudes.** Aristotle's doctrine that perception has a λόγος (*logos* "ratio, proportion"), that excess destroys the ratio, *and* that stronger stimuli override weaker ones (*DA* II.12, III.2, plus *De Sensu* 7, 447b–448a, on how stronger stimuli mask weaker ones), establishes a **magnitude-structure at the basic perceptual level**. The perceptual ratio (*logos*) admits of degree; basic valence has μέγεθος (*megethos* "magnitude") as a structural feature. The fourth Δ 21 moment is thus satisfied at the basic level *as a structural feature of the perceptual ratio*, not just at the rhetorical-existential level of "great misfortune."

So all four Δ 21 moments are present at *resonant orexis*. **Heidegger's reading of the fourfold as a "progressive narrowing"** (BCAP analysis, U-FP3c-P11) is not a counter to the synthesis; it specifies the magnitude-axis along which the same structure is realized at varying intensities and articulations. The basic valence and the higher-order πάθη sit at different points on the same axis, articulating the same fourfold structure.

### 1.4 The *Rhet*. B 1 Three Constitutive Aspects at the Basic-Valence Level

GA 18, §16, p. 115 (BCAP analysis, phase2-u-fp3b.md) extracts three constitutive aspects of πάθος from *Rhet*. B 1, 1378a20–25:

1. μεταβάλλοντες (*metaballontes* "those-undergoing-change") — "we change from one disposition to another, shifting πρὸς τὰς κρίσεις (*pros tas kriseis* 'with respect to position-takings')."
2. διαφέρουσι πρὸς τὰς κρίσεις (*diapherousi pros tas kriseis* "we differentiate ourselves with respect to position-takings") — "we differentiate ourselves before the change… the hearer's task is 'to take a position, to form a view'."
3. οἷς ἕπεται λύπη καὶ ἡδονή (*hois hepetai lupē kai hēdonē* "with which pain and pleasure are co-given") — "the πάθη are co-given with λύπη (*lupē* 'pain') and ἡδονή (*hēdonē* 'pleasure'); not 'following' but a 'being-disposed-as-higher-or-lower' of the being-there in question."

Each aspect is satisfied at the basic valence level:

**(1) μεταβάλλοντες (*metaballontes* "those-undergoing-change").** Trivially satisfied. Perception with valence is κίνησις (*kinēsis* "motion") of the soul. Aristotle states this directly at *DA* I.4, 408b5–7 (Smith trans., Barnes ed., p. 658):

> "λυπεῖσθαι δὲ καὶ χαίρειν καὶ διανοεῖσθαι κινήσεις" — "being pained or pleased, or thinking, are movements (each of them a being-moved), and the movement is originated by the soul."

The basic valence *is* a being-in-change.

**(2) διαφέρουσι πρὸς τὰς κρίσεις (*diapherousi pros tas kriseis* "we differentiate ourselves with respect to position-takings").** This is where the synthesis is strongest, supported by:

- *DA* II.11, 424a4ff (Heidegger's own concession at GA 18, p. 126–127): αἴσθησις (*aisthēsis* "perception") is κριτικόν (*kritikon* "ability-to-discriminate"), a "definite being-positioned."
- *DA* III.7, 431a8–12 (Smith trans., Barnes ed., p. 686): the soul makes "a sort of affirmation or negation" — κατάφασις (*kataphasis* "affirmation") / ἀπόφασις (*apophasis* "negation").
- *DA* III.10, 433a14–b30 (Smith trans., Barnes ed., pp. 687–688): ὄρεξις (*orexis* "desire") itself is the moved mover, present in all animals; the basic ὄρεξις (*orexis* "desire") is already a *being-toward* (an orientation, a stance).

The position-taking at the basic level is **pre-propositional but not pre-positional**. It is a *taking-as-pursuable* or *taking-as-avoidable* without a propositional articulation. δόξα (*doxa* "opinion") will later articulate this position-taking propositionally — convert "this-as-good" into "this *is* good" — but the position-taking itself is operative without δόξα.

**(3) οἷς ἕπεται λύπη καὶ ἡδονή (*hois hepetai lupē kai hēdonē* "with which pain and pleasure are co-given").** Trivially satisfied at the basic level: this is *just* what *resonant orexis* is — pleasure and pain co-given with the perceptual encounter. *DA* III.7, 431a8–10 again: "when the object is pleasant or painful, the soul makes a sort of affirmation or negation, and pursues or avoids the object."

So all three *Rhet*. B 1 aspects are present at the basic level. The Heideggerian objection that the *Rhet*. B 1 articulation is "stated as the general structure of any πάθος" is not a problem for the synthesis — it is **evidence for** the synthesis. Aristotle states it as the general structure precisely *because* it applies at every level of articulation. Heidegger reads this correctly; the synthesis specifies *how* the general structure differentiates as it concretizes from basic valence to higher-order πάθη.

### 1.5 *MA* 701a25–b1 in Its Strongest Reading

The textual ground that *forces* the articulational distinction onto Heidegger's undifferentiated treatment is *Movement of Animals* 701a25–b1 (Farquharson trans., Barnes ed., pp. 1090–1091):

> "ἃ ἀσκέπτως ποιοῦμεν, ταχέως ποιοῦμεν… ὅταν γὰρ ἐνεργήσῃ ἢ τῇ αἰσθήσει ἢ τῇ φαντασίᾳ ἢ τῷ νῷ, οὗ ὀρέγεται, εὐθὺς ποιεῖ· ἀντὶ γὰρ τοῦ ζητῆσαι ἢ νοῆσαι ἡ ἐνέργεια τῆς ὀρέξεως. πιεῖν, φησὶν ἡ ἐπιθυμία· τοδὶ δὲ ποτόν, ἢ ἡ αἴσθησις εἶπεν ἢ ἡ φαντασία ἢ ὁ νοῦς· εὐθὺς πίνει."
>
> "What we do without reflection, we do quickly. For when a man is actually using perception or imagination [φαντασία (*phantasia* "imagination/presentation")] or thought [νοῦς (*nous* "intellect")] in relation to that for the sake of which, what he desires he does at once. *For the actualizing of desire is a substitute for inquiry or thinking* [ἀντὶ γὰρ τοῦ ζητῆσαι ἢ νοῆσαι ἡ ἐνέργεια τῆς ὀρέξεως (*anti gar tou zētēsai ē noēsai hē energeia tēs orexeōs* "for in place of inquiring or thinking [is] the activity of desire")]. 'I want to drink,' says appetite [ἐπιθυμία (*epithumia* "appetite/craving")]; 'this is drink,' says sense [αἴσθησις (*aisthēsis* "perception")] or imagination [φαντασία (*phantasia* "imagination")] or thought [νοῦς (*nous* "intellect")]; straightaway I drink."

Three observations establish the decisive reading:

**(a) The ἐνέργεια (*energeia* "actuality") of ὄρεξις (*orexis* "desire") substitutes for ζήτησις (*zētēsis* "inquiry") and νόησις (*noēsis* "intellection").** This is a strong Aristotelian claim. The actualization of desire is *not nothing* — it is *positively occupying the structural role* that inquiry would otherwise occupy. The drinking case is not a case of *no* position-taking; it is a case of position-taking *collapsed into orectic immediacy*. The διαφέρουσι πρὸς τὰς κρίσεις (*diapherousi pros tas kriseis* "we differentiate ourselves with respect to position-takings") moment is satisfied — the soul has *taken a position* that "this is drink, to be pursued" — but the position-taking is not articulated through deliberative ζήτησις (*zētēsis* "inquiry").

**(b) The "or…or…" disjunction (αἴσθησις ἢ φαντασία ἢ νοῦς) explicitly includes αἴσθησις (*aisthēsis* "perception") alone as sufficient** for the recognition that triggers desire-actualization. The case is therefore not restricted to non-rational animals; it covers the rational human acting on basic appetitive valence without engaging deliberative cognition. Rational animals also move on basic valence, not only non-rational ones. This is the textual phenomenon Heidegger's undifferentiated treatment cannot accommodate without losing descriptive grip.

**(c) The "what we do without reflection, we do quickly" (ἃ ἀσκέπτως ποιοῦμεν, ταχέως [*ha askeptōs poioumen, tacheōs* "what unreflectingly we-do, quickly"])** specifies that the unreflective swiftness is *normal* for rational animals in appetitive cases, not an aberration. The chain runs without δόξα-articulation, but not without position-taking. The position is *built into* the orectic-perceptual conjunction.

This is the textual phenomenon that *forces* the articulational distinction within Aristotle's own corpus. If Heidegger's undifferentiated treatment cannot distinguish the drinking case from anger, it loses descriptive grip on the very phenomena Aristotle's psychology is most concerned with. The synthesis restores the grip without abandoning the single-structure thesis: drinking and anger share one ontological structure (both are σωτηρία-paschein), but at different articulational concretions.

### 1.6 The ἕξις (*hexis*) - πάθος (*pathos*) Chiasm

Heidegger himself opens the structural space for the synthesis at GA 18, p. 125 (BCAP analysis, phase2-u-fp3c.md, p. 125):

> "ἕξις (*hexis* "settled disposition / having-itself") is nothing other than a how of πάθος (*pathos* 'affect'), being-out-of-composure, in relation to being-composed-as-to."

This is what the BCAP analysis calls the *hexis-pathos chiasm* — "the chiastic point where the ethical-practical analysis of composure intersects with the ontological analysis of being-affected" (BCAP, phase2-u-fp3c.md, "Structural Observations"). If ἕξις (*hexis* "settled disposition") is "a how of πάθος (*pathos*)," then πάθος (*pathos*) admits of multiple "hows" — multiple modes-of-being-disposed. Heidegger explicitly grants that πάθος has different modalities (composed/decomposed; settled/agitated). The synthesis's articulational distinction between basic valence and higher-order πάθη is *just one such how-distinction* within the single πάθος-structure.

The synthesis's distinction is not in tension with Heidegger's chiasm; it is an *instance* of it. *Resonant orexis* and higher-order πάθη are two "hows" of the same πάθος-structure, distinguished by whether λόγος (*logos* "speech/reason") has articulated the position-taking propositionally (δόξα-mediated higher-order πάθη) or whether the position-taking is collapsed into orectic immediacy (*resonant orexis*).

### 1.7 The σωτηρία (*sōtēria*) / φθορά (*phthora*) Pillar — The Unifying Ground

The deepest ontological resource for the synthesis is the distinction Heidegger isolates at GA 18, p. 132 between two senses of πάσχειν (*paschein* "being-affected"):

> "Aristotle recognizes that πάθος (*pathos*) includes μεταβολή (*metabolē* 'change'), κίνησις (*kinēsis* 'motion'), ἀλλοίωσις (*alloiōsis* 'alteration'), in which πάσχειν (*paschein* 'being-affected') has the character of φθορά (*phthora* 'destruction') and σωτηρία (*sōtēria* 'preservation'). Heidegger highlights the connection to Hegel: 'Hegel took the phenomenon of σώζειν (*sōzein* "to preserve") from Aristotle in the expression *Aufhebung* (sublation)' (GA 18, p. 132). As opposed to the μεταβολή (*metabolē* 'change') through which the ἕξις (*hexis*) is saved, there is a kind of πάσχειν (*paschein*) that has the character of στερητικόν (*steretikon* 'depriving') — 'something happens to me, by which I lose the ἕξις (*hexis*).'"

The Aristotelian textual substrate is *DA* II.5, 417b2–16 (Smith trans., Barnes ed., p. 665), where Aristotle explicitly distinguishes two kinds of πάσχειν (*paschein* "being-affected"):

> "There are two kinds of πάσχειν (*paschein* 'being-affected'): (a) the extinction of one contrary by its opposite [φθορά (*phthora* 'destruction')]; (b) the preservation/development of a potential by an actual [σωτηρία (*sōtēria* 'preservation')] — being in reality 'a development into its true self or actuality' [εἰς αὑτὸ γὰρ ἡ ἐπίδοσις καὶ εἰς ἐντελέχειαν (*eis hauto gar hē epidosis kai eis entelecheian* 'for [it is] development into itself and into actuality')]." (417b2–7)

Aristotle then applies the distinction directly to perception: perceiving its proper object is σωτηρία-paschein, not φθορά-paschein — the perceiver is *fulfilled* by encountering its proper object, not destroyed.

Now apply this to the synthesized structure:

- **Basic πάθος / *resonant orexis*** is σωτηρία-paschein at the perceptual-orectic level: the perceiving-desiring being is *fulfilled* by encountering the pleasant or painful object. The valence-laden αἴσθημα (*aisthēma* "perceptual content") is the ἐνέργεια (*energeia* "actuality") that preserves the δύναμις (*dunamis* "capacity") of the ὀρεκτικόν-αἰσθητικόν (*orektikon-aisthētikon* "desiderative-perceptive") composite.
- **Higher-order πάθη** are σωτηρία-paschein at the existential-rhetorical level: the ζωὴ πρακτικὴ μετὰ λόγου (*zōē praktikē meta logou* "life-as-acting-with-speech") is *constituted* by being-affected through δόξα-articulated mattering. ὀργή (*orgē* "anger"), φόβος (*phobos* "fear"), ἔλεος (*eleos* "pity"), αἰσχύνη (*aischunē* "shame") are not destructions but *determinate fulfillments* of the disposing-toward-the-world that the rational animal *is*.

**Both are forms of σωτηρία-paschein.** This is the unifying ontological ground. The structure of πάσχειν-as-fulfillment runs continuously through both basic valence and higher-order πάθη, with magnitude- and articulation-graduation as specified above. The fourfold of Δ 21 articulates this graduated σωτηρία-paschein at successively narrower levels of concreteness; the three constitutive aspects of *Rhet*. B 1 articulate it at every level.

This is what makes the synthesis ontologically rigorous rather than merely descriptively useful. The position is not just *observing* that there are two phenomenally-distinct kinds of πάθος; it is *showing* that there is **one ontological structure of σωτηρία-paschein realized at two articulational levels**, with the transition mediated by δόξα.

### 1.8 The Strongest Counter-Objection and the Response

The strongest Heideggerian counter-objection runs: position-taking (διαφέρουσι πρὸς τὰς κρίσεις [*diapherousi pros tas kriseis* "we differentiate ourselves with respect to position-takings"]) is *not* the same as primitive valence; it is specifically the *self-differentiation of being-there in respect of how it stands toward the matter*, which requires the ζωὴ πρακτικὴ μετὰ λόγου (*zōē praktikē meta logou* "life-as-acting-with-speech") dimension that perception-alone lacks.

The response is direct:

> The Heideggerian διαφέρουσι (*diapherousi* "we differentiate ourselves") requires self-differentiation in respect of stance toward the matter. But αἴσθησις (*aisthēsis* "perception")-as-κριτικόν (*DA* II.11, 424a4ff; GA 18, p. 126–127) is *precisely* a "definite being-positioned" — a stance toward the perceived object as pursue-able or avoid-able. The rational animal's basic valence is not bare perception of a neutral object plus a separate desiderative reaction; it is *valenced perception itself*, in which the perceiver is *already differentiated* with respect to the object as good-or-bad-for-it. The self-differentiation is not absent at the basic level; it is *minimally articulated* — operative as orectic stance, not yet as propositional yes-saying.

This response holds because Heidegger himself grants the perception-as-κριτικόν structure. He cannot deny perception its position-taking character without abandoning his own reading of *DA* II.11.

## Part II — The [T1] Synthesis: Directional Dependence Resolved

### 2.1 The Structural Picture

Once the [T2] synthesis is in place — basic πάθος and higher-order πάθη as two articulational hows of the same single structure — the [T1] directional question disentangles cleanly:

> **At the basic-valence level**: πάθος (as *resonant orexis*) → λόγος (*logos* "speech/reason") → δόξα (*doxa* "opinion") — Heidegger's direction holds. The disposition-disclosing basic valence grounds λόγος and the φάσις-character (*phasis* "yes-saying") of δόξα. Without basic πάθος having already disclosed the world as mattering, there would be nothing for δόξα to ratify.
>
> **At the higher-order level**: δόξα → higher-order πάθη — Aristotle's *DA* III.3 direction holds. The doxastic articulation of "this-as-good" into "this-IS-good" is the gate through which basic valence concretizes into anger, fear, pity, shame.

The two directional dependencies operate at *different articulational levels of the same πάθος-structure*, and they describe *different transitions*. Heidegger's direction describes the *grounding* of δόξα by basic disposition; the chapter's direction describes the *articulational concretion* of basic disposition into determinate πάθη via δόξα. Both directions are correct; they were never about the same transition.

The resulting actualization chain:

```
A₀: αἴσθησις (aisthēsis "perception") with basic valence
        │  (basic pathos ALREADY operative — "this-as-good/bad")
        │  Heidegger's direction: pathos grounds logos
        ▼
A₁: φάντασμα (phantasma "image/presentation") carrying basic valence
        as latent disposition
        │  (logos engages — for rational animals)
        ▼
A₂: δόξα (doxa "opinion") as φάσις (phasis "yes-saying")
        │  (Aristotle's direction at DA III.3:
        │  doxa produces higher-order pathē)
        │  articulational concretion: this-as-good → this-IS-good
        ▼
A₃: higher-order πάθη (anger, fear, pity, shame)
        │  (somatic preparation — heating/cooling of blood)
        ▼
A₄: action
```

At A₀–A₁, πάθος is the prior disclosure of mattering on the basis of which λόγος and δόξα will operate (Heidegger's direction). At A₂–A₃, δόξα gates the further articulation that produces determinate higher-order πάθη (Aristotle's direction). The two directional theses are not in tension because **they describe different transitions in the chain**.

### 2.2 Heidegger's Direction at the Basic Level

Heidegger's master claim at GA 18, p. 176 (BCAP analysis, phase2-u-fp3e.md, "macro-conclusion") runs:

> "the πάθη (*pathē* 'passions') are not merely an annex of psychical processes, but are rather *the ground out of which speaking arises, and which what is expressed grows back into*; they are *the basic possibilities in which being-there itself is primarily oriented toward itself, finds itself*. The primary being-oriented is *not a knowing, but rather a finding-oneself*."

This is the πάθη → λόγος direction. It holds at the *basic* articulational level: the disposition-disclosing basic valence (in which the world is *already* given as mattering) is the *ground* from which λόγος and its yes-saying mode (δόξα) can take their position. Without basic πάθος having already disclosed the world as mattering, λόγος would have nothing to articulate and δόξα would have nothing to ratify.

The chapter draft's δόξα-gate thesis does not contradict this. The δόξα-gate produces *higher-order* πάθη (anger, fear, pity, shame); it does not produce the basic disposition. The basic disposition is precisely what δόξα presupposes as its ground.

### 2.3 Aristotle's Direction at the Higher-Order Level

Aristotle's *DA* III.3, 427b21–24 (Smith trans., Barnes ed., p. 681) establishes the higher-order direction:

> "ὅταν μὲν γὰρ δοξάσωμεν δεινόν τι ἢ φοβερόν, εὐθὺς συμπάσχομεν, ὁμοίως δὲ κἂν θαρραλέον· κατὰ δὲ τὴν φαντασίαν ὡσαύτως ἔχομεν ὥσπερ ἂν εἰ θεώμενοι ἐν γραφῇ τὰ δεινὰ ἢ θαρραλέα" — "when we think (δοξάζωμεν, *doxazōmen* 'we-opine') something to be fearful or threatening, emotion is immediately produced [εὐθὺς συμπάσχομεν (*euthus sumpaschomen* 'immediately we-co-undergo'), 'we are at once moved with the like emotion'], and so too with what is encouraging; but when we merely imagine (κατὰ τὴν φαντασίαν, *kata tēn phantasian* "according to imagination") we remain as unaffected (ἀπαθῶς ἔχομεν, *apathōs echomen* "we have ourselves un-affectedly") as persons who are looking at a painting (ἐν γραφῇ, *en graphē* "in a painting") of some dreadful or encouraging scene."

This passage is the textual ground for the δόξα-gate at the higher-order level. δόξα (*doxa* "opinion") produces the higher-order πάθη "immediately" (εὐθύς, *euthus* "immediately"). The bare φάντασμα (*phantasma* "image"), entertained at will, does not.

Aristotle further establishes that δόξα requires λόγος in *DA* III.3, 428a19–24 (Smith trans., Barnes ed., p. 682):

> "πάσῃ μὲν γὰρ δόξῃ ἀκολουθεῖ πίστις, πίστει δὲ τὸ πεπεῖσθαι, πειθοῖ δὲ λόγος· τῶν δὲ θηρίων ἐνίοις φαντασία μὲν ὑπάρχει, λόγος δ' οὔ" — "every opinion (δόξα, *doxa*) is accompanied by belief (πίστις, *pistis* "trust/conviction"), belief by conviction (τὸ πεπεῖσθαι, *to pepeisthai* "the being-persuaded"), and conviction by discourse of reason (λόγος, *logos*); and some of the brutes have imagination (φαντασία, *phantasia*), without discourse of reason (λόγος)."

The chain is: δόξα requires πίστις (*pistis* "conviction"), πίστις requires τὸ πεπεῖσθαι (*to pepeisthai* "being-persuaded"), τὸ πεπεῖσθαι requires λόγος (*logos* "speech/reason"). Animals have φαντασία (*phantasia* "imagination") but lack λόγος (*logos* "speech/reason"), and thus lack δόξα — and on the synthesis, accordingly lack the higher-order πάθη.

The synthesis preserves both:

- *DA* III.3, 428a19–24 establishes that δόξα requires λόγος. Logos is necessary for δόξα.
- GA 18, p. 176 establishes that πάθη (basic) ground λόγος. Basic πάθος is necessary for λόγος.
- *DA* III.3, 427b21–24 establishes that δόξα produces higher-order πάθη. δόξα is necessary for higher-order πάθη.

The chain runs: basic πάθος → λόγος → δόξα → higher-order πάθη. Heidegger's direction holds at the basic level (πάθος grounds λόγος); Aristotle's direction holds at the higher-order level (δόξα produces higher-order πάθη). The two are not in conflict because they describe different transitions.

### 2.4 The Painting Passage Handled Cleanly

The *DA* III.3, 427b21–24 passage's ἀπαθῶς ἔχομεν (*apathōs echomen* "we have ourselves un-affectedly") looks like a counter-example to the synthesis: how can the soul be ἀπαθῶς (*apathōs* "un-affectedly") if basic valence is supposedly always operative?

The synthesis handles this cleanly:

> When we look at the painting of a dreadful scene, we are *not entirely* unaffected. We register the painting as more or less compelling, more or less aesthetically pleasing or displeasing — basic valence is operative. What we lack is the *higher-order* πάθη: there is no real fear, no real grief. The "ἀπαθῶς (*apathōs*)" specifies absence of the *determinate higher-order πάθη*, not absence of all πάθος.

The textual evidence for this reading lies in the *Poetics*. **N.B.: The *Poetics* is not present in the user's `corpus/rhetorical_ontology` directory; this citation is therefore drawn from Aristotle, *Poetics*, trans. I. Bywater, in *The Complete Works of Aristotle* (Barnes ed., Princeton: Princeton University Press, 1984), Vol. 2, pp. 2316–2340.** Aristotle explicitly deploys the painting/tragic-representation case to argue for κάθαρσις (*katharsis* "purification, cleansing") at *Poetics* 6, 1449b27–28: tragedy "through pity and fear effecting the proper purgation [κάθαρσις (*katharsis*)] of these emotions" (δι' ἐλέου καὶ φόβου περαίνουσα τὴν τῶν τοιούτων παθημάτων κάθαρσιν, *di' eleou kai phobou perainousa tēn tōn toioutōn pathēmatōn katharsin*). If looking at the dramatic representation of dreadful scenes were *entirely apathic*, tragedy could have no cathartic function. So Aristotle himself does not commit to "absolute apathy" in the painting case; he commits to the absence of *the determinate higher-order πάθη that real fear or real grief would produce*.

The painting passage thus becomes *evidence for* the synthesis rather than against it. It establishes precisely that the δόξα-gate is required for the *higher-order* level while the basic level remains operative throughout. The "ἀπαθῶς (*apathōs*)" of *DA* III.3 is the absence of the determinate higher-order πάθη; the basic valence remains in σωτηρία-paschein with the imaginatively-given object.

### 2.5 The φάντασμα (*Phantasma*)'s Structural Role

The synthesis assigns φαντασία (*phantasia* "imagination/presentation") and the φάντασμα (*phantasma* "image, mental presentation") a precise function: the φάντασμα is the **carrier of basic valence forward into the doxa-gate**. The φάντασμα is what δόξα operates upon; what δόξα ratifies as true is the *phantasma's* taking-as. The basic valence is the dispositional charge the φάντασμα carries from perception forward.

Aristotle establishes this directly at *DA* III.3, 429a1–2 (Smith trans., Barnes ed., p. 683):

> "ἔστιν δὲ φαντασία ἀπὸ τῆς αἰσθήσεως τῆς κατ' ἐνέργειαν γιγνομένη κίνησις" — "*phantasia* is a movement (κίνησις, *kinēsis* 'motion') resulting from actual sensation (αἴσθησις τῆς κατ' ἐνέργειαν, *aisthēsis tēs kat' energeian* 'perception according to actuality')."

The φάντασμα is therefore a residual κίνησις (*kinēsis* "motion") of the soul, derived from but not identical with αἴσθησις (*aisthēsis* "perception"). It carries the basic valence — pleasant/painful — as a dispositional property. This makes φαντασία structurally indispensable in a precise way:

- *Without phantasia*: basic valence would be locked to immediate perception. There would be no temporal depth, no anticipation, no memory-grounded fear or hope. The painting passage's "imagining" would be impossible.
- *With phantasia*: basic valence persists, recombines, projects. The φάντασμα of the snake — even if the actual rope is no longer in question — carries the basic valence. δόξα can then articulate: "this *is* a snake" (or fail to articulate, in which case the basic startle persists without becoming determinate fear).

This handles the canonical examples the chapter draft raises:

- **False beliefs produce real emotions**: the rattle-mistaken-for-rattlesnake case. Basic valence registers the danger-pattern; φαντασία carries it forward as a φάντασμα; δόξα articulates "this is a rattlesnake"; higher-order φόβος (*phobos* "fear") arises. The δόξα is false but the production-structure is intact.
- **Art produces emotion**: the vivid description in tragedy. φαντασία is induced; basic valence is engaged with the imagined scene; partial δόξα may form (the audience may, in the moment, "take it as true"); partial higher-order πάθη arise (real but mitigated grief or fear). The "ratification of phantasma" structure handles the partiality.
- **Emotion can contradict judgment**: knowing the rope is not a snake but still feeling fear. Two δόξαι (*doxai* "opinions") compete: the involuntary "this is a snake" (engaging higher-order fear) and the deliberative "this is a rope" (the corrective). The phantasmatic vividness can keep the involuntary δόξα active even when the deliberative δόξα is settled.

### 2.6 Involuntariness at Both Levels

The chapter draft emphasizes δόξα's involuntariness — the taking-as-true *befalls* the agent. The synthesis observes that **basic πάθος is also involuntary**: we don't *choose* to find perceived objects pleasant or painful; we *find* them so. *DA* III.3, 427b16–21 (Smith trans., Barnes ed., p. 681) supports this:

> "τὸ μὲν φαντάζεσθαι ἐφ' ἡμῖν ἐστιν, ὅταν βουλώμεθα… τὸ δὲ δοξάζειν οὐκ ἐφ' ἡμῖν· ἀνάγκη γὰρ ἢ ψεύδεσθαι ἢ ἀληθεύειν" — "imagining lies within our power whenever we wish (e.g., we can call up a picture)… but forming opinions is not in our power; we must hold either a false or a true opinion."

The involuntariness runs through both levels of the synthesized chain. Both basic πάθος and higher-order πάθη befall the agent rather than being elected. δόξα, mediating between them, is itself involuntary. The whole chain is structurally *passive* in the σωτηρία-sense: the agent is the *site* of being-affected, not the *origin* of the affecting.

This connects to Heidegger's "being-taken" (*Genommenheit*) at GA 18, p. 133:

> "πάθος (*pathos*) is a *being-taken* of being-there. Being-there is taken with that which is there in the world — *from without*, but from without *in the sense of the world as the wherein of my being*."

The synthesized three-level chain is the structural articulation of this being-taken: at the basic level, perception is taken with valence; at the doxastic level, δόξα is taken with φάσις on the basis of valence; at the higher-order level, the πάθη are taken as the determinate concretion of valence-articulated-by-doxa.

The involuntariness is structural to σωτηρία-paschein at every level. This is the strongest defense against any "voluntarist" reading of δόξα that might want to read the δόξα-gate as an *active* judgment rather than a *passive* taking-as-true.

## Part III — Architectural Implications and Refinements

### 3.1 The Variable-Presence Cases Re-Examined

The synthesis handles the three variable-presence cases of the chapter draft with precision:

**Case 1: Simple appetition (drinking).**
*MA* 701a25–b1: "I want to drink, says appetite; this is drink, says sense or imagination or thought; straightaway I drink." On the synthesis:
- A₀–A₁: basic πάθος with valenced perception of drink-as-pursue-able is operative
- δόξα may form ("this is drink") but its content is not evaluatively complex
- Therefore no higher-order πάθη arises
- The chain runs A₀–A₁ → ὄρεξις (*orexis* "desire") → action without engaging the higher-order articulation
- The position-taking is operative throughout (perception is κριτικόν [*kritikon*], basic valence is a phasis-like stance) but the doxastic articulation does not concretize into higher-order πάθη

**Case 2: Habitual-rational action.**
*MA* 701a7–25 (Farquharson trans., Barnes ed., p. 1090): the practical syllogism that fires from settled δόξα. On the synthesis:
- Basic πάθος operative
- δόξα engages (the universal premise as settled ἕξις [*hexis* "settled disposition"])
- BUT the δόξα's content is not evaluatively complex
- Therefore no higher-order πάθη
- The chain: basic πάθος → δόξα → ὄρεξις → action, without higher-order concretion

**Case 3: Evaluatively complex action.**
On the synthesis: the canonical case — basic πάθος → δόξα → higher-order πάθη → action. δόξα's content engages the rhetorical evaluative categories; the gate concretizes basic valence into determinate higher-order πάθη; somatic preparation specific to the emotion follows.

The pattern: **δόξα is necessary but not sufficient for higher-order πάθη.** What's required is (a) δόξα forms (involuntary taking-as-true) AND (b) the δόξα's content engages evaluatively complex categories. When (a) holds without (b), one has habitual-rational action without higher-order πάθη. When (a) and (b) both hold, one has evaluatively complex action with full higher-order πάθη.

This sharpens the gate-structure: it is not just that δόξα gates higher-order πάθη; it is that the **content of δόξα** determines whether the gate fires or not. The gate is content-conditional.

### 3.2 The Feedback Loop, Topologically Refined

The chapter draft notes the *De Insomniis* feedback loop. On the synthesis, the loop runs through both articulational levels:

> Higher-order πάθη (e.g., sustained anger) produce a *Stimmung*-like dispositional shading; this shading **biases the basic valence** at the perceptual level (perception with anger-Stimmung sees enemies in shadows); subsequent φαντάσματα (*phantasmata* "images") carry the biased basic valence; the corrective faculty is impaired; δόξα forms unopposed on the biased φαντάσματα; new higher-order πάθη arise, intensifying the *Stimmung*.

The topological refinement: **higher-order πάθη feed back into the basic-valence level**, modulating the very disposition-ground from which subsequent δόξαι (*doxai* "opinions") will arise. This is structurally analogous to Heideggerian *Geworfenheit* (German: "thrownness") — once thrown into a mood, subsequent disclosure is shaped by the mood. The synthesis explains *why*: the higher-order πάθος becomes a sustained dispositional shading that re-specifies basic valence.

The architectural implication: the linear A₀ → A₄ chain needs a **return arrow from A₃ back to A₀** — not as a "loop" in the engineering sense but as the *Stimmung*-saturation of subsequent perception.

The Aristotelian textual basis is *De Insomniis* 460b3–11 (Hett trans., Barnes ed., p. 730):

> "We are easily deceived respecting the operations of sense-perception when we are excited by emotions [πάθεσι κινούμενοι, *pathesi kinoumenoi* 'being-moved by passions'], and different persons according to their different emotions; for example, the coward when excited by fear, the amorous person by the object of his desire; so that, with but little resemblance to go upon, the former thinks he sees his foes approaching, the latter, that he sees the object of his desire; and the more deeply one is under the influence of the emotion, the less similarity is required to give rise to these impressions."

This is the empirical textual ground for the topological refinement: emotional excitement biases perception, generating φαντάσματα that the corrective faculty cannot reject, producing further δόξα and further higher-order πάθη. The loop is real; the synthesis specifies its topology in terms of *Stimmung*-saturation of basic valence.

### 3.3 The Non-Rational Animal Case

The synthesis's restriction to rational animals follows *DA* III.3, 428a19–24 (animals have φαντασία but lack λόγος, and so lack δόξα). On the synthesized structure:

- Non-rational animals have αἴσθησις (*aisthēsis* "perception") + φαντασία (*phantasia* "imagination") + basic ὄρεξις (*orexis* "desire")
- They lack λόγος (*logos* "speech/reason"), therefore lack δόξα (*doxa* "opinion"), therefore lack higher-order πάθη
- They are motivated by basic πάθος alone

But Aristotle elsewhere attributes pathos-like states to animals — fear, courage, etc. (cf. *Historia Animalium* IX, *NE* III.10). **N.B.: *Historia Animalium* and *NE* are not present in the user's `corpus/rhetorical_ontology` directory; these citations are drawn from Aristotle, *Historia Animalium* trans. d'A. W. Thompson, and *Nicomachean Ethics* trans. W. D. Ross, in *The Complete Works of Aristotle* (Barnes ed., Princeton: Princeton University Press, 1984), Vol. 1, pp. 774–993 (HA), Vol. 2, pp. 1729–1867 (NE).** Two responses preserve the synthesis:

**Response (i) (preferred)**: These attributions are *analogical*. Animal "fear" is a sustained dispositional orientation grounded in basic valence + persistent φάντασμα, structurally similar to but not identical with the rational πάθη. The δόξα-gate is precisely what differentiates the merely-similar from the genuine higher-order πάθη.

**Response (ii)**: These attributions involve *quasi-doxa* — animal φαντασία in some species may have a settled-conviction character that approaches the δόξα-function structurally without involving full λόγος. This would be more controversial textually but would let some animals (e.g., higher mammals) approach higher-order πάθη.

Response (i) is cleaner and aligns more closely with the *DA* III.3 textual ground; the chapter's restriction to rational animals lets it bracket this question, but if engaged explicitly, response (i) is the safer move.

### 3.4 Sharpening the Formulation

Two refinements remain pertinent for the chapter:

**(a) "Evolution" → "articulational concretion."** "Evolution" carries developmental-temporal connotations that may obscure the structural point. What the synthesis describes is not a temporal development from basic valence to higher-order πάθη in the life of the soul (though that may also be true); it is a *structural relation*: higher-order πάθη are *more concretely articulated actualizations of the same structure* that basic valence actualizes minimally. The Aristotelian register here is δύναμις-ἐνέργεια (*dunamis-energeia* "potentiality-actuality"): basic valence is the ἐνέργεια (*energeia* "actuality") of the πάθος-structure at the perceptual level; higher-order πάθη are the ἐνέργεια of the same πάθος-structure at the doxa-mediated level. Both are ἐνέργειαι (*energeiai* "actualities"); neither is δύναμις (*dunamis* "potentiality") only. The cut is between two ἐνέργειαι with different articulational concretions, not between δύναμις and ἐνέργεια.

**(b) "Magnitude" needs disambiguation.** Heidegger's reading of moment (4) of the Δ 21 fourfold is *quantitative-existential* — magnitude as "size of misfortune." But the magnitude-axis the synthesis invokes has at least three independent dimensions:
- *Hedonic intensity* (how pleasant/painful)
- *Existential weight* (how mattering)
- *Cognitive articulation* (how propositionally specified)

Higher-order πάθη are not just *more intense* than basic valence — they are *more articulated*, *more existentially weighted*, *and* (often, but not always) *more intense*. The drinking case in *MA* 701a25–b1 may even involve high hedonic intensity (real thirst is intense) without propositional articulation or existential weight in the rhetorical sense. So the magnitude-axis is multi-dimensional, and the articulational dimension is the one carrying most of the structural argument.

### 3.5 What This Means for the Chapter's [T1] and [T2] Defenses

For [T2] specifically, the chapter's defense becomes a positive claim:

> The architectural cut between *resonant orexis* and the higher-order πάθη is not a categorical cut between two ontological strata but an articulational cut within a single πάθος-structure. The single ontological structure — σωτηρία-paschein — is realized at two articulational concretions: minimal (basic valence) and maximal (δόξα-mediated higher-order πάθη). The four moments of *Met*. Δ 21 and the three constitutive aspects of *Rhet*. B 1 are present at both concretional levels. The cut refines Heidegger's undifferentiated treatment by introducing the distinction the variable-presence cases of *MA* 701a25–b1 require, while preserving Heidegger's commitment to the single ontological structure of πάθος.

For [T1] specifically, the chapter's defense becomes:

> δόξα gates the higher-order πάθη; basic πάθος (as *resonant orexis*) grounds λόγος and δόξα. Heidegger's directional thesis (πάθη ground λόγος) and Aristotle's directional claim (δόξα produces higher-order πάθη) operate at different transitions in the actualization chain and articulate the same single πάθος-structure at different concretional levels. The chapter's δόξα-gate is the articulational gate from minimal-concretion πάθος (basic valence) to maximal-concretion πάθος (higher-order πάθη) — not a generative gate in the strong sense of *creating* πάθος *ex nihilo*, but a concretional gate through which the prior dispositional disclosure (basic valence) is articulated into determinate πάθη via δόξα's propositional yes-saying.

Combined, the [T1] and [T2] defenses constitute a unified theoretical position:

- *One* ontological structure of σωτηρία-paschein
- *Two* articulational concretions (basic and higher-order)
- *One* mediating gate (δόξα) that converts the hermeneutical "as-pursue-able" into the apophantic "is-pursuable"
- *Two* directional theses, each operating at the appropriate transition
- *Multi-level* feedback through *Stimmung*-saturation of basic valence by sustained higher-order πάθη
