The phantasma is the medium through which the past persists into the present and the present is projected into the future; it is, in the fullest sense, the soul's temporal medium. Aristotle's primary substance, the unmoved mover, "produces the primary eternal and single movement" (Aristotle, *Metaphysics*, pp. 146–147); by analogy, the phantasma, once formed, produces within the soul a continuing movement that organizes experience, motivates action, and makes thought possible. Thus the T0–T3 framework reveals phantasia not as a marginal psychological curiosity but as the foundational faculty through which the soul inhabits time — receiving, retaining, and transforming the motions of the world into the appearances that constitute its inner life. The deliberative and rational grades of phantasia, which Aristotle distinguishes as bouleutike phantasia in rational beings (Gonzalez, *The Meaning and Function of Phantasia in Aristotle's 'Rhetoric' III.1*, pp. 22–23), represent further elaborations of this temporal medium, articulations that presuppose the kinetic and affective ground we have traced from T0 to T3. Accordingly, the analysis developed in this chapter provides the necessary foundation for the investigations that follow, in which the phantasma's role in memory, rhetoric, and practical deliberation will be examined in their full complexity.
