## II. T0–T3: Motion, Time, and the Genesis of Phantasmata

### II.1 T0 — Motion and Time as Ontological Horizon

(250–350 words)

Define chronos as "the number of motion with respect to before and after" (Physics IV), dependent on kinesis but not identical with it. Time as counted motion requires a soul capable of retaining before/after — this is where phantasia enters. Set up the need for a temporal medium inside the soul that holds onto motion across successive nows.

### II.2 T1 — Aisthesis: First Motion and Pure Reception

(250–350 words)

Aisthesis as reception of form without matter — the wax and signet-ring analogy — alloiosis as qualitative change. At T1 there is no judgment, only being-affected (pathos in the broad, non-emotional sense). Taking-as is NOT yet in play; the soul is merely open, in a state of dynamis becoming energeia.

### II.3 T2 — Residual Motion and Apoleipomene Kinesis

(250–350 words)

Introduce apoleipomene kinesis: the "left-behind motion" persisting in the sense organ after the object withdraws. This is the kinetic side of the dual trace. This residual motion allows us to see motion as continuous rather than as disconnected "stills." Crucially: no explicit phantasma or memory yet — just structured potential for them.

### II.4 T3 — Phantasmata and the Dual Trace: Resonant Motion and Resonant Affect

(350–450 words)
