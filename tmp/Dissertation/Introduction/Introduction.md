\section{Introduction}

The advent and increasing pervasiveness of virtuality such as that found in video games and virtual reality simulations has been steadily increasing for the last few decades. For instance, as of 2018 \textit{Grand Theft Auto V} (\textit{GTAV}) was estimated to be one of if not the highest grossing media titles in history; and this is without including the revenue generated from the various microtransactions available in \textit{GTAV} online. This massive growth in gaming and gaming revenue that has occurred over the last decade (Fig.~\ref{fig:Gaming Revenue}), and recent mass commercial availability of virtual reality (VR) devices like Meta's Quest line of products has highlighted what I believe to be a false dichotomy: namely, the misrepresented opposition between the virtual and the real. I believe this to be a mistaken assumption with substantial implications; for this ontological distinction has a profound impact on how we understand the reality of our experiences that take place within virtual environments.

\noindent For example, trolling, griefing and other forms of harassment experienced within virtual platforms can, and very often do, elicit strong negative affective responses from those that experience it. Our experiences within virtual worlds and any cognitive or behavioral changes which result do not simply become null and void once we leave the virtual space. Rather, they are carried with us into our existence within physical reality.

My goal is to understand the reality of our experiences within such virtual worlds; and, more importantly, to consider how a virtual event can be intentionally constructed to not only elicit a desired affectual, intellectual, and/or behavioral response in the users who engage with it, but also evoke an ontological change. To put it more succinctly: I intend to understand and demonstrate how a virtual event can be capable of influencing human consciousness and behavior by engaging it at a fundamental rhetorical, phenomenological, and ontological level. The heart of this project and my theoretical position is the idea that all environments, natural or virtual, exert an inescapable rhetorical and ontological force which works to attune the human beings that exist within its bounds; and, this capacity to be-attuned, our inherent affectability or what Heidegger calls “disposedness,” is one aspect of the fundamental rhetorical nature of human being. 

Perhaps the best way to introduce the core idea of my work is by discussing my title in more depth. Malicious overtones aside, for the moment, I have chosen this title in homage to \textit{On The Soul}: the English translated title of Aristotle’s \textit{De Anima}. As this work and others by Aristotle are arguably one of the largest influences in the major scholars I will be addressing as well as in my own thinking, it seemed fitting that Aristotle be given a preeminent position in my project. Beyond this, I have selected this title because I believe it most adequately captures the thrust of what I hope to demonstrate. Indeed, part of this is the malicious connotation most commonly associated with “hacking.” “Hacking,” is generally understood as gaining unauthorized control or access to a network, computer system, etc. For example, in April 2021 it was reported that The University of California among other organizations was hacked and a ransomware group stole the personal data of students and faculty, threatening to publish said information unless given payment (\cite{jablon2021}). But “hacking,” like most things in life, need not necessarily be negative. It can refer to applying “an unorthodox strategy or expedient solution to adapt (something) to suit one’s particular needs or preferences (\textit{OED}, “hack” e.). Indeed, it is common for many to “hack” various aspects of their lifestyle, generally referred to as “life hacks."\footnote{A “life hack,” according to Merriam-Webster Dictionary is “a usually simple and clever tip or technique for accomplishing some familiar task more easily and efficiently.” For instance, one of my favorite kitchen hacks is to use a muffin baking sheet to poach multiple eggs in the oven.} Both of these aspects along with their good and bad alignment must be addressed in the handling of this project \hl{and I will conclude with a brief discussion of such later}. 

As a branch of metaphysics, ontology studies the nature of being: the fundamental ways or processes through which we perceive and understand our existence and the existence of other beings. In other words, ontology studies how the world, and our existence within it, appears or is made present to us. Rhetorical Ontology, \hl{as I will discuss later on,} premises that the nature of being is fundamentally rhetorical. That is, as all things are cable of moving and being-moved, rhetorical ontology's materialist influence attempts to recast our engagement with environments, things, and entities not as mere objects, but rather as active constituents that take part in who we are, what and how we know, say, and do. By taking such an approach to game analysis, I hope to demonstrate why our engagement with digital games has the potential to change how we understand various elements of ourselves and the world around us, and also how they can be intentionally designed to evoke such changes by creating a game world that takes into account our fundamental rhetorical ontological and phenomenological processes. Furthermore, creating a game world which is intentionally designed to engage our fundamental rhetorical ontological and phenomenological processes, I believe clever designers can enhance the persuasiveness of their world by, what I call, hacking the human soul.
