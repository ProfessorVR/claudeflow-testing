## 2. Tier 1 — Substantive problems a committee will seize on

### T1-1. Is emotion *a form of orexis*, or *a composite that includes orexis*? The section says both.

**Where.** The thesis, title, and conclusion make emotion **identical to a determinate form of *orexis*:**
- 1166: "Emotion… is not a node in the chain but the *form* that desire takes within it."
- 1170: "emotion *is* the form desire takes… Fear *is* the desire to flee insofar as it is structured by the cognitive evaluation… Anger *is* the desire to retaliate…"

But the **Somatic-Preparation** subsection argues the **opposite**, from the *De Motu Animalium* chain:
- 1134: "the fact Aristotle gives the affections a step of their own, distinct from desire… suggests that **emotion includes desire without being reducible to it**… The ontological status of emotion as separate from desire consists precisely in its **composite character: emotion is desire *together with* physiological change *structured by* cognitive content**… It is this composite—not the desire alone—that prepares the organic parts."

**The problem.** "Emotion is a form of *orexis*" (a *species/determination* of desire) and "emotion is a composite of which *orexis* is one proper part (alongside soma and cognition)" are different ontological claims. The first makes emotion a *kind of desire*; the second makes desire a *constituent* of emotion. An analytic Aristotelian (and any careful reader) will catch the oscillation immediately — and note that the DMA argument you build at 1134 to *distinguish* emotion from desire (it gets "a step of its own") directly undercuts the identity thesis the conclusion asserts.

**Sharper version (the DMA placement).** At 1130–1134 you read DMA's "affections/*pathē*" as the *generic somatic substrate* (heating/chilling) "common to all action," and say the higher emotions are "realized *through*" that stage. So in the DMA sequence (perception→*phantasia*→desire→affections→organic parts→movement) the higher emotion appears to straddle **two** nodes — the *desire* node (where the thesis locates it) **and** the *affections* node (where 1134 locates its somatic realization) — while also reaching back to A₃ for its "cognitive content." "Emotion" is thereby identified with three different things. Where, exactly, does a higher *pathos* sit in the DMA chain?

**Suggested fix.** You already own the tool to resolve this: the **enmattered-account / hylomorphic** frame (1116). State it once, explicitly, at the identity moment: *emotion is the eidetic form-determination of orexis that is necessarily enmattered in a somatic affection and structured by doxa-ratified content.* Then "emotion is the form of desire" (formal account) and "emotion is desire-realized-in-affection-under-cognition" (the enmattered whole) are the *same hylomorphic object under its formal and full accounts* — not two competing claims. Make the form/matter relation carry the reconciliation explicitly; right now the reader has to supply it.

### T1-2. The *doxa*-gate is under-defended against the phantasia-based reading — and your own examples are that reading's best evidence.

**The thesis.** Higher-order emotion requires *doxa* = involuntary *taking-as-true*, which requires *logos*/*pistis*/*pepoithēsis* and so is exclusive to rational animals (1079 fn; 1118–1122; 1136; §6 1312–1319). This "*doxa*-gate" is the engine of the whole section.

**Problem A — your gate sits athwart Aristotle's own definition of the paradigm emotion.** Aristotle defines fear as resting on ***phantasia***: "a pain or disturbance due to *imagining* some destructive or painful evil" (you quote this at 1061, 1219, 1260). Yet your mechanism makes ***doxa***, not *phantasia*, the thing that concretizes fear (1122). You install the gate on DA III.3 427b21–24 ("merely imagine → unaffected; *think* fearful → affected"), reading "think" as *doxa*. But (i) Aristotle's word there is not *doxa*, and (ii) his fear-definition cites *phantasia*. The section never reconciles "*phantasia* grounds fear" (Aristotle's definition) with "*doxa* gates fear" (your mechanism). A reader will suspect the *doxa*-emphasis is imported from **Heidegger's** *oíesthai* ("believe") gloss (which you lean on at 1124, 1203) and read back into Aristotle.

**Problem B (the serious one) — your counter-judgment cases are the rival camp's evidence.** At 1217 you write: "emotion can contradict one's standing judgment, as when the knowledge that the coiled rope is not a snake still fails to forestall the startle… the startle is built on the involuntary taking of the shape *as* a snake." Also: the sun-illusion (1030, 1042), art-emotion (1120 fn, 1217), "false beliefs generate real emotions" (1217). **These are precisely the cases that Jamie Dow, David Konstan, and others use to argue Aristotelian emotions require *phantasia* and *not* full belief** — because the emotion persists *contrary to* the agent's actual *doxa* (I know it's a rope; I believe the sun is large; I know the play is fiction). On your account, *doxa* requires conviction (*pistis*) and "discourse of reason" (1028). A sub-reflective 200ms startle, or fear at fiction one knows to be fiction, is exactly what cannot plausibly carry conviction-laden, *logos*-articulated *doxa*. Your reconciliation — a "momentary *doxa* (snake!) overridden by standing *doxa*" — strains the rich *doxa*-concept you built in §4, and the *euthys* ("immediately," 1122/1317) you cite *as evidence for* the gate is exactly the speed the rival camp cites *against* it.

**What's missing.** The section engages Caston and Gonzalez (who are friendly), but **does not name or answer the phantasia-based rival** (Dow, *Passions and Persuasion in Aristotle's Rhetoric*; Konstan, *The Emotions of the Ancient Greeks*; cf. Striker, Moss on *phantasia* and the good). For a thesis this central, the absence is conspicuous.

**Suggested fix (choose one and commit).**
1. **Hold the gate but defend it:** argue explicitly that the rope-snake/sun/art cases involve a fleeting or "as-if" *doxa*, and explain how that is compatible with *doxa*'s conviction-condition — or weaken the conviction-condition for the reflex case and say so.
2. **Relocate the gate to a thin "taking-as-real" that is still *phantasia*-internal** (a *doxa*-like cresting of *phantasia*'s *taking-as*), conceding ground to Dow/Moss while preserving the rational/civic articulation for the *full* emotions. This is cleaner but costs you the sharp *phantasia*/*doxa* boundary §4 built.
3. At minimum, **name the rival and the cases, and show why your reading wins.** Currently the cases appear *as your own illustrations* without acknowledging they are the opposition's exhibits.

This is the single highest-value fix in the section.

### T1-3. The genus of *epithymia* is unstable, and the "*resonant epithymia* → the emotions" concretization skips the *orexis*-species problem.

**You already know half of this.** Your own `\inlinenote` at **1065**: "Need to clarify that 'hedonic tonality' is the category of which *epithymia* and *resonant epithymia* are species. hedonic tonality is the genus, whereas the other two are temporally specific."

**The inconsistency.** Two incompatible genus–species trees are in play:
- **§3 / glossary tree:** *orexis* (genus) → {*epithymia*, *thymos*, *boulēsis*} — partition by *orectic kind* (924, 1071 fn, line 86 fn).
- **§5 tree (1073):** *hedonic tonality* (genus) → {*epithymia*, *resonant epithymia*} — partition by *temporal presence*.

So *epithymia* is filed under two different genera (*orexis* vs. hedonic tonality) with two different partition principles. These cannot both be its genus. Lock the taxonomy before submission.

**The deeper architectural gap (not yet flagged).** §3 argues the orectic residue co-given with perception is *epithymia* **specifically**, on the ground that *epithymia* "alone requires no cognitive enrichment" (924, 1071). But the higher emotions that §5 says *doxa* "concretizes the *resonant epithymia* into" are **thymotic and bouletic**: anger = *thymos* (§6 1294; "*thymos*… depends on an honor-laden appraisal," 924); pity/shame involve *boulēsis*. So the residue is *epithymia*, but the concretized product mobilizes *thymos*/*boulēsis*. **Does *doxa* transmute *epithymia* into *thymos*?** That needs an argument the section does not give. As written, the dual-resonance thesis (residue = *epithymia*, because only *epithymia* needs no cognition) is in tension with the concretization thesis (this same residue becomes the cognitively-enriched *thymotic*/*bouletic* emotions).

**Suggested fix.** Either (a) make the residue *generic resonant orexis* (hedonic-tonal charge not yet differentiated into species), which *doxa*-articulation then differentiates into *epithymia*/*thymos*/*boulēsis* — but then drop the §3 claim that the residue is *epithymia*-specifically; or (b) keep the residue as *epithymia* and explain how the *thymotic* emotions recruit *thymos* at the *doxa* stage rather than "concretizing" the *epithymetic* residue. Option (a) is cleaner and probably what you mean (the 1073 "hedonic tonality as genus" move points this way), but it requires editing §3's "*epithymia*-specifically" rationale to match.

### T1-4. The "realizable good" gate contradicts emotions whose object is a non-realizable good.

**The three commitments that collide:**
- **§4, 1060:** A₃ originates the M₃→A₄ motion **only if its content discloses a *realizable* good**; where the object "cannot be otherwise," "no realizable good is disclosed and the chain terminates at A₃." Speculative thought "moves nothing."
- **§5, 1166–1168:** "Emotion… is the determinate form *orexis* takes in the **M₃→A₄** motion."
- **§6, 1296:** *boulēsis* "may aim where *prohairesis* cannot: there may be a wish even for impossibles, e.g. for immortality," or for what depends on others.

**The contradiction.** Take grief over an irrevocable loss, longing for the dead, nostalgia, or *boulēsis* for immortality — emotions whose orectic core is a wish for a **non-realizable** good. By 1060, no realizable good ⇒ no M₃→A₄ motion ⇒ chain terminates at A₃. But by 1166, emotion just *is* the form *orexis* takes *at* M₃→A₄. So these states are emotions with no A₄ — which the identity thesis forbids. The section itself invokes exactly such cases: "the considered grief one carries quietly" (1085) and *resonant emotion*, "to remember grief is to feel grief again" (1219). A grief re-felt in memory mobilizes no realizable good and issues in no *praxis*, yet is paradigmatically an emotion.

**Why it matters.** An affect theorist will say your thesis over-restricts emotion to the **action-mobilizing** case and cannot accommodate **contemplative / retrospective / mournful** emotion — which your own text relies on. An analytic Aristotelian will press the internal inconsistency among 1060, 1166, and 1296.

**Suggested fix.** Decouple "emotion is the *form of orexis*" from "emotion necessarily issues in M₃→A₄." Either (a) allow that emotion is the determinate form of *orexis* whether or not the *orexis* reaches a realizable good (so grief = *orexis* under evaluative disclosure that *terminates without action*), explicitly amending 1060's "chain terminates at A₃" to "terminates at A₃ *without bodily praxis*, though the affective actualization is complete"; or (b) restrict the thesis to *action-issuing* emotions and treat contemplative emotions as a distinct case. (a) is more faithful to your ambition.

### T1-5. The temporal-range element equivocates on "primary ecstasis."

**The two claims you endorse:**
- **Heidegger's thesis (you quote and affirm it, 1160, 1205):** mood / *Befindlichkeit* temporalizes ***primarily* in *having-been* (*Gewesenheit*)** — for *every* mood, regardless of object.
- **Your per-emotion assignment (1193, 1203):** each emotion has a *different* "primary ecstasis" — fear *primarily futural*, anger *primarily having-been*, pity *primarily present*.

**The tension.** If, with Heidegger, the primary ecstasis of *any* mood is *Gewesenheit*, then "fear is *primarily* futural" uses "primary" in a *different* sense. You are running together (i) **grounding/temporalizing primacy** (Heidegger's "primarily having-been" — about how mood discloses thrownness) and (ii) **phenomenal dominance / the tense of the emotion's intentional object** (the feared evil is future; the slight is past). The slight's *pastness* is object-time; *Gewesenheit* is an ecstasis of Dasein's self-temporalizing, explicitly **not** a location of objects in a past-present-future series. Aristotle's "temporal distribution" (De Memoria; the Rhetoric definitions) is object-time; Heidegger's ecstases are not. The section slides between them while claiming the convergence is "no mere analogy" (1162) and "not a Heideggerian retrojection" (1199).

**Credit where due.** You partly see this: 1193 distinguishes "varying phenomenally dominant ecstases" from "a having-been that every *pathos* shares as a mode of disposedness." That is the right move — but it is **your** distinction, not Heidegger's, and it needs to be *named and defended*, because it introduces two senses of "primary" precisely where a Heidegger reader will pounce.

**Suggested fix.** Explicitly distinguish, in the text, **(a) the grounding ecstasis** (always *Gewesenheit*, per BT 390) from **(b) the phenomenally-dominant / object-tense anchor** (varies by emotion). Concede that (b) is closer to Aristotle's object-time distribution than to Heidegger's ecstatic temporality, and argue that your five-fold *coordinates* the two rather than identifying them. This converts an equivocation into a deliberate, defended two-level account — and it is exactly the element you say "bears the section's heaviest ontological weight" (1199), so it must be the most carefully guarded.

## 3. Tier 2 — Under-support, exegetical exposure, engagement gaps, originality framing

### T2-1. The synchronic/diachronic split resolves the "judgment-modifying" puzzle by stipulation that may not fit the phenomenon.
The puzzle you pose is real and well-posed (1140): if *doxa* is necessary for emotion, how can emotion *modify* judgment (Rhetoric's definition)? Your answer: *doxa*→emotion is **synchronic**; emotion→*doxa* is **diachronic** (prior-pass residue shaping the next pass) (1148–1150). Two concerns:
- **Individuation of passes is undischarged** (and you flag it, 1152). What principled criterion individuates "one chain-actualization" from the next? Without it, "synchronic vs. diachronic" can be gerrymandered to keep the chain directional. The resolution of the section's central circularity currently rests on an un-individuated T₁/T₂.
- **The *De Insomniis* mechanism may be synchronic, not diachronic.** Aristotle's coward "sees enemies approaching" *while currently afraid* — the present fear shaping the present perception *within one ongoing episode* (rumination, escalating panic). You assign this wholesale to the diachronic register (1150, 1154; repeated §6 1393–1395) to protect the chain's forward directionality. An examiner can reasonably argue the *De Insomniis* feedback is **synchronic** (emotion at *t* biases *doxa* at *t+ε* in the same episode) — which reintroduces exactly the circularity the split was meant to dissolve. Defend the diachronic assignment against the within-episode reading, or concede a synchronic feedback and show the chain survives it.

### T2-2. The Metaphysics fourfold strains for *pleasant* emotions.
You treat the Met. V.21 fourfold (alterability / actual alteration / harmful alteration / magnitude of harm) as the underlying ontology every *pathos* instantiates, "each of the four senses… structurally satisfied" (908, 1099–1101). But senses **(3) injurious alteration** and Heidegger's gloss "tones life down" (899, 1085) are *harm*-specific. The Rhetoric catalog you cite includes **joy, kindness, *philia*, confidence, emulation** (1071, 1073) — pleasant emotions that do not instantiate "injurious alteration." Two repairs, pick one:
- If the fourfold is a **progressive narrowing** (genus→ever-narrower species, as 906/1101 suggest), then only senses (1)–(2) are universal; (3)–(4) are restricted sub-cases. Then stop saying every *pathos* satisfies *all four*; say pleasant emotions realize (1)–(2) [and (4), which admits "pleasant or painful on a large scale"] but not (3).
- If the fourfold is a **necessary conjunction**, pleasant emotions are counterexamples and the claim fails.
Either way, specify the **modal status** of the fourfold and handle the positive emotions explicitly. As written, the section's machinery is built around painful/negative *pathē* (fear, anger-pain, pity-pain, shame-pain, Angst) and under-treats the pleasant half of the catalog it invokes.

### T2-3. The three-way faculty identity (aisthētikon = phantastikon = orektikon) is your synthesis and bears enormous weight.
The 5-step syllogism at **863–876** concludes that *aisthēsis*, *phantasia*, and *orexis* "converge as one underlying faculty—one in substrate, different in being." You concede it goes beyond Aristotle ("I would go a step further and include the *orektikon*," 876, following Nussbaum). This tri-identity is what licenses "*resonant epithymia* co-given with perception," which is what §5's whole concretization story runs on. An analytic Aristotelian will object that Aristotle's repeated "**but their being is different**" (431a13–14) is meant to **preserve** the distinctness of these powers, not to collapse them into "one faculty under three aspects." The premises license *coextension* and *close dependence*; they do not obviously license *numerical identity of faculty*. Because §5 inherits this as settled, any weakness here propagates downstream. Recommend: (i) soften "one underlying faculty" to "one actualization-event articulable under three faculty-accounts," and (ii) add a sentence defending why coextension + the DI/DA identity-claims warrant *substrate*-identity rather than mere co-occurrence.

### T2-4. The OED etymology of "emotion" overclaims and is methodologically incongruous.
The section *opens* (1067–1069) with an extended etymology: "emotion" ← *emovēre* "move out" ← PIE *meue-*, linking *emotion*/*motion*/*momentum*. As rhetorical framing it is attractive. As argument it is exposed:
- A *modern* word's etymology has no evidential bearing on Aristotle's *pathos* / *kinēsis* or Heidegger's *Stimmung*. The footnote at 1069 overreaches: "the etymological argument I draw from the OED… **tracks an ontological doctrine Aristotle himself underwrites**." That is an etymological fallacy (conceptual content inferred from word-origin); the main text's "**not only confirms but amplifies** the ancient insight" (1069) should be "illustrates" at most.
- It is **incongruous with your own method**: the dissertation insists on Greek-untranslated (*phantasia*, *pathos*), distrusts English renderings, and even argues *pathē* ≠ "emotion" simpliciter — yet here leans on the English word's etymology as quasi-evidence.
Fix: keep it (it's a nice hook) but reframe as *avowedly heuristic* — "the English word happens to preserve, as a mnemonic, what the Greek doctrine establishes on independent grounds" — and delete the "tracks an ontological doctrine Aristotle underwrites" clause.

### T2-5. Engagement gaps (you asked for these).
- **Emotion literature (affect lens):** no engagement with the *phantasia*-vs-belief debate that your thesis directly enters — **Jamie Dow** (*Passions and Persuasion*), **David Konstan** (*The Emotions of the Ancient Greeks*), **Jessica Moss** (*Aristotle on the Apparent Good*, on *phantasia* and the practical good), **Gisela Striker**. Moss in particular argues the apparent good is a *phantasia*-phenomenon — directly relevant to your "*doxa* discloses the apparent good" move (1060). At least one of these must be answered (ties to T1-2).
- **Heidegger lens:** the BCAP→BT "structural-successor" thesis (1089–1097) is well-supported on the *continuity* side (Pöggeler, Michalski, Gadamer, Kisiel). It does not acknowledge the *discontinuity* worry — that BT's Angst/being-toward-death and world-disclosive moods arguably leave Aristotle's object-directed, civic *pathē* behind, and that Heidegger's appropriation is *selective* (he drops the Rhetoric's deliberative-political *telos*). "No mere analogy" / "structural identity" (1097, 1162) are strong; a hedge or a paragraph engaging the discontinuity would inoculate you.
- **Rhetoric lens:** strong already (Gross, Hawhee, Rickert, O'Gorman, Burke, Gonzalez). The one gap: the *payoff* claim — that this is the cognitive-architectural ground for rhetorical-affordance dynamics — is asserted (1075 fn, 1081 fn, 1225) more than argued. Consider one worked rhetorical case showing the chain *doing explanatory work* a rival account can't.

### T2-6. Originality framing — flag contributions *as* contributions, with calibrated warrant.
You are generally good about "I coin" / "what I call." Calibrate the weaker ones:
- **"Resonant emotion"** (1219) is introduced **in the conclusion**, in a footnote, almost in passing — yet it is a substantive new category (doxa-articulated emotional content stored in memory) that *adds a third resonance* to the dual-resonance thesis (now: *resonant aisthēma*, *resonant epithymia*, *resonant emotion*). Introducing a new theoretical term in the last pages is structurally odd. Either develop it earlier (it would help T1-4, the grief/memory case) or cut it.
- **"Articulational concretion"** is the section's central original category but is more *deployed* than *defined*. Sharpen, in one place, exactly how "articulational concretion" differs from ordinary "determination/specification of a determinable." Right now a skeptic could say it relabels Aristotle's genus→species without adding content; you need the half-paragraph that shows it adds the *cognitive-articulation axis* (1085's three independent dimensions) that plain determination lacks.
- **The Corcilius–Gregoric use (1071 fn).** C&G argue the *soul* is **not** the proper subject of emotional motion (the *body* is; the soul is only origin/terminus). You quote this correctly and even cite DA I.4 408b13–14 ("the man, not the soul"). But the **main text repeatedly says "*kinēsis* of the soul"** (1069: "a movement (*kinēsis*) of the soul"; subsection title 1067; 1111). ### T2-7. The dark-alley example (Angst as *minimal* articulation) cuts against your Heideggerian frame.
