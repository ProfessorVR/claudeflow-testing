# Chapter Plan: 2.1 — The Tutorial: A Disposition Built on Rails

*Compiled 2026-06-29 from the full corpus index (`corpus/index/Red Dead Redemption 2 (Salvo Playthrough)/`): `rdr2-tutorial-RODA.md` (§0–§8), `rdr2-element-matrix.md`, `phase0-overview.md`, and all 21 beat-maps in `frame-traces/`. Clips 1–16, ~6.7 h, the engineered hexis-bundle.*

---

## Governing thesis and structure

The existing 2.1 draft opens correctly but is far too compressed: it names the *paideia* and the rails-vs-freedom contrast, then collapses six hours into one paragraph of mechanic-nouns. The chapter needs to *perform* that paideia — to trace, moment by moment across a selected set of key scenes, how the design installs both a *technē-hexis* (horsemanship, gunplay, tracking, heist-craft) and an **ethical hexis** (gang-loyalty, belonging-through-labor, the honor-scored moral self) in the same breath. The RODA apparatus — the actualization chain A₀→A₄, the Burkean motive-attribution machine, the bia/agency arc, the Calleja channels, the VR/2D differential — should show through the scene-reading, not be declared before it.

**The dual-hexis structure is the chapter's analytical spine.** Clip 3b threads the first three honor-forks *through* the control tutorial; clip 6 builds belonging through trivially-scored chores; clip 7 is a *moral* run, not a skills run. The ethical hexis is not a by-product of the technē curriculum — it is the primary design objective, cultivated underneath the skill-set from the very first interactive clip.

**The orekton arc in three phases** (from `rdr2-tutorial-RODA.md` §0):
1. **Blizzard-survival** (clips 1–4): food, warmth, shelter for the gang — posited before the player holds the stick.
2. **Standing/belonging** (clips 5–7): being-of-use within Dutch's gang; the camp as home.
3. **The outlaw economy** (clips 8, 13, 16): theft-as-livelihood; the fence-unlock converting a one-off robbery into a standing income node.

The chapter tracks phases 1 and 2 in full; phase 3 needs only a closing note since it feeds the protracted L5 layer rather than requiring its own scenes.

**Chapter arc:** Patiency and the ethical horizon (clips 1–2) → the verb-set and its ethical shadow (clips 3–4) → the collective and its home (clips 5–6) → the honor machine at work and the craft at ceiling (clips 7, 9) → [optional] the body of intoxication (clip 12) → closing frame (clip 10's bar fight as the discrete pass; the bundle now complete for the Sonny capstone).

**Relation to G&G.** Each major section should carry its comparison implicit: where G&G *withheld instruction and let the player discover*, RDR2 *withholds freedom and scripts discovery*. Where G&G's goblin looked but would not act-with, RDR2's gang acts-together from clip 5. The contrast should emerge from the analysis; the direct pairing belongs to Section 3.

---

## §2.1.0 — Opening frame (½–¾ page)

**What it does:** A single tight paragraph setting up the organizing figure — the *paideia* — and marking the first contrast with G&G.

**Content:**
- Where G&G throws the player into a wordless forest and lets dwelling accrete from free play, RDR2 does the opposite: it opens onto a cutscene the player cannot touch, and it keeps the player on a tight rein for hours before the world opens.
- Name the tutorial's double object: not one hexis but two, interwoven from the first interactive clip — a *technē-hexis* (skill-craft) and an **ethical hexis** (disposition toward the gang, toward the world's moral scoring).
- The long linear introduction and the open world are not two phases; they are one device — the leash cultivating the want of the open road, the open road answering a want the leash produced.
- The orekton arc in brief (three phases); name the home-base shift (Colter → Horseshoe Overlook) as the A₀ migration the whole tutorial is built against.
- Note: each of the selected scenes that follows is one increment in a protracted sediment; no single moment constitutes the hexis — only the accumulation does.

## §2.1.1 — Patiency and the Ethical Horizon: The Opening Cinematic (Clip 1)

**Source:** `frame-traces/RDR2 - 1 - Opening Cinematic -- beatmap.md`; `rdr2-tutorial-RODA.md` §0, §2, §4, §7.

**What it does analytically:** Establishes bia as the tutorial's *baseline register* — the player is patient before they are agent; installs the ethical horizon (gang-loyalty, Dutch's authority) before a single mechanic is taught; primes the phantasmatic reservoir that the high-phantasia 2D pole will draw on for the rest of the game.

**Key moments to trace:**

1. **04:11–05:01 — Dutch's grief-and-rally speech.** The tutorial's real opening. Before the player holds the stick, Dutch names the dead ("Davy… Jenny… Sean, Mac"), grieves them, and rallies the living ("Stay with me. We ain't done yet."). This is not scene-setting: it is the **installation of an ethical hexis**. Gang-loyalty is posited as the orienting good of the whole experience — not after the player has acted with the gang, but *before* they have moved a thumb. Analyze as: A₀ ethical framing prior to any A₄; the Burkean scene–act ratio running for the first time (losses attributed to *circumstance/the storm*, not to any agent — "There was nothing more you could have done," 03:42); Dutch's charged image becoming what the RODA apparatus will call a **master A₂ phantasma** — a determinate, orectic image that will orient every subsequent loyalty-doxa the player forms. The VR/2D note: this speech is where the high-phantasia-load pole announces its method most clearly, because a speech on a 2D screen is *exactly* where the data-discrepancy is total and *exactly* where phantasia-load is loaded — Dutch's face, gang's desperation, the moral economy of survival, all of it supplied from the flat image by the player's own image-making.

2. **03:42–05:18 — The exculpatory frame / the scene–attribution engine.** Track the Burkean scene-attribution logic that Dutch will reuse throughout the game: the Blackwater catastrophe was a *storm*, not a failure; Davy died from *circumstances*, not negligence; "a storm like this" shelters the gang. This is the moral lens handed to the player for reading their own future acts, and the honor system will later score against it. The ethical hexis seeded here is not a specific virtue but a **frame** — a habitual tendency to read bad outcomes as circumstantially produced.

3. **05:47 — "We've got some work to do" / the control-transfer.** The candidate hinge from patient to agent: the controller is handed over. Identity ("Arthur") has preceded embodiment. On the 2D pole, naming precedes being — the player is told who they are before being given a body to inhabit.

4. **06:00 — "What really went down back there on that boat?"** The off-screen Blackwater catastrophe: the originating event is never shown. Phantasia must constitute it from dialogue alone — **maximal data-discrepancy as narrative method**. This is the tutorial's opening gift to the high-phantasia pole: the world the player is thrown into has a history the screen will never render, and that history is entirely theirs to fill.

**RODA summary for this section:** Pure A₀; no A₄; the orekton (survival) posited before agency is granted; hexis = ethical (gang-loyalty); bia = the cinematic form itself (soft, total — the player is patient/spectator before they are agent); dominant Calleja channels: affective + narrative + shared; VR/2D: the data-discrepancy is total here (cutscene, no body to enact), its work is priming the reservoir, not embodying.

## §2.1.2 — The Leash: A Disposition Built on Rebuff (Clip 2)

**Source:** `frame-traces/RDR2 - 2 - Opening Restrict Exploration -- beatmap.md`; `rdr2-tutorial-RODA.md` §4.

**What it does analytically:** Twenty-nine seconds. The most important negative-space teaching moment in the tutorial: before any combat, hunting, or honor-scoring, the game installs the most basic hexis it will build on — **obedience to the designed path, the hexis of being-led** — and does so not by instruction but by Rickert recalcitrance.

**Key moments to trace:**

1. **00:00–00:06 — The whiteout establishing.** The corpus's purest exhibit of the 2D pole's data-discrepancy. The screen gives near-total white, wind audio, and the felt cold/terrain/depth must all be supplied by phantasia from prior experience. Analyze as: A₀ framing with the spatial channel *deliberately starved* (the world withholds itself); dominant Calleja channels = affective (the cold, the dread); the scene is present yet withdrawn — a diegetic *phantasmatic subtraction* enacted by weather. The VR note: where a VR headset would supply stereoscopic depth, the 2D blizzard hides everything — the horizon is a blank that phantasia must fill.

2. **00:11–00:13 — "I think you're going the wrong way."** The leash fires. Analyze in full: this is Rickert's recalcitrant world actively refusing an off-trail vector, a **soft bia** — the player's free movement-vector gently overridden by scene, a micro agent→patient inversion. No instruction is offered; the world simply will not yield. Crucially: the rebuff is masked by the whiteout — because the screen withholds visible walls, the spoken NPC line *stands in* for the invisible boundary; the high phantasia-load lets a hard corral read as diegetic guidance. Where VR would betray a wall by stereoscopic depth, the 2D blizzard conceals it. This is the 2D pole compensating for its transparency by using the same data-scarcity that is the pole's burden as the means of concealment.

3. **00:13–00:29 — Re-correction and sustained follow.** The hexis deposited: not a skill but a disposition — *sticking to the trail, being-led* — one increment in the protracted bundle. No A₄ completes; by design, nothing is finished here. The clip's contribution is *privative*: it defines the horizon's edges by enforcing them, so that the eventual open A₀ registers as release.

**RODA summary:** A₀-constraint + A₁-onset only; no A₄; hexis = the ethical-technē of *being-led* (the disposition that the open world will later invert into felt freedom); bia = soft (Rickert recalcitrance); honor = inactive (pre-system); dominant Calleja channels = spatial (starved) + kinesthetic (symbolic only); VR/2D: the whiteout simultaneously demonstrates the 2D pole's high phantasia-load *and* weaponizes that load to conceal the tutorial's constraint.

## §2.1.3 — The Verb-Set: Naturalized Under Fire (Clip 3b, with Clip 3a)

**Source:** `frame-traces/RDR2 - 3 - Movement, Combat, Inventory, Camera -- beatmap.md`; `frame-traces/RDR2 - 3 - Choke vs. Spare -- beatmap.md`; `rdr2-tutorial-RODA.md` §1, §2, §3, §4, §7.

**What it does analytically:** The core of the tutorial. Clip 3b installs the entire verb-set of the game — mounted movement, camera/free-look, shooting, inventory/looting, melee grapple, horse-handling, tracking/Eagle Eye, weapon-selection — under the cover of story, and it threads three ethical forks through that technical curriculum. The hexis is **dual from the first interactive clip**: techne and ethos installed in the same breath.

**Key moments to trace:**

1. **00:00–04:17 — The blizzard corridor: mounted movement + the leash extended.** The tutorial is the on-rails Colter corridor. The player follows the gang through the blizzard; "stay close," "stay to the trail," "careful over this bridge" are the leash tightened. Companion patter scripts every footfall; navigational agency is the companion's, not the player's. The hexis of *compliance with the guided corridor* deepens. Track how the corridor recruits the same phantasia-load the whiteout opened: the felt cold, the horse's sway, the depth of the ravine — all supplied from the thin 2D image by the player's own prior-perception reservoir. The **camera/free-look** beat (04:17, "Arthur, in that cattle shed on the left"): name this as the 2D surrogate for the head. Where a VR player turns to look, here look is a stick-driven symbolic act — the phantasia-load of "being there" rests entirely on the camera.

2. **07:08 — "Shit! Look out! There's more of the bastards!" — The first combat.** The flinch fires for the first time. "Look out!" routes embodiment through phantasia, not an HMD: the involuntary R2-flinch at the muzzle-flash supplies the body the stick cannot. Analyze as: A₂ (the armed-O'Driscoll phantasma) → A₃ (committal doxa: this is an enemy, take-as-hostile) → A₄ (the shot). Hexis = technē (aim/fire). The kill is licensed by *scene* (they shot first) — the Burkean motive assignment the honor machine will henceforth run on combat. The wolf waves at **33:00–36:00** are the climax of this thread: A₂→A₄ on repeat under time-pressure, the combat-hexis consolidated, "Good work, Arthur" the act attributed back to the agent's competence.

3. **09:00–13:52 — The cabin loot: the inventory schema and the acquisitive verb.** "Turn the place upside down. Grab as many supplies as you can. We need the essentials. Food, medicine, whiskey." The satchel/inventory UI is introduced as a pure abstraction — a menu standing in for rifling a dead man's home. This is the tutorial's sharpest data-discrepancy beat: the *techne* of provisioning is installed not through felt handling but through icon and menu-navigation. Note the ethical residue planted alongside it: "poor bastard was married too" — a single line that the Burkean motive-attribution has to work against (the looting is attributed to *purpose/gang-survival*, not the agent's cupidity, but the line makes the dead man a person before the scene-justification closes over him).

4. **14:52–15:30 — The barn grapple: bia suffered, then inflicted.** Arthur is seized in the barn ambush — control momentarily inverted, recovered by button-mash counter. Analyze as: **bia suffered** (the first acute agent→patient inversion in the game — the player made patient by an NPC). Immediately followed by its inversion: the prisoner is beaten for information (**bia inflicted**, 15:30) — the player now the inflicting agent. This is the tutorial's first demonstration of what the bia-arc will do across six hours: alternate and reciprocate, the player inhabiting both poles.

5. **16:00–17:00 — The prisoner release: the first ethical fork, the first honor event.** Dutch hands Arthur lethal discretion — "Do what you want with him. I don't care." The prisoner begs. Arthur releases him: "Get the hell outta here! Go!" This is the tutorial's first **prohairetic act** — a deliberated, freely-chosen mercy over a fully-realized patient of bia. Analyze as: A₃ (the charged doxa: this man's life, in my hands) → A₄ (the mercy-act). The honor machine fires its first score here: the act attributed to the **agent's** disposition. The RODA implication: the bundle is dual from the *first* interactive ethical moment; the techne curriculum is already interwoven with the ethical scoring. Flag that the honor-meter delta is invisible to audio — the moral weight lives in a UI the transcript cannot see.

6. **18:00–20:12 — Sadie Adler: the gang's self-disclosure.** Micah manhandles a hidden woman ("Get away from me!"); Arthur and Dutch intervene ("Leave her alone. She ain't one of them"); the gang names itself against its opposite: "We're bad men. We ain't them." Analyze as: **bia de-inverted** (Micah's agency-stripping of Sadie stopped); the gang's ethical self-definition offered not as a rule but as a contrast — defined against the O'Driscolls, not against a positive ideal. The hexis being seeded is not virtue but *in-group distinction*: the ethical disposition that will be tested all game. Affective incorporation peaks here — the pathos of the new widow is carried by face and voice on a flat frame; all the moral weight is phantasia-supplied.

7. **[Clip 3a interleaved] — "Choke vs. Spare" (the honor-INERT fork).** Read against the prisoner-release above. The *first true ethical fork* of the playthrough (11:31–11:45 within clip 3a): Dutch hands Arthur the same lethal discretion over a disarmed captive — and the honor system **scores it as nothing**. The only phenomenal residue of a life-or-mercy act is a tolling bell — an aesthetic/affective surrogate for the motive-score the machine withholds. Analyze as the diagnostic: the machine that scored the prisoner-release (mercy = honor-positive, agent-term) is *silent* over the intimate choke-or-spare fork. The selectivity is already legible from the outset: **the honor machine scores the witnessed/legible good deed and withholds on the maximal intimate choice**. The bell's toll does the embodiment-work the meter declines to do.

**RODA summary:** A₀ sustained (Colter corridor); full A₀→A₄ chains inaugurated (first combat, first ethical fork); hexis = dual (technē-genesis + ethical) from the first interactive clip; bia oscillates (suffered in the grapple, inflicted on the prisoner, de-inverted for Sadie); honor runs selectively (active on prisoner-release, inert on choke/spare); dominant Calleja channels = kinesthetic + ludic (technē clips) + affective + narrative + shared (ethical clips); VR/2D = first flinch instance, Eagle Eye as first perception-prosthesis, inventory menu as sharpest data-discrepancy beat.

## §2.1.4 — The Hunter's Eye: A Legible Loop (Clip 4)

**Source:** `frame-traces/RDR2 - 4 - Hunting -- beatmap.md`; `rdr2-tutorial-RODA.md` §1.

**What it does analytically:** The corpus's cleanest single RODA chain: Charles voices every stage aloud, making this the tutorial's most *legible* materialization of the actualization-of-desire. The full A₀→A₄ arc runs once, didactically.

**Key moments to trace:**

1. **00:58–02:10 — Camp scarcity: the designed A₀ that makes the kill needful.** "A few cans of food and a rabbit… for 10, 12 people?" The orekton (food, survival) is installed before any animal appears; the hunt is motivated by *camp scarcity, not sport*. This is the honor-motive attribution in advance: the kill will be coded as provision (scene/purpose), so it will accrue no dishonor.

2. **06:10–06:44 — "Get your eye in": A₁ as trained perception.** Charles models tracking — "I see something. How can you tell? How can you not?" — and the Eagle Eye vision-mode is toggled: the screen's track-highlights do the eidetic work the unaided body would supply. Analyze this as the tutorial's explicit naming of what the RODA apparatus calls M₀→A₁: completed perception of the world's offered residues (tracks, scat, wind-direction), leaving an orectic charge (the deer-as-killable). Eagle Eye is the game granting a *perception-prosthesis* — the 2D substitution for what a hunter's trained gaze would hold without a UI. "It's easier in the snow, but once you get your eye in, you'll be able to track them nearly as well in grass and woods" — the hexis is explicitly *portable*, a disposition that will carry across contexts.

3. **07:52–08:04 — "Good tension on the string before releasing. Now, Arthur." — A₂→A₃→A₄ in one stroke.** The kill: bow drawn (the phantasma of the target, charged with the orekton) → held breath (the doxa: shot-as-true) → release (A₄, the completed act). The felt body of the drawn bow is entirely phantasia-supplied behind a button-hold and stick-aim. Note the mechanical specificity: "good tension" is a draw-indicator (the 2D compensation for the felt weight of a real bow), "steadied reticle" is the held-breath mechanic (the 2D compensation for a steadied body). The kill happens in four seconds of screen-time; it took three paragraphs of natural-skill cultivation to build to it.

4. **12:35 — "Easier when they ain't shooting back."** The script's own placement of hunting on the low-flinch end of the data-discrepancy axis. The clip self-marks its position: hunting is where the phantasia-load is *low-threat* (no flinch event; the deer does not charge). This is useful as the contrast with clips 9 and 11 where the load spikes.

5. **19:09 — "Takes a lifetime of practice to master."** The hexis is *named as a hexis*. Not a skill-set but a disposition that takes time to build; the game explicitly frames what it is doing. The clip's last note: A₄ dissolves into the camp economy (carcasses to Pearson), the orekton's residue donated to the collective. The hunt was always, in the end, *the camp's*.

**RODA summary:** Full A₀→A₄ once, clearly voiced; hexis = technē (the hunter's eye-and-hand); bia = absent (volitional throughout; the prey cannot resist); honor = quiet and legitimate (provision = scene/purpose); dominant channels = spatial + ludic + kinesthetic; VR/2D = Eagle Eye as perception-prosthesis; the clip marks the low-flinch end of the phantasia-load axis.

## §2.1.5 — The Collective Orekton: Heist, Cruelty, and the Home-Camp (Clip 5)

**Source:** `frame-traces/RDR2 - 5 - Train Robbery, Camp -- beatmap.md`; `rdr2-tutorial-RODA.md` §2, §4, §5.

**What it does analytically:** Introduces the *collective* dimension of the ethical hexis — the gang as a body of co-agents with a shared orekton — and alternates that collective with its dark underside (Kieran's capture, the hostage fork). Ends with the home-base horizon permanently installed at Horseshoe Overlook.

**Key moments to trace:**

1. **09:30–12:41 — Dutch cedes the lead: "Just make the call. You want to take the lead? Go."** The O'Driscoll firefight: the first time Dutch actively *hands agency* to the player mid-combat. Analyze as: the first moment of **co-disclosed agency** — Dutch and Arthur sharing the same orekton (clear the camp) but with the player now directing the means. The "WE" that G&G withheld begins to appear. Dominant channels: ludic + shared. The kills are licensed by *scene* (they shot first, pre-emptive defense); the honor-score is accordingly quiet.

2. **25:29–27:00 — "Outlaws for life."** Dutch's victory oration after the lake-raid. The in-group creed named: the ethical hexis of gang-belonging condensed into a phrase. The orekton is re-formulated: not survival but *identity*. "Outlaws for life" is not a statement of fact but of *orexis* — the gang's desire to persist as what it is, against a world that would eliminate it.

3. **27:25–33:08 — Kieran Duffy's capture: bia inflicted, the other face of the gang's ethos.** The ethical hexis of in-group loyalty has an immediate outward face: dominative cruelty toward the out-group. Arthur runs down Kieran, lassos/hogties him, drags him to camp, threatens "save your breath for screaming." Analyze as: **bia inflicted** (the player as the inflicting agent, Kieran stripped of agency, reduced to saddle-cargo); the hexis cultivated = callousness toward the enemy as *in-group routine* — not a special act but an ordinary one. The Burkean motive: the violence is attributed to *scene* (war with the O'Driscolls); Arthur's cruelty toward Kieran is identical in form to Micah's cruelty toward Sadie, but the motive-attribution runs differently because the scene is different. Track this as the moral economy's internal logic: in-group belonging and out-group cruelty are *the same disposition*, seen from different sides.

4. **40:01–51:00 — Heist-techne taught directly (detonation, moving-train, safe-cracking).** Unlike clip 3b where the skill-curriculum was naturalized under narrative cover, here the game *scripts skill-acquisition overtly*: "set up the detonator… unspool the wire and then attach it." The detonator-wiring, moving-train traversal ("I'm slipping! Pull me up!"), and safe-cracking are each a discrete technē taught in the same moment it is used. The train-slip (42:42) is a **high phantasia-load PEAK**: vertigo on a moving train, entirely phantasia-borne. Co-agency: Arthur hauls a partner up — a joint act, two bodies on the same train under the same threat.

5. **51:00–52:30 — The hostage fork: the player's prohairesis scored by the machine.** "It's up to you. Kill them, leave them here, take them with you." The honor machine is waiting. Analyze as: the same orekton (no witnesses, get away clean) is satisfiable by murder or mercy; the *prohairesis* — the deliberated choice — is the machine's input; what the machine reads is the *act*, and it attributes the act to the agent. This is the clearest demonstration of the honor machine as a motive-attribution engine: it does not deliberate, it scores.

6. **58:00–65:00 — The wagon-repair and Charles's counter-horizon.** Arthur and Charles lift and reseat a broken wagon wheel in the dark; Charles talks about Native dispossession of the Heartlands, his own mixed Black/Native origins, what "Dutch saved most of us" means. The moral counter-horizon is seeded here, against which the gang's plunder is silently weighed. This is the tutorial planting the other side of the ethical ledger without scoring it.

7. **65:00–70:06 — "This place is perfect": Horseshoe Overlook.** The A₀ home-base horizon shifts permanently. Dutch's oration: "we have survived… now it is time to prosper." The camp economy installed: "every day one of you bring food, the camp gets its slice." Analyze as: the **orekton re-collectivized** — the gang's prosperity as the shared desire-object; the contributive in-group disposition installed as the ground of belonging. "The camp gets its slice" is the belonging-through-contribution ethic that the chore-cluster (clip 6) will cultivate into a felt home.

**RODA summary:** Full collective A₄ series (O'Driscoll raid, heist, hostage-fork, camp-arrival); hexis = dual (heist-technē + ethical in-group creed); bia = inflicted (Kieran), suffered (train-slip), scored (hostage-fork); honor = active (hostage-fork scored); dominant channels = shared + narrative (ethical clips) + kinesthetic + ludic (heist clips); VR/2D = train-slip as high phantasia-load peak; the Horseshoe arrival as the home-base A₀ the rest of the tutorial dwells against.

## §2.1.6 — Dwelling: The Camp as Home (Clips 6a–d)

**Source:** `frame-traces/RDR2 - 06 - Camp, Coffee, Chop wood -- beatmap.md`; sibling beatmaps for clips 06-Chores, 06-Coffee-Chores-Hunting, 06-Question-Mark-Donating-Meat-Crafting; `rdr2-tutorial-RODA.md` §2, §6; `rdr2-element-matrix.md` §B.

**What it does analytically:** This cluster is the dissertation's most distinctive analysis site. Mechanically trivial, analytically load-bearing: the camp-chore sequences are where the tutorial proves that **low ludic + high shared/spatial/affective = dwelling, not goal-pursuit** — the belonging-through-labor that the G&G comparison never achieves, and the 2D pole's deepest form of incorporation.

**Key moments to trace:**

1. **"Morning, Mr. Morgan!" — the hail-by-name as belonging engine.** The clip opens with an NPC greeting Arthur by name. Analyze this as the shared-channel incorporation engine at its simplest: being named by a co-present other within the world. The felt warmth of the hail is entirely phantasia-supplied — the 2D screen delivers audio and a figure; the social bond is invested across the gap by the player's own image-making. This is the opposite of the flinch: no threat, no spike, only the quiet accumulation of *being known here*. The hail recurs throughout the camp-cluster, building by pure repetition.

2. **Coffee at the fire (~00:20).** A single context-prompt: approach the camp pot, press the button, watch the drink animation. The A₂→A₃→A₄ arc runs in seconds on a trivial act: the coffee-as-phantasma (charged "to-be-taken") → the doxa of morning/belonging/routine → the completed act. Analyze the hexis being seeded: not a skill and not a moral choice but a *domestic disposition* — the rhythm of a morning in a place that is becoming one's own. The warmth, bitterness, and heft of the cup are entirely phantasia-load; the 2D screen gives an animation and a context-prompt, no more.

3. **Wood-chopping at ~01:00.** The body of labor on a 2D screen. The chop-mechanic (timed/held button, the log splits) runs the full A₂→A₃→A₄ arc on a single act of whole-body effort. Analyze as: the gap between what the mechanics deliver (a button hold + animation) and what the act means (labor for the gang's warmth, sweat, the axe's weight, the split's resistance) is the phantasia-load peak of the camp cluster. The ludic channel is near-zero (barely scored, no honor flash); the shared channel and the affective channel carry the weight. The chore registers as camp-contribution — minor, uncharged, entirely voluntary. That triviality *is the point*: to belong to this camp is to chop its wood because it needs chopping, not because a reward waits.

4. **"Laundry and sewing. The exciting life of an outlaw… if they only knew how boring it really is."** A camp NPC's quip from clip 6b (attr. Molly or Karen). This line deserves a pause: the game surfaces the *boredom of dwelling* explicitly, naming the cost of the belonging-by-labor it is cultivating. Read this as the Heideggerian second form of boredom: *being-bored-with* the camp-situation, the passing of empty time within a horizon that affords nothing beyond its tedium. Unlike the first form (being left empty at the canceled train), this is a boredom that is *lived-within* rather than endured-from-without — the camp's labor is dull and the NPC knows it, and the player is meant to know it too. This is the cost the ethical hexis of belonging-by-labor levies: you have to *be here*, tediously, for it to work.

5. **"In our skin now" (clip 6c, 02:52).** The tutorial's verbal index of incorporation. A companion says the campfire is "in our skin now" — the dwelling is no longer a place visited but a place *inhabited*, its routines carried in the body. Analyze as: the standard RODA apparatus (A₀, the dwelling horizon) has been interiorized; the home-camp has crossed from the world's side to the agent's side, and what crosses with it is the gang's way of being. The ethical hexis is no longer just seeded — it has begun to sediment.

6. **Donation at Pearson's wagon (clip 6d, 05:54–06:07); the "?" marker and crafting-menu (clip 6d, 06:22).** The camp's donation mechanic and the crafting-menu are the cluster's two **data-discrepancy peaks**: the contribution act (handing meat to Pearson) is a button-press + animation, its moral weight (feeding the gang, being-of-use) entirely phantasia-invested. The crafting-menu is the sharpest: "bring me the right materials" — the orekton thinned to text-labels, the craftable supplied from a symbolic ledger the player charges with imagination. Incorporating exits diegesis entirely; the player leaves the dwelt world and enters a UI. Flag this as an **incorporation rupture** — not a disruptive one, but a momentary gap in the fantasy of dwelling that the screen's symbolic economy must repeatedly produce and paper over.

**RODA summary:** A₀ dwelling throughout; A₂→A₄ arcs on trivial acts (coffee, wood-chopping, donation); hexis = ethical (belonging-through-labor) as the primary seam; bia = absent (the voluntary counter-pole); honor = quiet (chore-completion scores minor honorable-character recognition, Burke's AGENT-term, not ACT-term — the machine idles in the ethical background); dominant channels = shared + spatial + affective; VR/2D = no flinch; the felt body of labor and domesticity is entirely phantasia-supplied; the crafting-menu as the cluster's sharpest data-discrepancy beat.

## §2.1.7 — The Honor Machine at Work: Five Resolutions and One Keystone (Clip 7)

**Source:** `frame-traces/RDR2 - 07 - Mission, General Store, Honor -- beatmap.md`; `rdr2-tutorial-RODA.md` §3, §2.

**What it does analytically:** The densest single honor-run in the tutorial — five consecutive resolutions in ~30 minutes — and the scene in which the ethical hexis is most fully named by the player-character himself. The corpus's richest material for showing the honor machine as a Burkean motive-attribution engine, including its most diagnostically revealing moment of self-attribution.

**Key moments to trace:**

1. **06:18–08:10 — The old man's horse (and its immediate disclaimer).** Arthur retrieves a stranger's stray horse (lasso/calm/return); the honor machine scores +honor; the stranger calls him "a gentleman." Arthur's immediate response: "No, not really. I was just trying to impress the women." Uncle closes it: "if you lot hadn't been here, I probably would have robbed them." Analyze as: the machine scores the *act* and attributes it to the *agent* (Arthur's honorable character), but the agent immediately re-attributes it to *purpose/attitude* (social performance), and the bystander re-attributes it to *scene* (the presence of witnesses). The Burkean ratio is written into the scene three ways: the machine reads one term; the actor reads another; the bystander reads a third. This is the tutorial teaching the player to be skeptical of the machine's scorekeeping — the honorable deed was done, and the machine is right that it was honorable, but *why* it was done is already contested.

2. **23:00–26:00 — Tilly's grab + Karen's beating (twin bia de-inversions).** Tilly is gripped by a pursuer; Karen is being struck in a hotel room. In each case Arthur intervenes: **bia de-inverted** (the women's agency restored by force applied to their oppressors). The honor machine scores both interventions as +honor (defense of the vulnerable). Analyze as: the same structure as Kieran's capture — an agent→patient inversion — but with the polarity reversed: Arthur now produces bia on the predators rather than suffering it. The flinch fires at the grab and the strike (felt threat via phantasia, no HMD); the moral weight of the scene is supplied by image and voice on a flat screen. The double-beat here is analytically rich: two bia-reversals in quick succession, both honor-scored, both building the ethical disposition of the *protective agent* — the gang member who cares for his own.

3. **28:38–31:00 — Jimmy Brooks: "I'm not a good man."** Arthur has run down and lassoced a witness who recognized him from Blackwater; Jimmy dangling on a ledge begs for mercy. Arthur hauls him up, then delivers the keystone speech: "I'm not a good man, Jimmy Brooks. I was in Blackwater. I kill people. And maybe I should have killed you. But I didn't. And I'm not going to. But if you ever breathe a word about me or my friends…" The honor machine scores the spare as +honor. Analyze this moment in full:
   - **The A₃ fork**: the same orekton (gang secrecy/survival) is satisfiable by murder or mercy; the doxa that crystallizes is not "virtue demands mercy" but "mercy serves this orekton better than murder." The prohairetic choice is calculated, not idealistic.
   - **The self-disclosure**: "I'm not a good man" is Arthur narrating his own ethical hexis — not virtue but *self-aware disposition*, an agent who knows what he is, does what he judges effective, and whose judgment happens to align with the honor machine's score. The machine's reading (honor+) and Arthur's self-reading ("not a good man") are both accurate and incompatible.
   - **The machine's blindness**: the honor machine scores the *spare* but cannot score the *coercion* — Arthur has just extorted a man's silence by threatening his life, which the machine does not mark. The machine reads acts, not intentions; it scores the mercy but is blind to the menace that surrounds it. The diagnostic: the machine scores the legible/witnessed term of the pentad and is silent on the complex moral texture behind it.

4. **32:10–32:40 — Returns the commandeered horse.** Arthur took a bystander's horse to pursue Jimmy; he returns it. Honor+. The closing loop: the sequence opened with a retrieved horse and closes with a returned one; the machine scores both. The hexis accumulated across this clip: the player-agent who intervenes for the vulnerable, takes what is needed, and gives it back. Not virtue, but a working rule of conduct the machine can score.

5. **11:45–13:20 — The general store (econo-technē beat).** Brief but analytically important as the clip's purest data-discrepancy exhibit: the store catalog is a menu of text-labels and icons, the goods unreachable by hand, the orekton (whiskey, coffee, provisions) thinned to numbers. Flag as the 2D pole's standing condition whenever the game opens an economy screen: the player exits diegesis, dwelling ruptures, and the world is replaced by a symbolic ledger.

**RODA summary:** Dense A₄ series (five honor-resolutions); hexis = ethical (the protective agent, the morally-scored self); bia = inflicted (on Jimmy, Tilly's pursuer, Karen's john) and briefly suffered (Jimmy before the mercy); honor = active across the clip, and the machine's behavior diagnostically rich (it scores the mercy but not the coercion; it scores the returned horse but not the commandeering); dominant channels = narrative + affective + ludic; VR/2D = flinch at Tilly's grab + Karen's strike; general store as data-discrepancy peak.

## §2.1.8 — The Craft at Ceiling: Silence as Signature (Clip 9)

**Source:** `frame-traces/RDR2 - 09 - Horse, Bear Hunt, Legendary Animal -- beatmap.md`; `rdr2-tutorial-RODA.md` §1, §7.

**What it does analytically:** The technē-apex. The skill-disposition seeded under Charles's narration in clip 4 here runs untutored and at ceiling across two crafts simultaneously — horsemanship and the hunter's eye-and-hand. The clip's transcript is pure whisper-padding: no dialogue. That silence is the diagnostic.

**Key moments to trace:**

1. **00:00–02:30 — The whistle and the horse's answer.** The horse-call is a one-button abstraction; what the game delivers is: the whistle → the animal turns → it trots to the player → mount. Analyze as: **the co-agency handshake** — the horse answers not because it is commanded but because a bond has been built. The animal's response to a signal is the closest RDR2 comes, outside of combat, to a *felt* co-presence: something with its own *orexis* (moving toward, or refusing to move) that the player must attend to. The bond is the ethical-technē seam — neither pure skill nor pure moral disposition, but care-toward-an-animal that partakes of both.

2. **07:30–09:30 — Eagle Eye tracking without a tutor.** The same mechanic Charles narrated in clip 4 ("get your eye in") runs here in silence: the player toggles Eagle Eye, reads sign, follows the trail. No instruction. The hexis is enacted, not explained; what clip 4 seeded, clip 9 demonstrates. Note that Eagle Eye is still a perception-prosthesis — the 2D screen still cannot give the tracker's trained gaze without the UI filter — but the player now *expects* it and uses it as a practiced tool, not a tutorial hint.

3. **11:00–12:30 — The bear charges: the corpus's purest flinch specimen.** The bear closes on the player: a charging predator, screen-bound, no HMD, symbolic controls only. The involuntary flinch at the on-screen charge — the real body ducking a flat image — is the 2D pole's defining embodiment register. If a maul animation fires (the bear knocking Arthur prone, control stripped), that is **bia suffered**: the hunter momentarily made patient by the hunted, agency stripped by the world's co-agent asserting its own *orexis*. Analyze both: the flinch as R2 embodiment via phantasia, the maul as an agent→patient inversion that the bear *inflicts* rather than the design scripting it (a genuinely co-agential rupture, different in kind from the soft bia of the leash).

4. **20:30–22:30 — Dead Eye and the legendary kill: A₄ as consummation of three dispositions.** The legendary animal is taken down through Dead Eye (time-slow + painted target marks). Analyze as: three simultaneous dispositions converge in one act — the horse (horsemanship, the bond, getting to the site) + the Eagle Eye (the hunter's trained perception) + Dead Eye (composure-as-time-slow, the steady shot-placement the trained body would hold). The legendary kill is A₄ as consummation, where three separately-seeded technē-hexeis complete in a single gesture. Dead Eye is the third and final phantasia-prosthesis: the screen grants slowed time as the 2D substitution for the poised concentration a body in stillness would supply.

5. **The diagnostic silence.** The clip runs ~25 minutes with no diegetic dialogue (only whisper-padding). Against clip 4 — where Charles narrated every stage, because the hexis was being seeded — clip 9's silence is the sign that the hexis is now fully formed. A mature disposition needs no commentary; it only acts. This is the tutorial proving its own thesis: the skill built through narrated repetition eventually withdraws from narration and becomes simply what the agent does.

**RODA summary:** A₀ established (the open terrain, the bond); A₁ (Eagle Eye tracking, silent); A₂→A₄ (bear hunt, legendary hunt); hexis = technē-APEX (the mature, untutored craftsman's competence) + the bond (ethical-technē seam); bia = the bear maul (if confirmed — co-agential, not scripted); honor = quiet, legitimate (hunting = provision/scene-purpose); dominant channels = kinesthetic + spatial + ludic; VR/2D = the bear flinch (R2, no HMD) as corpus's purest embodiment-via-phantasia; Eagle Eye + legendary aura + Dead Eye as the three stacked phantasia-prostheses.

## §2.1.9 — [Optional] The Body of Intoxication: The Drunk-Cam (Clip 12)

**Source:** `rdr2-tutorial-RODA.md` §7; `rdr2-element-matrix.md` §C.

**What it does analytically:** The tutorial's most concentrated single-scene demonstration of the 2D pole's method: the game must deliver the phenomenal state of intoxication entirely through image-corruption, because the screen has no other instrument. Include this section if chapter length permits; it earns its place as the most compact statement of the whole VR/2D thesis.

**Content:**
- The drunk-night with Lenny: whiskey, stumbling, the camera that will not hold, unreliable button-prompts, the slurred comic dialogue.
- Analyze as: **phantasia-conscription of the intoxicated body**. A VR headset would need actual vestibular manipulation to deliver the felt body of drunkenness; a 2D screen cannot do any of that. RDR2 instead *warps the image* — the screen tilts, doubles, pixelates, delays — so that the sober player's visual field mimics what an intoxicated field would feel like. Phantasia must then supply the entire embodied state of inebriation from a warped image: the sluggish limbs, the uncertain footing, the loss of mastery over one's own acts.
- This is the 2D pole's method in its most compressed form: a phenomenal body-state that cannot be delivered by the screen's data is substituted by a *designed perturbation of the image itself*. The player's phantasia fills the gap — and does so involuntarily, the same way it involuntarily flinches at the bear's charge.
- The camaraderie that frames it: the Lenny-bond is an ethical hexis beat as well as a technē-joke. Getting drunk together is its own form of belonging-through-shared-act — a collective orekton (oblivion/camaraderie) that is willingly entered, a **consensual bia** in which the player cheerfully cedes mastery.

## §2.1 closing frame (~½ page)

**What it does:** Pulls the protracted analysis together, names the dual hexis now complete, and bridges forward to §2.2 (the Sonny disruption) and Section 3 (the differential).

**Content:**
- Across approximately six hours of early-game play, two dispositions have been built by repetition, interruption, narration, and silence: the *technē-hexis* of a craftsman-outlaw (mounted traversal, gunplay, tracking, heist-craft — all demonstrated at untutored ceiling in clip 9) and the **ethical hexis** of a desiring, dwelling, gang-loyal, morally-scored agent (installed in the opening cinematic before the stick is held, deepened by chores and camp-labor, calibrated by the honor machine through five resolutions in a single clip, named by the agent himself as "not a good man" who nevertheless acts well by the machine's lights).
- The orekton has been re-pitched three times: blizzard-survival → standing/belonging ("in our skin now") → the outlaw economy (the fence-unlock that converts theft into livelihood). Each pitch carried the same dual-hexis bundle, adding a new layer of disposition onto what came before.
- This constructed agent — with his skills, his belonging, his scored moral self, his stake in the gang's survival — is precisely what the later disruption (the Sonny/homestead-assault scene, *outside* clips 1–16) will seize upon and partially supplant. The protracted tutorial is the *precondition* for the disruption's ontological force: it is the dwelling that makes the breaking matter.
- Note the contrast with G&G on the co-disclosedness seam: where G&G reached co-disclosedness only to its threshold — the goblins looked but would not act-together — RDR2 has consummated the acting-together "WE" across the train heist, the O'Driscoll raid, and the Rescue Sean episode (clip 14, outside the selected scenes but confirmed in the protracted RODA). The bar fight (clip 10) handles the discrete validated RODA pass and is treated separately in the corpus; here it functions as the demonstration that the accumulated hexis can be tested in a single scene with full methodological transparency.
- One sentence forward: *The disruption will be the moment this whole structure folds.*
