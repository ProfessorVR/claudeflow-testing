## I. METAPHYSICS — *What Are Virtual and Phantasmic Things?*

### A. Strengths

- **The Aristotelian first/second actuality frame** is the section's best move. The digital object as first actuality (developed, unexercised potency), the virtual object as its second actuality (rendered exercise), and the analogous resonant/phantasmic pair for the mental case — this is genuinely rigorous and original. It gives the data-discrepancy concept a principled ontological basis rather than a technical description.
- **The Chalmers critique is specific and fair.** The two-HMD counterexample (same digital object, different output blueness across displays) is philosophically tight and the argument from causal individuation via Kim is well-deployed.
- **The closing paragraph** ("The data discrepancy, then, is the axis along which these three kinds of thing are ranged…") does the bridge work well — it transitions from ontology to method without resolving the latter prematurely.
- **The disanalogy footnote** (the resonant trace carries an orectic charge no data structure possesses) is one of the most important observations in the section and deserves more than footnote treatment.

### B. Weaknesses and Gaps

**B1. The HMD-restriction undermines the dissertation's own argument.** Virtual reality is defined at the outset as requiring HMDs: "Head Mounted Displays (HMD) and handheld controllers." The entire Chalmers discussion, the virtual-object ontology, and the digital/virtual distinction are built on this HMD-mediated case. But the dissertation's central comparative case — *Red Dead Redemption 2* — is explicitly NOT HMD-based. The metaphysics never explains what ontological category a screen-rendered game world occupies. Is a game on a flat display a "virtual environment" in the same sense? If yes, the definition needs broadening. If no, the section needs to say so and explain the relationship. As written, the metaphysical framework technically excludes RDR2 from the category it's supposed to instantiate.

**Recommended fix:** Add a paragraph or subsection that explicitly extends the virtual-object framework to screen-based rendering, noting that the HMD is one instance of the mediating device (alongside monitor/display) and that the ontological structure (digital object → renderer → perceptible virtual object) remains the same. The data-discrepancy concept is where this can be anchored: HMD-VR has a smaller discrepancy, screen-based games have a larger one, but both produce virtual objects in the defined sense.

**B2. The phantasmic/screen distinction is analytically crucial but not drawn.** The dissertation's core empirical claim is that screen-based games rely *more heavily on phantasia* (high data-discrepancy → higher phantasia-load) than VR games (low data-discrepancy → lower phantasia-load). This claim is central to the analysis chapters but it has no explicit support in the metaphysics. The metaphysics defines phantasmic objects as what *phantasia* renders from resonant traces — but it doesn't explain the relationship between a 2D screen's low-data output and the phantasia that supplements it. A reader finishing the metaphysics section would not understand why screen-based games engage *phantasia* more heavily than HMD VR.

**Recommended fix:** In the three-kinds section, explicitly state that the 2D screen's data-discrepancy forces *phantasia* into compensatory labor — the player's image-making (a faculty producing phantasmic objects) supplements the thin data supply. This makes the metaphysics chapter bear on the analysis from the beginning.

**B3. The inline development note is still visible.** The `\inlinenote{Follow-up, to develop later…}` block (p.10 of the LaTeX) is clearly a drafting placeholder — it discusses Gestell and the inauthenticity reading of total VR. This should either be developed into a proper paragraph or deleted. As left, it breaks the reader's experience and signals unfinished work to any examiner.

**B4. The elaborate digital/virtual distinction is philosophically rich but analytically underused.** The virtual blueness / digital blueness distinction, the Kim principle of explanatory exclusion, the argument from causal individuation — none of this apparatus is ever invoked again in the analysis chapters. The G&G and RDR2 analyses don't refer back to "digital objects" at all. This suggests the section is doing more philosophical groundwork than the dissertation actually draws on. Consider: is the Chalmers rebuttal worth its 3–4 pages if the analysis never returns to it? The section could be condensed significantly, or the analysis chapters need a moment where the virtual/digital distinction is doing active work (e.g., when the screen breaks down, when the program crashes — what exactly is disrupted?).

**B5. The orectic dimension of the resonant trace is confined to a footnote.** The footnote identifies the critical disanalogy: "the perceptual trace carries an orectic charge — it matters (recalled grief is felt as grief) — that no data structure possesses, so the soul's rendering is desire-laden in a way the computer's is not." This is arguably the most important claim in the section for the analysis that follows — the RODA chain depends entirely on the orectic charge of the resonant image (A₁ deposits a resonant epithymia; A₂ is the phantasma fused with desire). But this is a footnote, not a subsection. It should be integrated into the main text and developed as the bridge to the method.

**B6. The data-discrepancy section feels thinner than the ontology above it.** The D-Day example (photo → documentary → *Saving Private Ryan*) is excellent pedagogically, but the whole data-discrepancy section (~1.5 pages) is doing the heaviest analytical lifting for Part II and receives the least sustained argument. The leap from "more sensorial data → felt as more real" to the VR/screen axis used throughout the analyses is made quickly. A more careful argument is needed: what exactly counts as "data" in the discrepancy? Is it only visual, or also haptic, auditory, proprioceptive? How does the discrepancy concept relate to Calleja's six channels of involvement?

**B7. Minor terminological concern:** "resonant kinēsis" appears in a footnote referring back to Part I. Since the metaphysics section is in Part II, readers should not need Part I to follow the argument. The term should be briefly defined in situ.

## II. METHODOLOGY — RODA DRAFT-v3

### A. Strengths

- **The three-word justification** (diachronic / ontological / rhetorical) is precise and each word earns its place.
- **The producing art / using art distinction** (*Phys.* II.2) is the section's most original move and its cleanest — the designer can't supply the player's desire, only the world the desire acts in.
- **The sublation structure** (preserve/transform/elevate) for both Calleja and Burke is philosophically rigorous and explicitly marks what is the dissertation's own contribution versus inherited framework.
- **The co-disclosedness / world-disclosedness naming** is well-motivated and the footnote tracing both terms to Heidegger (BT 145, BT 160) is appropriately specific.
- **The "blind to each other" convergence argument** (Calleja and Burke both arriving at "joined and separate") is strong corroboration for the union-structure claim.

### B. Weaknesses and Gaps

**B1. *Bia* (βία) is absent from the methodology entirely.** The concept appears in every analysis chapter as a primary analytical tool — "bia suffered," "bia inflicted," "bia-lite," "bia-cinematic" — but is never introduced or defined in the methodology. A reader who reads the methodology carefully and then opens the analysis chapters will encounter bia as if from nowhere. Since bia is a full element of the analytical apparatus (it marks the agent→patient inversion that is central to both the disruption scenes and the ethical fork readings), it belongs in the methodology.

**Recommended fix:** Add a paragraph defining bia and its variants in the chain-description section. It is the exception to the orektikon motion (M₃→A₄): it fires when the agent's orexis is *opposed* by external force rather than consummated — the player as patient. Its forms: cinematic (cutscene = total patiency), soft (NPC rebuff, the leash), suffered (grapple, bear charge), inflicted (player as agent of force on another), de-inverted (player restores another's agency).

**B2. The hexis type-distinction is missing.** The method uses *hexis* in both a general sense (the deposit of any completed act) and a specific double sense (*technē*-hexis vs. ethical hexis). This distinction is load-bearing in the RDR2 chapter (the dual hexis thesis) but is not drawn in the methodology. A reader cannot anticipate from the methodology that *hexis* will be bifurcated into two tracks.

**Recommended fix:** Add one sentence in the chain-description noting that A₄→A₀' deposits can bifurcate: deposits of skill-craft settle as *technē*-hexis; deposits of morally-weighted choice settle as ethical hexis. These are not separate chains but two strands of one sediment.

**B3. The R1–R7 criteria appear only in a footnote.** The criteria are the method's empirical gauges for whether incorporation has been achieved or is merely partial. They are used in both analysis chapters, but a reader who doesn't follow up the footnote won't know what "R3 simultaneity" or "R4 the spatial-kinesthetic cornerstone" mean. The footnote lists them as labels only; the main text should at least name the purpose: these are the seven conditions a designed world must satisfy for incorporation to be the verdict rather than mere involvement.

**B4. The "concealment diagnostic" is underexplained.** "In banishing the term, far from banishing its functions one merely conceals them" — this is the move that generates the honor-machine reading in the RDR2 chapter (Arthur disclaims the agent-term and the machine silently re-attributes to the scene-term). But in the methodology this is one sentence without elaboration. What are the diagnostic signs of a displaced motive? How does the analyst identify concealment versus honest attribution? This needs at least a short paragraph.

**B5. Citation error (internal).** The Burke quotation "a way of life is an acting-together" is correctly cited in the methodology as `(Burke, *Rhetoric*, 21)`. But in the RDR2 Tutorial DRAFT it is cited as `(Burke, *Grammar*, 21)`. This is a citation error in the analysis chapter.

**Recommended fix in RDR2 draft, Heist section:** Change `(Burke, *Grammar*, 21)` → `(Burke, *Rhetoric*, 21)`.

**B6. The "diachronic" emphasis is least developed of the three.** The method reads "forward, motion by motion, yet understood backward from its end." This is a genuine methodological claim (it distinguishes RODA from synchronic content analysis), but it receives the least argumentative development. What does it mean to read backward from the orekton? How does retroactive sense-making work in the chain? The claim deserves a sentence or two of illustration — the orekton only names itself when the chain closes; earlier nodes are only recognizable as what they are from the vantage of the completed act.

**B7. The seven-causes / pentad crosswalk is declared but not shown.** "The crosswalk from those four to the pentad is mine, built on Burke's own move." This is one of the dissertation's most original claims, but the crosswalk itself is deferred to an appendix. For a reader who wants to follow the argument, this is unsatisfying. At minimum, one bridging sentence should state the structural logic: the seven causes (chance, nature, compulsion, habit, reason, anger, desire) map onto the pentad's terms as the motivational sources that an act can be attributed to — chance to scene, habit to agent, compulsion to agency, and so on.

## III. ANALYSIS INTRODUCTION

### A. Strengths

- **Movement 1 (rhetorical ecologies)** is the user's own voice and is the strongest writing in the section. The Uexküll → Heidegger → Lefebvre → Rickert progression is tightly argued; the distinctions between environment and ecology are drawn carefully; the closing move ("the virtual environment is that proposition carried to its limit") is one of the best sentences in the whole document.
- **The virtual turn paragraphs** (end of Movement 1) handle Baudrillard carefully — the simulacrum invocation is limited and the dissertation's own position (virtual objects are real in kind) is clearly distinguished.
- **Movement 3 (veri(dis)similitude)** is efficient and precise. The double condition (verisimilar enough to be entered / dissimilar enough to carry force) is well-stated.
- **Movement 4** sets up the two cases cleanly without over-explaining.

### B. Weaknesses and Gaps

**B1. Movement 2 (Incorporation) is too short for its analytical weight.** The shift from "presence" to "incorporation" is the conceptual pivot on which the whole analysis rests, but it receives three short paragraphs. The reader does not come away understanding what incorporation *looks like* versus mere presence or involvement. Calleja's double axis ("the player incorporates the virtual environment into consciousness while simultaneously being incorporated through the avatar into that environment") is quoted but its content is not unpacked. Why does the double-axis structure matter for how the analysis proceeds?

**Recommended fix:** Expand Movement 2 by one or two paragraphs. Briefly contrast a case of mere presence (watching a non-interactive 3D film) with a case of incorporation (the fruit scene in G&G or a camp morning chore in RDR2) to show what the concept's teeth look like. This also gives the reader a preview before the formal analyses.

**B2. The veri(dis)similitude concept and the data-discrepancy concept are not connected.** The Introduction develops veri(dis)similitude (verisimilar enough to be entered / dissimilar enough to carry force); the analysis chapters primarily use "data-discrepancy" and "phantasia-load." These are the same underlying idea from different angles — data-discrepancy is the technical dimension of veri(dis)similitude, and phantasia-load is its experiential consequence. But this connection is never made explicit in the Introduction or anywhere else. A reader might reasonably wonder whether data-discrepancy is a separate concept or a sub-case.

**Recommended fix:** In Movement 4 (the two cases), add one sentence making this explicit: the VR/screen contrast the two cases enact is a controlled variation in data-discrepancy, which is the technical specification of veri(dis)similitude's two poles.

**B3. Four verbatim close-reads are still pending.** Lefebvre 129, Edbauer 10, Rickert 254, and Perelman 116 are flagged as unverified. Two of these (Rickert 254: "persuasion gains its bearings from an affectability…" and BT 94: "that world of everyday Dasein which is closest to it, is the environment") are weight-bearing citations in Movement 1. The BT 94 verbatim was already identified as misquoted in a prior session ("closet" → "closest"). The Rickert 254 verbatim likewise had a suspected typo ("with out" → "our"). These must be verified before the section is finalized.

**B4. The Baudrillard citation generates a tension the Introduction doesn't resolve.** Calling the virtual environment a "simulacrum" imports Baudrillard's claim that the simulacrum precedes and replaces reality with no referent behind it. But the dissertation's metaphysical position is that virtual objects are real in kind, grounded in real digital objects, and not copies of anything. Baudrillard's simulacrum is the opposite of what the metaphysics argues: for Baudrillard, there is no original to be grounded in; for the dissertation, the digital object is exactly that real ground. The invocation should be either more explicitly limited ("in a minimal Baudrillardian sense — rendered rather than found — without Baudrillard's anti-realist claim") or replaced with a less tension-generating formulation.

**B5. Gibson's affordance concept appears once and is dropped.** "It implies the complementarity of the animal and the environment" (Gibson, 119) is cited in Movement 1 and never developed. Affordance theory would be useful in the analysis chapters — the fruit *affords* picking, the bell *affords* ringing, the camp chores *afford* contribution — but the Introduction doesn't set it up enough to license later use. Either develop Gibson slightly more or remove the citation if it won't be used.

**B6. The structural relationship between the Introduction and the G&G chapter is undefined.** The Introduction ends "The first case is *Gnomes & Goblins*" and the G&G chapter begins. But the Introduction is labelled neither "§0" nor given a section number. The reader doesn't know whether the Introduction is part of §1 or preceding it. The final word on this is a layout question, but the in-text transitions need to be coordinated.

## IV. G&G CHAPTER DRAFT

### A. Strengths

- **The Fruit** is the dissertation's finest piece of writing — analytically precise, stylistically controlled, phenomenologically acute. The "curation not coercion" formula at the close is exactly the right level of abstraction.
- **The Miniaturization** section's phenomenological description of the scale-shift is one of the best descriptive passages in the document. The mood analysis (anxiety as wonder rather than dread) is philosophically careful.
- **The Carry-Over** section is strong in both its analysis (the 15-minute recalibration datum) and its theoretical elevation (the substantial alteration that strains toward a remaking of character but cannot become one, because the virtual keeps its kind).
- **The Tavern** section's reading of co-disclosedness at its threshold — the joining that stops at the look — is the chapter's most original analytical finding.
- **Voice** is consistent throughout. The "figured-but-plain" register is achieved.

### B. Weaknesses and Gaps

**B1. The "soap bubble" Uexküll metaphor is duplicated.** "A 'soap bubble' which encloses the subject on all sides" (Uexküll) appears in both the Analysis Introduction (Movement 1) and the Forest section of the G&G chapter. Within the same dissertation, verbatim repetition of a striking metaphor reads as inadvertent redundancy rather than deliberate echo. One of the two instances needs to be reformulated. The Forest section's use is more central (the dwelling-as-enclosure is the point), so the Introduction's instance is the candidate for revision.

**B2. The Candle section is analytically explicit in ways that blur the "overt/figured-but-plain" distinction.** The chapter note identifies The Fruit as the "one overt worked example" and other sections as "figured-but-plain." But The Candle section already names hexis explicitly ("the disposition the rest will build upon"), technē explicitly ("the first skill (*technē*, τέχνη)"), and dwelling explicitly. By the time the reader reaches The Fruit, the apparatus is not being introduced for the first time. This muddies the rationale for calling The Fruit uniquely "overt." Either The Candle needs to be more rigorously "figured-but-plain" (apparatus implied, not named), or the distinction in the chapter note should be softened to "more explicit" vs. "less explicit."

**B3. The Rock passage needs citation verification.** "What refuses in this way is, in Rickert's sense, a *thing* rather than an *object* — something whose being is not exhausted by what can be done with it, exceeding the use-relation and withdrawing into a depth no dealing reaches (Rickert, *Ambient Rhetoric*, 199–201)." The Heidegger provenance of this distinction (thing vs. object, ready-to-hand vs. unready-to-hand) is more fundamental than Rickert's. Rickert's treatment at pages 199–201 needs to be verified against the PDF; if Rickert is primarily summarizing Heidegger there, the citation may need to be reformulated as "(see Heidegger, *BT*, 98–107; Rickert, *Ambient Rhetoric*, 199–201)" to give proper primary credit.

**B4. The analysis says G&G "withholds" the acting-together but doesn't explain the design choice.** "The design discloses… the acting-together kept just beyond reach. These are not two unions but one incorporation disclosing along its two poles." This is the G&G chapter's central finding, but the chapter is silent on whether this is a deliberate authorial choice or a technological constraint. This matters for the dissertation's claim about "designed worlds": was the limitation of co-disclosedness chosen (the designer wanted to give presence, not participation) or constrained (the AI/NPC system couldn't deliver genuine reciprocity)? Even a brief hedging sentence would strengthen the reading: "Whether by design or technical constraint, the outcome is the same analytically — the co-world is disclosed as preview."

**B5. The first-person "Salvo" citation in the Carry-Over section needs framing.** "(Salvo, *Veri(dis)similitude*, 58 n.15)" — this is the dissertation citing the author's own earlier work (the MA). A reader who doesn't know this provenance might not realize the citation is self-referential. Given that first-person citation of one's own earlier published/submitted work is standard in academic writing, a brief parenthetical note ("the author's earlier study") would contextualize it without being awkward.

**B6. The Bell/prohairesis identification is the chapter's most theoretically compressed moment.** "The act itself is small, and entirely the player's. Curiosity moves it… a desire (*epithymia*, ἐπιθυμία) sourced in the player and in no goal the design has set; and the means is chosen, the bell raised and sounded as a deliberate probe (the *prohairetic*, the deliberative completion of a wish rather than a fourth kind of motive)." This parenthetical brackets a technical distinction (that prohairesis completes boulēsis rather than being a fourth coordinate type of orexis) that is important enough — and was contested enough in the methodology development — to need slightly more than a parenthetical. The point is that ringing the bell is not a bouletic act sourced in a goal, but a prohairetic act completing an epithymetic impulse by a deliberative path. The distinction between epithymia and boulēsis and their relation to prohairesis deserves a sentence in the main text.

## V. RDR2 TUTORIAL DRAFT

### A. Strengths

- **The dual hexis thesis** is clearly stated in the opening frame and consistently developed across nine sections.
- **The three-pitch orekton arc** (blizzard-survival → standing/belonging → outlaw economy) gives the chapter structural clarity and momentum.
- **The honor-machine as Burkean motive-attribution machine** is the chapter's most original analytical contribution and the Town section's development of it (the five-event sequence with Arthur's own disclaimers) is excellent.
- **The bell-toll diagnostic** in the Colter Corridor section (choke/spare = machine-inert, only the bell remains) is analytically sharp and a strong contrast with the prisoner-release event three minutes earlier.
- **The Legendary Hunt section** is the best-written section: the silence-as-diagnostic, the three stacked phantasia-prostheses (Eagle Eye / legendary aura / Dead Eye), the bear charge as the purest flinch specimen. It earns its position as the technē-hexis apex.
- **The Camp section** brings in second-form boredom (Heidegger FCM) exactly where it belongs: dwelling without desire-toward, hexis-by-repetition, the greeting-by-name as agent-attribution.

### B. Weaknesses and Gaps

**B1. The chapter is heavier on description than argument relative to the G&G chapter.** The G&G chapter's three-section tutorial makes a compressed but tight argument: discovery (The Candle), dwelling (The Forest), actualization (The Fruit). Each section earns its claim. The RDR2 tutorial chapter's nine sections often describe the beat, then label the RODA locus, without always showing why that labelling follows. The Colter Corridor section in particular catalogues a great many mechanics without the argumentative tightening that the same density of material receives in The Fruit. The G&G chapter demonstrates; the RDR2 chapter sometimes only names.

**Recommended fix:** In each section, identify the one analytical claim the section is making — not just what RODA labels apply, but what the beats prove about the dual hexis thesis or the 2D-pole thesis — and make sure the section builds to that claim explicitly.

**B2. "Clip 3a" is introduced without preparation and the transition is jarring.** "Against this entire sequence, the choke/spare fork (from the interleaved clip 3a) stands as the tutorial's diagnostic zero." This is the first time the reader encounters "clip 3a" or the concept of an "interleaved clip." The reader has been tracking clips 1, 2, 3, 4 chronologically; suddenly a "3a" appears embedded in the Colter Corridor discussion. The structural relationship between clips 3 and 3a — that clip 3a is the choke/spare fork that occurs within the same in-game session as clip 3 but was analyzed as a separate beatmap — needs one preparatory sentence at the beginning of the Colter Corridor section.

**B3. The Burke citation in the Heist section is wrong.** `(Burke, *Grammar*, 21)` should be `(Burke, *Rhetoric*, 21)`. "Substance… was an act; and a way of life is an acting-together" is from the *Rhetoric of Motives*, page 21, not the *Grammar of Motives*. This is confirmed by the methodology DRAFT-v3, which correctly cites the same passage as `(Burke, *Rhetoric*, 21)`. This is an error that will be noticed by anyone familiar with Burke.

**B4. The Drunk Night section lacks an evidential caveat.** The beatmap for clip 12 is flagged as "100% whisper hallucination — ZERO coherent English dialogue" and rates itself as carrying "lower evidential weight than transcript-anchored siblings." The draft prose reads as if the mechanics are established fact. For academic integrity, the section needs a sentence acknowledging that clip 12's beats are inferred pending frame verification: the drunk-cam mechanic and the brawl arc are analytically hypothesized from the clip's title and role, not transcript-grounded.

**B5. The closing frame is thinner than the G&G chapter's Carry-Over.** "The Disposition Built" is about 350 words and stops at "What it built was the world that will break." The G&G chapter's Carry-Over section runs nearly 600 words and performs three distinct argumentative moves: (a) names the unconcealing that rupture produces; (b) gives the 15-minute recalibration datum as evidence; (c) elevates to the principle (designing worlds is designing dispositions). The RDR2 tutorial's closing needs to perform equivalent work: (a) state what the dual hexis bundle amounts to as a prepared agent; (b) connect back to the VR/2D thesis (what the tutorial's specific route to incorporation — high phantasia-load, prostheses, flinch — means for the disposition deposited); (c) point forward to the disruption with more than the final sentence.

**B6. The Camp section is too short for its conceptual load.** The camp-dwelling material (~450 words) is demonstrating the distinction between goal-oriented play and dwelling — one of the deepest insights in the whole dissertation (dwelling without an active orekton, hexis deposited by sheer repetition). The second-form boredom observation is correct and important. But the section needs slightly more development: the phenomenological distinction between camp-dwelling (a mode of being-in-the-world) and the ludic channel being at near-zero should be explicitly connected to Calleja's channels (spatial + kinesthetic + affective dominant; ludic near-absent). This is where Calleja's apparatus earns its place most clearly in the RDR2 analysis.

**B7. The Town section's five-event structure needs explicit organization.** Five consecutive honor-positive events are described in sequence (horse/stranger, Tilly, Karen, Jimmy, returned horse), but the reader may lose the cumulative structure. The section's analytical claim is twofold: (a) the machine scores acts it can attribute to the agent, and (b) the character twice disclaims the virtue attributed to him. These two claims run throughout the five events but aren't announced as what the section is arguing. An orienting sentence at the section's opening ("Within thirty minutes the player is handed five consecutive honor-positive events and, in the space between them, Arthur twice disclaims the virtue the machine just credited him") is already there — but the subsequent events should be organized more explicitly around these two claims rather than described in sequence.

**B8. The Hunt section's "Charles narrates aloud" formulation risks misunderstanding.** "The chain begins before any animal appears, at the horizon. 'A few cans of food and a rabbit… for 10, 12 people?'" — and so on, with Charles narrating each stage. The implication seems to be that the RODA apparatus is only visible here because a character verbalizes it. But the method's claim is that the A₀→A₄ chain runs whether or not anyone narrates it — the narration is just a diagnostic convenience. The section should make this explicit: "the clip is analytically useful not because the chain requires narration but because Charles verbalizes each stage, making the apparatus's operation unusually legible."

## VI. CROSS-CUTTING STRUCTURAL ISSUES

### 1. The VR/2D differential is promised but deferred

Both analysis chapters contain systematic VR-2D comparison notes at nearly every beat. The Introduction promises a differential analysis that "closes these readings." The Differential draft (already existing) delivers this. But neither analysis chapter pays off the comparison on its own terms — they both remain in the state of building-toward rather than arriving. This creates a cumulative feeling of incompleteness in each chapter. The fix is not to fold the Differential into the chapters but to give each chapter a single closing sentence that names its contribution to the differential: G&G = the VR pole achieves world-disclosedness fully but co-disclosedness only in preview; RDR2 = the 2D pole achieves both, but via the high-phantasia-load route, and the prostheses (Eagle Eye, Dead Eye, drunk-cam) are the 2D-specific instruments of compensation.

### 2. Citation style normalization is not complete

The five sections use at least three different citation styles:
- Metaphysics: LaTeX `\cite{chalmers2017}` and `\cite{aristotle2014soul}`
- Methodology: `(Burke, *Rhetoric*, 21)`, `(Calleja, *In-Game*, 169)`
- Analysis Introduction: mixed formats
- G&G and RDR2: `(Author, *ShortTitle*, Page)` throughout

A final citation pass is needed across all sections before submission. The target convention appears to be `(Author, *ShortTitle*, Page)` for inline citations in the analysis chapters. Dialogue citations are already consistent: `(clip N, MM:SS)`.

### 3. Voice variation between G&G and RDR2 chapters

The G&G chapter holds the "figured-but-plain" register consistently — apparatus implied from scene-reading, named only after the phenomenology earns it. The RDR2 tutorial chapter is more variable: the Legendary Hunt and Town sections achieve the same register; the Colter Corridor and Heist sections are more expository, leaning on RODA labelling as a substitute for argumentative movement. Before the disruption chapter is drafted, it would be worth re-reading both analysis chapters back-to-back to calibrate the register.

### 4. The Uexküll "soap bubble" repetition

Appears verbatim in both the Analysis Introduction (Movement 1) and the G&G chapter's Forest section. One must be revised. Recommendation: keep in the Introduction (it's building the ecology concept), revise in the Forest section to describe the enclosure-structure without the Uexküll metaphor directly.

### 5. Structural asymmetry between the two analysis cases

G&G has three tutorial sections and three disruption sections — balanced. RDR2 has nine tutorial sections and no disruption sections yet (§2.2 is blocked pending Sonny-scene footage). Part II is currently heavily weighted toward RDR2's tutorial and may feel lopsided. This is not fixable until the footage arrives, but the RDR2 tutorial chapter's closing frame should explicitly signal that the disruption is coming and what it will test: the dual hexis bundle is about to meet the moment it was built for.

### 6. The Metaphysics' detailed virtual/digital distinction is not used in the analysis

The Chalmers critique, the digital-blueness / virtual-blueness distinction, the Kim explanatory-exclusion argument — none of these appear in the analysis. This is a significant investment in philosophical groundwork that the dissertation doesn't cash. Either (a) the analysis chapters should invoke the virtual/digital distinction somewhere (e.g., when the game crashes or the controller disconnects, one event happens to the digital object and another to the virtual object the player was inhabiting), or (b) the Metaphysics section should be trimmed to the minimum needed to establish the real-in-kind claim and the data-discrepancy axis.

### 7. Burke appears in the Methodology and Analysis but not in the Metaphysics

This is correct — Burke is a rhetorical analyst, not a metaphysician, and his motive-grammar should not appear in the ontological section. But the gap means the transition from Metaphysics to Methodology may feel like a discipline shift without a bridge. A single sentence in the Methodology's opening paragraph naming the turn from ontology to rhetoric-and-method would smooth this.

## VIII. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

The five sections form a coherent and philosophically serious project. The strongest single document is the G&G chapter, which demonstrates both the analytical method and the dissertation's voice at their best. The RDR2 tutorial chapter is analytically richer in its content but slightly looser in its argumentative discipline — it has more material than the G&G chapter and needs its own Fruit section equivalent: one place where the apparatus is made overt and the whole chain is walked through explicitly. The methodology is rigorous and philosophically honest about what is and isn't the dissertation's own contribution. The metaphysics is over-invested in the Chalmers rebuttal relative to what the analysis uses, and it has one significant structural problem (the HMD restriction) that must be addressed before the section is sound. The Introduction is the weakest section: Movement 2 needs expansion, Movement 3's connection to the analysis chapters needs a bridge sentence, and four verbatim citations are unverified.

The most significant cross-cutting gap is the absence of *bia* and the hexis type-distinction from the methodology — two analytical tools used throughout the analysis that have no methodological home. Fixing this is the single highest-impact edit available to the whole project.

The dissertation's central argument — that designed virtual environments are rhetorical ecologies capable of reshaping the standing disposition of those who dwell in them, and that the medium's data-discrepancy sets the route to that reshaping without setting a ceiling on its depth — is sound, supported, and original. What the repairs above would do is bring the evidential and methodological scaffolding to the level the argument deserves.
