# Tracing the Reshaping: A Method for Reading Virtual Worlds

## Rhetorical-Ontological Diachronic Analysis: The Mechanism of a Reshaping

The metaphysics section closed a question and opened another. It discharged what I will call the reality-burden: it argued that virtual and phantasmic things are real, that they are distinct in kind from the ordinary furniture of the world and from each other, and that each is a potency rendered into act — a *dynamis* (a being-able, *Met.* Θ.1 1046a11) drawn over into *energeia* (a being-at-work, *Met.* Θ.6 1048a25). That much is settled. What it set aside, and set aside deliberately, is the question that follows once the worlds are granted their reality: not *whether* a rendered world is real, but *how* such a world comes to work upon, and to reshape, the one who takes it up.[^1] The burden was ontological. The deferred question is operational. This section answers it with a method.

I call the method Rhetorical-Ontological Diachronic Analysis. It is diachronic because it does not freeze play into a single arrested moment and read off its structure; it follows a stretch of play across its motions, in the order they actually run. It is ontological because what it follows is not a sequence of behaviors but a single change in being. And it is rhetorical because the change it follows is, at its center, an act of one thing being taken up into another — an incorporation that the older art of rhetoric would have recognized at once.

The method reads a stretch of play as the actualization-of-desire chain. It does not read play as the unfolding of a desire already finished and waiting; rather it reads desire as something brought to completion across the motions of engagement, motion by motion, until it issues in an act. A designed world drives that actualization forward. It supplies the horizon, the image, the occasion; it leans the whole chain toward its end. And the end toward which it leans is not mere proximity, not the player standing beside a world and looking on, but a *substantial union* — what I will call rhetorical incorporation: the convergence in which player and world become, for the duration of play, one in substrate while remaining different in being.[^2] This is the mechanism. The reading I develop traces it as it runs and shows where, precisely, the union is forged.

That union does not pass without remainder. It sediments. What is forged across the motions of play does not evaporate when the controller is set down; it settles into the player's *hexis* (a settled bearing or disposition, *NE* II.1 1103a17) — a reshaping carried out of the world and into the one who engaged it. Here the division of labor is exact. The metaphysics carried the reality-burden; the method carries the mechanism. The one says what these worlds *are*; the other says what they *do* to the one who enters them.

## The Producing Art and the Using Art: What the World Supplies and What the Player Does

If the method is to trace one change across two agents — the maker who builds and the player who plays — it must first show that the two are joined closely enough for a single analysis to read across the seam between them. I draw from the inherited ontology two features, and both bear directly on this.

The first concerns the unity of the change. On the account inherited from Part I, the four causes are not four parcels to be distributed but the four aspects under which one and the same change is intelligible — material, formal, efficient, final, all answering for a single becoming. This raises an obvious difficulty for any analysis of play, for here there appear to be two agents and two acts: a designer who builds and a player who plays, separated by months and machines. If the causes cannot be split between two agents, and play plainly has two agents, then either the four-cause frame fails for play or the two agents are joined more intimately than they look. They are. Aristotle marks the joint at *Phys.* II.2 194a36–b8, where he distinguishes the producing art from the using art and binds them: the art that makes a thing and the art that uses it are two, yet the made thing is one — what the producing art turns out as its *telos* (end, 194b1) becomes, without remainder, the very means the using art takes up.[^3] By "becomes the means" I mean only this: the finished product passes over, intact, into the materials and form with which the second act works. The designer's *telos* is the player's given. So the world is not split off from the play but folded into it as its standing condition — what the producing art completes, the using art begins from. Designing and playing remain two acts. The change they jointly compose is one.

From this the division of causal labor follows. The world supplies what the player cannot supply and could not make alone: the material it is built from and the formal order it is built into — the *Umwelt* (surrounding-world) already shaped, the affordances already laid down, the *telos* of the producing art deposited as a usable thing. The player supplies the rest. The player is the efficient cause, the source from which the actual playing issues; the player is the final cause, the one for the sake of whom — and toward whose *orekton* (object of desire, 194b9 nearby) — the using act moves. The world gives the formal and material; the player works the efficient and final. Two arts, one artifact; two causal contributions, one actualization.

The second feature names the union itself, and it is here that I will lean hardest in what follows. The relation between the player and the engaged world is not contact between two separate things, nor the absorption of one into the other, but a structure Aristotle states for perceiver and perceived and that the dissertation extends to this larger union: they are "one in substrate, different in being" (*DA* III.2 425b26–27).[^4] One *hypokeimenon* (substrate, 425b27) underlies the act; in being — in what it is to be the world, and what it is to be the player — they remain two. This is the formula the method inherits. It lets the analysis read the play and the world as a single actualization without collapsing them into one kind, and it is the lock against the mistake the movements that follow must keep guarding off: that an utter joining of player and world would make the virtual something other than virtual. One in substrate, different in being — joined where they act, distinct in what they are.

## Rhetorical Incorporation: The Substantial Union of Player and World

With the two arts bound on one product and the union-formula in hand, the method can now run its trace. The actualization chain has a direction. It runs forward, motion by motion; it is understood backward, from the *orekton* that draws the whole and lends each prior motion its sense. To trace the chain is to watch a designed world recruit the player's faculties one after another toward an end that was, all along, what governed the recruiting.[^5]

It begins before the player does anything. A₀ is the ontological horizon: a sensible object and the sense-faculty given together within motion and time — the designed *Umwelt* into which the player is already thrown. Nothing has yet been done; everything stands ready. Then the perceptual motion runs, M₀→A₁, and deposits at A₁ a completed perception whose remainder is dual, the *resonant kinēsis* (the lingering motion): a *resonant aisthēma*, the persisting eidetic trace, and a *resonant epithymia*, the persisting hedonic-orectic tone. The world is now not merely seen but carried — carried as image-to-be and as want-to-be. From that lingering motion the phantastic motion generates, at M₁→A₂, the *phantasma* proper: the determinate, available, charged image, the pivot on which every later operation works. A₃ completes the cognitive motion. Here the *phantasma* is engaged by the three orientational modes — intellection, memory, and the discursive or deliberative — and is taken up at once by the committal *doxa* (the holding-as-true) and by the concretized emotion. Perception gathered; *phantasia* held; cognition judged. The world has been let all the way in.

What happens next is the spine. At the A₃→A₄ transition the *orektikon* motion completes into action — *praxis* or *poiēsis*, doing or making — and in completing it the chain does not merely terminate but fuses. This is the event the reading I develop calls rhetorical incorporation: a substantial union of player and world, one in substrate, different in being. The player and the world are, in the act, one in substrate — there is a single ongoing actualization, not two parallel ones — yet different in being, since the player remains the player and the world remains the world. They are correlative poles of a single actualization, and the union holds them as such.

The union has two correlative poles, and the central claim is that these are not two unions but one held under two descriptions. There is the world-pole — incorporation, the absorption of the player into the equipmental world and of that world into the player. And there is the with-others pole — consubstantiation, the binding of the player into a "we," a being-with-others folded into the affective architecture of bodily-being-in-the-world-with-others. I will argue that the act at A₃→A₄ realizes both at once: to be taken into a world is, in the same stroke, to be taken into the company that world is shared with. This fusion — both poles held as one event, grounded in the actualization of desire — is the dissertation's own. Neither source carries both poles; the reading that joins them is mine, and the movements that follow earn each pole in turn.

One guard. The union is an experiential and ontological convergence in act, not a change of kind. The player and the world converge — they do not exchange natures. A wholly incorporated player has not thereby made the virtual non-virtual; the prior section discharged the reality-burden, and that result is not undone here. Incorporation is the deepest entry the act allows, not a passage across the boundary the metaphysics drew.[^6] The fusion is total in intensity and bounded in kind. And the act, once complete, does not vanish: it sediments into the player's *hexis*, the cross-pass build-up that is temporal sedimentation, not residue — the slow becoming-someone that repeated incorporations lay down.

## Incorporation, Re-Grounded: Calleja and the Channels of Involvement

The chain, read forward from A₀ to A₄, establishes that a designed world works upon the player's perception, cognition, and desire. It establishes that and little else about the where. The chain tells us *that* engagement happens; it does not tell us through which seams of the world it enters, and it offers no test by which we might say of a given stretch of play that the player has, in fact, been taken into the world rather than left merely watching it. I will argue that Brian Calleja's Player Involvement Model supplies both at once — a vocabulary of channels and a criterion of union — and that it does so in terms which, with one declared transformation, fit the A₃→A₄ event exactly. The argument proceeds by sublation in three beats: what in Calleja's incorporation must be preserved, what in it must be transformed, and what it must be elevated into.[^7]

Preserve, first, incorporation itself as the world-pole of the union. Calleja's own definition is the thing to keep: incorporation is "the absorption of a virtual environment into consciousness, yielding a sense of habitation, which is supported by the systemically upheld embodiment of the player in a single location, as represented by the avatar" (Calleja, *In-Game*, 169). The structure here is doubled, and the doubling is what makes it serviceable. Incorporation "operates on a double axis: the player incorporates (in the sense of internalizing or assimilating) the game environment into consciousness while simultaneously being incorporated through the avatar into that environment" — and Calleja insists that "the simultaneous occurrence of these two processes is a necessary condition for the experience of incorporation" (169). World taken into consciousness; consciousness set down into world: not two experiences but one, and one only when both run at once. Preserve, with the definition, the six dimensions of the model as the designed channels through which a world can reach into the player at all — the kinesthetic, the spatial, the shared, the narrative, the affective, and the ludic.[^8] These are not zones of the psyche but seams cut into the artifact by the producing art; they are *where* the engagement the chain merely posited gets in. And two of them carry the rest: Calleja holds that "two particular dimensions—spatial and kinesthetic—form the cornerstone of the incorporation experience. Without them, incorporation cannot take place" (169–170).

Transform, second, what incorporation *is*. Calleja means by it a psychological intensification — the deepening of involvement past a threshold, ratified by a cognitive science whose terms are not phenomenological but constructive: he grounds the model in "their experientialist ontology" and in "a view of consciousness as an internally generated construct (Dennett, 1991; Damasio, 2000; Lakoff and Johnson, 2003)" (168, 169). The reading I develop takes the same event and reads it ontologically: not a stronger feeling of being-there but an instance of the union the chain delivers at A₃→A₄. The bridge I cross to do this is the dissertation's own, not Calleja's. I tie incorporation to the concernful absorption already established in Part I — to "the characteristic absorption of concern in its equipmental world" and to the circumspective concern in which equipment withdraws, the hammer disappearing "into the hammering" (BT 405, 98). Habitation is what absorption looks like when the world that absorbs is a designed one. Let me be plain about the warrant and the liability together. Heidegger is absent from Calleja's chapter; the re-grounding is my construction, and I claim it not as Calleja's view but as the supply for a lack in his. The lack is one Calleja half-confesses: incorporation is, by his own word, "ultimately a metaphor like presence or immersion" (5). A metaphor is a reaching — and what it reaches for is precisely the vocabulary of inhabited world that his experientialist ground (168) cannot itself underwrite. The gap between the ground he stands on and the phenomenological language he keeps borrowing is the opening my transformation fills.

Elevate, third, incorporation into the world-pole of a union whose other pole the channels themselves anticipate — for one of the six, the shared dimension, already names involvement with one's fellows, and so points past the world toward being-with-others. What raises the diagnostic from impression to test are the seven criteria the model yields, R1 through R7, which I take as the union-test for the world-pole: a stretch of play has reached incorporation only when assimilation, embodiment, and above all their *simultaneity* hold, with the spatial-kinesthetic cornerstone in place, the axes blended, and the temporal and intrusive conditions met.[^9] R3, simultaneity, is the strictest gatekeeper, and it is Calleja's own: no double axis, no incorporation.

Why Calleja, and not another theorist of the absorbing world? Not because the rivals are weaker, but because Calleja has already done, inside his own model, the corrective work that a chain-based reading requires; rather than import a critique from outside, I route it through his text. He excises the magic circle — refuses the bounded space in which play is sealed off from the world — and the chain needs exactly this refusal, since A₀ is a horizon the player is *thrown* into, not a ring stepped across. And he corrects the older metaphors, immersion and presence, which figured the relation as a one-way flooding of the subject by the world; his double axis restores the missing direction, the player's reaching back. A union has two poles. Only a model that already runs in both directions can be lifted into one.

## Consubstantiation and the Grammar of Motives: Burke at the Other Pole

Calleja carries the player into the world and the world into the player. He does not carry the player toward anyone else. Two things go unaccounted for, and neither is a residue in the sense this study reserves for the *resonant kinēsis*. The first is a bond that is itself a source: a consubstantial "we," several agents made substantially one, from which an act can issue as though from a single locus. The second is the converse of sourcing — a grammar by which a finished act is *given* its motive, and by which the true locus of that motive can be quietly displaced. I will argue that Kenneth Burke supplies both, and that he supplies them at exactly the pole Calleja cannot reach.

Consubstantiation I preserve as Burke states it. To be identified with another is to be "substantially one" with "a person other than himself," and yet to remain "an individual locus of motives"; one is thereby "both joined and separate."[^10] The bond is not sentimental but practical, for "substance," Burke writes, "was an act; and a way of life is an acting-together." And it arises against a lack: "Identification is affirmed with earnestness precisely because there is division. Identification is compensatory to division." So much I keep. What I transform is the engine. Burke drives identification by symbolic persuasion — by the rhetorician's address. The reading I develop replaces that engine with the one already running in the chain. The "we" is not talked into being; it is *desired* into being, ratified at A₃, where the *phantasma* is taken-as-true under a committal *doxa* and the emotion is concretized, so that the player's fellows enter the actualization not as an audience persuaded but as a co-source already folded into the orectic motion that completes at A₄. What persuasion explains externally, desire grounds internally. This is the elevation: consubstantiation, so re-sourced, *is* the with-others pole — the with-others pole that Part I had folded into the affective architecture of bodily-being-in-the-world-with-others, now given its grammar.

The bridge from the chain's seven motive-springs to Burke is Burke's own. He "refers human actions to seven causes (*aitia*): chance, nature, compulsion, habit, reason, anger, and desire."[^11] That single sentence licenses the whole crosswalk: it lets *Rhetorica* I.10 (1369a–b) be read not as a doctrine of springs of conduct but as *categories of motive*, the kinds under which an act may be filed. I must be exact about the provenance. Burke's explicit map of Aristotle onto his own apparatus runs through the *four* causes, not the seven; the seven-causes-into-pentad reading is this dissertation's construction, built on Burke's invocation of the seven but not found ready-made in him.

The pentad is that apparatus. Its five terms — act, scene, agent, agency, purpose — answer "what was done... when or where it was done... who did it... how he did it... and why."[^12] What matters for method is the register. The pentad is not a theory of why people act; it is a grammar of how an act is *assigned* a why — a scheme of motive-attribution. When a stretch of play is filed under the pentad, what gets traced is the account a finished act invites, the locus to which it would refer itself. And here the second unaccounted thing finds its instrument. Burke's concealment diagnostic is narrow, and I keep it narrow. "In banishing the term," he writes, "far from banishing its functions one merely conceals them"; "we may detect its covert influence even in cases where it is overtly absent."[^13] The claim is not that every account hides its ground — a reading that would make the diagnostic say everything and so nothing. The claim is the precise one: where a motive-locus has been displaced, struck from the overt account yet still doing its work, the displacement leaves a detectable trace. The diagnostic catches a *covertly imported* source, not a universal mauvaise foi.

Two methods, then, built on different grounds and reaching, blind to each other, the same structure. Calleja, grounding incorporation in an experientialist ontology, arrives at a with-others channel — the shared dimension, cohabitation and cooperation and competition — as one engagement among six. Burke, grounding identification in symbolic action, arrives at the consubstantial "we" as the very basis of an acting-together. Neither read the other on this point; neither needed to. That two analyses converge upon a single with-others pole — one from cognitive science, one from rhetoric — is not coincidence but corroboration: the convergence is the evidence that the pole is no artifact of either vocabulary but a structure both vocabularies were tracking. Not Burke against Calleja, nor Calleja absorbing Burke; rather two instruments, calibrated apart, returning one reading.
