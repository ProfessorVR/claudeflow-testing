## 3 — The Two Routes: What the Pairing Isolates

Set side by side, *Gnomes & Goblins* and *Red Dead Redemption 2* are not two demonstrations of one thing but a controlled variation: the medium changed, the rest held as steady as two such worlds allow. Each incorporates the player; each, by its rupture, can carry that incorporation past the world's edge. What differs is the *route*, and the route is set by a single variable — the gap between the sensory data a medium supplies and the full field of the world it renders, the data-discrepancy, and the labor of image-making (*phantasia*, φαντασία) that gap demands.

The difference shows along four seams. At the horizon, the virtual world *supplants*: the headset occupies the sensory channels, and the surrounding room is gone. The screen can only *subtract*: the room stays present and the bezel stays in view, and it is phantasia that must withdraw them from notice — the same enclosure reached by withdrawal where the headset reached it by occupation. At the body, the virtual world *supplies*: room-scale tracking renders a real step as a step among the trees and a real reach as a reach, and the hands in the forest are the player's own. The screen *conscripts*: a stick stands for a stride and a button for a punch, and the felt body of all that motion is supplied by phantasia — its surest index the involuntary flinch, the real body ducking a flat image. And so the third seam, phantasia's load, runs inverse to the data: low in the headset, high on the screen, and sharpest of all where the screen's data is thinnest — the whiteout in which terrain, depth, and cold are supplied entirely from the backlog of prior perception.

The fourth seam is the rupture itself. The virtual world breaks from *within* — the miniaturization re-scales the surround around a player who goes on standing in it, the disclosure undergone from inside a dwelling it does not end. The screen, holding the player always a little outside the image, breaks otherwise: through the image that floods or corrupts, and through the cutscene that strips agency and makes the player patient to a scene — a rupture worked not on the player's scale but on the player's standing. Each route can reach the ontological end; what changes is how the breaking is delivered.

From all of which the comparison yields its one real lesson, and it is not that either medium is the stronger. Incorporation, and the ontological change it can carry, is reachable from either pole; the data-discrepancy sets the route, not a ceiling, because phantasia scales to the gap — supplying more where the medium supplies less. The pairing earns its place precisely by varying the gap and letting phantasia's labor be watched varying with it: it is, in the end, a study not of two games but of phantasia, of how much of any world is always the perceiver's own supply.

One result drives the point home, because it cuts the other way. Of the two unions an incorporation can reach — the world-disclosedness of dwelling in a place and the co-disclosedness of being-with others — the virtual world reached only the first in full, holding the second at its threshold: the goblins were disclosed and looked, but acting-together was designed out, the consubstantial "we" withheld. The flat screen reached the union the headset withheld. *Red Dead Redemption 2* admits the player to acts genuinely sourced by a collective — the heist worked to assigned roles, the raid fought alongside the posse, the rescue ridden three abreast — the "we" consummated where the virtual world showed only its edge. The deeper of the two unions was reached not in the headset but on the screen. It is a contingency of design and not of the medium — the virtual case is a preview that withholds on purpose — but that is just the moral: the medium sets the route, and neither pole holds a monopoly on the end.
