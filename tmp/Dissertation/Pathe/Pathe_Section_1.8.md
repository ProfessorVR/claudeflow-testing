\subsection{Emotion's Function in the Chain}

What the chain requires at $M_3 \rightarrow M_4$, under evaluatively complex conditions, is the conversion of \textit{orexis} into a determinate form of desire directed at a specific object under a specific evaluative description. Emotion is precisely this conversion. Without it, the chain would have a generic moved mover (desire, set in motion by the apparent good) but no mechanism to specify \textit{which} form of desire corresponds to \textit{which} disclosure of the apparent good. The desire to flee is not the desire to retaliate; the desire to relieve is not the desire to conceal. Each determinate conative orientation is individuated by the cognitive structure of the emotion that specifies it, and each draws upon a determinate pattern of somatic preparation---the cooling of fear, the heating of anger, the sympathetic pain of pity---that configures the body for the action the emotion calls for.

Emotion, then, does not precede desire as a separate stage within the chain; emotion is the form in which desire is actualized when \textit{doxa} has disclosed evaluatively complex content. Fear is not something that happens before the desire to flee; fear \textit{is} the desire to flee insofar as it is structured by the cognitive evaluation of an anticipated destructive evil and experienced in the somatic alteration of chilled blood. Anger is not something that happens before the desire to retaliate; anger \textit{is} the desire to retaliate insofar as it is structured by the perception of an unjustified slight and experienced in the somatic alteration of heated blood. The \textit{Rhetoric}'s definitions confirm this composite structure: each emotion is defined as a desire of a certain kind, attended by pain or pleasure, directed at a certain kind of object, under a certain evaluative description. The three-factor schema of \textit{DA} III.10 is preserved---\textit{orexis} remains the moved mover, the realizable good remains the unmoved originator, the animal via its bodily instrument remains that which is moved---but the moved mover acquires, in the evaluatively complex case, an internal complexity that the schema's original formulation leaves unspecified. Emotion names this internal complexity. It is the actualization of the \textit{orektikon} capacity into a specific form of desire directed at a specific object under a specific evaluative description, the form in which the soul's being-moved becomes the body's being-moved, and the mechanism through which the world's disclosure as mattering in a certain way becomes the animal's motion in the corresponding direction.
