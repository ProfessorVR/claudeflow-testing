\subsection{The Feedback Loop: Synchronic Chain, Diachronic Saturation}

However, this structure alone does not fully encompass the role emotion plays in animal movement as Aristotle presents it. The \textit{Rhetoric}'s definition of emotion as ``judgement modifying'' entails a feedback loop: the emotion produced by \textit{doxa} alters the conditions under which subsequent \textit{doxai} are formed. Aristotle specifies this mechanism in \textit{De Insomniis}: when the soul is in the grip of emotion, the ``controlling sense''---the corrective faculty that normally intercepts misleading \textit{phantasmata} before they can generate \textit{doxa}---is ``disabled'' (460b3-16).\footnote{``we are easily deceived respecting the operations of sense-perception when we are excited by emotions, and different persons according to their different emotions; for example, the coward when excited by fear, the amorous person by the object of his desire; so that, with but little resemblance to go upon, the former thinks he sees his foes approaching, the latter, that he sees the object of his desire; and the more deeply one is under the influence of the emotion, the less similarity is required to give rise to these impressions. Thus, too, both in fits of anger, and also in all states of appetite, all men become easily deceived, and more so the more their emotions are excited.... The cause of these occurrences is that the faculty in virtue of which the controlling sense judges is not identical with that in virtue of which images come before the mind. A proof of this is, that the sun presents itself as only a foot in diameter, though often something else gainsays the imagination'' (460b3-20).} \textit{Phantasmata} that would, under normal conditions, be corrected by the rational faculty are instead \textit{taken-as} true, generating further \textit{doxai} and thereby further emotions. The result is a self-reinforcing loop: an initial \textit{doxa} produces an initial emotion; the emotion impairs the corrective faculty; subsequent \textit{phantasmata} pass through the gat into \textit{doxa} unopposed; the new \textit{doxai} produce further emotions; and so on. This recursive structure explains a range of phenomena that Aristotle recognizes: the escalation of anger through sustained rumination on the perceived slight; the intensification of fear through repeated imagining of the anticipated evil; the susceptibility of the dream, the ill, and the passionately agitated to \textit{phantasmatic} deception (\hl{(CITE)}\footnote{}. It also explains the rhetor's strategy in \textit{Rhetoric}: by first establishing an emotional attunement in the audience, the rhetor weakens their corrective faculties and thereby makes them more susceptible to the \textit{phantasmata} that vivid language (\textit{energeia}) presents to their imaginations. The loop is not a defect of the system but an intrinsic feature of the relationship between emotion and \textit{doxa} in rational animals. 

The \textit{Rhetoric}'s definition of emotion as judgment-affecting (\textit{Rhetoric} II.1, 1378a20--22) entails a structural feature that the linear chain cannot fully capture: a feedback loop through which emotion, once produced, alters the conditions under which subsequent \textit{doxai} are formed. Aristotle specifies the mechanism in the \textit{De Insomniis}. ``We are easily deceived respecting the operations of sense-perception when we are excited by emotions,'' he observes, ``and different persons according to their different emotions; for example, the coward when excited by fear, the amorous person by the object of his desire; so that, with but little resemblance to go upon, the former thinks he sees his foes approaching, the latter, that he sees the object of his desire; and the more deeply one is under the influence of the emotion, the less similarity is required to give rise to these impressions'' (\textit{De Insomniis} 460b3--11). The controlling sense---the corrective faculty that in ordinary waking cognition intercepts misleading \textit{phantasmata} before they can generate \textit{doxa}---is impaired under the sway of active emotion, and \textit{phantasmata} that would normally be corrected are instead taken as true, generating further \textit{doxai} and thereby further emotions.

The result is a self-reinforcing loop. An initial \textit{doxa} produces an initial emotion; the emotion impairs the corrective faculty; subsequent \textit{phantasmata} pass through the gate into \textit{doxa} unopposed; the new \textit{doxai} produce further emotions; and the process continues until some external intervention, some exhaustion of the somatic preparation, or some recovery of the corrective faculty breaks the recursion. The structure explains a range of phenomena that Aristotle recognizes: the escalation of anger through sustained rumination on the perceived slight; the intensification of fear through repeated imagining of the anticipated evil; the susceptibility of the dreamer, the ill, and the passionately agitated to phantasmatic deception (\textit{De Insomniis} 460b3--16; 461b3--8). It also explains the rhetor's strategy in the \textit{Rhetoric}: by first establishing an emotional attunement in the audience---pity for the client, indignation toward the opponent, fear of the projected consequence---the rhetor weakens the audience's corrective faculties and thereby makes them more susceptible to the \textit{phantasmata} that vivid language (\gk{ἐνέργεια}) presents to their imaginations. The loop is not a defect of the psychological system but an intrinsic feature of the relationship between emotion and \textit{doxa} in rational animals.

It should be noted that the feedback loop, as developed here, is an interpretive reconstruction rather than a doctrine Aristotle states in these terms. The \textit{De Anima} passage at 427b21--24 establishes that \textit{doxa} produces emotion immediately; the \textit{De Insomniis} passage at 460b3--16 establishes that emotional states permit \textit{phantasmata} to be taken as true when the controlling faculty is impaired; the \textit{Rhetoric} passage at 1378a20--22 establishes that emotions modify \textit{kriseis}. That these passages describe aspects of a single recursive mechanism is a plausible but not indisputable interpretive claim; each passage addresses, strictly speaking, a different phenomenon. The synthesis is, however, philosophically productive, and the linear $A_0 \rightarrow A_4$ notation of the chain cannot fully represent it. A complete topological representation would require a return path from the emotion node at $A_3$ back to the gatekeeping condition within $M_2 \rightarrow M_3$.

Two registers are at work here, and they must be distinguished. The synchronic register concerns the structural transitions at any single moment of \textit{pathos}-actualization: basic valence grounds \textit{doxa}, \textit{doxa} concretizes the higher-order \textit{pathē}, the higher-order \textit{pathē} mobilize action. The diachronic register concerns how prior actualizations modulate subsequent actualizations: a \textit{pathos}-actualization at \textit{$T_1$} persists as dispositional ground for the \textit{pathos}-actualization at $T_2$. The \textit{De Insomniis} phenomenon belongs to the diachronic register, not the synchronic register. The chain remains structurally directional from $A_0$ to $A_4$ within any single actualization; the ``loop'' is the temporal persistence of prior actualizations as ground for subsequuent actualizations, not a third synchronic transition within a given chain-pass. Two distinct mechanisms operate within this diachronic register. The first---\textit{Stimmung}-saturation---modulates the \textit{input} to \textit{doxa}: a sustained higher-order \textit{pathos} persists as a tonal coloring that biases subsequent perception. The angry person sees enemies in shadows because the anger-\textit{Stimmung} re-saturates the perceptual encounter. The second---corrective-faculty impairment, the mechanism Aristotle specifies---modulates the \textit{gating} of \textit{doxa}: emotional excitement disables the controlling sense, so that \textit{phantasmata} that would normally be intercepted pass into \textit{doxa} unopposed. The two are commonly conflated under ``feedback loo'' but operate at different points: (1`) at the basic-valence input to the chain, (2) at the corrective-gate within the chain. Both are diachronic modulations of synchronic transitions, not new synchronic transitions. 
 
This temporal-dispositional persistence is what Aristotle elsewhere analyzes under the doctrine of \gk{ἕξις} (\textit{hexis} ``settled disposition'')---repeated actualizations sediment into stable comportments that condition subsequent activity (\textit{NE} II.1, 1103a17-25)---and what Heidegger's \textit{Being and Time} names \textit{Geworfenheit} (thrownness) (section 29): Dasein is always already in a \textit{Stimmung} that conditions its disclosure, the \textit{stimmung} itself carrying forward from prior actualizations. The diachronic dimension of the present chain is, in these vocabularies, the \textit{pathos}-grade \textit{hexis}-character or \textit{pathos}-grade thrownness. 

Aristotle's examples consistently demonstrate that emotion is ``appearance-laden'' cognition. Emotion warps the field of appearance, which in turn warps judgment. \textit{Phantasia} thus conditions both the genesis of emotion and emotion’s feedback effects on cognition. 

In summary: \textit{pathē} feed back into the basic affective valence level, modulating the very disposition-ground from which subsequent \textit{doxai} will arise. This is structurally analogous to Heideggerian \textit{GeworfenheitI (``thrownness''). }

This similarity rests on a single shared phenomenon: \textit{prior dispositional actualization persists as the ground from which present disclosure takes its character}. The Aristotelian and Heideggerian articulations of this phenomenon operate at different ontological registers: Aristotle's at the level of the perception-and-affection within the soul-structure, Heidegger's at the level of Dasein's fundamental disclosedness. But they name the same structure of disposition-already-having-been-shaped. 

\textit{On Dreams} 460b3-11 locates the feedback in the perceptual-affective register: easily deceived respecting operations of sense-perception when excited by emotions and different people according to different emotions. The claim is precise: the \textit{pathos} already-actualized (the coward's settled fear) modulates the present \textit{aisthesis} so that the ambiguous perceptual situation discloses itself \textit{as} threatening. The prior \textit{pathos} is the disposition-from-which the current perceptual disclosure articulates itself. 

Heidegger's \textit{Being and Time} (29) names the corresponding existential structure \textit{Befindlichkeit} (``state-of-mind''): ``A state-of-mind discloses Dasein in its thrownness and its submission to that world which is already disclosed with its own Being'' (178). ``This characteristic of Dasein's Being---this `that it is'---is veiled in its ``whence'' and ``whither'', yet disclosed in itself all the more unveiledly; we call it the ``\textit{thrownness}'' [\textit{Geworfenheit}] of this entity into its ``there''; indeed, it is thrown in such a way that, as Being-in-the-world, it is the ``there''.'' (174). Mood is not a pirvate inner episode that follows perception; it is the \textit{disclosive medium} in which entities can show up at all as mattering, as approachable, as threatening. The decisive temporal claim: ``Understanding is grounded primarily in the future; one's \textit{state-of-mind}, however, temporalizes itself \textit{primarily} in \textit{having been}. Moods temporalize themselves---that is, their specific ecstasis belongs to a future and a Present in such a way, indeed, that these equiprimordial ecstases are modified by having been'' (390). \textit{Befindlichkeit}'s having-already-found-itself-disposed is not a past now stored in memory but a \textit{Gewesenheit} (``having-been'') that \textit{currently} conditions how the world's entities can come close. The thrown disposition is operative \textit{as} the present disclosure's condition. 

The structural identity holds because Heidegger himself identifies the \textit{pathē} with modes of \textit{Befindlichkeit}: Aristotle's \textit{Rhetoric} is ``the first systematic hermeneutic of the everydayness of Being-with-one-another'' (178), and the \textit{pathē} are \textit{Stimmungen} (moods/attunements). Whatever the temporal analysis of \textit{Befindlichkeit} yields applies \textit{eo ipso} to the \textit{pathē}. So when Heidegger grounds state-of-mind in having-been, it grounds the \textit{pathē} in having-been; and the \textit{On Dreams} feedback is precisely the Aristotelian articulation of how that having-been operates concretely: prior fear \textit{as having-been} shapes the present perceptual encounter such that the ambiguous shape discloses itself as enemy-coming-close. 

The Aristotelian regist has its own vocab for this dispositional persistence: \textit{hexis} (``settled disposition'') and Heidegger's lectures prefigures \textit{Being and Time}'s analysis, where He derives four basic aspects of being-there from \textit{hexis}, the fourth of which: ``Being-there must, \textit{for itself}, take the opportunity to cultivate this being-composed as a possibility'' (122). \textit{Hexis} can have its genesis only ``in being-there itself.'' This formulation is what will develop into the \textit{Geworfenheit} of \textit{Being and Time}.

What this analogy reveals is the \textit{temporal} dimension of the actualization chain. The feedback is not a third synchronic direction added to the two specified; it is the \textit{thrownness-character} of the chain---the way each present actualization is \textit{always already} shaped by prior actualizations sedimented as dispositional ground. The chain runs synchronically from $A_0 \to A_4$ in any given moment, but each moment's $A_0$ is itself the \textit{having-been-shaped} of prior runs. \textit{Geworfenheit} is the ontological name for this temporal structure; \textit{On Dreams} is its Aristotelian-perceptual articulation; \textit{hexis} is the Aristotelian-ethical articulation; and the feedback loop is this chapter's name for the same structure operative at the \textit{pathos}-level of the chain. 

Two directions: D.1. (Heidegger's basic level): basic \textit{pathos} \ra logos \ra \textit{doxa}. The disposition-disclosing basic valence grounds \textit{logs} and \textit{doxa}. D.2. (Aristotle's, pathe): \textit{doxa} \ra pathe. The doxastic articulation concretizes the determinate higer-order pathe. 

On Dreams feedback appears to add D.3: pathe \ra biased basic pathos \ra biased subsequent doxa \ra baised pathe

D.3. is a diachronic feature of the chain's temporality, captured under the aristotelian \textit{hexis} as settled disposition and the heideggerian thrownness. 

2 distinct phenomenon in ON Dreams: (1) Stimmung-saturation of basic valence. Pathe, once actualized, produce a sustained dispositional shading. Subsequent perception is conducted \textit{under} this Stimmung---the perceptual basic valence is biased. The coward sees enemies in shadows because the fear-Stimmung re-saturates the perceptual encounter. (2) Impairment of the corrective faculty. The controlling sense---the corrective faculty that intercepts misleading phantasmata---is disabled under emotional excitement and pass into doxa unopposed. Theese are two distinct mechanisms. (1) modulates the \textit{input} to \textit{doxa} (basic valence); (2) modulates the \textit{gating} of \textit{doxa}(corrective faculty). These two directions are synchronic: they describe the structural transitions at any given moment of \textit{pathos}-actualization. At the moment of perception with basic valence ($A_0 \to A_1$), Direction 1 holds; at the moment of \textit{doxa}-articulation ($A_2 \to A_3)$), direction 2 holds. The feedback phenomena are diachronic: they describe \textit{how prior actualizations of the chain modulate subsequent actualizations}. A prior actualization of higher-order \textit{pathe} at $T_1$ modulates the basic valence and \textit{doxa} formation at $T_2$. This is not a structural transition \textit{within} a single chain-actualization; it is a \textit{temporal-historical} modulation of one chain-actualization by a prior one. 

Mechanism (1) specifies that the basic valence at A0 is itself a function of prior actualizations: the basic valence brought to a perceptual encounter is shaped by prior dispositional history. This is a \textit{temporal modification of the input} to Direction 1. The \textit{hexis} character of \textit{pathos} is precisely the persistence of prior actualizations as dispositional ground fro subsequent encounters. Mechanism (2) specifies that \textit{the doxa-gate's content-conditionality is itself modulated by prior actualizations}. The corrective faculty is the discriminative capacity that filters which \textit{phantasmata} become\textit{doxa}; emotional excitement disables this filter. So the \textit{doxa}-gate fires more readily under sustained pathe. It is a \textit{temporal modification of the gate-conditions} of Direction 2. 

Heidegger's analysis of state-of-mind establishes precisely this temporal structure. State-of-mind has three essential characteristics: (1) it discloses Dasein in its thrownness---the `that it is and has to be' which is veiled in its `whence' and `wither'; (2) it has already disclosed Being-in-the-word as a whole, making it possible first of all to direct oneself toward something; (3) it discloses the worldhood of the world in the mode of circumspective concern. Thrownness is the structural name for the diachronic persistence of prior dispositional states. Dasein is \textit{always already} in a mood that conditions its disclosure; the mood carries forward from prior actualizations. Heidegger's lectures on \textit{hexis} prefigure this: the four basic aspects of being-there derived from \textit{hexis}---concern, particularity, orientation toward the moment, \textit{and self-cultivation through prior actualizations}. The fourth aspect---that the genesis of \textit{hexis}``can have the opportunity and type of cultivation only \textit{in being-there itself}''---is the lectures statement of the diachronic dispositional persistesnce that thrownness will name in BT. Accordingly, the feedback is not a structurally distinct third direction; it is the \textit{thrownnes}-character of \textit{pathos}, the temporal persistence of prior actualizations as mood-ground for subsequent encounters. 

``moral excellence comes about as a result of habit'' (\textit{NE} II.1, 1103a16-17). We for \textit{hexeis} from repeated actualizations: ``For the things we have to learn before we can do, we learn by doing, e.g. men become builders by building and lyre-players by playing the lyre; so too we become just by doing just acts, temperate by doing temperate acts, brave by doing brave acts'' (\textit{NE} II.1, 1103a34-b2). Each actualization of a \textit{pathos}-chain (actualization of desire) leaves a residual modification of the dispositional ground. over time, repeated actualizations consolidate into \textit{hexeis} that condition subsequent chain-actualizations. The \textit{hexis} is not a third stage of the chain; it is the \textit{temporal sedimentation} of repeated chain-actualizations into a dispositional ground.
