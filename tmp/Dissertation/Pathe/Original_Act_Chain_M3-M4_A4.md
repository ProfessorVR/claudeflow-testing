  \item $M_3 \rightarrow M_4$: \textbf{Orektikon Motion: Desire Converging
          in Action}
          \begin{itemize}
              \item Judgment and desire converge in overt action, drawing upon the
              full kinetic-affective history of the \textit{phantasma} as it has been
              elaborated through the preceding stages of the chain. Where deliberative
              \textit{phantasia} has operated, the desire is informed by foresight:
              the animal acts not merely from what is at hand but toward what has been
              projected and evaluated as the greater good. Where only sensitive
              \textit{phantasia} is at work, appetite responds to the immediately
              present \textit{phantasma} without foresight---``because of want of
              foresight into what is farther away in time'' (\textit{DA} III.10,
              433b9--10). Where doxa has operated and emotion has been produced, the
              desire is informed both by the soul's truth-commitment and by the
              specific evaluative structure of the emotion: the animal acts not merely
              from how things appear but from how they are \textit{taken to be}, and
              the form of the desire---whether it is the desire to flee (fear), to
              retaliate (anger), or to relieve (pity)---is determined by the
              cognitive evaluation that structures the emotion. The affective force
              of doxa-mediated desire is, Aristotle indicates, both immediate
              (\gk{εὐθύς}, \textit{DA} III.3, 427b23) and involuntary
              (\gk{οὐκ ἐφ' ἡμῖν}, 427b20)---the soul that believes something
              dangerous is already mobilized toward avoidance before deliberation can
              intervene, precisely because doxa, unlike phantasia, is not subject to
              the will.

              \item \textit{Somatic preparation internal to this motion}: the
              affections (\gk{πάθη} in the higher-order sense)---the physiological
              component of the emotion-desire composite---prepare the organic parts
              through heating, cooling, trembling, and muscular tension (\textit{MA}
              702a17--19; \textit{MA} 7, 701b16--32). Aristotle's compressed causal
              chain at \textit{MA} 702a15--21 places ``affections'' between desire
              and the organic parts: ``the organic parts are suitably prepared by the
              affections, these again by desire, and desire by imagination.'' The
              somatic preparation is thus internal to $M_3 \rightarrow M_4$ rather
              than a discrete stage in the macro-architecture: as desire translates
              into bodily action, the emotional state of the animal produces the
              somatic alterations---the boiling of blood in anger that prepares the
              body for aggressive action, the cooling in fear that prepares it for
              flight, the trembling and cardiac acceleration that the man who imagines
              something fearful undergoes (701b16--32)---that configure the organic
              parts for the specific locomotor act. The chain's $A_0$--$A_4$
              macro-skeleton is accordingly preserved: the \textit{MA}'s Level 4
              (affections) names a functionally distinguishable moment within the
              $M_3 \rightarrow M_4$ transition, not a separate node requiring
              architectural insertion.

              \item Kinetic Roles:
              \begin{itemize}
                  \item Unmoved Originator: the realizable good as apprehended in
                  $A_3$ (\textit{DA} III.10, 433b10--18).
                  \item Moved Mover: \textit{orexis} (\gk{ὄρεξις})---``that which at
                  once moves and is moved'' (433b17--18); ``appetite in the sense of
                  actual appetite \textit{is} a kind of movement'' (433b18). In
                  evaluatively complex actions, desire takes the form of the
                  emotion-desire composite (fear-as-desire-to-flee,
                  anger-as-desire-to-retaliate); in simple appetitive actions, desire
                  operates as basic appetite responding to the pleasant or painful
                  object without emotional specification. Desire remains the moved
                  mover in both cases; what varies is its internal complexity.
                  \item That Which is Moved: the animal via the bodily
                  instrument---the ball-and-socket joint where ``pushing and
                  pulling'' execute the action (433b21--25).
                  \item Time ($T_3 \rightarrow T_4$): the interval between the
                  formation of desire and the completion of the motor act---an
                  interval whose duration varies with the complexity of the action,
                  the resistance of the material conditions, and the degree of
                  somatic preparation required. The \textit{MA}'s speed-claim
                  (702a15--17: ``thinking that one ought to go and going are virtually
                  simultaneous'') indicates that under normal conditions the entire
                  transition fires with a rapidity that compresses the preparation of
                  the organic parts into a temporal interval that is functionally
                  instantaneous, rendered possible by ``the natural correspondence of
                  the active and passive'' (702a21)---each level of the chain being
                  already disposed, in its first actuality, to receive the impulse
                  transmitted from the level above.
              \end{itemize}
              \item Named by its terminus: this is \textit{appetitive action}
              because it terminates in $A_4$.
          \end{itemize}
      \end{itemize}
  \end{itemize}

  \begin{itemize}
      \item $A_4$ --- Completed Action (\gk{πρᾶξις})
      \begin{itemize}
          \item The perceptual-\textit{phantasmatic}-motor loop is closed, and the
          ensouled body has engaged the moving world. The completed action draws upon
          the full kinetic-affective history of the \textit{phantasma}---from the
          original perceptual encounter through its retention as residual trace,
          its cognitive reactivation in the orientational modes of thought, memory,
          and anticipation, its doxastic commitment where doxa has operated, the
          emotional mobilization where evaluatively complex content has been
          disclosed, and the appetitive appropriation of the realizable good that
          moves the animal to act.
          \item $A_4$ names $M_3 \rightarrow M_4$. The completed action, however,
          does not merely close the chain; it changes the world, producing new
          sensible conditions that can serve as $A_0$ for a new perceptual cycle.
          The chain is thus not linear but recursive: each completed action
          reconstitutes the perceptual environment and thereby occasions a new
          sequence of actualizations, a new passage from the sensible object through
          phantasma, cognition, desire, and movement.
      \end{itemize}
  \end{itemize}
