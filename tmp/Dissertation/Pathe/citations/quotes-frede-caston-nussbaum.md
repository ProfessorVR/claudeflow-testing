## Frede 1992 — "The Cognitive Role of Phantasia in Aristotle"

### Q-FRD-01
- Locus: p. 281
- Verbatim: "'Phantasia is a motion that does not happen without sense-perception but comes to be as the result of the activity of sense-perception and is like the perception' (428'II—15; 429'1; Somn. ### Q-CAS-01
- Locus: p. 22 (near n. 8–9)

### Q-CAS-04

## Nussbaum 1985 — "The Role of Phantasia in Aristotle's Explanation of Action"

### Q-NUS-01
- Locus: p. 221 (opening)
- Verbatim: "phantasia and desire are jointly necessary conditions for animal motion (DA 4321>15 ff.), and phantasia is a necessary condition for desire (43 3h26-30), which must 'prepare' desire before desire can lead to action (MA 8, 702n 18 ff.)."
- Supports: The chain "phantasia → desire → action" — direct corroboration of Section 1.6's perception-to-movement chain.
- Insertion: Section 1.6 near "Sense-perception (or thinking) delivers the \textit{resonant aisthema} (formal content) together with the \textit{resonant orexis}" — and Section 1.5 at the chain reiteration.
- Use as: footnote (load-bearing for the chain)

### Q-NUS-02
- Locus: p. 233
- Verbatim: "For the affections suitably prepare the organic parts, desire the affections, and phantasia the desire; and phantasia comes about either through thought or through sense-perception (702 11 18 ff.). Phantasia, then, is involved in every action; it must 'prepare' the desire whether or not actual perceiving is going on."
- Supports: The MA causal chain affections → organic parts; desire → affections; phantasia → desire — direct support for Section 1.5's somatic-preparation claim.
- Insertion: Section 1.5 near "sense-perception or thinking → imagination → desire → affections → organic parts → movement" — quoting the original MA passage and Nussbaum's gloss.
- Use as: in-text citation (load-bearing — this is the *single most important* secondary citation for Section 1.5)

### Q-NUS-03
- Locus: p. 246
- Verbatim: "Phantasia for Aristotle seems to be as wide-ranging a notion as the notion of the phainomena---which, as Owen has argued, embraces far more than the data of perception. His interest is in how an object or state of affairs registers with the creature, what content it has for him."
- Supports: The breadth of phantasia as concerning *how things register / appear / matter* — corroborates Section 1.9's thesis that phantasia "renders them as \textit{something}."
- Insertion: Section 1.9 near "\textit{phantasia} renders them as \textit{something}—pursue/avoid-able, dangerous, insulting, pleasurable, enviable—and in doing so makes them significant."
- Use as: in-text citation

### Q-NUS-04
- Locus: p. 246
- Verbatim: "for Aristotle to talk of a sensory phantasia would be to ascribe some (potentially motivating) content to the animal's perception, and that the animal who 'phantasizes' will not just perceive an object, but perceive it as a thing of a certain sort, a thing that could become for him an object either of pursuit or of avoidance."
- Supports: Phantasia as the seeing-as that makes pursuit/avoidance possible — directly supports Section 1.2's "this-as-pursue-able" formulation.
- Insertion: Section 1.2 near "Basic \textit{pathos} as affective valence or \textit{resonant orexis} (involved position-taking, pre-propositional, 'this-as-pursue-able')."
- Use as: footnote (load-bearing for the hermeneutical-as analogy)

### Q-NUS-05
- Locus: p. 257
- Verbatim: "Phantasia, or thought accompanied by phantasia, works by presenting the animal with the form or essence of its object, which has an effect like that of the object itself (701h19 ff., 703h19 ff.). The forms presented by phantasia were called the forms 'of the pleasant or painful' (701h21), or, equivalently, 'of that which produces the affections' (703h19-20). These remarks seem to imply that whereas in aisthesis the animal becomes just like the object, when phantasia is operative he becomes aware of the object as a thing of a certain sort."
- Supports: Phantasia presents the object *as* pleasant/painful (i.e., already valenced) — substantiates Section 1.6's "the object is registered as pleasant or painful, and the soul's assertoric or apophantic pursuit/avoidance orientation is engaged."

### Q-NUS-06
