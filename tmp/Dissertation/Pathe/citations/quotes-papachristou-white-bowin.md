# Mined Quotations: Papachristou (2013), White (1985), Bowin (2017)

For the chapter on Aristotelian *pathos* / *pathē* (two articulational concretions) and the temporal/feedback architecture of emotion. All quotations are verbatim from the supplied text-cache files. Page loci correspond to the page numbers visible in those PDFs (Papachristou: pp. 19–48 of *Journal of Ancient Philosophy* 7.1, 2013; White: *Dialogue* 24, 1985, pp. 483–505; Bowin: pp. 175–193, in volume on Aristotle and time, 2017).

## 1. Papachristou (2013) — "Three Kinds or Grades of Phantasia in Aristotle's *De Anima*"

### Entry P-1 — three-grade thesis (programmatic statement)
- **Citation:** Papachristou 2013, p. 20.
- **Verbatim:** "I shall try to demonstrate that when we study in depth the notion of phantasia (φαντασία), as it is described in Book III of De Anima, we can realize that Aristotle speaks about three and not two kinds or grades of phantasia."
- **Supports:** The grades-of-*phantasia* thesis directly underwrites the articulational-concretion claim that *pathos*-structure realizes at distinct cognitive grades (basic valence vs. higher-order *pathē*).
- **Insertion:** §1.2, near "two articulational concretions of one ontological structure"; specifically near the footnote articulating "(a) realizes the structure at minimal cognitive articulation … (b) realizes it a maximal cognitive articulation."
- **Use as:** footnote (programmatic acknowledgement that a graded reading of *phantasia* has independent textual warrant).

### Entry P-2 — *phantasia* situated between sensation and thought
- **Citation:** Papachristou 2013, p. 44 (Conclusion).
- **Verbatim:** "Phantasia is a faculty of the soul, the imaginative (φανταστικόν), that is placed between sensation (αἴσθησις) and thought (διάνοια). On the one hand it depends on sensation, is a kind of affection (πάθος), and on the other is a necessary condition for memory, motion, desire, dreaming, thinking etc."
- **Supports:** *Phantasia* itself is named a *pathos*, and its mediating position grounds the chain *aisthēsis → phantasia → doxa → pathē*.
- **Insertion:** §1.4, near "While this passage is used by Aristotle to distinguish between *phantasia* and *doxa*, he does so on an affective basis."
- **Use as:** in-text citation.

### Entry P-3 — indefinite/indeterminate *phantasia* (lowest grade)
- **Citation:** Papachristou 2013, p. 34.
- **Verbatim:** "imperfect or indefinite creatures, which have no sense except that of touch, possess an indefinite/indeterminate kind of phantasia. Representations of touch (phantasmata) or tactile representations are the products of this kind of phantasia. In imperfect animals tactile representations are usually diffuse and indefinite, and do not remain in them, after the sense object is gone."
- **Supports:** The minimal grade of *phantasia* is bound to *contact* and to a representation that does *not* persist after the object is removed — precisely the threshold below which the basic affective valence cannot detach from present perception.
- **Insertion:** §1.1 inline-note (the user's own gloss: "when the object of sense is removed, the residual affective valence … is the *resonant orexis*"); also fits §1.2 where *resonant orexis* is distinguished from basic affective valence.
- **Use as:** footnote.

### Entry P-4 — sensitive *phantasia* and persistence after the object
- **Citation:** Papachristou 2013, pp. 35–36.
- **Verbatim:** "Aristotle says that sensitive phantasia is generated by sense perception (αἰσθάνεσθαι). It does not occur without sense perception. Such kind of phantasia is found in all animals other than man (see Aristotle's classification of animals, table 4), namely the irrational animals."
- **Supports:** The middle grade — *aisthētikē phantasia* — is what carries affective valence forward as a *phantasma* after perception, supplying the dispositional bridge from the *resonant aisthēma* to *resonant orexis*.
- **Insertion:** §1.6, near "The *phantasma* that results from perception carries this affective charge as a dispositional property---the image of the perceived object retains its valence---but the charge is latent rather than [mobilizing]."
- **Use as:** in-text citation.

### Entry P-5 — capacity to retain a *phantasma* after the sense-object is gone
- **Citation:** Papachristou 2013, p. 36.
- **Verbatim:** "in animals other than indefinite creatures, in other words in animals that possess more than one sense (normal animals), and for this reason they can discriminate even the objects at a distance, the product of phantasia or phantasma remains in them even after the sense object is gone. Therefore, the difference between the indefinite and sensitive kind or grade of phantasia is based on their capacity to perceive and retain a sense object."
- **Supports:** Direct textual warrant for the user's distinction between (a) basic affective valence (object present) and (b) *resonant orexis* (object removed but charge retained as a *phantasma*-borne disposition).
- **Insertion:** §1.1, near the inline-note: "when the object of sense is actually present, we have the basic hedonic tonality … *resonant orexis*, on the other hand, is part of the *resonant kineseis* that persists after the object has ceased to be."
- **Use as:** footnote.

### Entry P-6 — calculative/deliberative *phantasia* as the highest grade
- **Citation:** Papachristou 2013, p. 44.
- **Verbatim:** "Calculative, or deliberative, phantasia (λογιστικὴ or βουλευτικὴ φαντασία) which is the highest development of the faculty of phantasia. It appears only in human beings, because they have the power of thinking and the ability to combine several mental images (phantasmata) into one."
- **Supports:** Identifies the topmost grade with *logos*-bearing animals — the same population for whom *doxa* is possible — anchoring the chapter's restriction of higher-order *pathē* to rational animals.
- **Insertion:** §1.2, footnote restricting the analysis to rational animals: "The present analysis restricts itself to rational animals: those possessing *logos* and therefore capable of *doxa* …"
- **Use as:** footnote.

### Entry P-7 — *doxa*, inference, and the human-animal cut
- **Citation:** Papachristou 2013, pp. 41–42.
- **Verbatim:** "other animals than man are thought not to have opinion (δόξα), because their desires have no deliberation. Only animals with intellect—and therefore language—have the phantasia that comes from inference. This kind of phantasia appears as an intermediate between sense perception (αἰσθάνεσθαι) and nous or mind (νοῦς). It involves having and combining several mental images (phantasmata) into one."
- **Supports:** Provides the *phantasia*-side correlate of the user's *doxa*-gate: *doxa* is what differentiates the merely-similar (animal "fear") from the genuine higher-order *pathē* in *logos*-bearing beings.
- **Insertion:** §1.4, near "What concretizes them is *doxa*---the involuntary belief that the object is genuinely fearful---and the concretization is immediate"; also §1.6 on the *logos*-conditioned engagement of higher *pathē*.
- **Use as:** in-text citation.

### Entry P-8 — *phantasia* as *pathos* "in our own power whenever we wish"
- **Citation:** Papachristou 2013, p. 30 (citing *De Anima* III.3, 427b14–21).
- **Verbatim:** "Phantasia, according to Aristotle, is an affection (πάθος) and 'this affection is in our own power whenever we wish'. So, it is up to us to imagine, but it is not up to us to believe, 'for the belief that we hold must be either false or true'."
- **Supports:** The voluntary/involuntary asymmetry between *phantasia* and *doxa* — central to the user's claim that *doxa* is what "befalls" the agent and so concretizes emotion as something undergone rather than chosen.
- **Insertion:** §1.4, near "the involuntariness of *doxa* is what gives emotion its distinctive phenomenological character as something that *befalls* the agent rather than something the agent elects to undergo."
- **Use as:** footnote.

### Entry P-9 — the dual-content *phantasma* (propositional and pictorial)
- **Citation:** Papachristou 2013, p. 43.
- **Verbatim:** "the activity of calculative or deliberative phantasia involves the use of phantasmata with (a) propositional and (b) pictorial or quasi-pictorial content"
- **Supports:** The dual-content *phantasma* is precisely what *doxa* takes up to concretize a *pathos*: pictorial content yields the still-affective image; propositional content yields the assertoric structure into which *doxa* commits.
- **Insertion:** §1.9, near "If *phantasia* supplies the appearance, *doxa* is the moment of apophantic assertion: an unfolding from *taking-as-something* to *taking-something-as-true*."
- **Use as:** footnote.

### Entry P-10 — *phantasia* as motion produced by actual sensation
- **Citation:** Papachristou 2013, p. 30 n. 43 (glossing *DA* III.3, 428b11–13 and 429a1–2).
- **Verbatim:** "Phantasia is a type of motion that arises by actual sensation. Sensation is activated by the presence of the external object."
- **Supports:** *Phantasia* is itself a *kinēsis*, and so the *phantasma*-borne residual affective charge is a *kinēsis* — exactly the user's "*resonant kineseis* that persists after the object has ceased to be."
- **Insertion:** §1.1, inline-note ("the residual affective valence that forms part of the residual motion (*resonant kinesis* is the *resonant orexis*").
- **Use as:** footnote.

### Entry P-11 — three-grade summary table (Conclusion)
- **Citation:** Papachristou 2013, pp. 44–45.
- **Verbatim:** "Aristotle … discriminates not two —as it is commonly argued— but three kinds or grades of phantasia: (a) Indefinite/indeterminate phantasia (ἀόριστος φαντασία) which is to be found in the imperfectly developed creatures … (b) Sensitive phantasia (αἰσθητικὴ φαντασία) which is to be found in animals that possess more than one sense (normal animals) … (c) Calculative, or deliberative, phantasia (λογιστικὴ or βουλευτικὴ φαντασία) which is the highest development of the faculty of phantasia."
- **Supports:** Compact statement of the tripartite scheme that maps onto the user's articulational-concretion gradient (minimal-contact → retained-image → *logos*-articulated) without collapsing into a categorical division.
- **Insertion:** §1.2, near the body-text articulating the two articulational concretions, used as a footnote to register that the *phantasia*-side likewise admits internal grading rather than a binary cut.
- **Use as:** footnote.

### Entry P-12 — *phantasma* as substratum on which *nous* operates
- **Citation:** Papachristou 2013, p. 32.
- **Verbatim:** "the word «φάντασμα», which is mentioned twelve times in De Anima, may conveniently and aptly be translated as: (a) «representation» or «image» in contexts where «φάντασμα» is related only with the faculty of phantasia … and (b) as «mental representation» or «mental image», when «φάντασμα» is described by the philosopher as the substratum upon which the mind works."
- **Supports:** The terminological distinction supports the user's claim that thinking and considering "rely on the *resonant orexis* as it is the *phantasma* on which they operate" (§1.1).
- **Insertion:** §1.1, inline-note where the user states that "thinking and considering relies on the *resonant orexis* as it is the *phantasma* on which they operate."
- **Use as:** footnote.

## 2. White (1985) — "The Meaning of *Phantasia* in Aristotle's *De Anima*, III, 3–8"

### Entry W-1 — *phantasia* as the linking faculty between sensing and thinking
- **Citation:** White 1985, p. 495.
- **Verbatim:** "the theme of phantasia as a distinct activity of the soul arises, as in the De anima, the De memoria, and the De insomniis, in the gap opened up by the separation between sensing and thinking. Phantasia seems to resemble each of these in certain ways, which may explain the Protagorean confusion between them, while it also serves as the link between them in the Aristotelian explanation of knowledge."
- **Supports:** *Phantasia* occupies the structural position between *aisthēsis* and *dianoia/doxa* — exactly the rung at which the user's chain transitions from basic affective valence to *doxa*-articulated *pathē*.
- **Insertion:** §1.4, before "What concretizes them is *doxa*"; or §1.9 introduction to the *phantasia → doxa → pathos* transition.
- **Use as:** in-text citation.

### Entry W-2 — *phantasia* defined as motion produced by active sensation
- **Citation:** White 1985, p. 496.
- **Verbatim:** "the definition of phantasia as 'a movement produced by sensation actively operating' (kinesis hypo tes aistheseos tes kat'energeian gignomenes). Such a secondary movement necessarily resembles sensation, which in turn is an assimilation to the sensible object; moreover, the movement of phantasia evidently continues in the absence of the sensible object and the inoperancy of the sense-power."
- **Supports:** Direct textual basis for the user's "*resonant kineseis* that persists after the object has ceased to be" — *phantasia* is the secondary motion that retains the affective-perceptual charge once the object is gone.
- **Insertion:** §1.1, inline-note about residual motion / *resonant kinesis*; §1.6, where *phantasma* "carries this affective charge as a dispositional property."
- **Use as:** footnote.

### Entry W-3 — *phantasia* as preserving past encounters
- **Citation:** White 1985, p. 498.
- **Verbatim:** "The animal soul, therefore, is not merely one which is in touch with its environment through the cognition of sensation, but one which carries the reverberating effects of its past encounters with sensible objects; it is not only a receiver, but also a preserver and a storehouse, of the sensible forms of things."
- **Supports:** The diachronic, dispositional persistence of prior actualizations that the user names *hexis*-character / *Geworfenheit* in §1.7.
- **Insertion:** §1.7, near "the diachronic register concerns how prior actualizations modulate subsequent actualizations: a *pathos*-actualization at *T₁* persists as dispositional ground for the *pathos*-actualization at T₂."
- **Use as:** in-text citation (sets up the *On Dreams* feedback discussion).

### Entry W-4 — *phantasia* and the inner appearance: "wholly interiorized"
- **Citation:** White 1985, pp. 487–488.
- **Verbatim:** "These inner phantasmata are clearly a special kind of appearance, since, as has been said, an appearance usually seems to involve a union between something which appears and someone to whom it appears, or a union of the outer world and an inner act of recognition. It seems, however, that an appearance can become wholly interiorized, so that the subjective pole becomes overwhelmingly predominant: the inner appearance is not only for the individual to whom it appears, but it is for him alone, since he contains it."
- **Supports:** The interiority of the *phantasma* is what allows emotion to be elicited by the absent or imagined; it is the basis on which *doxa* operates over a content that no longer requires the object's presence.
- **Insertion:** §1.9, near "*phantasia* is not a decorative adjunct to emotion; it is the condition of possibility for the soul being-moved by absences, possibilities, appearances, and counterfactuals."
- **Use as:** footnote.

### Entry W-5 — *phantasia* and *nous* as analogous originators of motion
- **Citation:** White 1985, p. 493 (quoting *DA* III.10, 433a9–12).
- **Verbatim:** "These two, then, appetite (orexis) and mind (nous) are clearly capable of causing movement if, that is, one regards imagination (phantasia) as some sort of thinking process (noesin); for men often follow their imaginations (phantasai) contrary to knowledge … Thus, from the point of view of animal activity, as well as of human activity in so far as it is generated by the passions of the body, phantasia and nous seem to perform analogous originating functions and to be interchangeable one for the other."
- **Supports:** Underwrites the user's claim that emotion-driven action operates at the appearance-level: "Emotion follows not judgment (*krisis*) but appearance-laden belief: e.g. that *seems* dangerous." Cf. §1.6.
- **Insertion:** §1.6, near "Emotion responds to *how things appear*, not to how reason later adjudicates them."
- **Use as:** in-text citation.

### Entry W-6 — *phantasia* as the "noun" by which any *phantasma* comes to be in us
- **Citation:** White 1985, p. 487 (commentary on *DA* III.3, 428a1–2).
- **Verbatim:** "Aristotle says that phantasia is that in virtue of which we say that any phantasma comes to be in us: kath'hen legomen phantasma ti hemin gignesthai. … phantasia as it is commonly understood would seem to signify the hypothesis of an underlying action of the soul, a moving cause which serves to account for the presence in us of phantasmata."
- **Supports:** Locates *phantasia* as the moving cause of the *phantasma*; clarifies that the *phantasma* (image-with-affective-charge) on which *doxa* operates is itself the work of a prior *kinēsis* of the soul.
- **Insertion:** §1.4, near "The bare *phantasma*---the image entertained at will (the fearful scenario merely imagined)---leaves the soul unaffected."
- **Use as:** footnote.

### Entry W-7 — interplay of *phantasia*, desire, and practical thinking
- **Citation:** White 1985, p. 502 (commentary on *DA* III.7, 431b2–10).
- **Verbatim:** "So the thinking faculty (noetikon) thinks the forms (eide) in the mental images (phantasmasi), and just as in the sphere of sense what is to be pursued and avoided is defined for it, so also outside sensation, when it is occupied with mental images (phantasmaton), is moved. … But sometimes by means of the images (phantasmasin) or thoughts (noemasin) in the soul, just as if it were seeing, it calculates and plans for the future in view of the present"
- **Supports:** Direct textual warrant for the user's claim that the future-directed temporal reach of fear (and other futural *pathē*) is grounded in *phantasia*'s capacity to operate over images of the not-yet.
- **Insertion:** §1.9, near "Fear---'a pain or disturbance due to imagining some destructive or painful evil in the future' (1382a22–23)---is paradigmatic. The feared event does not exist; the *phantasma* does."
- **Use as:** in-text citation.

### Entry W-8 — *phantasia* and the imperfection of human transcendence
- **Citation:** White 1985, p. 503.
- **Verbatim:** "the imperfection of this transcendence may be attributed to the origins of phantasia in the time-conditioned circumstances of sensation. But while phantasia may be the source of the failure of human noein to fully realize itself, it is also what makes this activity possible at all."
- **Supports:** A general anchor for the chapter's thesis that the *pathē* are not a defect *over against* cognition but the very medium in which cognition takes shape — *phantasia* both limits and enables.
- **Insertion:** §1.9 conclusion, near "Emotion is not a barrier to action but the very medium in which purposive action takes shape."
- **Use as:** epigraph (could open §1.9) or footnote.

### Entry W-9 — *phantasia* as connected to the passions
- **Citation:** White 1985, p. 504 n. 88.
- **Verbatim:** "the passions of anger, fear, confidence, and shame involve the activity of phantasia (Rhetoric, II, 2, 1378a30-b10; 5, 1382a21ff., 1383a17; 6, 1383b12-1384a22); the cowardly, the erotic, and the angry are easily deceived by slight resemblances between the inner appearances and the objects of their passions (De insomniis, 2, 460b4-11)"
- **Supports:** Explicit secondary attestation that the *Rhetoric*'s catalog of higher-order *pathē* is built on *phantasia*; and that the *De Insomniis* feedback (cowardly/erotic/angry deceived) belongs to the *phantasia*-emotion nexus.
- **Insertion:** §1.7, near the *De Insomniis* passage on the controlling sense and the coward/amorous/angry; or §1.4 to anchor the *phantasia*-*pathē* link.
- **Use as:** footnote.

### Entry W-10 — *phantasia* and dreams as evidence for persistence
- **Citation:** White 1985, p. 497 (gloss of *De Insomniis* 1, 459a15–22).
- **Verbatim:** "the imaginative (phantastikon) is the same as the sensitive faculty, although the imaginative and the sensitive are different in essence (einai); and since imagination (phantasia) is the process set up by a sense faculty in a state of activity (he hypo tes kat'energeian aistheseos gignomene kinesis), and a dream appears to be some sort of mental image (phantasma) … it is clear that dreaming belongs to the sensitive faculty, but belongs to it qua imaginative"
- **Supports:** The same-but-different-in-being relation between *aisthētikon* and *phantastikon* matches the user's "single ontological structure with two articulational concretions" — same faculty/structure, distinct mode.
- **Insertion:** §1.2, near the "two articulational concretions of one ontological structure" thesis.
- **Use as:** footnote (analogical structural parallel: same faculty, two modes).

### Entry W-11 — *phantasia*, memory, and the prolongation of the now
- **Citation:** White 1985, p. 503.
- **Verbatim:** "In souls characterized by sensation alone, phantasia gives rise to memory and desire, prolonging the now of sensation into past and future for the sake of the animal's preservation. In the noetic or human soul, however, phantasia is a step in man's progression beyond time altogether, enabling him at certain points and for certain periods to pass beyond the confinements of sense to a consideration of the timeless natures of things."
- **Supports:** Direct articulation of *phantasia*'s temporal reach — past *and* future — which underwrites the temporal structure of each higher-order *pathos* (§1.9).
- **Insertion:** §1.9, near "*phantasia* is indispensable because it supplies the *material* for practical reasoning and action"; or §1.7, on the temporal structure of state-of-mind.
- **Use as:** in-text citation.

### Entry W-12 — *phantasia*'s permanence-conferring function
- **Citation:** White 1985, p. 505.
- **Verbatim:** "By internalizing and preserving such features, the power of phantasia provides man with a familiar interior landscape which, in contrast with the sensible beings in themselves, he may consult with confidence in its permanence. The relative permanence conferred on the sensible beings by phantasia is necessary and interesting to man in many practical ways"
- **Supports:** The "interior landscape" *phantasia* provides is what *doxa* takes up to articulate *pathē*; it is also the substrate on which the *De Insomniis* feedback operates (the "coward sees enemies in shadows" because *phantasia*-borne images persist and bias).
- **Insertion:** §1.7, near "the *Stimmung*-saturation … modulates the *input* to *doxa*: a sustained higher-order *pathos* persists as a tonal coloring that biases subsequent perception."
- **Use as:** footnote.

## 3. Bowin (2017) — "Aristotle on the Perception and Cognition of Time"

### Entry B-1 — perception of time without intellect
- **Citation:** Bowin 2017, pp. 175–176.
- **Verbatim:** "Aristotle is willing on many occasions to simply say that we perceive time (Phys. 4.11.219a3–4, Mem.1.449b29, 450a19, 451a17), and he says at Phys. 4.11.219a30–b1 that we perceive the now as both one and as before and after in a motion. He also says we perceive motion and time together (219a3–4)."
- **Supports:** Establishes that the *temporal grasp* required by basic affective valence is already available at the perceptual level — i.e., the temporal structure does not first arise with *doxa*. This is necessary for the user's claim that the higher-order *pathē* have a *characteristic temporal profile* without that profile being the source of the temporal element itself.
- **Insertion:** §1.9, near "Each *pathos* is structured by a determinate temporality."
- **Use as:** footnote.

### Entry B-2 — perceiving time = perceiving a number of nows ordered as before/after
- **Citation:** Bowin 2017, p. 180.
- **Verbatim:** "to perceive time is to perceive a number of motion stages ordered as before and after."
- **Supports:** Compact statement of the perceptual time-structure; useful as a benchmark when situating the *pathē*'s temporal moments (having-been / present / projected-futural).
- **Insertion:** §1.9 conclusion-list, near the temporal moment of each *pathos* ("having-been, present, projected-futural").
- **Use as:** in-text citation.

### Entry B-3 — perception of time depends on *phantasia*
- **Citation:** Bowin 2017, p. 184.
- **Verbatim:** "It is clear from this that the perception of time depends on φαντασία."
- **Supports:** Anchors the chapter's claim that *phantasia* is the condition of possibility for the soul's being-moved by absences/futures — the temporal range of emotion is built on *phantasia*'s temporal reach.
- **Insertion:** §1.9, near "*phantasia* is not a decorative adjunct to emotion; it is the condition of possibility for the soul being-moved by absences, possibilities, appearances, and counterfactuals."
- **Use as:** in-text citation.

### Entry B-4 — memory and perception of time interdefined via *phantasmata*
- **Citation:** Bowin 2017, pp. 183–184.
- **Verbatim:** "Both memory and the perception of time involve φαντάσματα and therefore φαντασία; two φαντάσματα, in fact, one representing the event remembered and one representing the time elapsed since the event. To remember, or to recognize that an event happened before is to have a φαντάσμα of a movement corresponding to the time match a φαντάσμα of a movement corresponding to the event"
- **Supports:** The "having-been" pole of each *pathos* is realized via *phantasma*-pairing; this is the cognitive-perceptual correlate of the user's *Gewesenheit* and the *hexis*-sedimentation feedback in §1.7.
- **Insertion:** §1.7, near the *On Dreams* discussion of how prior fear ("*as having-been*") shapes the present perceptual encounter.
- **Use as:** footnote.

### Entry B-5 — perception of the future via *phantasmata*
- **Citation:** Bowin 2017, p. 185 (citing *DA* III.7, 431b6–8).
- **Verbatim:** "Perceiving time in those texts amounts to perceiving an ordered series of past or present events. Here, perceiving time is perceiving the future. Strictly speaking, though, one does not perceive the future, as Aristotle points out in De Memoria 1, 449b27. But we can perceive φαντάσματα (Mem.1.450b17–18), and in De Anima 3.7, he says this is very much like perceiving the future: 'Sometimes by means of the φαντάσματα or thoughts which are within the soul, just as if it were seeing, [the faculty of thinking] calculates and deliberates what is to come (τὰ μέλλοντα) by reference to what is present'"
- **Supports:** Direct textual basis for fear's "futural-coming-toward" temporal moment; it grounds the soul's being-moved by an evil that does not yet exist in *phantasia*'s capacity to render the future as if perceived.
- **Insertion:** §1.9, near "Fear---'a pain or disturbance due to imagining some destructive or painful evil in the future' (1382a22–23)---is paradigmatic. The feared event does not exist; the *phantasma* does. And the soul moves as though the feared thing were present."
- **Use as:** in-text citation.

### Entry B-6 — deliberative *phantasia* and the synthesis of many *phantasmata* into one
- **Citation:** Bowin 2017, p. 186 (citing *DA* III.11, 434a6–15).
- **Verbatim:** "Perceptual φαντασία, as we have said, is found in the other animals also, but deliberative [φαντασία] in those which are capable of calculating [ἐν τοῖς λογιστικοῖς] (for the decision whether to do this or that is already a task for calculating [λογισμοῦ ἔργον]; and one must measure (μετρεῖν) by a single standard; for one pursues what is greater (τὸ μεῖζον); hence one has the ability to make (ποιεῖν) one image out of many (φαντασμάτων)."
- **Supports:** The deliberative *phantasia* of *logos*-bearing beings is what enables the *doxa*-mediated articulation that turns valence into determinate *pathē*; it is the temporal-cognitive correlate of the articulational concretion thesis (Papachristou's third grade).
- **Insertion:** §1.6, near the *aisthēsis*/*phantasia*/*doxa* chain for the rational agent.
- **Use as:** footnote.

### Entry B-7 — intellect's effect on the perception of time
- **Citation:** Bowin 2017, p. 185.
- **Verbatim:** "perceiving time does not presuppose grasping it intellectually, but grasping time intellectually, I will now argue, has an effect on how one perceives time for those beings who have intellect."
- **Supports:** Structurally identical to the chapter's articulational thesis at the *pathos* level: the *doxa*-articulated higher-order *pathos* does not introduce a *new* temporal stratum; it modifies how the same temporal structure is perceptually inhabited.
- **Insertion:** §1.7, near the synchronic/diachronic register distinction, or §1.9, on the temporal moment of each *pathos*.
- **Use as:** epigraph for §1.9 (or in-text citation).

### Entry B-8 — *phantasmata* as inner movements representing time
- **Citation:** Bowin 2017, pp. 186–187.
- **Verbatim:** "Aristotle talks of inner 'movements' by which we discriminate greater and smaller times in De Memoria 2. Though called 'movements', these are also φαντάσματα, as De Memoria 1, 451a3 makes clear, and so the representation of time periods is a function of φαντασία."
- **Supports:** Underwrites the user's claim that the *phantasma* carries forward not just affective but *temporal* charge — the having-been-aspect is borne by the same medium that carries valence.

### Entry B-10 — having a *phantasma* of time linking past event to present

### Entry B-11 — *nous* "holds back" because of the future via *phantasmata*
- **Citation:** Bowin 2017, p. 185 (commentary on *DA* III.10, 433b5–11).
- **Verbatim:** "Aristotle claims that perception (αἴσθησις) of time is necessary in beings where reason (ὁ λόγος) and desires (αἱ ἐπιθυμίαι) conflict because 'νοῦς bids us hold back because of what is future'. Now the perception of time, here, is obviously not of the sort we encountered in Physics 4.10–14 or in the De Memoria. ### Entry B-12 — deliberative *phantasia* and the normalized image
- **Citation:** Bowin 2017, p. 187.
- **Verbatim:** "while perceiving a day as a day for the purpose of measuring time might employ a sortal concept, the perceptually disambiguated image of a day in one's mind – the normalized image of a day shorn of the rough edges of its particularity such as seasonal variations in length of daylight – may nonetheless be non-conceptual."
