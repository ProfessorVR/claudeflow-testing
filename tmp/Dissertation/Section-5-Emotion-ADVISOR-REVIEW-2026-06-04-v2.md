## 2. Tier 1 — substantive problems

### T1-1. Is emotion *a form of orexis*, or *a composite that includes orexis*? The section asserts both.

**The identity claim.** Line 1166: "Emotion, accordingly, is not a node in the chain but the *form* that desire takes within it." Line 1170: "fear *is* the desire to flee insofar as it is structured by the cognitive evaluation of an anticipated destructive evil and experienced in the somatic alteration of chilled blood."

**The composite claim (same section).** Line 1134: "the fact Aristotle gives the affections a step of their own, distinct from desire, within the causal chain suggests that **emotion includes desire without being reducible to it**." And: "The ontological status of emotion as separate from desire consists precisely in its **composite character: emotion is desire *together with* physiological change *structured by* cognitive content** … It is this composite—not the desire alone—that prepares the organic parts for movement."

**The problem.** "Fear *is* a (form of) desire" and "emotion … is *not reducible to* desire … [but] desire *together with* physiological change structured by cognitive content" are different ontological claims; a committee will press whether emotion is a *species of orexis* or a *composite whole* of which *orexis* is a proper part, and will note that the *De Motu* argument you build at 1134 to *distinguish* emotion from desire undercuts the identity thesis at 1170.

**Fix (your own tools).** The enmattered-account frame is already in the section — line 1116: "anger is the *desire* for retaliation *realized in* the boiling of blood around the heart." State once, at the identity moment, that emotion is the *eidetic form-determination of orexis necessarily enmattered in an affection and structured by doxa-ratified content* — then "the form of desire" (formal account) and "desire-plus-soma-plus-cognition" (the enmattered whole) are the *same hylomorphic object under two accounts*, not two claims.

### T1-2. The *doxa*-gate is under-defended — but the cluster supplies a precise, citeable repair. *(Headline; fully rewritten.)*

**What the dissertation claims.** *Doxa* (involuntary belief) is the necessary gate for higher emotion. Line 1122: "What concretizes them is *doxa*—the involuntary belief that the object is genuinely fearful … *doxa*, for rational animals, serves as not only a condition but also as a **cognitive gate through which emotion must pass** to enter the actualization chain." Line 1136: "the person who fears *takes-something-as-truly-fearful-now*, and it is this *taking-as-true* that produces the fear." And the *doxa* you invoke is *strong* — it requires conviction; the section endorses Aristotle's layering (line 1028): opinion "involves belief … belief by conviction, and conviction by discourse of reason" (*DA* III.3 428a20–23).

**The vulnerability.** Your own recalcitrance case shows emotion running *against* belief — line 1217: "emotion can contradict one's standing judgment, as when the knowledge that the coiled rope in the corner is not a snake still fails to forestall the startle … the startle is built on the involuntary taking of the shape *as* a snake." A 200 ms startle cannot carry "belief … conviction … discourse of reason." So the strong *doxa*-gate is in tension with the very phenomenon you use it to explain.

**The rival (Moss) — now grounded in verbatim.** Moss is the cluster's non-judgmental pole. Her keystone, quoting *EE* VII.2 1235b25–9: "the pleasant is desired, for it is an apparent good; for some believe it good, and to some it appears so **even though they do not believe it so**. For *phantasia* and belief (*doxa*) do not reside in the same part of the soul" (Moss, *Akrasia and Perceptual Illusion*, p. 129). The motivating evaluative cognition is therefore *phantasia*, not belief, and can stand against belief — "the qualifiedly pleasant appears pleasant and good without qualification **just as the sun appears a foot wide**" (Moss, p. 127). And the uptake is *not* positive assent: "such following is best understood as **default yielding rather than as active rational assent**" — "she does not actively assent to the appearance as true, but with her rational faculty impaired neither does she reject it as false" (Moss, pp. 138). In her monograph she makes this the explicit thesis: "Aristotle holds what we might call a **phantastic account of the passions: passions are based on exercises of *phantasia***" (Moss, *Aristotle on the Apparent Good*, p. 70), seated in "a non-rational part of the soul **incapable of belief**" (Moss, *AG*, p. 98). **Your rope-snake, sun (1030), and fiction (1120) cases are her paradigm exhibits.**

**The ally you misclassified (Dow).** Dow argues *against* the "mere appearance" reading and *for* a subject-level taking-as-true — but one **weaker than belief/conviction**, which is exactly what your startle needs:

- *The affirmation thesis.* "Aristotle's view seems to be that all passions involve **taking their representational contents to be the way things are, if not wholeheartedly, then at least to some extent**. On such a view, cases of recalcitrant passion also involve **some affirmation of their contents**, with the result that the subject is conflicted, simultaneously affirming (in some way) conflicting appraisals of how things are" (Dow, *Passions and Persuasion in Aristotle's Rhetoric*, p. 106). And: "having a passion constitutes a kind of **affirmation by the subject that things are the way they are represented** in their passionate experience … where the contents of a person's passions are inconsistent with the contents of their beliefs, that person holds (to that extent) inconsistent attitudes about how things are in the world" (Dow, p. 198).
- *The disanalogy that defuses your recalcitrance worry.* Dow distinguishes "cases of mere appearance to which the subject gives no endorsement, **such as the sun's appearing about a foot across when it is known to be huge**" (Dow, p. 183 n.) from genuine emotion: the sun-case involves *no* subject-level inconsistency, whereas a recalcitrant *emotion does* — which is precisely why the emotion (unlike the sun-illusion) carries affirmation. **This inverts Moss's use of the sun passage and is the resource you need:** concede the bare illusions to Moss, keep the emotions as affirmation-bearing. (Dow even flags his own change of view: "I defended a somewhat different view in Dow (2009)," p. 183 n.)

**The foundational ally (Fortenbaugh).** The belief-pedigree your thesis descends from: "Aristotle construes the thought of outrage as the **efficient cause mentioned in the essential definition**. Anger is not a pain which happens to occur together with (*meta*) the thought of outrage. On the contrary, anger is necessarily caused by the thought of outrage, so that such a thought is mentioned in the essential definition of anger" (Fortenbaugh, *Aristotle on Emotion*, p. 12). But Fortenbaugh is *purely* doxastic (no *phantasia*), so he cannot answer recalcitrance — which is why you need Dow's affirmation, not Fortenbaugh's belief.

**The two-tier seam is in the sources already.** Moss concedes the doxastic overlay: "even though it is *phantasia* that gives rise to passions … evaluative beliefs are their **normal, default accompaniments**: unless the agent actively withholds assent, the evaluative *phantasia* which gives rise to a passion will also give rise to a corresponding evaluative belief" — "passions normally entail beliefs, because the *phantasiai* on which they are based normally trigger beliefs. But passions are not themselves a function of belief. Just as seeing something as round can result from, and will normally entail, and yet is essentially independent of, the belief that it is round, so feeling anger at someone can result from … yet is essentially independent of, the belief that they have done you wrong" (Moss, *AG*, pp. 98–99). **This is your articulational-concretion split in Moss's own words** — and it matches your co-givenness thesis (line 1038: "the *epithymia* … is co-given with the *aisthēma* … as a single disclosure").

**Recommended rewrite of the gate.**
1. **Concede the basal tier to the phantastic model** (cite Moss *AG* pp. 98–99): basic hedonic tonality / *resonant epithymia* is non-doxastic *phantasia*; the rope-snake/sun/fiction cases live here. (Note this is also where Moss's "a pleasurable *phantasia* *is* an evaluative cognition" *agrees* with your dual-resonance, 922–934.)
2. **Recast the higher-order civic emotions' gate as *Dovian affirmation*** — person-level, gradable, *weaker than conviction* (cite Dow pp. 106, 198), and argue that for *those* emotions the affirmation is *constitutive* rather than a "normal accompaniment." This keeps a "taking-as-true" while explaining recalcitrance (Dow's disanalogy, p. 183 n.).
3. **Replace "involuntary belief … conviction … discourse of reason" (1028/1122) as the gate-condition** with affirmation; reserve full *doxa*-with-conviction for the explicitly deliberative cases.
4. **Name the dialectic:** Moss the rival; Dow/Fortenbaugh/Konstan the allies. Add the two loci the cluster flags: *EE* VII.2 1235b25–29 (to *answer*) and *Rhet.* II.1 1377b31–1378a5 (*pathē* generate appearances — supports your loop's input side).

This converts the section's biggest vulnerability into a defended, four-source-grounded two-tier contribution.

### T1-3. The genus of *epithymia* is unstable, and the *resonant-epithymia*→*thymos* concretization skips a step.

**You already flag half of it.** The live `\inlinenote` at line 1065: "Need to clairfy that 'hedonic tonality' is the category of which *epithymia* and *resonant epithymia* are species. hedonic tonality is the [genus], whereas the other two are temporally specific."

**The inconsistency.** Two incompatible genus–species trees: §3 makes *epithymia* a species of ***orexis*** (line 86 fn: "*orexis* … consisting of three species: *epithymia* … *thymos* … *boulēsis*"), but §5 (line 1073) makes it a species of **hedonic tonality**: "it is the genus, if you will, of which *epithymia* and *resonant epithymia* are the temporally specific species." *Epithymia* cannot have two genera with two partition principles.

**The deeper gap.** §3 argues the residue is *epithymia*-specifically because (line 924) it "alone requires no cognitive enrichment." But the higher emotions §5 says *doxa* "concretizes the … *resonant epithymia* into" (1046) are *thymotic*/*bouletic* (anger = *thymos*; §6 1294). *Doxa* turning *epithymia* into *thymos* needs an argument. **Fix:** make the residue *generic resonant orectic charge* that *doxa*-articulation differentiates into the species; edit §3's "*epithymia*-specifically" rationale to match.

### T1-4. The "realizable good" gate contradicts emotions toward non-realizable goods — and Konstan is your witness.

**The collision.** §4 (line 1060): where the object "cannot be otherwise … no realizable good is disclosed and the chain terminates at A₃; speculative thought moves nothing." §5 (line 1166–1168) places emotion *at* M₃→A₄. §6 (line 1296) lets *boulēsis* "aim … even for impossibles, e.g. for immortality." So grief over the irrevocable / longing for the impossible has *no* A₄ — yet is an emotion. The section invokes exactly such cases: "the considered grief one carries quietly" (1085); "To remember grief is to feel grief again" (1219).

**The scholarly witness (Konstan).** Konstan argues grief sits *outside* Aristotle's emotion-category for precisely this reason: "Aristotle omits to discuss a sentiment — grief — that is central to most modern accounts of the emotions, and here I argue that **grief did not fit Aristotle's conception of an emotion**" (Konstan, *The Emotions of the Ancient Greeks*, p. xi); on the classical conception "grief and anger look radically different, even to the point that **grief might not qualify as a *pathos* at all**" (Konstan, p. 258). **Fix:** either exclude grief (with Konstan) from the catalogue you universalize, or argue it in via the phantastic route — but don't silently treat grief as a paradigm *pathos*.

### T1-5. The temporal-range element equivocates on "primary ecstasis."

**Claim A (you endorse Heidegger).** Line 1160 quotes *BT* 390: "one's *state-of-mind* … temporalizes itself **primarily** in *having been*." Line 1205: "disposedness … primarily grounded in having-been."

**Claim B (per-emotion).** Line 1193: "Fear is *primarily* futural … Anger is *primarily* having-been … Pity is *primarily* present." Line 1203 repeats this with the per-emotion objects.

**The tension.** If *every* mood is *primarily* *Gewesenheit* (A), "fear is *primarily* futural" (B) uses "primary" in a *different* sense — grounding-primacy vs. the tense of the emotion's *object*. The slight's pastness is object-time; *Gewesenheit* is an ecstasis, "not … a sequence of 'nows'" (cf. your own 809). You partly see this (line 1193: "Beneath these varying phenomenally dominant ecstases lies a having-been that every *pathos* shares"), but that two-level distinction is *yours*, not Heidegger's, and must be named and defended where a Heidegger reader will pounce — the element you call the section's "heaviest ontological weight" (line 1199).

## 3. Tier 2 — under-support, exegesis, engagement, originality

### T2-1. The synchronic/diachronic split: the *De Insomniis* mechanism reads as *synchronic*.
The dissertation files the *De Insomniis* loop in the **diachronic** register (line 1150: "the *De Insomniis* phenomenon belongs to the diachronic register, not to the synchronic register"). But the cluster's two readers of that exact passage treat it as a *within-episode* (synchronic) mechanism — Moss: the appetite vies, *then* impairs intellect, *then* one acts, all in one pass (Moss, *Akrasia*, pp. 137–38, "default yielding"); Dow likewise grounds the persisting pull in evaluative *phantasmata* whose motivational consequences are necessary, with reason "covered over … by degrees" (Dow, *Passions*, p. 198 + ch. 10). **Defend the diachronic assignment against the within-episode reading, or concede a synchronic feedback** (you yourself flag the undischarged individuation at line 1152). *See `contested-readings.md` §9.*

### T2-3. The tri-identity (*DA* III.7 431a13–14): **Moss deploys your keystone against you.**
You build *resonant epithymia* on the identity (line 876: "I would go a step further and include the *orektikon* in this identification as well"). Moss uses the *same* "one in substrate, different in being" identity to conclude the seat of passion is **"incapable of belief"** (Moss, *AG*, p. 98). So the identity-claim has a co-deployer (good) but also yields an objection you must answer (the seat can't bear *doxa*). *See `contested-readings.md` §3.*
