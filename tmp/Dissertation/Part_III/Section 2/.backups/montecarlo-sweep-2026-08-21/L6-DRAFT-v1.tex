The scope is the first limit, and it is stated first because it is also an argument. This
study measures the eye and the retrospective report, and nothing else: no heart signal
survived the equipment, no brain signal survived reprocessing at conventional thresholds, and
the vendor's cognitive-load figure was never admissible. What the narrowing leaves behind is
instructive. The published headline for this data rested on the most elaborate recording in
the room, the EEG, and did not survive its own reprocessing; the strongest physiological
association in the study comes instead from the simplest measurement available---where the
eye pointed, scored against where that participant's eye usually pointed.

One limit belongs to the platform rather than to this analysis, and it is stated here so that
any future study using the same equipment inherits it knowingly. The headset's heart-rate and
heart-rate-variability sensors must rest against the forehead, and the housing is shaped to a
narrow range of facial structures. On a number of participants---most consistently those of
Asian descent---the sensor housing did not sit flush against the skin, the required contact
was never established, and both channels were removed from the analysis for that reason. This
is an equipment finding, not a null result: nothing was measured and found flat; the
measurement never became possible. A headset that cannot take a forehead reading from a
substantial share of its users constrains what any study built on it can measure, whoever
runs it and whatever it asks.

The statistical bounds are those of twelve people recorded on two generations of
instrumentation. Both rounds enter every pooled analysis, and the pooling is not a
convenience: split by round, all eight of the load-bearing criterion coefficients point the
predicted way in both rounds, and neither round alone would carry the section. In the first
round, gaze deviation reaches $+$0.475 against reported boredom and eye closure $+$0.420,
directions without significance at eight participants; in the second, recorded at the
sensor's full rate, gaze deviation reaches $+$0.868 against boredom and $-$0.928 against
engagement---significant under the within-participant permutation despite four
participants---while closure at $+$0.538 and $-$0.452 remains directional. Together the
thirty-six episodes give the section its findings, and the split shows the findings are not
the property of either round.\footnote{The full per-round table appears in the appendix. The
one test four participants cannot support at any strength is the paired within-round
comparison, whose exact floor at four pairs is 0.125; nothing of that kind is claimed for the
second round alone.} Every analysis is episode-level:
one summary number per episode set against one set of ratings, so nothing in this section
time-locks a moment of experience to a moment of signal. One further bound deserves its own
sentence, because the raw numbers invite a mistake. How much of an episode a person spends
with the eyes closed varies enormously between people---8.2% and 77.8% occur on the same film
in the same round---and the within-participant scoring absorbs that spread for every test
this section reports, but nothing absorbs it for a raw comparison of one participant's
percentage against another's. Wherever such raw values appear side by side in this section,
they describe; they do not test.

The viewing order is confounded by design, and the design had its reason. Ten of the twelve
participants watched the boring video sample last: after the first two sessions, the order
was fixed on the hypothesis that the boring sample depressed whatever followed it, and the
published account of the study says so.\footnote{The published account is reproduced in
Appendix ******.} The two participants who watched before the change are the only control
the data contain, and their episodes ran against the order account---the last film each
watched carried the lower eye closure---but two participants are a check, not an argument,
and the section has claimed nothing stronger from them. The two rounds, finally, are two
generations of instrumentation: a different engine, a different axis convention, logging at
roughly 3 Hz against 120, a different recording window, and a movement instruction given in
the first round and not the second. Raw magnitudes are therefore never compared across rounds
anywhere in this section; what crosses rounds is the within-participant shape of each
person's three episodes, which the scoring protects from the rate difference by construction.

Two limits remain, and neither is softened. The first is the mismatch between the survey and
one of the structures this analysis reads through it, and it rests on two facts that stand
together. The first fact: the survey's items operationalize a definition on which boredom is
caused by its environment---they ask how bored the participant was by the video and when that
boredom arrived, questions with a culprit built into their grammar. The second fact: the
second form has no culprit---its surface is genuinely occupied, and its emptiness, on
Heidegger's account, arises from the person rather than from anything in the room---and yet
this analysis has used the survey to reach that form, because the survey ratings collected
during the break after each episode are where the second form's belated verdict lands, the
only channel in the study that records what an episode amounted to for the person after it
ended. The mismatch is what makes the divergence legible: an instrument shaped to the first
form registers the second only as a verdict that contradicts the recorded surface. It also
remains a mismatch, though a narrower one than it first appears. Because the survey asks
boredom and engagement as two separate questions, an episode both engaging and empty at once
can surface in it, and in four episodes it did. What the survey lacks is not the question but
the definition that would make such an answer signify: under the definition its items
operationalize, boredom and engagement oppose each other, and a participant affirming both
reads as noise or error; under Heidegger's definition, on which the boring holds us and
leaves us empty at once, the same answer is the second form's exact signature. The instrument
could record the conjunction; only the frame could read it. The second limit
is the caution the frame itself imposes. An attunement cannot be ascertained and ought not to be; nothing in
this section makes that claim, and its strongest sentences claim structure, resemblance, and
grammar, never the mood itself. The category caution is the single limit no design revision
could lift; every other limit named here is something a future study could repair.
