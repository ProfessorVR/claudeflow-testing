In the physiological experiment, every node of the chain can be pointed to. A\textsubscript{0}, the situation
of exposure, is a scheduled session: an electrode cap, a headset fitted over it, a calibration
routine, then seventeen minutes of footage the participant did not choose, watched because
watching is what the appointment is for. A\textsubscript{1} and A\textsubscript{2}, perception
and the charged image, arrive with the field to themselves---the headset hands the footage the
participant's entire view, and the room behind it offers nothing else to look at.
A\textsubscript{3} is the completed cognitive engagement with that image, and its anatomy
matters here: the image can be held under three orientational modes---intellection, memory, and
discursive thought---and through any of the three runs \textit{doxa}, the involuntary
taking-as-true, which is not a fourth mode but the commitment that ratifies what any mode
presents. A crossing to A\textsubscript{4} follows in
every episode, because the chain runs always; the question, here as in the deployment, is which
potentiality the crossing actualizes. The situation narrows the field of potentialities
available for actualization, and it does nothing more than narrow it---a field confers no worth
on what it contains. Toward the film it furnishes watching; beside watching it leaves standing
the counter-moves of a seated body---closing the eyes, shifting, drifting toward sleep. Which
of those was worth actualizing was settled in every episode by the participant, never by the
field, and the episodes show every one of them chosen. Sleep, where it arrived, is not missing
data or a failure of the protocol but an act with a structure of its own, and no episode of the
thirty-six presents a chain that went nowhere.

Boredom holds no position on that chain, and the analysis owes an explicit statement of where
it lives instead. Boredom is an attunement: not a state produced at one of the chain's stations
but the already-prevailing disclosure of the situation within which anything can matter at
all---felt and cognitive at once, which is why no single node can contain it. So, on the
reading this analysis adopts, is interest: not one determinate desire among others but the
situation mattering rather than flattening, the counter-attunement to boredom's emptiness. The
account of the emotions carried from the first part of this dissertation tells in favor of
reading it so: determinate emotions crystallize out of a prior, diffuse mattering when
\textit{doxa} ratifies what the image presents, and it is that diffuse level---the pull things
have, or have lost---that a film sustains or fails to sustain across seventeen minutes. An
attunement accordingly reaches the chain at three places. It shows at A\textsubscript{2}, in
the charge: whether the furnished image arrives with pull or arrives drained belongs to the
attunement in which the participant sits, not to the mechanism and not to the footage alone. It
shows at the crossing, in the worth: Dasein, the participant---never the field, never the
film---determines what is worth actualizing among the potentialities left standing. It becomes
sayable only at A\textsubscript{3}, in the \textit{doxa}: ``this is boring'' is not a mode of
engagement but a ratification, involuntary as every \textit{doxa} is---one cannot simply decide
that a film was interesting---and answerable, like every \textit{doxa}, to how it was
(\textit{De Anima} III.3, 427b17--21).

The three forms of boredom differ in how that ratification behaves over time, and the
difference is what this experiment's two kinds of evidence are positioned to catch. In the
first form \textit{doxa} affirms at once and names its culprit: the film is boring, the
affirmation arrives within minutes, and it holds unchanged through the retrospective report. In
the second form the affirmation is sincerely withheld in the moment---the surface is genuinely
occupied, and a participant asked mid-episode would deny boredom and mean it; the ratification
arrives only afterward, the way Heidegger's guest finds only at home that the evening was
boring after all (109), when memory and discursive reflection re-engage the episode and
\textit{doxa} can no longer withhold its verdict. The apparatus sits on both sides of that
delay. The eye measures record the episode while it runs, before any ratification exists---the
ongoing actualization of watching, or of its counter-moves, as the body performs them. The
survey, administered in the break after each film, records ratifications: every rating is a
\textit{doxa}, delivered at exactly the point where the second form's belated recognition falls
due. Two kinds of evidence that can disagree about one episode are therefore not two meters of
a single quantity, one of them faulty. They stand on opposite sides of \textit{doxa}'s
commitment, and where they part company, the disagreement has the same shape as the second form
itself---which is a resemblance the analysis will use, never an identification it will claim.
The three forms station the being-left-empty accordingly: at a nameable object that refuses, in
the form whose ratification is immediate; nowhere in the run at all, in the form whose chain
completes well while the emptiness forms in the person rather than in anything furnished; or
across the whole field at once, in the form this study approaches only as analog.

What separates this situation from the classroom deployment's is a single subtraction, and it
is worth being concrete about what was subtracted. The classroom deployment ran its world in a
window on a desktop machine, and a second window was always one gesture away; students who
stopped watching left for the same footage on YouTube, and wrote as much in the course surveys.
The experiment closes that gesture. The headset admits no second window, the proctor sits in
the room, and the session ends when the protocol ends, so a participant who has stopped
watching cannot leave the film for a better world---the counter-moves that remain are those of
a seated body. The hold itself is measurable in the survey. Participants reported how long each
film felt, and in twenty-five of the thirty-six episodes the film felt longer than its
seventeen minutes: held time ran long almost everywhere, whatever the episode held. How far it
ran tracked what the participant reported---a rank correlation of $+$0.494 with the boredom
rating---while leaving no trace in either eye measure, so the lengthening belongs to the
situation as lived and not to anything the eyes were doing. Quaranta, reading film viewership
through the same lecture course, states the principle on which the whole arrangement rests:
``the film alone is insufficient (in Heideggerian terms, moods are neither subjective nor
objective)''---the viewer must be disposed to let the mood take them (37). A film cannot bore a
person by itself, any more than this experiment's three films could; what an apparatus can do
is hold the encounter open long enough for whatever attunement is in play to become legible,
and that is the whole of what this apparatus does.

The walkthrough this analysis has been preparing can now be performed on a single episode. S08
sat through the clinical footage in the first round. Perception had the footage to itself for
seventeen minutes. The charged image was held for the episode's whole length: S08 followed the
footage, attended to it, took it up. The body's side of the evidence says so in two ways.
First, the eyes stayed open. S08's eyes were shut for 11.3% of the episode, within a point of
S08's most-open episode, the interest film at 10.5%; and no third of the episode---early,
middle, or late---saw closure above 14%. Second, the gaze stayed put. Across the episode S08's
eye held a median 7.5 degrees from S08's own habitual direction, the steadiest watching in any
of S08's three episodes: the corresponding figure is 10.3 degrees for the interest episode and
13.5 for the episode S08 rated most boring.\footnote{That episode's gaze figure is computed
from the 42\% of the episode S08's eyes were open, since where the eyes are closed there is no
gaze to record.} Watching was actualized at the crossing and stayed actualized; nothing in the eye
channel breaks off, wanders wide, or shuts down. Then the film ended, the headset came off, and
the ratification arrived. Asked in the break, S08 rated the episode 7 of 9 for boredom against
3 for engagement, reported fighting sleep at 7, dated the boredom from the episode's second
minute, felt the seventeen minutes as twenty, and came out one point more tired than going in.
Every item of the verdict is coherent boredom---and none of it is visible in what the eyes did.
The chain did not fail anywhere: the image was charged, the engagement ran, watching was chosen
and sustained to the end. What is hollow is not the mechanism but what the completed run
amounted to, and only \textit{doxa}---involuntary, answerable to how it was---could say so.

S07's episode of the same footage reads the same way in every bodily channel and differently in
the one place that matters more. The eyes: shut for 10.6% of the episode, S07's lowest; gaze at
a median 7.0 degrees from S07's habitual direction, again the steadiest of S07's three. The
break: sleep fought at only 3, fatigue one point lower after the episode than before it---and
then the two ratings. S07 rated the episode 7 of 9 for engagement and 6 of 9 for boredom, both
high, in the same breath, on the same sheet. The survey asks boredom and engagement as two
separate questions rather than as two ends of one scale, so a divided verdict is something it
can record, and S07's verdict divided. \textit{Doxa} is involuntary; where an episode genuinely
was both---surface occupied, depth gone empty---a two-item instrument permits both
ratifications, and S07 delivered both. R2-02's episode makes a third of the same shape: eyes
shut for under 4% of the episode---fewer than forty seconds of its seventeen minutes---a gaze
median of 8.3 degrees, the steadiest of R2-02's three episodes, against 8.5 and 11.2 degrees
in the other two, and a verdict of 8 for boredom against 4 for engagement, sleep fought at 7,
the episode felt at thirty minutes against its seventeen. Three participants, and in each the
same structure: an episode the eye tracker records as occupied---watched steadily, eyes
open---that the participant's own ratification declares empty. In Heidegger's vocabulary this
is the shape of the second form, being bored \textit{with} something: nothing in the situation
to point to as the culprit, an occupation genuinely carried out, and a being-left-empty that
forms in the person rather than in anything the episode furnished. The analysis reads these
three episodes as bearing that structure; it does not claim the attunement itself has been
measured.

Two qualifications confine the claim, and both strengthen it. The first concerns why a
disagreement between the eyes and the ratings can carry any weight at all. If the eye measures
bore no reliable relation to what participants report, an episode in which the eyes read
engaged while the participant reported boredom would mean nothing---one more mismatch from
measures that mismatch constantly. That is not the situation. Across all thirty-six episodes
the two eye measures track the ratings closely: gaze deviation rises with reported boredom at
$+$0.590 and eye closure at $+$0.475, and both fall correspondingly as reported engagement
rises. Against that background of agreement, the nine episodes in which the eyes ran engaged
while the verdict said bored are not random mismatches between unrelated measures; they are
departures from an otherwise consistent pattern. The category also does not depend
on how the two measures are combined: closure alone yields twelve such episodes, gaze alone
eight, the two together nine. The second qualification is that the survey could not have said more than it
did. Its seven items operationalize a definition on which boredom is an aversive state
attributed to its environment---a first-form definition, in the vocabulary this section has
carried---and an instrument built to that shape can render a second-form structure only as the
kinds of verdict just walked through: a ratification that contradicts the recorded surface, or
one that divides against itself. The divergence, in other words, is not a malfunction the
analysis has chosen to read charitably. It is what a first-form instrument produces when a
structure it cannot name is present, and its legibility depends on both halves of the apparatus
doing their work---the eyes recording the occupied surface before any ratification exists, the
survey collecting the ratification where reflection delivers it.

The first form needs no special care, because the survey was built for it and finds it everywhere.
Being bored \textit{by} something is the form with a nameable culprit and an immediate
ratification, and the ratifications came fast: of the twelve episodes participants rated their
own most boring, boredom was dated within the first five minutes in eleven---the twelfth
belongs to the participant who reported never becoming bored at all---and within the first
minute in five.\footnote{Ten of the twelve were episodes of the tutorial; S04's was the
clinical footage, and S06's the interest film.} The fight that follows the affirmation is just
as visible. How hard a participant reported fighting sleep tracks their reported boredom at
$+$0.770 across the thirty-six episodes and runs against reported engagement at $-$0.749; set
each participant's own most-boring episode against their own most-engaging one, and eleven of
the twelve fought sleep harder on the boring side, none fought harder on the engaging side, and
one gave the same figure for both---odds of about one in a thousand if the direction were
chance, and the strongest result twelve participants can express. The fight is not confined to
the survey's own pages: the harder the reported fight, the more of the episode the eyes
actually spent closed, at $+$0.665. All of this is the first form registering exactly as
Heidegger describes it---the culprit named, the fight open and datable. It registers so
abundantly because finding it was the survey's very design: the items were built under a
definition that locates boredom's cause in the environment---the first form's own
structure---and the experiment set out to measure exactly the state those items describe. The
abundance is the instrument succeeding at its task.

The tempting reading of the gaze finding is that a bored eye goes searching---that gaze
deviation operationalizes Heidegger's passing the time, the look that leaves the film to hunt
the room for anything better. The data withdraw that reading. If bored watching were continuous
searching, departures should grow more frequent as reported boredom rises. They do not: the
rate of departures bears no detectable relation to the ratings, at $-$0.08, while how far the
eye sits from its habitual direction rises strongly, at $+$0.590.\footnote{The two figures come
from differently constructed references: the departure rate counts crossings of a fixed
threshold measured from the headset's forward axis, while the deviation median is measured
against each participant's own habitual direction. Every gaze figure in this section names its
reference.} What the gaze data support is not a hunt around the field but a settling
elsewhere---the eye further from where that participant's watching normally sits. That settling
belongs to an expectation the study carried from its design. Boredom was hypothesized to show
itself behaviorally in two manifestations, and to show both in most bored episodes: a
low-arousal manifestation---drowsiness, the settling gaze, the eyes drifting shut---and a
high-arousal manifestation---restlessness, the body in agitated motion. The eye channel
documents the low-arousal side: the settling, the closures, and sleep where it arrived. Passing
the time is not a failure of the chain but its completion elsewhere: what the crossing at
A\textsubscript{4} actualizes in these episodes is the counter-move---the long excursion, the
eyes falling shut---rather than the watching the situation furnished toward the film. The
high-arousal manifestation is present in this study but not in its numbers. The room offered
that counter-move no exit---no second window, no task, nothing to walk to---and the protocol
narrowed it further: the eight first-round participants, who carry most of this evidence, were
asked to refrain from agitated movement so the EEG would not be corrupted, an instruction the
second round did not receive. Movements occurred anyway, and they were observed by the
experiment proctor in most sessions. The counter-moves this analysis can read are those the eye
performs; the restlessness of the rest of the body remains outside its evidence.

The third form enters this analysis under a prohibition that comes from the frame itself.
Heidegger, addressing directly whether an attunement can be observed, rules the question out
twice over: ``Not only can an attunement not be ascertained, it ought not to be ascertained,
even if it were possible to do so'' (65)---because ascertaining brings to consciousness, and
consciousness does not leave an attunement as it was. The scholarship devoted to profound
boredom reaches the same conclusion from the other side. Elpidorou and Freeman, testing
whether Heidegger's deepest boredom can be identified with any construct psychology measures,
conclude that ``it cannot be seamlessly assimilated to any known category of boredom'' (178);
what surveys and instruments reach is state boredom, and state boredom, in their words, ``does
not shake us up'' (188)---it carries none of the third form's disclosive weight. Nothing
measured in this experiment could therefore evidence profound boredom, and none of it is claimed to.
What keeps the third form inside the analysis at all is Heidegger's own dependency claim:
``the first is itself presumably still rooted in the possibility of the third,'' since ``Only
because this constant possibility---the `it is boring for one'---lurks in the ground of Dasein
can man be bored or become bored by the things and people around him'' (156--57). Every
first-form episode in this study stands on the possibility of the form no instrument can
reach, and the most the eye data can honestly show is a person's first-form boredom pressed
to its own limit.

Heidegger's account of the first form contains the arc the eye data trace. The person who
becomes bored by something fights, and passing the time is the fight's name; its weapons are
whatever the situation leaves within reach. The fight does not win. We ``cannot annihilate it
by passing the time, however intensively''; boredom ``can return at any time''; and
``precisely when we set upon it with all the means at our disposal it becomes stubborn,
obstinate'' (79). A person who comes to know this about their own fight has two endings
available: surrender to the boredom, or escape from the situation that holds them in it. The
experiment's episodes contain both endings, and the eye data give them their timelines. S08
fought: boredom dated from the episode's first thirty seconds, sleep fought at 8 of 9---and
the eyes record the fight being lost by thirds, closed for 20.8% of the episode's first third,
61.6% of its second, and 90.5% of its last, the longest single closure running 124.5 seconds
against a longest of ten seconds anywhere else in S08's session. S05 barely fought at all:
sleep fought at 9, the eyes closed for 60.6% of the first third already and 100% of the
second, one closure running seven and a half minutes, begun before the episode's fifth minute.
Whether such collapses belong to the film or to the person is settled inside each participant's
own session. S05's other two episodes, rated 6 and 5 for boredom, contain no closure longer
than about one second: the collapse belongs to the one episode S05 rated 9 for boredom, not to
sitting in a headset as such---nor to the session wearing on, since S05's collapse came in the
session's middle episode, with an alert final episode after it. S07's closure rises across both
episodes S07 rated boring---the most-boring one and the first-viewed one, rated 7---and falls
across the episode S07 rated more engaging than boring. The pattern follows the reported
boredom of the episode, wherever that boredom fell: the section's organizing claim performed
in miniature. Sleep, where the surrender reached it, stands as a fourth terminal category
beside the three forms---boredom's escape, not its depth. A participant who has fallen asleep
has not descended into profound boredom; they have given up the fight and left the situation
by the one exit the room could not close.

The anomaly set completes the argument by testing the reading on which the material itself
carries the boredom. That reading has a source and a logic, and both deserve to be stated. The
tutorial was selected under the operative definition, which holds that state boredom derives
from material that is monotonous, redundant, or meaningless: an obsolete interface, already
familiar to the students, was chosen because such material raises the likelihood of boredom.
If boringness were a property sitting in the footage the way its runtime does, every viewer
would meet the same boringness, because a property of the material is present wherever the
material plays. The design itself never claimed that much---raising a likelihood is not
fixing an outcome---but the weaker claim already concedes the decisive point: what an episode
becomes is settled between the material and the person meeting it. The sessions put that
concession on display, and the keystone is S04. The tutorial that put S05's eyes shut for
seven and a half unbroken minutes---rated 9 for boredom, with sleep fought at 9---is the same
tutorial S04 rated 1 for boredom and 7 for engagement, the most engaging of S04's three
episodes: S04 never became bored in it, fought sleep at 1, kept the eyes open through all but
8.2% of it, and felt the seventeen minutes as eight---time contracted rather than stretched.
S04 emerged six points less tired than going in, the only substantial restoration in the
study, while S05, held to the same material, collapsed. A property carried by the footage
cannot be present for S05 and absent for S04 while the footage plays identically for both.
What differed was what each brought to the meeting---the attunement each sat in---and what
each episode then became. This does not relocate the cause into the person, as though the
footage contributed nothing; it restates what Quaranta's principle already held, that the
mood of an episode arises between material and viewer and belongs to neither side alone. It
is exactly that between which an environment-located definition cannot describe.

The rest of the set repeats the lesson from different directions. S06, R2-03, and S03 each
watched in the study's standard order: the interest film first, the clinical footage second,
the tutorial last. S06 was bored by the film selected to interest---8 of 9, with boredom
dated from its first minute---and rated the tutorial, selected to bore, the most engaging of
S06's three episodes. R2-03 was bored by nothing: across the interest, clinical, and tutorial
episodes in that order, engagement ran 8, 8, and 6, boredom never rose above 3, and no onset
was ever reported. S03 was bored at twenty-five seconds by an episode that felt like an hour,
three and a half times its clock length. In four episodes across three participants, boredom
and engagement were affirmed at once, neither low---verdicts the two-item survey could record
because it never assumed the two exclude each other, and for which a boringness carried by
the material makes no provision.\footnote{Prior familiarity does not sort the set either.
Four of the twelve participants reported prior exposure to medical footage, and their ratings
of the clinical episodes sit beside the other eight's: mean boredom 6.0 against 5.1 of 9,
engagement 4.3 against 4.5, descriptive at four against eight.} These cases are the reason
this section analyzes episodes rather than films. Averaged with the rest of the cohort, S06
would appear only as a viewer slightly diluting the interest film's mean, S03's felt hour
would vanish into an average felt duration, and R2-03, whom nothing bored, would disappear
into slightly lowered averages. Read as episodes, each is a measurement of what this material
became for this person on this afternoon. The episode is the unit on which every number in
this section has been computed, and these are the cases that show why it has to be.

The fatigue arithmetic comes last because it is the easiest to misread. Boredom, as an
attunement, does not deplete and does not refresh; it is the state Heidegger defines,
``that which holds us in limbo and yet leaves us empty'' (87), and an attunement is not
something a fatigue scale measures. The two fatigue questions measure tiredness, before and
after each episode, and tiredness is a state of the body. Across all thirty-six episodes,
fatigue rose after twenty-five, held level after four, and fell after seven: sitting in the
hold of a headset for seventeen minutes tires most people somewhat, whatever they report
about the episode, and that wear belongs to the being-held (\textit{Hingehaltenheit}), the
situation's grip, rather than to boredom. How much fatigue an episode added, though, tracks
the boredom reported in it, at $+$0.521, and the eye closure recorded during it, at $+$0.418.
Read against Heidegger's two moments, the pattern is an echo, not a measurement of the
attunement: the holding wears on everyone, and the added tiredness runs with the emptying
(\textit{Leergelassenheit}, being-left-empty). The study's two extremes are S03 and S04.
S03 emerged five points more tired from the episode S03 rated 9 for boredom and felt as an
hour: emptied. S04 emerged six points less tired from the episode S04 rated 1 for boredom and
7 for engagement: filled rather than emptied, the one episode in the study that restored its
participant.\footnote{S03's felt-hour episode and S04's restoring episode were both episodes
of the tutorial.} An episode can empty its participant while the same material fills another,
and that is where this analysis has arrived from every direction it has taken: the emptying
and the filling belong to persons in situations, and the survey's numbers, the eye tracker's
fractions, and Heidegger's vocabulary have been found describing the same thing.
