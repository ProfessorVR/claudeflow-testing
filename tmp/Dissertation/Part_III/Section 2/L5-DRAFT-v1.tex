The data demonstrate an index of divergence. Four measurements from each episode build it:
the proportion of the episode the eyes spent closed, the distance the gaze wandered from that
participant's own habitual direction, and the boredom and engagement the participant reported
in the break. The two eye measures, each scored against the participant's own three-episode
baseline, combine into a single reading of how engaged the body's watching was. The two
ratings, scored the same way, combine into a single reading of how engaged the participant
said the episode was. The index is the first reading minus the second, and the sign is the
finding. A positive value marks an episode in which the body watched more engagedly than the
person reported---the direction the nine divergent episodes share. A negative value marks the
reverse, a body less engaged than the report claims---the direction of the study's three
reverse episodes. A value near zero marks agreement between the two, the common case at
twenty-four episodes of the thirty-six. What the index differences is never two physiological
channels but the two layers this analysis has kept apart: the surface recorded during the
episode, and the verdict delivered after it.

Read across the thirty-six episodes, the index sorts bored watching into three
states---restless search, occupied-but-hollow, and withdrawn---with sleep standing outside
the three as a terminal exit. The evidence behind each state is worth stating separately,
because it is not equal. The withdrawn state has the most direct evidence: eye closure speaks
for it immediately, and the study's largest single events belong to it, a 449-second closure
in S05's session and a 124-second closure in S08's. The occupied-but-hollow state is
evidenced by the nine divergent episodes, and those episodes carry evidential weight only
because of the agreement established around them: across the study as a whole the two eye
measures rise and fall with participants' own reports---gaze deviation at $+$0.590 against
reported boredom, eye closure at $+$0.475---so an episode in which both run engaged while the
participant ratifies boredom stands out as an exception to that documented agreement, not as
noise in the eye tracking.\footnote{The agreement is documented two ways: the pooled
correlations just cited, and the within-participant comparison, in which each eye measure
follows the participant's own most-boring-against-most-engaging distinction in ten of twelve
cases. The nine divergent episodes are exceptions against that background, and an exception
is identifiable as such only where the surrounding pattern holds.} Two of the nine episodes
are S06's, and S06 is the participant whose recordings ran against S06's own ratings on every
measure that moved: during the episode S06 rated most boring, S06's gaze held tighter rather
than wider, S06's eyes were closed less, and S06 emerged less tired, with the sleep item tied.
For S06 alone, then, an episode in which the eyes read engaged while the rating said bored may
reflect that standing disagreement rather than the structure the other divergent episodes
carry. Setting S06's two aside leaves seven divergent episodes across five participants, and
the occupied-but-hollow state keeps its evidence. The restless-search state---the
high-arousal manifestation---is present in the data, but only the eye's share of it: the gaze
channel registers the bored eye departing from where that participant's watching normally
sits, and episodes such as S04's with the tutorial show the same channel recording wide,
active looking when arousal runs high. What the data lack is the body's share---the
first-round protocol asked participants to refrain from agitated movement, and the movements
that occurred anyway were observed by the proctor and instrumented by nothing. Sleep-exit,
finally, has two documented routes: progressive, as in S08's episode, where closure climbed
by thirds; and immediate, as in S05's, where the episode's second third ran fully closed.

A closing fact bounds everything the index promises. Twelve participants ground
within-participant claims and nothing beyond them: every number in this section describes
these episodes and these twelve people, with no population estimate behind it. What the index
adds is portability---the camera it reads is already inside every headset of this kind, and
each participant serves as their own baseline---so the question it leaves open, how often the
occupied-but-hollow structure appears when watching is mandated at scale, awaits a larger
cohort.
