## cross-cutting/criterion-validity-appendix

LEFT: Spearman rho against Pearson r for every channel and criterion. Points sitting on the dashed identity line mean the rank and linear coefficients agree, i.e. the monotonic relationship is also close to linear. Median absolute difference is 0.032. That is why Spearman is the reported statistic: where the two agree, the rank test costs almost nothing in power, and it is chosen for robustness - no assumption of linearity, and no single extreme value able to dominate - rather than to manufacture a result. Circles are the boredom criterion, triangles engagement; grey are the APPENDIX-ONLY channels. RIGHT: the analytic p from the library against the within-subject permutation p, on a log axis, one line per test. EVERY analytic p is too small, by roughly 3-8x, and the inflation applies to Pearson exactly as it does to Spearman - so it is a property of the DESIGN, not an artifact of ranking. The cause: the 36 paired points are 12 participants contributing 3 films each, and within-subject z-scoring makes the dependence structural, since forcing three values to mean 0 and sd 1 means knowing two nearly determines the third. The permutation null preserves that clustering by shuffling film labels only WITHIN each participant. 3 test(s) cross alpha=0.05 when corrected: gaze deviation (device-forward baseline) vs boredom; gaze deviation (device-forward baseline) vs engagement; % time >10 deg off center (device-forward baseline) vs engagement. Both p-values are retained in the sibling CSV for every test.

## cross-cutting/gaze-baselines

The same angular deviation computed against three references, reported side by side so the choice is made in the open rather than buried. FORWARD is the round's own device axis and restores the published definition (`1 - X`, i.e. 1 - cos theta). FILE is each cell's own median gaze direction, which the 2026 reimplementation substituted and which removes any sustained posture by construction, since the reference moves with the participant. SUBJECT is one median per participant across their three films, preserving individual headset seating while still exposing between-film drift. Reported against the analyst's own prediction: the faithful restoration is not automatically the better instrument, and on the criterion test the FORWARD baseline does not survive at all. RULED 2026-08-04, REVISED 2026-08-05 (D-12): NEITHER baseline is primary; each is assigned to the question it answers, and every gaze result in this analysis names the baseline it was computed on. STIMULUS SEPARATION -> DEVICE-FORWARD BASELINE. AGREEMENT WITH SELF-REPORT -> PER-PARTICIPANT BASELINE, with the per-file baseline reported alongside it. The agreement analysis is entirely within-subject, and the per-participant baseline is the only one of the three built the same way: it removes what differs between people and does not matter - how the headset happened to sit on that face - while preserving what differs within a person and does matter, whether they held the eye differently during one film than another. The per-file baseline recenters inside each episode and erases that between-film difference by construction. The device-forward baseline keeps the difference but carries the headset-fit variation with it, which is why it is used for stimulus separation, where between-person differences are not what the test compares. The per-participant baseline was computed for this figure from v3 onward and was scored against self-report for the first time in the v5 pass (X-06), where it is the strongest criterion channel in the study. Gaze is EYE-IN-HEAD, not head pose: a participant who turns their head away from the film registers no deviation here. ## cross-cutting/gaze-swing-vs-offset

Each point is one participant-film. The horizontal axis is how widely the eye swings left and right; the vertical axis is how far it sits above or below center on average, with negative meaning looking down. The two are independent, and conflating them is what made the earlier probes contradict each other. A participant far to the RIGHT is swinging: their median vector can sit almost dead center while they are barely ever actually looking forward, because symmetric swings cancel in a component-wise median. A participant far DOWN is holding a sustained posture: a narrow spread around an off-axis position, which any per-file-recentered measure removes entirely. Neither published feature separates these - gaze deviation variance sees part of the swinging and is blind to the offset, and the median vector reports the widest swinger in the cohort as its quietest cell. Descriptive figure, no test. Round 1 circles, Round 2 triangles; Round 2 n=4 is DESCRIPTIVE. Gaze is EYE-IN-HEAD, not head pose: a participant who turns their head away from the film registers no deviation here.
