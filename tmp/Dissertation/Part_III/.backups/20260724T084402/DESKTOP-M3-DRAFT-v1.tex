\section{The Deployment Record}

% --- M3.1 The threshold (drafted 2026-07-24 — in review)

The two strata of the cohort do not complain about the same things. Install
and compatibility complaints concentrate in the video-only stratum---5.4 per
hundred responses there against 0.9 among app users---and hardware-access
complaints follow the same pattern (2.2 against 0.9), as does talk of never
using the platform at all (5.1 against zero). Sixty-one students, a quarter
of the 241, answered that they could not run the application; they appear in
the corpus only on the video route. The obstacles they name sit before the
world rather than in it: the Windows-only build---``More inclusive for
macbook users :('' (W25-R042/Q9)---the download and installation the
application requires, and an account gate that could itself fail: ``Make sure
the sign-in is actually possible'' (W26-R059/Q9). For these students the
virtual hospital never opened; their friction story was over before the
first room could load.

App users tell a different friction story, and it happens inside the world:
crashes (29 complaints corpus-wide; 4.1 per hundred app-stratum responses
against 2.5), buffering and latency (25; 3.0 against 2.7), and navigation
(21; 2.8 against 2.0). These interior complaints are the ones the analysis
was designed to test. The coding design, fixed before any response was read,
registered five predictions of where the deployed world would stall its
users, drawn from a walkthrough of the build on a development machine:
buffer latency, wayfinding, dark-room legibility, a must-stand-close viewing
constraint, and avatar occlusion. Three held. Latency and wayfinding are
confirmed outright, and the navigation complaints co-occur eleven times with
requests for guidance; dark-room legibility is attested in ten complaints of
blurry or hard-to-see footage. The must-stand-close constraint surfaces
twice, and avatar occlusion not at all---not disconfirmed but voided, since
solo desktop use removes the other avatars that could occlude. The corpus,
however, is dominated by frictions no prediction named: the crashes (29),
the installer and its compatibility wall (26), hardware access (13), and a
residual catalogue of 32 further named frictions. Counted across the corpus,
the predicted frictions total 58 coded applications; the unpredicted
total 100.

The shape of that imbalance is the finding. All five predictions concern the
world's interior, framed from a vantage for which the world was already
open: a walkthrough begins past the threshold, on a machine that already
runs the build, under an account that already works. From that vantage two
whole classes of foreclosure are invisible---everything that stops a student
before the world opens (the installer and compatibility complaints, the
hardware wall), and everything that varies with the student's machine rather
than the developer's (the crashes). The deployment's largest attested
foreclosures are exactly these. In the chain's terms the finding touches
A\textsubscript{0} alone: availability is the horizon's deployment face, and
for a quarter of the class the world closed at the installer, before
anything in it could solicit so much as a look.
