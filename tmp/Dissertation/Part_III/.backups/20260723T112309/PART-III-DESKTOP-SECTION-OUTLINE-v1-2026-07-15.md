# Part III — Desktop Section Outline (v1, 2026-07-15) — MODULAR DRAFT FOR EDIT

**Hypothesis spine (selected):** H1 — *the completion hypothesis*: in the desktop clinical-immersion
world, presence is achieved but not sufficient; the chain is solicited at A₂/A₃ and stalls at A₃→A₄;
boredom, flight, and the justification-demand are the phenomenal registers of that one stall.
Pedagogical effectiveness on the screen route is a function of chain-completion, not immersive bandwidth.
**Folded-in clause (H3):** veri(dis)similitude's second face — the desktop world must be *different
enough from the video of itself* to justify existing, and that difference can only be made of what
video cannot render: place, task, and the WE.
**Elevated focus (author directive, 2026-07-15):** the **spatial proximity-voice co-watching**
affordance is the desktop version's greatest affordance relative to BOTH YouTube AND the published VR
version (which had no collaborative capability). Since rhetorical incorporation is dual in being

is **as deployed** the program's only route on which co-disclosedness is available. Scope the claim
de facto, not de jure (AUTHOR 2026-07-15): spatial proximity-voice is technically implementable in
VR, but VR development ceased with the headset route, so the affordance exists only in the desktop
build — a developed asset of this route, not a metaphysical exclusion from the others. The WE thread runs through

## M1. Background — The Desktop Route in the Literature
*Function: frame the desktop environment as a deliberate pedagogical modality with its own
affordance profile — never a degraded HMD. Establish continuity between the education literature's
own findings and the dissertation's frame.* **This module is the primary [LIT-SLOT] zone — every
sub-module is provisional until the incoming index entry is perused.**
`MAP:` new module (existing outline has no literature-review movement; §III.0.2–.3 gesture at Mark only).

- **M1.1 Desktop 3D as a modality in its own right.** Affordances derive from representational
  fidelity + learner interaction, with presence and co-presence as their consequences, not HMD
  properties (Dalgarno & Lee 2010; their own 2012 follow-up largely failed to confirm task-specific
  benefit links and self-flags enthusiast bias — the model named alongside its own inconclusive
  self-test); "pedagogical immersion" as a learning-design construct, argued pedagogy-first against
  the model's technology-led derivation, with the inverted-U caution (Fowler 2015); desktop-specific
  dual pathways — affective (presence→motivation) and cognitive (usability→cognitive benefits),
  both converging on self-efficacy (Makransky & Petersen 2019); usability as the linchpin gating
  all downstream uptake within desktop-3D, no IVR arm so no sufficiency claim (Merchant et al.
  2012); desktop presence at or above published immersive-VR norms, presence relocated from
  hardware to fidelity + interactive control (Dubovi et al. 2017). `[LIT-SLOT — FILLED 2026-07-16,
  verified against ped-sec-04/24, 05, 11, 16, 18; Barrett et al. 2022 reserved for M1.3]`
- **M1.2 Presence on screens, and the missing bridge.** Medium-general presence — the perceptual
  illusion of nonmediation, a property of the person, not the medium, defined across television,
  film, and VR alike (Lombard & Ditton 1997 — deliberate continuity with the VR article's own
  anchor; their "we are together" transportation subtype and obtrusive-medium cause are banked for
  M1.6/M4.3 and M3.1 respectively); immersion as something the individual experiences, against
  Slater's objective-technology account, with the founding instrument's own admission of "no direct
  evidence" for a presence→learning link (Witmer & Singer 1998); the determinants shown user-side —
  computer self-efficacy the strongest predictor of self-presence in a desktop virtual world (Chow
  2016, scoped to self-presence, not spatial presence); the psychological-determination reading is
  the cluster's clearest cross-tradition majority verdict (DA-03), not a curated selection.
  Calleja's incorporation as the education literature's unmade bridge — which Part II has already
  built, and this section cashes empirically. `[LIT-SLOT — FILLED 2026-07-16, verified against
  ped-sec-01, 02, 03]` `[PART I–II: presence-as-floor (Part II §3);
  incorporation re-grounded (Part II §2)]`
- **M1.3 More presence is not more learning.** The comparison evidence, the field's weighted
  verdict rather than a stray result (DA-01 — six independent units, two at review level): the
  identical simulation ported desktop→HMD raises presence (d=1.30–2.20) and lowers knowledge
  gains, EEG overload as the mechanism trace (Makransky, Terkildsen & Mayer 2019); a
  content-matched slideshow beats IVR on factual learning (d=0.92–1.12) while IVR wins every
  affect measure, the deficit remediable by generative scaffolding (Parong & Mayer 2018), and the
  mediation follow-up closes the loop — presence-linked high-arousal affect and extraneous load
  significantly mediate the retention loss (Parong & Mayer 2021); twenty years of media comparison
  yield no reliable IVR learning advantage ("methods, not media" — Mayer, Makransky & Parong
  2022); learning *accuracy* statistically equivalent across VR/desktop-3D/2D versions of one task
  while response time, fixation behavior, and attrition differ (Barrett et al. 2022 —
  surname-corrected 2026-07-16 against the PDF's own citation line; equivalence verified as
  accuracy-specific; 2D arm n=8, flagged) — the field's empirical mirror of
  presence≠incorporation. The mechanism dispute (affective route, Makransky & Lilleholt 2018 ↔
  cognitive-load route, Parong & Mayer 2021; CAMIL holds both paths open) stays in the entry,
  cite-where-used at M4.1. The skills-yield of screen-based virtual patients: nothing added on
  knowledge (SMD=0.11, ns) but moderate-to-large gains on skills and clinical reasoning (SMD=0.90)
  (Kononowicz et al. 2019), over the simulation-wide base rate — large pooled effects vs. no
  intervention across 609 studies, the sim-vs-nothing question declared exhausted (Cook et al.
  2011; scope note: technology-enhanced simulation broadly, explicitly *excluding* screen-based
  virtual patients — the VP meta-analysis is Cook, Erwin & Triola 2010, a different paper) —
  evidence the modality's value lives in *doing*; Kononowicz's active/passive-control asymmetry
  (VP large vs. passive controls, negligible vs. active) banked for the M2.3/M6 reading of the
  APP–VID strata. `[LIT-SLOT — FILLED 2026-07-16, verified against ped-sec-10, 08, 13, 07, 14,
  19, 15]` `[PART I–II: presence as floor]`
- **M1.4 The asynchronous/scale/equity rationale.** Home ≡ classroom equivalence for desktop
  simulation — null d=0.02–0.14 across learning, motivation, and self-efficacy, robust to
  prior-knowledge and goal-orientation covariates, felt presence the proposed (untested) mediator
  that makes physical location unimportant (Makransky, Mayer, Veitch et al. 2019 — author-ordering
  corrected from "Petersen et al." against the unit; DA-08's context-equivalence verdict; scope
  note: the study varies *where* the same desktop simulation is completed, not *whether* the 3D
  layer is present — it licenses the async/home deployment and leaves the APP–VID contrast
  untouched); the field's own deployment gap as structural — immersive VR in higher education
  remains prototype-stage, rarely embedded in routine teaching (Radianti et al. 2020 — which also

  IVR-specific, non-uniform tax — mixed severity across 25 studies, oculomotor symptoms most

- **M1.6 Learning together in shared virtual space.** (Adopted 2026-07-16.) Social presence as a
  construct in its own right, distinct from spatial presence — the sense of being *with* another,
  not of being *in* a place — with the criteria a robust theory must meet and the field's sharpest
  instrument critique (Biocca, Burgoon & Harms 2003: the inherited scale is medium-oriented and
  funder-shaped, an attitude toward the medium rather than a state of the interaction); its
  predictive weight — social presence alone carries ≈60 percentage points of satisfaction variance
  in the origin study, and "presence can be cultured" (Gunawardena & Zittle 1997); r=.68 with
  perceived learning, strongest in discussion and group activities (Richardson & Swan 2003 —
  lineage note: adapts G&Z's scale, one research program, not independent convergence); pooled
  r=0.56 satisfaction / r=0.51 perceived learning across 25 studies, moderated by course length,
  discipline, and scale type, with no two scales equivalent (Richardson et al. 2017 — outcomes are
  self-report perceived learning, never grades; does not pool Biocca or Kreijns); the measurement
  caveats carried honestly (Tu 2002: tripartite decomposition, "medium-low" reliabilities, "a
  first step"; Terry & Doolittle 2019: contests CoI's entanglement of the construct, decomposes it
  into five factors — group cohesion, co-presence/salience of others, affective association,
  immediacy, intimacy — and relocates social presence as a non-mediated psychological construct
  independent of any technology, the entry's strongest warrant for the WE as a psychological
  achievement the design activates rather than a medium property; also interactivity ≠ social
  presence); social presence located in communication context, not medium, with teaching presence
  the binding element whose absence is the usual diagnosis when mediated education fails
  (Garrison, Anderson & Archer 1999 → M5.2); the CSCL corrective — availability of media is
  necessary but not sufficient; sociability must be designed for, not assumed (Kreijns, Kirschner
  & Jochems 2003) — the field's own version of the F7 student-side activation gap, specifically
  Pitfall 2: the VLE provisions the channel and assigns the groups, and what defaults is the
  social-psychological scaffolding; immersed collaborative learning produces gains with an inverse
  group-size gradient (De Back, Tinga & Louwerse 2021 — scope note: cite for collaborative-learning
  design and outcomes only, NOT social presence, which never appears in its text); and the field's
  own practice is overwhelmingly desktop — 78.4% of 139 VRCL studies monitor-based vs. 7.2% HMD,
  with verbal audio essential and socialization support among the nine required design features
  (VanderMeer et al. 2023 → M5.2). Honesty clause (DA-10, the entry's central finding): the CMC
  social-presence program and the VR-pedagogy program share a 1976 ancestor and no contemporary
  citation traffic — the bridge between them is a documented research gap, and joining these
  literatures to a VR co-presence phenomenon is Part III's own synthesis, made with the field's
  vocabulary but not its prior validation (→ stated openly at M4.3; → M7.1's mirror-check
  evidence). Prepares the literature base that M3.5/M4.3/M5.2 inherit rather than import
