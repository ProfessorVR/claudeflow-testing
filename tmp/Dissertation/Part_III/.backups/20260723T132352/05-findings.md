# 05 — Survey Findings Memo (T4): The VLE in Deployment, Read Through the Frame

**Data:** 241 respondents / 873 open responses, three terms (W25 N=102, S25 N=79, W26 N=60), 2D-PC-primary era (author-confirmed). Coded corpus per 02/03; quant per 01; stats per t3-stats.md. **Provenance:** FE = the counts/quotes themselves; P = every form/RO reading (`anticipatory-application`). Standing limits: single self-report channel (no depth channel → no second-form *detections*, only echoes); retrospective instrument (temporal phenomenology largely mute); category caution in force.

## F1. The deployment's first foreclosure happens BEFORE the world — at the threshold
The two strata tell different friction stories [FE]: install/compatibility and hardware complaints belong overwhelmingly to the **video-only** stratum (A1.install 4.7 vs 0.6 per-100; A1.hw 4.4 vs 0.9), while in-world frictions (navigation 3.9 vs 1.7, lag 3.5 vs 2.7, crashes) belong to **app-users**. Reading [P]: the environment's A₀ begins at the *installer*, not the lobby — for nearly half the class (video-only ≈ 44%, term-stable, Cramér's V=.009), the designed world never opened at all; its horizon was foreclosed by a download, a sign-in, a Windows-only build ("More inclusive for macbook users :("). Wendt's grid needs a node the seed-read didn't score: **the approach to the world** — availability as the zeroth design condition. This materially extends the friction catalogue: the biggest attested foreclosures (crash 24, install 22, hw 22) were the ones the dev/QA walkthrough *could not see*, because the walkthrough machine already ran the build.

## F3. The design headline: the chain stalls at A₃→A₄ — a world that gives something to see and nothing to do
The Wendt node scorecard [FE]: content/interest (D2) is the environment's one massive afford channel (159 mentions; 21 co-occur with incorporation-positive codes) — the clinical footage itself carries the value. But action-affordance mentions (D3, 36) co-occur with **zero** F-codes and read almost entirely as *absence*: "just being playback of videos," "add something more interactive," "make assignments based on the VR" (G4-INTERACT = 51 requests, APP stratum 7.6 vs VID 3.9 per-100). Reading [P]: students report a world that solicits attention at A₂/A₃ (the image, the interest) and then forecloses completion — no task, no needs-capture, no *praxis* — precisely the seed-read's predicted first-form-boredom risk at A₃, now student-attested. The passivity surface shows in the monotony/length cluster: "a lot of space of inactivity. Videos were unnecessarily long"; "many hours long, and I'm not sure what exactly to be looking for during that time" — an *orekton* withheld: nothing determinate to be looking FOR [P].

## F4. The flight to video is *Zeitvertreib*-shaped — and it has two anatomies
B1-FALLBACK (51) splits along the strata [FE]: (a) **excluded flight** — couldn't get in (61 respondents Q7-No∧video-only; install/hw complaints; the Korean-language case); (b) **hassle flight despite access** — 47 respondents Q7-Yes∧video-only, whose talk is effort-arithmetic: "not worth the effort to even learn how to use it… when I could just pull it up on youtube"; "the youtube format ended up being simpler." Reading [P]: (b) replays P3's convenience finding at scale as a *Zeitvertreib*-shaped exit — the path of least resistance out of a world that demands setup labor before it gives anything; not evidence of no-boredom but of boredom's *avoidance route*. Fallback co-occurs with install (8), lag (4), crash (3) [FE] — friction is the exit's proximate occasion; convenience is its standing pull.

## F5. The divergence register: mostly form-1 on inspection; a small genuine form-2 residue
18 A4-DIV candidates [FE]. Applying the §D grid honestly [P]: most resolve INTO the first-form family — the hollowness has a *determinate culprit* (quality, lag, clunkiness): "the idea is amazing, the videos and images… not so much"; "cool idea but the quality is so low I don't gain from the atmosphere." A small residue lacks any culprit and stands as genuine second-form *echo*: W26-R016 ("allows for more immersion, wasn't the highlight of the course" — nothing named); S25-R009 ("cool concept, but… not doing much to help"). And one within-respondent pair (W26-R037: "puts you into a VR clinical environment… watch with others" ∧ "not significantly more immersive then a YouTube video") is the survey's best surface-positive/hollow divergence. Honest ceiling stated: with no depth channel, these remain echoes, not detections — but the *grid worked*: it sorted the register instead of inflating it.

## F6. Value-skepticism is the deployment's late-stage attunement question [FE for counts; P for reading]
Term-neutral keyword sweep: value-skepticism in all terms, **rising W25 1.3 → S25 0.7 → W26 4.2 per-100** (the H-NEW-skeptic code itself was a batch/coder artifact; see 02). Meanwhile raw friction complaints *decline* across terms (crash 17→3→4; hw 15→6→1). The arc [P]: as the platform stabilized, the complaint changed kind — from "it doesn't work" (form-1 culprits) to "why does this exist?" ("youtube videos were enough"; "least gimmicky"; "less dependency on the VR aspect altogether"). The environment survived its frictions and then met the emptier question — the justification-demand that no bug-fix answers. This is the deployment-scale echo of P3's expert disenchantment, and the strongest survey-side warrant for §III.5: design must answer *what the world is FOR*, not only whether it runs.

## F7. Presence happens on screens; the WE is wanted, not had
- F1-PRESENCE runs 4.3 vs 1.2 per-100 (APP vs VID) [FE] — presence talk comes from those who entered the world, on desktop: "The virtual space feels like you are in it, while just a video doesn't" — **screen-route world-disclosedness attested verbatim** [P]; the survey's Q8 immersion-Yes (~69% of app-users, pooled) is its closed-item shadow, echoing P3's 70%.
- E-SHA (67 mentions, stable across terms per-100) is dominantly **optative**: "more collaboration," "having more of a community since it just seemed isolating," "watch with others" as the named "main pro." Reading [P]: co-disclosedness appears in the deployment mostly as an *unmet want* — the async-online course starves the WE, and students ask the virtual world to supply it. (The avatar-keyword fade Agent-recon saw was vocabulary, not desire.)
- The provisioning requests ("this class should provide students with VR headsets"; "the VR experience feels off when seeing it in the laptop screen") show students *themselves* locating a missing intensity on the phantasia-load gradient [P] — the 2D route is felt as the thinner route by some of its own users.

> **CORRECTION (author input, 2026-07-13) — the E-SHA "unmet want" reading is wrong at the mechanism level.** The platform has **spatial, proximity-based voice chat**: groups meet *inside the world* and talk while watching — this is the environment's central differentiator from the YouTube fallback. Moreover, **every student is assigned to a procedure-paired group** (the whole class is structured on group↔procedure pairings). So co-disclosedness is neither a missing affordance nor a missing grouping. The only gap is **student-side activation** — actually convening the assigned group synchronously. Re-read E-SHA accordingly: (i) "the main pro is you can watch with others" (W26-R037) is *confirmation the WE was had* by students who activated it; (ii) "seemed isolating" (W25-R089) is a **non-activator** who defaulted to the solo path, not a design absence; (iii) requests to structure the meet — "Incorporating a required group meetup within the VR simulation could incentivize more interactive learning" (W26-R052/Q9) — ask to **mandate/schedule** the synchronous meet — offload the self-organization tax — not to supply capability. *[Quote corrected 2026-07-23: this clause previously presented the paraphrase "more directed collaboration" inside quotation marks; the phrase is not verbatim survey language.]* The WE splits into **activators** (co-presence had, named top affordance) vs **non-activators** (isolation felt, mis-attributed to a design lack). Phenomenological point: the differentiating affordance carries a **coordination cost** the passive alternatives don't, so the convenience/*Zeitvertreib* gradient (F4) actively **cannibalizes the one affordance that answers F6's "why not just YouTube?"** — the synchronous WE was available to every student and declined by many for the frictionless solo/YouTube route. **§III.5 design brief, re-corrected:** the lever is not "build co-presence" (present) nor "assign groups" (present) but **collapse the activation energy of the synchronous meet** (default/scheduled co-watch blocks; graded group session). See cross-refs at F4 (flight to video = failure to activate the differentiator) and F6 (the differentiator is the non-YouTube-replicable answer). *Provenance: FE-author (platform/course facts); P (the re-reading).*

## F8. What the survey cannot show (stated limits)
Temporal phenomenology is nearly mute (C1-DRAG = 1) — retrospective course surveys don't elicit felt-time; the lab dataset carries that thread (see boredom brief). No depth channel → no form-2 detections. Content items at ceiling → no learning-outcome claims. Composition differs by term (W25/W26 junior-heavy; S25 mixed) and one cross-term duplicate response exists — term-trend claims stay directional, not inferential.
