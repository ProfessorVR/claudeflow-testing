## 1. Overall goal

The dissertation reads virtual worlds through an Aristotelian actualization-of-desire chain (A₀ threshold
→ A₁ perception → A₂ *phantasma* → A₃ committed judgment/emotion → A₄ act/*praxis*), joined to Heidegger
(world-/co-disclosedness; the FCM boredom apparatus) and a rhetorical frame (rhetorical incorporation,
veri(dis)similitude). Part I builds the frame; Part II demonstrates it against the richest of authored
worlds (RDR2/G&G); **Part III tests it empirically against two poles**:

1. **The desktop/VLE deployment pole — COMPLETE.** The virtual clinical-immersion hospital deployed
   2D-PC-primary to N=241 students across nine UC campuses over three terms (W25/S25/W26), read through
   the frame against the 873-response survey corpus. Drafted as modules M0–M7; assembled 2026-07-29.
2. **The laboratory/VR pole — NEXT.** The Boredom Experiment (VR Attention Study): P1 (EEG, ASEE 2023)
   + P2 (eye-tracking, ASEE 2024) + the unpublished raw N=8 dataset, read through the FCM three-forms
   apparatus with the two-channel (surface/depth) rule made literal in instrumentation.

Part III's threefold goal: (i) analyze student **boredom** phenomenologically/rhetorical-ontologically —
the data yield the findings, the frame explains how and why; (ii) analyze **engagement** in the VLE;
(iii) show the frame licenses **designing better virtual learning environments**.
