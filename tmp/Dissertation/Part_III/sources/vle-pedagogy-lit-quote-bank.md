  meaningful and the system easy to use" (p.562 — digest said 564; PDF's "the learners' perceive"
  is its own typo). Path-estimate/presence-null statistics are on **p.560** (digest said 561).
- **Dubovi:** the presence-relocation sentence includes "**but it is** induced by…" (digest dropped
  "but it is"); it is on **p.25** (digest said 26). PQ-norms sentence **p.23** (digest said 24).
  **Table 3 (correlations) and the embedded Witmer & Singer quotation are BOTH p.25** (digest said
  24 for both).
- **Makransky & Petersen:** co-author's byline is **Gustav Bøg Petersen** (PDF), not "Gustav B." —
  cite accordingly. Two-path verbatim lives in the **abstract** (barred by standing rule 1); body
  alternate: "These are labeled the aﬀective and the cognitive paths" (24).
- **Fowler:** the field-wide pedagogy-gap claim has no first-person verbatim — the quotable line is
  **embedded second-hand**: "All the other reviewed articles do not refer explicitly to a learning
  theory" (Mikropoulos & Natsis 2011, 775, **qtd. in** Fowler 412). Never attribute to Fowler's
  voice. Fowler's own "more pedagogical description" sentence is **abstract text** (barred).
- **Dalgarno & Lee 2012:** its reference list mis-cites the parent model as "40(6), 10-32"; correct

- **King & Salvo:** PDF prints "11.%" for one distribution figure (sic — 11.1%); distributions live
  in Appendix Figs. 9–12; the correlation is stated at hedged modality — "a possible correla-
  tion … While more research needs to be conducted for validation, superficially it appears as

**1.10 — Dubovi presence-relocation — KEEP as QUOTATION OF EXPANSION AND DISAGREEMENT (user)**
> "…presence is not a unique attribute of the environment, but it is induced by the ﬁdelity of
> representation and the high degree of interaction and user control."
(Dubovi et al. 2017, 25.) → *Disposition (user 2026-07-19):* agree with the relocation
(presence ≠ hardware attribute); **disagree with fidelity's billing** — fidelity is one tool, not
the ground; the operative condition is the world's constitution as an ecology developing
world-/co-disclosedness (a world that moves the student and is moved in turn); low-fidelity
counterexamples (*Minecraft*, *World of Warcraft*) defeat any fidelity-led account.
**REANALYSIS CONFIRMS (2026-07-19, against the PDF):** the fidelity clause is **not supported by
the study's own data** — (i) Table 3 (p.25): the ONLY presence component correlating with
learning is Involved/Control (overall r=0.211*, Admin-Sequence r=0.231*); the realism-adjacent
columns run 0.003–0.162, all ns; (ii) the discussion's own design principle is control, not
fidelity: "ensuring that participants gain sufficient control of the environment is an important
condition for learning" (25); (iii) fidelity's mention is literature-carried (Lee, Wong & Fung
2010 cited), not evidenced in-study. The disagreement is thus supportable **from inside Dubovi's
own table** — drafted accordingly (agree-then-correct movement closing M1.1).

**1.11 — Dubovi PQ at/above IVR norms — KEEP (numbers in footnote)**
> "…the three PQ subscales: Involved/Comparison (5.2 ± 0.7), Natural (4.7 ± 1.03) and Interface
> Quality (5.0 ± 1.1) were either higher or similar to those reported by Witmer and Singer (1998;
> 5.2 ± 0.8; 4.1 ± 1.1; 4.8 ± 1.1, respectively) in their work with immersive VR."
(Dubovi et al. 2017, 23.) Used in draft as prose claim; numbers in footnote.

**1.12 — King & Salvo 2024, gamer-correlation — NEW (user-directed), VERIFIED p.389 — KEEP**
> "While we expected that increased familiarity with VR and video games would lead those students
> to lean toward the VR versions, this was not the case. In fact, it seemed that the students with
> a higher level of familiarity with VR and/or video games were more likely to select the 2D video
> content as more educationally beneficial."
and the hedged conclusion:
> "…there is a possible correlation between one's familiarity with video games and their opinion
> as to the more educationally beneficial version of our content. While more research needs to be
> conducted for validation, superficially it appears as if the more a student plays video games,
> the more likely they are to select the 2D version as the most educationally beneficial."
(King and Salvo 2024, 389.) FE distributions (Appendix Figs. 9–12, stated 389): 2D-pickers' gaming
— 66.7% weekly, 22.2% once/3mo, 11.1% child/infrequent; VR-pickers' gaming — 33.3% weekly, 33.3%
child/infrequent, 22.2% monthly, 11.1% once/3mo. Function: the pilot's in-house echo of Fowler's
inverted-U — paired with 1.6 in draft, at the paper's own hedged modality.

**1.13 — King & Salvo 2024, the interview mechanism — KEEP (clause)**
> "Interviews indicated that this is due to the sheer lack of functionalities, visual fidelity,
> and overall polish of the VR versions as they do not meet the production standards of
> contemporary VR and traditional video games."
(King and Salvo 2024, 389.) Function: the mechanism behind 1.12 — expectation-calibrated judgment;
draft quotes the "do not meet the production standards…" clause.
