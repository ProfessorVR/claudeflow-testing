I am writing the literature review section of an academic paper analyzing survey data from an online,
asynchronous undergraduate biomedical engineering course. The course uses an interactive, navigable 3D
virtual clinical-immersion environment, viewed and controlled on a standard desktop/laptop computer
(keyboard and mouse, standard monitor) — **not** a head-mounted display (HMD) or immersive VR headset.

**Context you need:** a prior published study by the same research team evaluated a head-mounted-display
VR version of essentially the same clinical-immersion content in a smaller, in-person pilot course, and
found that VR increased felt presence and embodiment but that students still preferred 2D/desktop delivery
for convenience and to avoid discomfort with the headset. When the course was scaled up to a much larger,
fully-online, asynchronous format, the team moved the primary delivery to the desktop version of the same
3D environment (headsets were not distributed at scale), because immersive VR does not scale well to a
large, fully-remote, self-paced student population. I already have strong secondary literature on
immersive/HMD VR pedagogy and on presence/embodiment theory in VR specifically — **do not** return general
"VR is good for education" papers unless they directly address the desktop/non-HMD comparison described
below. I need literature that supports and contextualizes the desktop-3D modality itself, on its own terms,
not as a lesser substitute for VR.

**Please find peer-reviewed, scholarly sources (prioritize 2010–2025, note if something older is a
foundational/frequently-cited source) on the following specific angles:**

1. **Desktop/non-immersive virtual environments as a distinct pedagogical modality** — literature that
   treats desktop 3D virtual environments (sometimes called "desktop VR," "non-immersive VR," "screen-based
   VR," or simply "interactive 3D learning environments") as a legitimate, theoretically-grounded modality
   in its own right, not merely "VR without the headset." I'm especially interested in sources that discuss
   what pedagogical affordances survive (or change) when moving from HMD immersion to a monitor-and-mouse
   interface — navigation, agency, spatial exploration, first-person or third-person perspective, etc.

2. **Presence, engagement, and embodiment theory specifically for screen-based/desktop 3D environments** —
   I already use Lombard & Ditton's definition of presence, which is medium-general rather than HMD-specific.
   Find sources that apply presence/telepresence/embodiment theory (or games-studies frameworks such as
   Gordon Calleja's "incorporation" model of player involvement, which was developed primarily for desktop
   games) specifically to educational or training contexts on a standard computer/monitor.

3. **Empirical comparisons of learning outcomes, engagement, or satisfaction between desktop-3D and
   immersive HMD-VR versions of the same or comparable educational content** — direct head-to-head studies,
   ideally in health/clinical/medical/STEM education, comparing a desktop 3D simulation to a headset-based
   VR version of the same simulation or training module.

4. **Feasibility, scalability, and equity rationale for choosing desktop-based delivery over immersive VR
   at scale** — literature (educational technology, instructional design, or higher-ed administration
   sources) discussing why institutions choose desktop/browser-based interactive simulations over
   headset-based VR when scaling to large enrollments, fully-online delivery, or students' own personal
   devices — cost, hardware distribution logistics, technical support burden, motion sickness/discomfort,
   accessibility, and digital equity (not all students can be issued or afford a headset) are all relevant
   angles.

5. **Interactive 3D virtual environments / simulations in fully online, asynchronous course design
   specifically** — literature on designing and evaluating self-paced, navigable 3D learning environments
   (not live/synchronous virtual classrooms, and not passive instructional video) for asynchronous online
   higher education, ideally in clinical, healthcare, or other hands-on/experiential STEM disciplines where
   in-person practice is normally required.

6. **Virtual patient simulations or browser/desktop-based clinical immersion tools in medical/health
   professions education** — if you can find sources specifically on desktop or browser-delivered (non-HMD)
   virtual patient encounters, clinical observation simulations, or similar tools used to teach clinical
   needs-finding, clinical reasoning, or clinical observation skills remotely.

**For each source, please provide:** full citation (APA 7th format), a 1–2 sentence summary of its specific
relevance to one or more of the six angles above (state which angle(s)), and a DOI or stable URL where
available. Please flag clearly if a source is a review/meta-analysis versus a single empirical study, and
flag any source that is not peer-reviewed (e.g., conference proceedings, dissertations, preprints) as such
rather than presenting it as equivalent to a peer-reviewed journal article.
