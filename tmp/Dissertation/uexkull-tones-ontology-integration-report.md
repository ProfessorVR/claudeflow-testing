## 1. Uexküll concept map for the four focus topics

The foundation report's five master theses (Uexkull-Tones-and-Umwelt.md §10) consolidate into four concept clusters that bear on §§1.0--1.5:

### 1.1 \textit{Wirkton} (effect tone) as constitutive of perceptual disclosure

The \textit{Wirkton} is the meaning-coloration an object bears under the active \textit{Funktionskreis}; it is not added to a prior neutral perception but constitutive of the perception itself (Foundation Report §3.2). Three load-bearing formulations:

- (*Foray* p.~97, on the hermit crab and sea anemone): "The same cylinder-shaped object---in this case a sea anemone---can change its meaning in the environment of the same crab depending upon the mood the crab happens to be in." [verified against PDF /tmp/uex-text.txt L3298--3306]

- (*Foray* p.~99, generalizing): "We have worked out an effect image for all the acts we perform on objects in our environment, and \ldots\ we alloy this effect image so effectively with the perception images we receive from our sense organs that the two acquire a new characteristic, which announces its meaning to us. We will designate this simply its effect tone." [verified at /tmp/uex-text.txt L3373--3379]

- (*Foray* p.~99, the chair-as-weapon case): "A chair can on occasion be used as a weapon and then acquires a different effect image, which expresses itself as a `battering tone.' Even in this very human case, just as with the hermit crab, the subject's mood is crucial for which effect image gives a tone to the perception image." [verified at /tmp/uex-text.txt L3383--3388]

### 1.2 \textit{Stimmung} (mood) as the dispositional ground that selects which \textit{Wirkton} fires

\textit{Stimmung} names the subject's internal dispositional state that determines which of several possible \textit{Wirkbilder} (effect images) activates for a given perception image. Two formulations from the hermit-crab analysis (\textit{Foray} pp.~95--97):

- (*Foray* p.~95): "The anemone changed its meaning for the crab according to each of the three different moods. In the first case, in which the crab's housing must dispense with the protective outer layer of anemones which defends against squid, the perception image of the sea anemone (pink) had a `protective tone.' \ldots\ If the same crab is deprived of his shell, the perception image of the sea anemone (orange) takes on a `dwelling tone,' which expresses itself in the fact that the crab tries to crawl into it, albeit in vain. In the third case, that of the starving crab, the sea anemone (green) takes on a `feeding tone,' inasmuch as the crab begins to feed on it." [verified at /tmp/uex-text.txt L3313--3325]

- (*Foray* p.~99): "Even in this very human case, just as with the hermit crab, the subject's mood is crucial for which effect image gives a tone to the perception image." [verified at /tmp/uex-text.txt L3384--3385]

The structural homology with Heideggerian \textit{Befindlichkeit}/\textit{Stimmung} is established in the foundation report §3.4: "*Stimmung* names, in Uexküll's biology, what *Befindlichkeit* will name in Heidegger's existential analytic: the *prior, constitutive disposition that determines how things show up as significant before any explicit judgment, perception, or volition*" (Foundation Report L75).

### 1.3 \textit{Funktionskreis} (functional cycle) as closed loop with extinction-of-perception-mark-by-effect-mark

The \textit{Funktionskreis} is the closed loop through which subject and object are mutually constituted: perception-organ $\to$ perception-mark-carrier $\to$ counterstructure $\to$ effect-mark-carrier $\to$ effect-organ, where "the effect mark extinguishes the perception mark" (Foundation Report §2.3; verified at /tmp/uex-text.txt L1900).

- (*Foray* p.~49, the closure principle): "Figuratively speaking, every animal subject attacks its objects in a pincer movement---with one perceptive and one effective arm. With the first, it imparts each object a perception mark [\textit{Merkmal}] and with the second an effect mark. \ldots\ The effect mark extinguishes the perception mark." [verified at /tmp/uex-text.txt L1885--1900]

- (*Foray* p.~50, the perfection principle): "All animal subjects, from the simplest to the most complex, are inserted into their environments to the same degree of perfection. The simple animal has a simple environment; the multiform animal has an environment just as richly articulated as it is." [verified at /tmp/uex-text.txt L1942--1946]

The extinction structure has direct architectural relevance for the dissertation's \textit{kritikon}-discrimination reading (§1.2 L67--L69): the \textit{Funktionskreis}'s closure-by-extinction is the biological-side analogue of \textit{aisthēsis}'s discriminative completion through the perceptual act.

### 1.4 \textit{Moment} and the time-as-subject-relative doctrine

Time, for Uexküll, is constituted by the subject's \textit{Moment} (the species-specific temporal quantum below which no event can be perceived as differentiated). The doctrine is explicitly credited to Karl Ernst von Baer --- the same figure Heidegger cites in *Being and Time* §12 (H.58) when distancing his ontological *Umwelt* from "biologism" (cf. \textit{tmp/heidegger-uexkull-bt-report/Heidegger-on-Uexkull-in-BT-and-FCM.md} §II).

- (*Foray* p.~70, von Baer credit): "To Karl Ernst von Baer belongs the merit of making time intuitively understandable as a product of the subject. Time as a sequence of moments changes from environment to environment, according to the number of moments that the subjects experience in the same amount of time. Moments are the smallest indivisible vessels of time because they are the expression of indivisible elementary sensations, so-called moment signs. For the human being, as we already said, the length of a moment is one-eighteenth of a second." [verified at /tmp/uex-text.txt L2604--2613]

- (*Foray* p.~48, paraphrased and contextualized in the corpus index): "Without a living subject, there can be no time" (\textit{corpus/index/.../phase2-uex-f0.md} §2.B.5, citing *Foray* p.~48).

The von Baer credit is particularly significant: it specifies the same biological lineage Heidegger acknowledges at BT §12 (H.58 / M&R p.~84) and locates Uexküll's time-doctrine within that lineage. The structural parallel with Aristotle's claim --- "If nothing but soul, or in soul reason, is qualified to count, it is impossible for there to be time unless there is soul, but only that of which time is an attribute" (\textit{Physica} IV.14, 223a21--26, quoted at §1.1 L127) --- is the highest-leverage Uexküll-anchor in the section.

### Opportunity #7 — §1.4 existential weight / dispositional ground: stone as path-and-projectile (MEDIUM)

**Dissertation anchor**: §1.4-v2 L78--L82 (existential-weight dimension of the three-dimensional magnitude axis), and/or L161--L173 (the five-fold structure's second element: *toward-which* and *toward-whom*). The proposed Uexküll addition would specify the biosemiotic articulation of the rank-of-properties claim: which properties of an object are "leading" (essential) and which are "supporting" (accidental) is determined by the active *Wirkton*, not by the object's intrinsic structure.

**Uexküll verbatim** (*Bedeutungslehre* p.~140, the stone-path-and-projectile case):

> "Neither the shape, nor the weight, nor the other physical and chemical properties of the stone have changed. \ldots\ And yet it has undergone a fundamental transformation: it has changed its meaning. As long as the stone was integrated into the country road, it served as a support for the hiker's foot. Its meaning was in its participation in the function of the path. It had, we could say, a `path tone' [\textit{Wegton}]. That changed fundamentally when I picked up the stone in order to throw it at the dog. The stone became a thrown projectile---a new meaning was impressed upon it. It received a `throwing tone' [\textit{Wurfton}]" (von Uexküll, *A Theory of Meaning*, p.~140).

> "The stone, which lies as a relationless object in the hand of the observer, becomes a carrier of meaning [\textit{Bedeutungsträger}] as soon as it enters into a relationship with a subject" (von Uexküll, *A Theory of Meaning*, p.~140).

**Why it would expand the ontology**: §1.4's per-emotion analyses specify a propositional evaluation, a conative orientation, and a hedonic tonality for each emotion in the *Rhetoric* catalog (anger, fear, pity, shame). The per-emotion specification turns on the rank-of-properties claim: the slighter is *for-anger* not because of his intrinsic properties but because of the active evaluative *Funktionskreis* in which he figures; the threat is *for-fear* not because of intrinsic dangerousness but because of the agent's evaluative engagement; and so on. Uexküll's stone-path/stone-projectile case is the canonical biosemiotic demonstration of this rank-of-properties principle: the same physical stone bears entirely different *Wirktöne* (path-tone, throwing-tone) under different *Funktionskreise*, with no change in intrinsic properties. The Aristotelian-Heideggerian *pathos*-architecture and the Uexküllian biosemiotic-architecture converge at this site: each names the way determinate evaluative engagement constitutes which properties of an object are leading and which are supporting.

## 3. Discussion: How Uexküll relates to Aristotle, Heidegger, and the dissertation's existing apparatus

### 3.1 Uexküll and Aristotle

Uexküll's biology was developed in deliberate dialogue with Kant (the *Umwelt* as biological articulation of Kantian subject-constitution) and only secondarily with Aristotle. The Aristotelian touchpoints in Uexküll are:

- *Aisthēsis* and *kritikon*: Uexküll's *Merkmal*/*Wirkmal* closure is structurally homologous with Aristotle's discriminative-perceptual reading (\textit{De Anima} III.7, 431a8--12), though Uexküll does not engage Aristotle directly. The convergence is at the level of structural architecture.

- *Hēdonē*-*lypē* co-given with *aisthēsis*: Uexküll's *Wirkton* (the meaning-tone constitutive of perception) is structurally homologous with Aristotle's hedonic-tonality co-given with every perceiving (NE X.4, 1175a10ff.). The convergence is what the foundation report's §9.4 develops as the central dissertational gain from Uexküll.

- *Hexis* and *Bauplan*: Uexküll's species-body-plan + cultivated dispositional structure is structurally related to Aristotle's *hexis* doctrine, though Uexküll's *Bauplan* is inherited-evolutionary while Aristotle's *hexis* is cultivated-ethical.

- *Energeia ateles* and *Funktionskreis*: Uexküll's closed loop (*Merkmal* $\to$ *Wirkmal* extinguishing *Merkmal*) is structurally related to Aristotle's *energeia ateles* (\textit{Physica} III.1, 201a10--14), though Uexküll's closure is biological-functional while Aristotle's is metaphysical-categorial.

The Aristotelian register the dissertation occupies as primary is therefore lateral to Uexküll's primary focus. Uexküll's contribution to the dissertation would be at the level of *contemporary biosemiotic articulation* of theses the dissertation derives from Aristotle directly. Citing Uexküll is therefore appropriate as *cf.* or *contemporary structural correlate*, NOT as primary scholarly anchor.

### 3.2 Uexküll and Heidegger

Uexküll's relationship to Heidegger has a more complex texture. *Being and Time* (1927) contains **zero direct citations of Uexküll** (verified at \textit{tmp/heidegger-uexkull-bt-report/Heidegger-on-Uexkull-in-BT-and-FCM.md}), even though Uexküll's *Umwelt und Innenwelt der Tiere* (1909/1921) had been in circulation for ~18 years by the time of BT's composition. Heidegger's only adjacent biological-tradition reference in BT §12 (H.58) names Karl Ernst von Baer (the embryologist) --- the same figure Uexküll credits at *Foray* p.~70 for "making time intuitively understandable as a product of the subject." The von Baer pivot is the only direct architectural-philological link between BT and Uexküll, and it operates through silence rather than citation.

The explicit engagement happens two years later in *The Fundamental Concepts of Metaphysics* (GA 29/30, 1929--30), §§42--63, especially §50 (animal poverty in world) and §61b (Driesch + Uexküll as "two essential steps in biology"; Heidegger renames Uexküll's *Umwelt* as *Enthemmungsring* and reframes the animal's mode of being as *Benommenheit* / *weltarm* vs. Dasein's *weltbildend*).

The dissertation's existing Uexküll citations (§1.2 L115, §1.3 L144, §1.5 L104) correctly route through the *Foray* (1934) and *Bedeutungslehre* (1940), which are the works the user has in the corpus. The chronological caveat is important: the works Heidegger engaged in FCM 1929--30 were Uexküll's *earlier* texts (*Umwelt und Innenwelt der Tiere* 1909/1921, *Theoretische Biologie* 1920/1928), not the *Foray* (which postdates both BT and FCM). When the dissertation cites Uexküll, the verifications run cleanly against the *Foray*-corpus the user has, but the BT/FCM-Uexküll engagement engagement specifically operates on the earlier works.

The Heidegger-secondary register the dissertation occupies is therefore directly contiguous with Uexküll via three channels:

- *Stimmung*-as-disposition (Foundation Report §3.4): Uexküll's biological-perceptual *Stimmung* is the structural correlate of Heideggerian *Befindlichkeit*. The convergence is what the foundation report identifies as "real philosophical convergence" rather than coincidental terminology.

- *Wirkton*-as-disclosure-tone (Foundation Report §6.2): Uexküll's tonal architecture is the biological-side articulation of what Heidegger's BT §29 *Befindlichkeit*-analytic develops at the existential level.

- *Funktionskreis*-as-closure (Foundation Report §2.3): Uexküll's closed loop is the biological-side articulation of what Heidegger's BT §15--§18 *Zuhandenheit*-analytic develops at the equipmental-totality level.

### 3.3 Uexküll and the dissertation's existing apparatus

The dissertation's §1.0 L74 footnote (BCAP p.~133--134, anti-internalist framing) implicitly announces the engagement with Uexküll: the rhetorical-constitutive thesis that the soul is *being-taken in its full being-in-the-world* is structurally identical with Uexküll's anti-mechanism thesis that the animal is *Subjekt-Umwelt* closure. The current footnote does not name Uexküll, but the structural argument it makes is one Uexküll articulates at the biological level.

The §1.2 L115 footnote already cites Uexküll for the *Umwelt*-as-structurally-productive-analogue claim. The proposed Opportunity #3 would extend this footnote with the *Funktionskreis* closure principle (the *Wirkmal* extinguishes the *Merkmal*), specifying what the existing citation operates with implicitly.

The §1.3 L144 footnote already cites Uexküll for the Heideggerian-Gibsonian-Uexküllian disclosure-occlusion synthesis. The current treatment uses the hermit-crab/sea-anemone case at the level of disclosure-withdrawal architecture; the proposed Opportunity #2 (at §1.4 L92) would deploy the same hermit-crab case at the level of three-aspect articulation of *pathos*, taking up a different architectural register of the same Uexküllian material.

The §1.5 L104 footnote already cites Uexküll for the magical-environment Hansel-and-Gretel case. The proposed Opportunity #8 (at §1.5 L107) would extend this engagement with the tick paradigm at the *pathetic* action register.

The user-specified "Rickert, eventually Uexküll, will be used to a much lesser extent" directive suggests Uexküll appears in §§1.0--1.5 only as foreshadowing or contemporary-structural-correlate, not as primary scholarly engagement. The eleven opportunities respect this directive: each is a single-footnote-level insertion with Uexküll as cf./contemporary-correlate rather than primary anchor.

### 3.4 Where Uexküll AGREES with the dissertation

- Anti-internalist constitution of the perceiving subject (\textit{Subjekt-Umwelt} closure; Foreword pp.~41--42)
- *Stimmung* as constitutive of disclosure rather than secondary coloring (hermit-crab analysis, *Foray* pp.~95--97)
- *Wirkton* as co-given with perception rather than added after (the chair-as-sittable analysis, *Foray* pp.~98--99)
- Time as subject-relative rather than homogeneous Newtonian (the *Moment* doctrine, *Foray* p.~70)
- Function-orientation prior to form-recognition (*Suchton* priority over *Suchbild*, *Foray* pp.~117--121)
- Dispositional ground conditions perceptual disclosure (the hermit-crab three-moods case)
- Closed-loop architecture of perceptual-effective engagement (*Funktionskreis*, *Foray* p.~49)

### 3.5 Where Uexküll EXTENDS the dissertation's ontology

- *Wirkton*-catalog (*Sitzton*, *Schutzton*, *Wohnton*, *Fresston*, *Klettertönung*, *Hindernis-Ton*, *Gefahrton*, etc.): each tone is the biosemiotic articulation of a determinate *Funktionskreis*. The dissertation's per-emotion analyses at §1.4 L161--L173 could be deepened by treating each higher-order *pathos* as the rational-evaluative analogue of a specific *Wirkton*-type, though this would require more sustained engagement than a single footnote can supply.

- *Bedeutungsträger* rank-of-properties principle: the same physical object bears entirely different *Wirktöne* under different *Funktionskreise* (the stone-path/stone-projectile case at *Bedeutungslehre* p.~140). The dissertation's *toward-which*/*toward-whom* analysis (§1.4 L165) could be deepened by treating the *toward-whom* slot as the *Bedeutungsverbraucher* position in the *Funktionskreis*.

- *Magische Umwelt* (magical environment) as free production of the subject (already cited at §1.5 L104). The dissertation could deepen this engagement at §1.4 L181 (fear as braiding the three ecstases around its primary futural anchor) by noting Uexküll's structural finding that *Töne* can be generated by the subject's *Stimmung* without external stimulus correlate --- the same principle that grounds fear-of-imagined-evil at the rational level.

- *Kompositionslehre* (composition theory of Nature): organisms as instruments composed contrapuntally with their environments. The dissertation's Chapter X (announced at §1.0) is the natural site for engagement with this thesis at the rhetorical-ontological level. For §§1.0--1.5, this register exceeds the operative scope.

### 3.6 Where Uexküll MIGHT CHALLENGE the dissertation

Uexküll's most distinctive philosophical claim --- that every *Umwelt* is closed and self-contained, with no inter-*Umwelt* communication possible at the biological level --- could be read as challenging the dissertation's account of *logos*-as-grounded-in-*pathē* (§1.4 L187--L191). The challenge would be: if every subject's *Umwelt* is closed, how can the *pathē* ground a *logos* that operates *between* subjects (as Heidegger's BCAP-rhetoric-as-public-discourse reading requires)?

**The dissertation's available response is direct**: the closure-thesis operates at the biosemiotic-perceptual level (each *Funktionskreis* is closed by the *Wirkmal*-extinguishing-the-*Merkmal* mechanism), but the rational soul, in operating on *phantasmata* through the three orientational modes plus *doxa*, opens the closure at the cognitive register. The Heidegger-FCM distinction between animal *Benommenheit* (captivation within a closed *Enthemmungsring*) and human *Weltbildung* (world-formation that opens the closure) is precisely Heidegger's articulation of the same challenge Uexküll's biology raises. The dissertation's chain-architecture already accommodates the response: the rational-animal chain at *M_2* $\to$ *A_3* and *M_3* $\to$ *A_4* operates on *phantasmata* and *doxai* that are not closed in the way *Funktionskreis*-closure is.

A more nuanced challenge: Uexküll's *Kompositionslehre* (Nature as composer) introduces a supra-subjective compositional agency that the dissertation's rational-soul-as-locus-of-actualization framework does not accommodate. The challenge would be: is the chain (perception $\to$ *phantasma* $\to$ *doxa* $\to$ emotion $\to$ action) a soul-internal architecture, or is it embedded in a supra-subjective compositional totality (Uexküll's *Partitur* of Nature)? The dissertation's response would mirror its response to Rickert's parallel challenge (\textit{tmp/Dissertation/rickert-ambient-rhetoric-ontology-integration-report.md} §3.6): the chain operates at the level of the rational soul's engagement with its world, and the question of supra-subjective compositional embedding belongs to a different ontological register that Chapter X may engage but §§1.0--1.5 do not.
