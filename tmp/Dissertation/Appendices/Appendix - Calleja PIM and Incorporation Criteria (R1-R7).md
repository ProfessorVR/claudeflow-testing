## A.1 The six dimensions of involvement (the PIM)

Calleja introduces the six dimensions in brief on pp. 43–44, each elaborated in its own later chapter, and indexes every one to "two temporal phases: the macro and the micro" (*In-Game*, 37; the phases are treated in the next section). The dimensions, he stresses, "are experienced not in isolation but always in relation to each other, the separation being made here for the sake of analysis" (37).

### Kinesthetic
> "Kinesthetic involvement relates to all modes of avatar or game piece control in virtual environments, ranging from learning controls to the fluency of internalized movement. This dimension of involvement requires more conscious attention when the controls make themselves present, either because the player hasn't fully mastered them or because a situation demands a complex sequence of actions that are challenging to the player." (*In-Game*, 43)

*RODA recast:* the designed control surface as a channel of skilled coping; bears on the *resonant kinēsis* laid down at A₁ and on the execution of the act at A₄ — control internalized is control that withdraws from attention.

### Spatial
> "The spatial involvement dimension … concerns players' engagement with the spatial qualities of a virtual environment in terms of spatial control, navigation, and exploration. It accounts for the process of internalizing game spaces that is a powerful factor in engaging players and giving them the sense that they are inhabiting a place, rather than merely perceiving a representation of space." (*In-Game*, 43)

*RODA recast:* space internalized as inhabited place — the cornerstone channel of **world-disclosedness**; the designed *Umwelt* of A₀ taken up and dwelt in.

### Shared
> "Shared involvement … deals with the engagement derived from players' awareness of and interaction with other agents in a game environment. These agents can be human- or computer-controlled, and the interactions can be thought of in terms of cohabitation, cooperation, and competition. Shared involvement thus encompasses all aspects relating to being with other entities in a common environment, ranging from making collaborative battle strategies to discussing guild politics or simply being aware of the fact that actions are occurring in a social context." (*In-Game*, 43)

*RODA recast:* the channel that points past the world toward those acted alongside — the "WE" ratified at A₃; the dimension along which incorporation opens onto **co-disclosedness**.

### Narrative
> "Narrative involvement refers to engagement with story elements that have been written into a game as well as those that emerge from players' interaction with the game. It addresses two interrelated dimensions of narrative in games: the narrative that is scripted into the game and the narrative that is generated from the ongoing interaction with the game world, its embedded objects and inhabitants, and the events that occur there." (*In-Game*, 43–44)

*RODA recast:* scripted script versus emergent *alterbiography* — the designed pull worked on at A₂/A₃, against the act's own deposit at A₄→A₀′.

### Affective
> "The affective involvement dimension encompasses various forms of emotional engagement. Emotional engagement can range from the calming sensation of coming across an aesthetically pleasing scene to the adrenaline rush of an on-line competitive first-person-shooter round to the uncanny effect of an eerie episode in an action-horror game. This dimension … accounts for the rhetorical strategies of affect that are either purposefully designed into the game or precipitated by an individual player's interpretation of in-game events and interactions with other players." (*In-Game*, 44)

*RODA recast:* mood and atmosphere, with the designed-versus-precipitated distinction — the tone carried in A₁'s *resonant epithymia* and the emotion concretized at A₃. (Calleja's own phrase, "rhetorical strategies of affect," is the chapter's nearest reach toward the rhetorical register the method makes explicit.)

### Ludic
> "[T]he ludic involvement dimension … expresses players' engagement with the choices made in the game and the repercussions of those choices. These choices can be directed toward a goal stipulated by the game, established by a player, or decided by a community of players. Decisions can also be made on the spur of the moment without relation to any overarching goal. Seasoned game players understand that well-balanced game systems emphasize the opportunity cost of any particular action taken. Without repercussions, actions lose their meaning." (*In-Game*, 44)

*RODA recast:* the rule-system as the field of choice-under-repercussion — the committal *doxa* at A₃ (the taking of a course as to-be-done) issuing in the act at A₄.

## Macro and micro phases

Calleja indexes every dimension to two temporal phases (*In-Game*, 37).

> "I refer to this form of ongoing motivation to interact with the game and the off-line thinking that fuels it as macro-involvement." (*In-Game*, 37)

> "This off-line involvement is described by the macro phase of the model. It concerns issues of motivations and sustained engagement with digital games through the long-term (as opposed to immediate) aspects of the six dimensions of involvement that make up the model." (*In-Game*, 39)

Micro-involvement is the converse register — "the moment-by-moment engagement of gameplay" (in the section headed *Micro-involvement: In Ludo Res*, p. 40).

*RODA recast:* the macro/micro phasing is the empirical correlate of the chain at two scales — the within-pass *resonant kinēsis* (micro: a single actualization of desire) against cross-pass **temporal sedimentation** into *hexis* (macro: the off-line disposition from which the next pass sets out). The two must not be conflated; they are different deposits at different scales.

## Incorporation and its double axis

Calleja's canonical definition — the cornerstone quotation for the whole method:
> "We can thus conceive of incorporation as *the absorption of a virtual environment into consciousness, yielding a sense of habitation, which is supported by the systemically upheld embodiment of the player in a single location, as represented by the avatar.*" (*In-Game*, 169)

The double axis and its simultaneity condition:
> "Incorporation thus operates on a double axis: the player incorporates (in the sense of internalizing or assimilating) the game environment into consciousness while simultaneously being incorporated through the avatar into that environment. The simultaneous occurrence of these two processes is a necessary condition for the experience of incorporation." (*In-Game*, 169)

Both traditional senses retained:
> "This conception retains the two traditional interpretations of the term incorporation: incorporation as a sense of assimilation to mind, and as embodiment. … the work to which the term is being put here treats both meanings as essential." (*In-Game*, 169)

*RODA recast:* incorporation is preserved as **world-disclosedness**, one of the two poles of the union consummated at A₃→A₄. The double axis — world taken into awareness, player given over into world — is precisely the two-way traffic that "one in substrate, different in being" requires; and Calleja's "necessary condition" of simultaneity is criterialized below as R3.

## A.2 The incorporation criteria (R1–R7)

R1–R7 are RODA's operationalization: the numbering and the criterial form are the dissertation's, drawn from conditions Calleja states in prose across *In-Game* pp. 169–179. R1–R2 fix the two levels of the double axis; **R3 (simultaneity) and R4 (cornerstone) are the two conditions Calleja himself marks as necessary**; R5–R7 criterialize the blending, temporal-intensification, and intrusion-integration claims. Fail any one and the state is involvement only, not incorporation. (The god-agent index normalizes Calleja's components to a five-part rubric; the seven-fold numbering here is RODA's, and is the canonical one used in the body and in DRAFT-v3.)

- **R1 — Assimilation.** The environment taken into awareness as immediate surroundings.
  > "the virtual environment is incorporated into the player's mind as part of her immediate surroundings, within which she can navigate and interact." (*In-Game*, 169)

- **R2 — Embodiment.** The player embodied, via the avatar, within that environment — the converse direction.
  > "the player is incorporated (in the sense of embodiment) in a single, systemically upheld location in the virtual environment at any single point in time." (*In-Game*, 169)

- **R3 — Simultaneity.** R1 and R2 held at once; Calleja's own necessary condition and the strictest gatekeeper.
  > "The simultaneous occurrence of these two processes is a necessary condition for the experience of incorporation." (*In-Game*, 169)

- **R4 — Cornerstone.** The spatial-and-kinesthetic base: an oriented, mobile body in a navigable place.
  > "two particular dimensions—spatial and kinesthetic—form the cornerstone of the incorporation experience. Without them, incorporation cannot take place." (*In-Game*, 170)

- **R5 — Blending.** Several involvement dimensions co-active and fused, not attended in turn.
  > "Incorporation can thus be described as an intensification of internalized involvement that blends a number of dimensions." (*In-Game*, 169) — and, at the temporal apex, the arc culminates "in the blending of involvement dimensions that result in incorporation" (179).

- **R6 — Temporal apex.** Incorporation as the culmination of a sustained arc; it deepens with duration.
  > "Incorporation can be sustained but is often fleeting, slipping back into involvement the moment any dimension requires the player's full, conscious attention. Incorporation tends to become more intense when it is sustained for an extended period of time." (*In-Game*, 171)

- **R7 — Intrusion-integration.** Real-world stimuli blend in rather than rupturing — the porosity a world real-in-kind requires (no magic circle to seal it off).
  > "Intrusions from sources unrelated to the game environment detract attention from the game, undermining involvement and thus incorporation." (*In-Game*, 171–172)

  Yet integrable intrusion does the opposite: a friend's cry for help "does not necessarily slip you out of a state of incorporation, since the call can be readily integrated into what is happening in the game environment. In fact, a call for help will most likely intensify the sense of incorporation … by blending stimuli from the game world and from the immediate surroundings." (*In-Game*, 172)

**The "viral" turn toward co-disclosedness.** Calleja's own analysis of the shared dimension carries incorporation past the world toward the others within it:
> "the presence of other agents, human or AI, within the environment is an important component of habitation. When shared involvement is internalized, one's location within the environment becomes further validated by the cohabitation. … The space inhabited becomes incorporated in this case, because others are there also. In some senses, incorporation becomes viral." (*In-Game*, 177)

*RODA reads this as the hinge at which world-disclosedness opens onto co-disclosedness — Calleja's own text reaching toward the second pole, which the method supplies in full through Burke.*
