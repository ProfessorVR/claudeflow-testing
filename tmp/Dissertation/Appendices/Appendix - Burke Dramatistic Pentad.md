## B.1 The dramatistic pentad (the five key terms + the sixth)

Burke fixes the generating set at the opening of the *Grammar*:
> "We shall use five terms as generating principle of our investigation. They are: Act, Scene, Agent, Agency, Purpose. … any complete statement about motives will offer some kind of answers to these five questions: what was done (act), when or where it was done (scene), who did it (agent), how he did it (agency), and why (purpose)." (*Grammar*, xv)

| Term | Burke's gloss (verbatim, *Grammar* xv) | RODA use (motive-attribution, after the act) |
|---|---|---|
| Act | "what was done" — "names what took place, in thought or deed" | what was done |
| Scene | "when or where it was done" — "the background of the act, the situation in which it occurred" | the situation an act is ascribed to ("the situation made me") |
| Agent | "who did it" — "what person or kind of person … performed the act" | who did it |
| Agency | "how he did it" — "what means or instruments he used" | by what means / instrument |
| Purpose | "why" | to what end |
| Attitude (sixth term, added) | "the manner (*quo modo*)"; "attitude being an incipient act" | the disposition-with-which — read by RODA as a *hexis* |

Attitude is Burke's own later addition, stated in the 1969 Addendum:
> "With regard to the Dramatistic pentad (act, scene, agent, agency, purpose), I have found one modification useful for certain kinds of analysis. … I have sometimes added the term 'attitude' to the above list of five major terms. … 'Agency' would more strictly designate the 'means' (*quibus auxiliis*) employed in an act. And 'attitude' would designate the manner (*quo modo*). To build something with a hammer would involve an instrument, or 'agency'; to build with diligence would involve an 'attitude,' a 'how.'" (*Grammar*, Addendum, 443)

Its gloss as incipient act is from the *Rhetoric*:
> "rhetorical language is inducement to action (or to attitude, attitude being an incipient act)." (*Rhetoric*, 42; the fuller form, "attitude being an incipient act, a leaning or inclination," recurs at 50)

The ratios — each pair read as a relation of constraint, marked by order:
> "by a 'scene-act ratio' one would refer to the effect that a scene has upon an act, and by an 'act-scene ratio' one would refer to the effect that an act has upon a scene. … all the ratios are essentially analogies." (*Grammar*, Addendum, 443–444)

*RODA use:* the ratios are used diagnostically — which term an act gets *attributed* to — never as a grid to fill out. Burke is explicit that the Grammar is "a concern with the terms alone, without reference to the ways in which their potentialities have been or can be utilized in actual statements about motives" (*Grammar*, xvi); RODA borrows exactly that terminological discipline — a grammar of motive-*attribution*, not a psychology of the drives beneath an act.

## B.2 Consubstantiation / identification

Burke's core statement of the joined-yet-separate bond:
> "In being identified with B, A is 'substantially one' with a person other than himself. Yet at the same time he remains unique, an individual locus of motives. Thus he is both joined and separate, at once a distinct substance and consubstantial with another." (*Rhetoric*, 21)

Its ground is act:
> "For substance, in the old philosophies, was an act; and a way of life is an acting-together; and in acting together, men have common sensations, concepts, images, ideas, attitudes that make them consubstantial." (*Rhetoric*, 21)

The bond is born of division and answers to it:
> "Identification is affirmed with earnestness precisely because there is division. Identification is compensatory to division." (*Rhetoric*, 22)

*RODA use:* "both joined and separate" is, word for word, the grammar of "one in substrate, different in being"; consubstantiation becomes **co-disclosedness**, the pole correlative to world-disclosedness. The engine is transformed — for Burke the "we" is worked by symbolic persuasion; in the method it is worked by *shared desire* (the "WE" ratified at A₃, where agents acting together fix a common *orekton*). And division is literal in the virtual case: an actual body set over against the avatar acted as.

## B.3 The paradox of substance (*Grammar*)

The etymological paradox — the move that lets consubstantiation dock onto an act/potency ontology:
> "the word 'substance,' used to designate what a thing is, derives from a word designating something that a thing is not. That is, though used to designate something within the thing, intrinsic to it, the word etymologically refers to something outside the thing, extrinsic to it." (*Grammar*, 23)

> "To tell what a thing is, you place it in terms of something else." (*Grammar*, 24)

And the methodological consequence Burke draws — a banished term keeps working covertly:
> "in banishing the term, far from banishing its functions one merely conceals them. Hence, from the dramatistic point of view, we are admonished to dwell upon the word … so that we may detect its covert influence even in cases where it is overtly absent." (*Grammar*, 21)

*RODA use:* the paradox grounds the concealment diagnostic — a displaced motive is a source smuggled in under another term's name. RODA frames this narrowly (it catches a displaced motive, and claims nothing more sweeping), and uses the substance-as-contextual structure to license treating consubstantiation in act/potency terms.

## Seven causes → pentad (the open question, resolved in DRAFT-v3)

Burke supplies the warrant for reading motive through Aristotle's causes:
> "[Aristotle] refers human actions to seven causes (*aitia*): chance, nature, compulsion, habit, reason, anger, and desire". (*Grammar*, 292)

This warrants reading the seven causes of *Rhetorica* I.10 (1369a5–6) as a table of motive. **The seven-causes→pentad crosswalk is the dissertation's own**, built on Burke's move; Burke's *explicit* map runs from the *four* causes, and DRAFT-v3 marks that difference rather than blurring it. The stub's open question — whether the seven causes are the cleaner bridge to Burke than "motive" imported wholesale, and whether the pentad then functions as the attribution/concealment layer over those causes — is answered in the affirmative in DRAFT-v3: the pentad is taken as the grammar of motive-*attribution* (with its concealment diagnostic) laid over the seven causes read as a table of motive, and the crosswalk is declared as RODA's construction, not Burke's.
