## 1. Headline findings

1. **The *orexis* genus and its species are not focal vocabulary in BCAP, and their treatment is concentrated, not diffuse.** They cluster almost entirely in two places: (i) **§13** (pp. 72–77), where ὀρεκτικόν appears in the λόγον-ἔχον / ἄλογον division of the soul's being-possibilities; and (ii) **§15** (pp. 96–99), where Heidegger expounds *Nicomachean Ethics* Γ.4 (1111b) — Aristotle's demarcation of προαίρεσις (resolution/choice) **against ἐπιθυμία, θυμός, βούλησις, and δόξα**. The aggregated Greek-term appendix the corpus maintains (sorted by frequency, covering λόγος, οὐσία, ψυχή, πάθος, ἕξις, etc.) contains **none** of the four terms — confirming they are secondary in this lecture course, which is built around *pathos*, *hexis*, *logos*, and being-in-the-world.

2. **Heidegger de-psychologizes all four terms; Metcalf & Tanzer leave them untranslated in Greek and Heidegger glosses them phenomenologically as modes of "being-after" (*Aussein auf*).** The conventional renderings the user names — "desire," "appetite" — **do not appear as standing translations**. Instead:
   - ὄρεξις → "being-after," "tendency," "striving" (p. 99);
   - ἐπιθυμία → "being-inclined," "having-an-impulse," "being-impulsive" (p. 97);
   - θυμός → "being-in-excitement," "being-aroused," "having-passion" (p. 97), "blind passion / arousal" (p. 99);
   - βούλησις → "wish," "wishing-for-something" (p. 97).
   This is itself the most important finding for the dissertation's *orexis*-family framework: BCAP does **not** supply a worked-out genus/species *psychology* of desire. It supplies an **ontological** reading in which all five phenomena (the four plus δόξα) share the structure of *being-after-something-with-the-character-of-having-it-in-advance*.

3. **The genus/species relation is present but only implicitly.** Heidegger never says "ὄρεξις is the genus of which θυμός, βούλησις, ἐπιθυμία are species" in those words. What he gives is: (a) the *orektikon* (ὀρεκτικόν) as one undivided being-determination of the soul that "shares in λόγος" (p. 72, citing *NE* A.13, 1102b30); and (b) the three species treated **together** as kindred "being-after" phenomena grouped with προαίρεσις and δόξα (pp. 97–99, citing *NE* Γ.4). The unifying thread he supplies is structural, not taxonomic: "**This being-after something with the character of the in-advance is found in ἐπιθυμία exactly as it is in θυμός. It is fully explicit in the case of wish [βούλησις]**" (p. 98).

4. **The one place ὄρεξις does real definitional work is the *De Anima* I.1 analysis of anger** (p. 137): anger defined "as ὄρεξις ἀντιλυπήσεως, being after pay-back" — the dialectician's *eidos*-side definition that the dissertation's emotion section already leans on.

## 2. ὄρεξις (*orexis*) — the genus — direct quotations

**18 occurrences.** The substantive ones:

**p. 51 [GA 18 72–73]** — *orexis* as concernful "being-after" that, lacking a *peras*, becomes empty:
> "It is impossible that we … 'take hold of one on account of another. For, in this way, one goes into the unlimited; in this manner, one obtains no πέρας, and so the **ὄρεξις, being after something, becomes κενὴ καὶ ματαία, empty and vain**.'" *(glossing NE A.1, 1094a19 sqq.; n. 47 gives the Greek: κενὴν καὶ ματαίαν τὴν ὄρεξιν)*

**p. 61 [GA 18 87–89]** — *orexis* directed at the *telos* / *hou heneka*:
> "The οὗ ἕνεκα is that being which stands in a willing, with which I have to do willingly, **that which I am after in a certain mode of ὄρεξις, characterized as the end, the ultimate**, something that is ultimately τέλος."

**p. 72 [GA 18 104–105]** — *orexis* as the mode of being proper to *praxis*; the *orektikon* "shares in *logos*":
> "This possibility of hearing, this ἀκουστικόν, is more precisely found together with **the mode of being that is fundamentally found in πρᾶξις, with ὄρεξις. Every concern has tendency in itself; it is after something, directed at an ἀγαθόν** that is always there as λεγόμενον, as 'something addressed.'"
> n. 4 (the textual anchor): *Eth. Nic.* A 13, 1102b30 sq.: **τὸ δ' ἐπιθυμητικὸν καὶ ὅλως ὀρεκτικὸν μετέχει πως [λόγου]** — "the desiring and, in general, the orectic [part] shares somehow in λόγος."

**p. 73 [GA 18 105–107]** — *orexis* as "hearing," not "speaking" (its place in the λόγον-ἔχον/ἄλογον scheme):
> "the speaking itself is not, as **ὄρεξις**, genuine speaking; it is only speaking insofar as it listens to the speaking. … **ὄρεξις is not primarily speaking**."

**p. 76 [GA 18 110–111]** — *orexis* as the "ability-to-hear," i.e. λόγον-ἔχον in the second (ἄλογον) sense:
> "This ability-to-hear is a determination of **ὄρεξις**. Aristotle designates λόγον ἔχον in this second sense as also ἄλογον. **The ὄρεξις is not speaking without qualification, but hearing.**"

**p. 77 [GA 18 111–113]** — ὀρεκτικόν listed among the soul's being-determinations:
> "These being-determinations (**θρεπτικόν, γεννητικόν, αἰσθητικόν, νοητικόν, ὀρεκτικόν**) are divided up according to λόγον ἔχον—ἄλογον."

**p. 98 [GA 18 144–145]** — προαίρεσις defined as ὄρεξις διανοητική (the bridge between *orexis* and deliberative choice):
> "In Book Ζ, Chapter 2 resolution is characterized as **ὄρεξις διανοητική**, 'considering being-after,' a being-after that is determined by thorough consideration, by deliberation." *(n. 117: NE Z.2, 1139b5)*

**p. 99 [GA 18 145–147]** — the explicit rendering of ὄρεξις as "a striving," contrasted with δόξα:
> "This being-after in δόξα does not have, say, the character of **ὄρεξις, of a 'striving.'** Δόξα is more of a certain yes; it comes to an end and stops."

**p. 137 [GA 18 203–204]** — ὄρεξις in the *De Anima* I.1 definition of anger (the *eidos*-side):
> "anger as **ὄρεξις ἀντιλυπήσεως, being after pay-back** [a certain implacability as a mode of being-toward-others]; the φυσικός defines anger as a determinate boiling up of blood…"
> n. 262 (Greek): *De an.* A 1, 403a29 sqq.: **ὁ μὲν γὰρ ὄρεξιν ἀντιλυπήσεως ἤ τι τοιοῦτον**, ὁ δὲ ζέσιν τοῦ περὶ καρδίαν αἵματος καὶ θερμοῦ.

**p. 158 [GA 18 188–189]** — ὄρεξις as one being-possibility of the living:
> "[the soul has] various being-possibilities … that it can perceive something (αἴσθησις), **that it is after something (ὄρεξις)**, that it can move toward it…"

**p. 165 [GA 18 180]** — the verb ὀρέγεσθαι (striving for pleasure), glossing *NE* K.4:
> n. 338: *Eth. Nic.* K 4, 1175a10 sqq.: **ὀρέγεσθαι δὲ τῆς ἡδονῆς** οἰηθείη τις ἂν ἅπαντας…

**pp. 238, 247 (Handwritten Manuscript / Appendix)** — ὄρεξις in schematic lists of the soul's activities:
> p. 238: "ψυχή: κρίνειν, κινεῖν (**ὄρεξις**, ποίησις), ἀκούειν…"
> p. 247: "Δίωξις—φυγή, **ὄρεξις**, νοεῖν, διανοεῖσθαι, προαίρεσις."

## 3. ἐπιθυμία (*epithymia*) — direct quotations

**13 occurrences**, all but one within §§13–15.

**p. 72 [GA 18 104–105]** — the *epithymētikon* as part of the *orektikon* that shares in *logos*:
> n. 4: *Eth. Nic.* A 13, 1102b30 sq.: **τὸ δ' ἐπιθυμητικὸν** καὶ ὅλως ὀρεκτικὸν μετέχει πως [λόγου].

**p. 97 [GA 18 142–144]** — the demarcation gloss (Heidegger's rendering of ἐπιθυμία):
> "Aristotle demarcates προαίρεσις itself in four directions: **(a) in opposition to ἐπιθυμία, 'being-inclined' to something, 'having-an-impulse' for something, 'being-impulsive' about something** …"
> n. 112: *Eth. Nic.* Γ 4, 1111b10 sqq.: **οἱ δὲ λέγοντες αὐτὴν ἐπιθυμίαν ἢ βούλησιν ἤ τινα δόξαν οὐκ ἐοίκασιν ὀρθῶς λέγειν** — "those who say it [προαίρεσις] is ἐπιθυμία or βούλησις or some δόξα do not seem to speak rightly."

**p. 98 [GA 18 144–145]** — the shared "being-after" structure, and the *akratēs*/*enkratēs* contrast:
> "**This being-after something with the character of the in-advance is found in ἐπιθυμία exactly as it is in θυμός.** It is fully explicit in the case of wish."
> "οὐ γὰρ κοινὸν ἡ προαίρεσις καὶ τῶν ἀλόγων, **ἐπιθυμία δὲ καὶ θυμός**. 'There is no προαίρεσις … for living things that do not speak.' … **Ἐπιθυμία and θυμός are also found in animals**; they are not the same as προαίρεσις since the latter only occurs in living things that speak."
> "καὶ ὁ ἀκρατὴς **ἐπιθυμῶν** μὲν πράττει, προαιρούμενος δ' οὔ … καὶ ἡ μὲν **ἐπιθυμία ἡδέος καὶ ἐπιλύπου**. 'He who lacks self-control acts ἐπιθυμῶν such that he goes off after the matter. … **Ἐπιθυμία and θυμός go after a ἡδύ and a λυπηρόν** that disposition supports and tones down.'" *(n. 118: NE Γ.4, 1111b13 sqq.)*

**p. 111 [GA 18 ~158]** — the desiring-and-hopeful person (Rhetoric, fear/confidence context):
> "καὶ τῷ μὲν **ἐπιθυμοῦντι** καὶ εὐέλπιδι ὄντι, ἐὰν ᾖ τὸ ἐσόμενον ἡδύ, καὶ ἔσεσθαι καὶ ἀγαθὸν ἔσεσθαι φαίνεται…"

**p. 113 [GA 18 ~161]** — ἐπιθυμία heads Aristotle's list of *pathē* (*NE* B.4):
> "λέγω δὲ **πάθη μὲν ἐπιθυμίαν ὀργὴν φόβον θάρσος**, and so on, 'as a whole, the sort of thing with which ἡδονή and λύπη are co-given.'" *(the πάθη / δυνάμεις / ἕξεις triad, NE B.4, 1105b21)*

**p. 133 [GA 18 148]** — ἐπιθυμεῖν among the bodily-involving *pathē* (*De Anima* I.1):
> "οἷον ὀργίζεσθαι, θαρρεῖν, **ἐπιθυμεῖν**, ὅλως αἰσθάνεσθαι" — anger, confidence, desiring, perceiving in general, none "without the body."

## 4. θυμός (*thymos*) — direct quotations

**Genuine θυμός (the species)** occurs at pp. 97, 98, 99, 139 (plus the compound ὀξύθυμοι "quick-tempered" at p. 173). *Caveat: a raw "θυμ" search also catches ἐνθύμημα / ἐνθυμεῖσθαι (enthymeme, pp. 85, 87) and ἐπιθυμία — these are **not** the species θυμός and are excluded here.*

**p. 97 [GA 18 142–144]** — the demarcation gloss (Heidegger's rendering of θυμός):
> "**(b) in opposition to θυμός, 'being-in-excitement,' 'being-aroused' about …, 'having-passion' for …**"

**p. 98 [GA 18 144–145]** — θυμός shared with animals; goes after the pleasant/painful:
> "**This being-after … is found in ἐπιθυμία exactly as it is in θυμός.**"
> "οὐ γὰρ κοινὸν ἡ προαίρεσις … **ἐπιθυμία δὲ καὶ θυμός**."
> "**Ἐπιθυμία and θυμός go after a ἡδύ and a λυπηρόν** that disposition supports and tones down."
> (closing the page) "Προαίρεσις is not a so-called act; it is a genuine possibility of being in the moment. **θυμός δ' ἔτι** …"

**p. 99 [GA 18 145–147]** — θυμός as "arousal / blind passion," least like deliberate choice:
> "**ἥκιστα γὰρ τὰ διὰ θυμὸν κατὰ προαίρεσιν εἶναι δοκεῖ.** Aristotle says about θυμός, '**that which is grasped in a state of arousal, in blind passion, has little to do with that which is grasped in clear, lucid resolution**.'" *(n. 119: NE Γ.4 — i.e. θυμός is furthest from προαίρεσις)*

**p. 139 [GA 18 154]** — θυμός in the *De Anima* I.1 "pathē are inseparable from matter" thesis:
> n. 270: *De an.* A 1, 403b17 sq.: τὰ πάθη τῆς ψυχῆς οὔ πως χωριστὰ τῆς φυσικῆς ὕλης τῶν ζῴων ᾗ δὴ τοιαῦθ' ὑπάρχει, **θυμὸς καὶ φόβος** — "the affections of the soul are not separable from the natural matter of animals … such as θυμός and fear."

**p. 173 [GA 18 188]** — the compound ὀξύθυμοι ("quick-tempered"), Rhetoric B.5:
> n. 373: *Rhet.* B 5, 1382b19 sqq.: … οὐχ οἱ **ὀξύθυμοι** καὶ παρρησιαστικοί, ἀλλὰ οἱ πρᾶοι … (the quick-tempered vs. the gentle/πρᾶοι).

## 5. βούλησις (*boulēsis*, rational wish) — direct quotations

**Genuine βούλησις (the species "wish")** occurs at pp. 97 and 99 (the verb βούλεσθαι at p. 79). *Caveat: βουλεύεσθαι / συμβουλευτικός (deliberation, deliberative rhetoric — 19 hits, pp. 41–112, 175) is the **deliberation** family and is **distinct** from βούλησις-the-wish; do not conflate them.*

**p. 79 [GA 18 ~94]** — the verb βούλεσθαι (to will/choose), in the thief example:
> "But **βούλεσθαι** belongs to the genuineness of the way of being of a thief, namely, that a thief chose to steal."

**p. 97 [GA 18 142–144]** — the demarcation gloss (Heidegger's rendering of βούλησις):
> "**(c) in opposition to βούλησις, 'wish,' 'wishing-for-something'**"
> n. 112: *NE* Γ.4, 1111b10: οἱ δὲ λέγοντες αὐτὴν ἐπιθυμίαν ἢ **βούλησιν** ἤ τινα δόξαν…

**p. 99 [GA 18 145–147]** — the decisive βούλησις/προαίρεσις contrast (wish can aim at the impossible):
> "προαίρεσις μὲν γὰρ οὐκ ἀδυνάτων … **βούλησις δ' ἐστὶν τῶν ἀδυνάτων, οἷον ἀθανασίας.** … Furthermore, προαίρεσις is not a βούλησις although it looks that way. The difference lies in that to which they are related. '**Προαίρεσις never goes after something that is impossible.** … **Wishing [βούλησις], on the other hand, can be directed at something that is impossible**' [e.g. immortality]." *(n. 120: NE Γ.4)*

## 6. How Heidegger renders each term (the "desire / appetite" question)

| Greek | Conventional translations | Metcalf & Tanzer in BCAP | Heidegger's gloss | Page |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ὄρεξις | desire, appetite, striving, longing | **left in Greek**; "striving" once | "being-after," "tendency," the mode of being in *praxis*; "not speaking but hearing" | 72, 73, 76, 99 |
| ὀρεκτικόν | the appetitive/orectic part | **left in Greek** | a being-determination of the soul that "shares in λόγος" | 72, 77 |
| ἐπιθυμία | desire, appetite, craving | **left in Greek** | "being-inclined," "having-an-impulse," "being-impulsive"; goes after the *hēdy*/*lypēron* | 97, 98 |
| θυμός | spirit, anger, passion, spiritedness | **left in Greek** | "being-in-excitement," "being-aroused," "having-passion"; "arousal, blind passion" | 97, 99 |
| βούλησις | wish, (rational) will | "wish," "wishing-for-something" | a "being-after" that can aim at the impossible; not deliberative choice | 97, 99 |

**Conclusion on translation:** BCAP offers **no** standing English rendering of ὄρεξις/ἐπιθυμία as "desire" or "appetite." Heidegger's whole move is to strip the psychological vocabulary and recover these as *ontological* structures of "being-after-something" (concernful directedness). For the dissertation's convention of keeping the *orexis*-family in Greek and refusing English glosses, **BCAP is a direct precedent and support** — but it does **not** provide a species-psychology (epithymia couplable to bare aisthēsis, thymos needing honor-cognition, boulēsis needing nous-deliberation); that articulation is the dissertation's own, drawn from the *De Anima* / *De Motu* / *Rhetoric*, not from Heidegger.

## 7. The load-bearing passage (the genus + three species together)

The single passage that gathers genus and all three species is **§15, BCAP pp. 97–99 [GA 18 142–147]**, Heidegger's reading of **Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* Γ.4, 1111b** — where προαίρεσις is demarcated from ἐπιθυμία, θυμός, βούλησις (and δόξα). Heidegger's organizing claim:

> "The five phenomena [ἐπιθυμία, θυμός, βούλησις, δόξα, προαίρεσις] are fully characterized as **being-after something, with the character of having-in-advance**, so that what one is after is there in advance in a particular way. … This being-after something with the character of the in-advance is found in **ἐπιθυμία** exactly as it is in **θυμός**. It is fully explicit in the case of **wish** [βούλησις]. A being-after something in the direction of the ἀληθές is also found in **δόξα**." (p. 98)

and the species' relation to *proairesis* / *orexis*:

- προαίρεσις = **ὄρεξις διανοητική** ("considering being-after," *NE* Z.2 1139b5) — i.e. *orexis* run through deliberation (p. 98);
- ἐπιθυμία and θυμός are sub-rational, shared with animals, "after a ἡδύ and a λυπηρόν" (p. 98);
- θυμός is *furthest* from προαίρεσις ("blind passion," p. 99);
- βούλησις differs from προαίρεσις by its object (can be impossible, e.g. immortality) (p. 99).

## 9. Caveats and what is *absent*

- **No genus/species taxonomy of desire as such.** Heidegger nowhere lays out "ὄρεξις (genus) → ἐπιθυμία / θυμός / βούλησις (species)" as a doctrine. The three species appear *together* only as the *NE* Γ.4 foils to προαίρεσις (pp. 97–99); ὄρεξις-the-genus and the species are never explicitly coordinated on one page. The dissertation's three-species framework is therefore **its own synthesis**, for which BCAP supplies (a) the de-psychologizing precedent and (b) the *orektikon*-shares-in-*logos* point (p. 72), but not the species-psychology.
- **The *De Anima* III.9–11 / *De Motu* orexis-as-mover material is not treated here.** BCAP is a Rhetoric/NE/Metaphysics course; the kinetic role of ὄρεξις as "moved mover" (the diagram's M₃→A₄) is *not* developed in it. For that, the dissertation's primary texts remain DA III.10 and MA 7.
- **ἐπιθυμία's couplability to bare *aisthēsis*** (the dissertation's reason for naming the A₁/A₂ residue "resonant epithymia") is **not** argued in BCAP; the closest support is the "ἐπιθυμία and θυμός … after a ἡδύ and a λυπηρόν, found also in animals" line (p. 98), which establishes their sub-rational, pleasure/pain-tracking character but not the specific aisthēsis-coupling thesis.
