## 1. Rickert concept map for the three focus topics

### 1.1 Attunement (Rickert's reading of Heideggerian *Stimmung*)

Rickert treats attunement (PHX-C-003 in his index, alias *Stimmung*, *mood*, *being-attuned*) as the **fundamental disclosure of the world that precedes cognition, volition, and intention**. Three load-bearing formulations:

- (Intro pp.~8--9): "Attunement involves the whole person; there is no person separable from the environment who can be `tacked onto' it... Attunement is not additive but a fundamental entanglement, with the individuation of particular facets being an achieved disclosure." [verified against indexed phase2-introduction.md]

- (Intro p.~9): "Heidegger's *Stimmung* is disposition in the world, usually translated `attunement' or `mood,' indicating how one finds oneself embedded in a situation; it is `neither inside nor outside' but arises from `the way of our being there with one another' (*FCM* 66)... attunement is ontologically prior: it opens us to the ambience of all, not just human existence, implicitly changing our view of human being and jeopardizing modernist conceptions of the autonomous subject." [verified]

- (Ch 2 p.~98): "Attunement [is] not as a subjective state of mind or willed comportment but as an ambient catalysis within what is most material and concrete, a gathering that springs forward." [verified — see phase2-ch2.md]

- (Ch 5 quote from *FCM* p.~67): Attunement is "like an atmosphere in which we first immerse ourselves in each case and which then attunes us through and through." [verified against book-level-ontology.md]

### 1.2 Environmental affordances (Rickert's reading of Gibson + extension into ambient-affordance)

Rickert treats affordance via two extensions: (a) Gibson's ecological visual perception (PHX-C-013 in his index, alongside embodied cognition), and (b) the ambient-affordance reading that Rickert credits to *Heidegger and Rhetoric* contributor Gross's *Uncomfortable Situations* (Rickert references this lineage indirectly through new-materialism).

- (Ch 3 ``Beyond the Network'' p.~129): "Rhetoric's action is material, affective, ecological, and emergent." [verified]

- (Ch 6 ``Rhetorical Things'' p.~217 on Sputnik/Earthrise): "Sputnik itself, as a technological object with unique powers and affordances, also already speaks in *Earthrise*. Sputnik hollows out a space for *Earthrise*, illuminating an originary material affectability." [verified]

- (Ch 7 ``Dwelling'' p.~243): "Dwelling means the flourishing of life through attunement to the surrounding environment." [verified — also cited in Pathe MASTER report Q-RIC-01]

- (Ch 7 p.~243, on jug example): "A jug is already a thing whose space is carved out by material affordances (clay, need for holding liquids)." [verified]

### 1.3 Ambience (the book's title-concept)

Rickert defines ambience as having two senses (Intro p.~5):

- (Intro p.~5, twofold sense from Heidegger): "(a) the background of intelligibility and practical coping from which things are disclosed, and (b) the complex give-and-take with material surroundings, including the materiality of what we dwell in and from." [verified]

- (Conclusion p.~281): "Rhetoric is ambience as affective disclosure." [verified — the book's culminating thesis]

- (Conclusion p.~285): "Rhetoric stems from and is not imposed on the world. Rhetoric, then, is ambient and cannot be confined to a human orbit." [verified]

- (Conclusion p.~285, the book's final sentence): "Ambience, then, brings us forward, just as it awaits us, not as the new, not as a marker distinguishing us from what has come before, but as an attunement disclosing what has been sent forward and continues in its sending." [verified]

- (Intro pp.~6--7, on *periechon*): "*Periechon* (`that which surrounds, encompasses, or makes room for') appears alongside *chora* in Plato's *Timaeus* and connects to the sense of a surrounding cosmos that is itself alive. The concept was already in play with Anaximenes, Hippocrates, Empedocles, and others — indexing awareness that spirit/atmosphere constitutes the human condition both materially and spiritually." [verified]

- (Conclusion p.~285, the book's closing claim): "The claim that rhetoric is ambient fits rhetorical studies moving forward inclusive of its past by making the world essential to rhetoricity, and perhaps coming to a renewed sensibility to what the ancient Greeks called *periechon*." [verified]

## 3. Discussion: How Rickert relates to Aristotle, Heidegger, and the dissertation's existing apparatus

### 3.1 Rickert and Aristotle

Rickert's *Ambient Rhetoric* is explicitly Heideggerian in primary orientation and only secondarily engaged with Aristotle directly. The Aristotelian touchpoints in Rickert are:

- *Chora* (Ch 1): treated as Platonic, not Aristotelian, but Rickert traces ancient-Greek philosophical lineage extensively
- *Kairos* (Ch 2): traced from earliest Greek usage as spatial-material; sophistic and Aristotelian articulations briefly noted
- *Periechon* (Intro + Conclusion): pre-Socratic; Aristotle is not the primary site, but Aristotle's natural-philosophical works are referenced
- *Pathē* (Intro p.~14--15, via Daniel Gross): Aristotelian *pathē* as ground of *logos*, citing GA 18 + Gross 2005 HR-Intro

The Aristotelian register the dissertation occupies as primary is therefore lateral to Rickert's primary focus. Rickert's contribution to the dissertation would be at the level of *contemporary rhetorical-ontological articulation* of theses the dissertation derives from Aristotle directly. Citing Rickert is therefore appropriate as *cf.* or *contemporary echo*, NOT as primary scholarly anchor.

### 3.2 Rickert and Heidegger

Rickert is one of the most thorough contemporary readers of Heidegger's GA 18 + BT + the later (post-1947) topology-of-being writings. The dissertation's §1.4 already engages BCAP + BT directly; Rickert's contribution is to articulate a *rhetorical-ontological* synthesis of these texts that converges with the dissertation's project-frame. Specifically:

- Rickert's *Stimmung*-as-ambient-catalysis (Ch 2) is structurally identical with the *Befindlichkeit*-as-pre-cognitive-disclosure thesis the dissertation develops at §1.4
- Rickert's dwelling-as-attunement (Ch 7--8) is the contemporary rhetorical-ontological analogue of the *hexis-pathos*-correlation reading the dissertation develops at §1.4 L207
- Rickert's disclosure-as-always-also-withdrawal (Ch 7 + Conclusion) is the contemporary articulation of the *phantasia*-presentation-versus-*doxa*-ratification structure the dissertation develops at §1.3

The Heidegger-secondary register the dissertation occupies is therefore directly contiguous with Rickert. Citing Rickert as contemporary echo of the Heideggerian-rhetorical lineage is structurally appropriate.

### 3.4 Where Rickert AGREES with the dissertation

- Rhetorical-ontological framing of being-in-the-world (Rickert is more contemporary; dissertation is more classical-philological)
- Pre-cognitive, pre-symbolic affective-disclosive structure of *pathē* (via Gross + Heidegger)
- *Stimmung*/*Befindlichkeit* as primary disclosure
- Distributed agency across human and nonhuman (Rickert via Latour/Harman; dissertation indirectly via Uexküll *Umwelt* + Heideggerian \textit{Geworfenheit})
- Embodied, emplaced cognition (Rickert via Clark/Dourish; dissertation via Aristotelian hylomorphism)

### 3.5 Where Rickert EXTENDS the dissertation's ontology

- *Periechon* as ancient-Greek philological precursor to ambience (the dissertation could surface this concept; currently absent)
- "Rhetoric is ambience as affective disclosure" (Conclusion p.~281) — culminating formulation that synthesizes Aristotle's *pathē*-as-*kinēsis* + Heidegger's *Befindlichkeit*-grounds-*logos* + Rickert's ambient-rhetoric thesis
- Dwelling-as-letting-be (Ch 7--8) — the ethical-political horizon the dissertation's *hexis*-cultivation analyses gesture toward but do not fully develop
- Withdrawal-as-form-of-supporting (Ch 7 p.~244) — a structural reading of *phantasia*-and-*doxa* divergence the dissertation already operates with but does not explicitly thematize

### 3.6 Where Rickert MIGHT CHALLENGE the dissertation

Rickert's strongest critique of subject-centered rhetoric — that "rhetoric stems from and is not imposed on the world" (Conclusion p.~285) — could be read as challenging the dissertation's focus on the rational *soul* as the locus of the actualization chain. The challenge would be: is the chain (perception → *phantasma* → *doxa* → emotion → action) a soul-internal architecture, or is it a soul-in-world-ambience architecture?

**The dissertation's answer is the latter** — the entire chapter §1.4 develops "emotion is the soul's *being-moved* by what appears significant to it" as an anti-internalist reading. Rickert and the dissertation are therefore aligned. No deep challenge.

**A more nuanced challenge**: Rickert's emphasis on nonhuman agency + ambient catalysis raises the question whether the dissertation's three-factor kinetic schema (unmoved originator + moved mover + moved) is too soul-centered. The dissertation's response is that the schema is scale-invariant and applies at cosmic, perceptual, *phantastic*, cognitive, and locomotive levels (§1.0 L104). Rickert would press that the cosmic and perceptual scales include nonhuman ambient agency; the dissertation's analysis allows this (the sensible object as unmoved originator is itself non-mental, and the *phantasma*-bearing-hedonic-tonality is co-given with the perceived object, which is itself part of the *Umwelt*). The challenge is therefore one of *register* rather than *substance*: Rickert would write more in the ambient-disclosive register; the dissertation writes more in the Aristotelian-categorial register. Both are recoverable.
