CHAPTER PURPOSE AND THIS SECTION'S ROLE WITHIN IT
====================================================================

The chapter's overall purpose is twofold, and the revision of §1.1 must orient itself toward both purposes from its opening pages:

**Purpose 1**: Establish the *complete actualization chain*. The chain of perception-to-movement (the *Movement of Animals* causal chain at *MA* 702a17--19: sense-perception or thought $\rightarrow$ imagination $\rightarrow$ desire $\rightarrow$ affections $\rightarrow$ organic parts $\rightarrow$ movement) **IS** the chain of the actualization of desire --- not two parallel chains but ONE chain described under one categorial frame. The source of all animal movement is desire (*De Anima* III.10, 433a31--b30: *orexis* (\gk{ὄρεξις} `desire') is the moved mover, that which at once moves and is moved), and every cognitive act --- including pure intellection (\textit{De Anima} I.4, 408b5--7: ``being pained or pleased, or thinking'' are themselves \textit{kinēseis} of the soul) --- is itself a motion grounded in desire. Accordingly, the chain $A_0 \rightarrow A_1 \rightarrow A_2 \rightarrow A_3 \rightarrow A_4$ is one structure: $A_0$ is the ontological horizon (motion + time), and the successive stages unfold the single chain through which desire moves the animal via perception, imagination, evaluation, and bodily preparation. The chapter must NOT speak of two chains, nor of one chain with two formal aspects; it must speak of one chain that is the actualization of desire and the perception-to-movement actualization in one and the same articulation.

**Purpose 2**: Demonstrate how Aristotle's *phantasia* (\gk{φαντασία}), as evolved by Heidegger in his Aristotle interpretations and in *Being and Time*'s analysis of productive imagination and ecstatic temporality, is the capacity of the human being that allows it to cognize time as a *coherent narrative of past, present, and future* --- memory, present perception, anticipation --- and therefore underwrites every aspect of animal movement, from cognitive activity to physical locomotion. *Phantasia* is the temporal-narrative-synthesizing capacity that knits the three temporal ecstases into the unified phenomenal manifold within which the actualization chain operates. Without *phantasia*'s temporal-synthetic function, the chain could not span memory and anticipation; it would be confined to the immediate perceptual now, unable to produce the higher-order *pathē* whose definitions presuppose past slights, anticipated evils, and remembered-or-projected suffering. With *phantasia*'s temporal-synthetic function, the chain becomes the apparatus through which a finite, embodied, speaking being moves through a world disclosed across the three ecstases as mattering.

**§1.1's role**: Section 1.1 does not develop *phantasia* directly --- the systematic treatment of *phantasia* belongs to subsequent sections. What §1.1 must do is establish *the ontological horizon within which phantasia later operates*: motion and time as the co-actual, co-eternal substrate without which the temporal-narrative-synthesizing capacity could not have anything to synthesize. The revised section must therefore (i) establish the motion-time substrate with full Aristotelian-Heideggerian rigor, (ii) close by explicitly foreshadowing *phantasia*'s temporal-narrative role in the subsequent sections, and (iii) prepare the reader to receive *phantasia* as the human-being's capacity for cognizing-time-as-coherent-narrative rather than as a mere imagistic faculty.

THESIS THE REVISED SECTION MUST EXECUTE
====================================================================

The actualization chain requires, as its first link, an ontological horizon: a stratum of actuality that is itself a process. Aristotle furnishes this stratum as motion ($\textit{kinēsis}$ / \gk{κίνησις}) and time ($\textit{chronos}$ / \gk{χρόνος}) --- two structures that are co-implicated, co-eternal, and prior in all three senses (account, time, substance) to every subsequent actuality the chain will articulate. Motion is $\textit{energeia ateles}$, the actuality of the potential $\textit{qua}$ potential, an incomplete actuality whose $\textit{telos}$ lies outside itself; time is the number of motion in respect of the before and after, the countable-and-counted aspect of motion that admits soul as the counter without which time's full actualization remains structurally arrested. $A_0$, accordingly, names the co-actuality of motion and time as the substrate from which $A_1$'s natural beings, perceptions, and rational actualizations proceed. The chain inherits from $A_0$'s primitive intermediate-register character (genuinely actual yet structurally incomplete, $\textit{energeia ateles}$ generating $\textit{entelecheia}$ generating new conditions for $\textit{energeia ateles}$) its iterated structure: each completed actuality functions as both $\textit{telos}$ for what preceded it and $\textit{archē}$ for what follows. Heidegger's reading of Aristotelian motion as the structural determination of a being's $\textit{there}$ --- being-in-movement ($\textit{Bewegtheit}$) --- and his radicalization of Aristotelian time as $\textit{Innerzeitigkeit}$, the most derivative level of temporality grounded in Dasein's ecstatic temporality, supply the existential-temporal framework within which the Aristotelian apparatus operates as the chapter's analytic spine. The two registers are not alternatives but co-developed levels of a single ontological analysis: Aristotle articulates the structural-categorial features of motion and time; Heidegger radicalizes these features as determinations of how a finite, embodied, comporting being finds itself in time and in motion. The section closes by foreshadowing the chapter's central capacity-thesis: $A_0$'s ontological horizon is what *phantasia*, developed in the sections that follow, will articulate into a coherent narrative manifold of memory, present, and anticipation --- the temporal-synthetic structure through which the actualization chain spans past, present, and future, and through which it discloses the higher-order *pathē* whose definitions presuppose this span.

Sources: GA 18, GA 61, *Being and Time* §41 (H.191--196) on $\textit{Sorge}$, §68b (H.342) on the temporality of taking care.

**Phase C (`Priority of Actuality and the Chain Structure`)**: Heidegger's hermeneutical circle. Sources: *Being and Time* §32--§33 (H.150--159) on the fore-structure of understanding; §63 (H.314--316) on the hermeneutical circle.

**Phase D (`Relational Structure of $\textit{Kinēsis}$`)**: Heidegger's analysis of Dasein as constituted by relational being-in-the-world. **Phase E (`Time as Number of Motion`)**: Heidegger's *Being and Time* §78 (H.404 ff.) and §81 (H.421--432) explicit treatment of Aristotelian time as $\textit{Innerzeitigkeit}$ --- the most derivative level of temporality, correct as far as it goes but presupposing more primordial structures (temporality, historicity) that the Aristotelian definition conceals. **Phase F (`Soul's Constitutive Role and the Threshold of $A_1$`)**: Heidegger's *Being and Time* §65 (H.323--331) on originary temporality as the unity of three ecstases (having-been, present, projected-futural); §74 (H.378) on historicity.
