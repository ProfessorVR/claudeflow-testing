**Subsection 1 — A₁→A₂: The Actualization of Perception (~400 words).**
Declare the architectural role. A₁→A₂ is the perceptual motion whose unmoved originator is the sensible object in actuality (inherited from A₁'s preconditions), whose moved mover is the sense faculty in its first actuality, and whose terminus A₂ is the completed perceptual act. State that this section proceeds by (a) specifying the A₁ preconditions, (b) developing Aristotle's hylomorphic account of form-reception, (c) applying the three-factor kinetic schema from A₀ to perception, (d) analyzing perception as enmattered *alloiosis*, (e) defining the residual motion (resonant *kinesis*) that persists in the sense-organ after the perceptual act, and (f) arguing that this residue has a dual structure: resonant *aisthēma* (formal-epistemic trace) and resonant *orexis* (affective-orectic trace). Cross-reference: *see the prior section on A₀ for the motion-and-time framework that grounds this analysis.*

**Subsection 2 — The Preconditions: A₁ and the Medium (~500 words).**
Specify what must already be actual for perception to occur. Two preconditions: (i) the sensible quality actually exercises its power (the color is visible, the sound audible); (ii) the sense-organ holds its first actuality as capacity to receive the sensible form. Develop the role of the medium — *Sense and Sensibilia* 6, 447a3-7 on the sequential affection of the medium ("each part of it successively is affected by the part contiguous") and *Sense and Sensibilia* 6, 446b8-9 on light as the exception to sequential transmission. Note that the medium's sequential affection is the physical correlate of the temporal horizon A₀ establishes. A₁ inherits its intelligibility from A₀: motion and time being already actual is what allows the sensible object to exercise its power through the medium and the sense-organ to be prepared to receive it.

**Subsection 3 — Hylomorphism and the Reception of Form (~700 words).**
Develop Aristotle's account of perception as the reception of sensible form without matter. DEPLOY *De Anima* II.12, 424a17-24 EARLY as the decisive textual warrant — the wax-signet-ring metaphor: "a sense is what has the power of receiving into itself the sensible forms of things without the matter, in the way in which a piece of wax takes on the impress of a signet-ring without the iron or gold." Argue that the sense-organ undergoes a formal (not material) alteration. Integrate *De Anima* II.12, 424a24-29 on the sense-organ as spatial magnitude but the sensory power itself as "a certain form or power in a magnitude." Develop the "sense as ratio (*logos*)" thesis at *De Anima* III.2, 426a27-b8: each sense is constituted by a proportion between the contraries proper to its domain (hot/cold, bitter/sweet), and perceptible qualities are themselves ratios of their underlying contraries. Use the signet-ring example as an extended illustration. Discuss the destruction condition: if the sensible exceeds the ratio of the organ, the motion acts on the organ *as matter* rather than *as sense*, and the ratio is destroyed (the perceptual act never reaches A₂). This is a "failed actualization" rather than an absence of motion.

**Subsection 4 — The Activity of Mover and Moved as One (~500 words).**
Apply the three-factor kinetic schema from A₀ to perception. The unmoved originator is the sensible object in actuality; the moved mover is the sense faculty; that which is moved is the perceiver via the sense-organ. Deploy *Physics* III.2, 202a13-20 ("there is a single actuality of both alike") to argue that the actuality of the sensible object and the actuality of the sense faculty are numerically one event. Deploy *De Anima* III.2, 425b26-27: "the activity of the sensible object and that of sense is one and the same activity, and yet the distinction between their being remains." Use *De Anima* III.2, 425b28-426a1 (hearkening and sounding): "when that which can hear is actively hearing and that which can sound is sounding, then the actual hearing and the actual sound come about at the same time." The perceptual event is analogical unity: one in substrate, two in being. Note: the sense faculty as "moved mover" is an analogical extension of *DA* III.10 433b10-18 (originally specified for locomotive action); the perceptual case is structurally analogous but not strictly isomorphic — the actualization of the sense faculty *is* the actualization of the organ's capacity under the same description. Cross-reference *De Anima* II.12, 424a17-24 and the A₀ section's three-factor treatment.

**Subsection 5 — Perception as Enmattered Alloiosis (~500 words).**
Develop perception as a qualitative change (*alloiōsis*). Cite *De Anima* II.5, 416b33-417a1: "Sensation depends, as we have said, on a process of movement or affection from without, for it is held to be some sort of change in quality." Distinguish two kinds of *alloiōsis* per *De Anima* II.5: ordinary qualitative change (corruption) versus preservation (the actualization of a potentiality brought to its proper fulfillment). Perception is the second kind: the sense-organ is "preserved" in its actualization, not corrupted. Kevin White's reading illuminates the ontological specificity of this. The *pathos* in the sense-organ is formally — not materially — identical to the sensible quality: the wax takes the signet's form without the iron, just as the eye takes the color without becoming colored as matter. Then transition: if perception is an *alloiōsis* that preserves rather than corrupts, and if the sense-organ is formally altered without being materially altered, then the motion must persist in some mode after the sensible object withdraws — otherwise the sensible form it has received would vanish with the object.

**Subsection 6 — Resonant Kinesis: The Persisting Impression (~800 words).**
Establish that the perceptual motion does not simply cease when the sensible object departs; it persists as a residual motion in the sensory apparatus. Deploy *De Anima* III.2, 425b23-26: "That is why even when the sensible objects are gone the sensings and imaginings continue to exist in the sense-organs." Deploy *On Dreams* 2, 459a24-28: "The objects of sense-perception corresponding to each sensory organ produce sense-perception in us, and the affection due to their operation is present in the organs of sense not only when the perceptions are actualized, but even when they have departed." Deploy *On Dreams* 2, 459b1-6 on the physical analogy: just as heat remains in water after the fire is removed, so the physical motions of perception remain in the sensory organs after the object is gone. Deploy *On Dreams* 2, 460b2-3 where Aristotle names these residual impressions *aisthēmata*. Introduce the coinage **resonant *kinesis*** for this residual motion: the persisting vibration within the sensory apparatus. Develop the signet-ring anatomy: (a) Potentiality — the wax pliable and the ring held above it; (b) Actualization — the ring presses into the wax and the pathos is impressed (the actualization ends when the ring is removed); (c) Residual Trace — the wax has undergone *alloiōsis* of the preservative kind, and the impression persists after the ring has departed. Argue that this residual motion is what distinguishes the living soul from a merely physical medium that ceases to be affected once the stimulus is withdrawn. Cite White on resonant reverberation: the living soul "carries the reverberating effects of its past encounters with sensible objects; it is not only a receiver, but also a preserver and storehouse, of the sensible forms of things" (White, "The Meaning of *Phantasia* in Aristotle's *De Anima*, III, 3-8," p. 498). Note that Aristotle himself uses no single technical term for this residual motion; "resonant *kinesis*" is introduced here as a coinage to mark its ongoing character and its structural role as the material cause of *phantasmata* at the next stage (A₃).

**Subsection 7 — The Dual-Trace Thesis: Resonant Aisthēma and Resonant Orexis (~1,000 words).**
This is the section's most philosophically consequential move. Argue that the residual motion (resonant *kinesis*) is not a single undifferentiated trace but a composite whose internal duality is formally distinct in two aspects: (a) **resonant *aisthēma*** — the formal-epistemic content (the "what it is" of the perceived object: the residual shape, color, sound, or smell); and (b) **resonant *orexis*** — the affective-orectic trace (the pleasure/pain valence, the pursue/avoid orientation that accompanied the original perceptual act).

Structure the argument as two converging proofs:

(A) **Analytic entailment from perception to valence.** Deploy *De Anima* II.3, 414b1-6: "whatever has a sense has the capacity for pleasure and pain and therefore has pleasant and painful objects present to it, and wherever these are present, there is desire, for desire is appetition of what is pleasant." This is a chain of analytic entailments: sensation → pleasure/pain capacity → pleasant/painful objects present → appetite. Every act of sensation therefore analytically entails affective valence; sensation without such valence is a contradiction in terms. Deploy *De Anima* III.7, 431a8-14: "To perceive then is like bare asserting or thinking; but when the object is pleasant or painful, the soul makes a sort of affirmation or negation, and pursues or avoids the object." Emphasize the decisive qualification Aristotle adds: "the faculty of appetite and avoidance are not different, either from one another or from the faculty of sense-perception; but their being is different" — numerically one in substrate, two in being. Apply this formula: the residual motion that persists after the object departs inherits the same structure — numerically one affection, formally dual.

(B) **Modus tollens from motivational inertness.** If the residual affection were exhausted by the formal-epistemic trace (the resonant *aisthēma* alone), then mere retention of sensible form would suffice to originate pursuit and avoidance — the knowledge *that the object is a wolf* would, by itself, move the animal to flee. But *De Anima* III.7, 431a8-14 insists that bare perception is "like bare asserting or thinking"; only *when the object is pleasant or painful* does the soul pursue or avoid. Hence the formal-epistemic content alone is motivationally inert; a distinct operation — the hedonic-orectic valuation — is required. If the residual affection were exhausted by the formal trace, the affective residue would have no independent operative force. But *On Dreams* 2, 460b4-11 (the cowardly, erotic, angry deceived by slight resemblances) shows that affective residue does modulate the threshold of formal recognition — the coward's existing fear primes recognition of enemies with minimal formal similarity. And *MA* 7, 702a17-19 specifies that "the organic parts are suitably prepared by the affections, these again by desire, and desire by imagination" — inserting *pathē* as a distinct causal step between desire and the organic parts. If the affective dimension were simply identical with the formal content, its insertion as a distinct causal step would be otiose. Therefore the residual affection harbors an internal duality.

Supplementary evidence: deploy *Rhetoric* I.11, 1370a27-b1 on pleasure as a kind of weak perception that attends remembering and expecting. The pleasure that attaches to memory is not a new judgment about the past; it is a residual affection carried by *phantasia* as part of its content — direct evidence that affective valence persists alongside the formal trace and is delivered by the residual motion without an intervening evaluative act. Deploy *De Anima* I.1, 403a25-b19 on *pathē* as "enmattered accounts": the definition of any affection combines formal account (what it is *about*) and material account (the bodily state in which it is realized). The enmattered-accounts framework supplies the hylomorphic warrant: since the residual motion is an enmattered composite, and the composite has distinguishable formal and material aspects, the formal-epistemic and affective-orectic dimensions are distinct formal aspects of a single substrate. They are one in substrate, different in being — the same formula Aristotle applies to perception and appetite at 431a13-14.

Close the argument by stating the dual structure: resonant *kinesis* is the residual motion as a whole; resonant *aisthēma* is the formal-epistemic imprint ("what the object is"); resonant *orexis* is the affective-orectic imprint ("what the object means for pursuit or avoidance"). These are one in substrate, different in being, mapped respectively onto the perceptual faculty (retaining the sensible form) and the appetitive faculty (retaining the pleasure or pain). Aristotle himself does not name the second trace; his commitments — that sensation entails valence, that valence is formally distinct from epistemic content, that the whole affection persists, and that the affective dimension has its own causal efficacy — collectively require it.

**Subsection 8 — Synthesis: A₂ as Ground of A₃ (~400 words).**
Close the section by stating A₂'s architectural role. A₂ is the completed perceptual actuality: the joint actualization of sensible object and sense faculty terminating in the reception of form without matter. A₂'s content — the dual-trace composite of resonant *aisthēma* and resonant *orexis* — serves as the unmoved originator of the next motion in the chain: the phantastic motion M₂→A₃ through which the residual trace is activated by *phantasia* and crystallized into the *phantasma* proper. Deposit the reader at the threshold of A₃ with a sentence flagging the genetic relationship: just as A₀'s motion and time grounded A₁'s preconditions and A₁ grounded A₂'s perceptual event, so A₂'s residual trace grounds A₃'s phantasmatic image. The chain is iterative and nested — each completed actuality is simultaneously the terminus of its antecedent motion and the unmoved originator of its successor. Attach the Heidegger deferral footnote specified above.

**Development Note (~300 words, own `\subsection*{Development Note}` AFTER subsection 8).**
Flag for future development: (i) the question whether every sensory affection inherits *both* traces in all animals, or whether certain organisms might retain only the formal aspect without the orectic valence, is textually underdetermined in Aristotle but bears on the wider thesis about appetite as the single source of animal movement; (ii) the precise mechanism by which the sense-organ's sequential affection through the medium translates into the unified perceptual act is not fully articulated in *De Anima* and requires supplementary analysis from *Sense and Sensibilia* and the *De Motu Animalium*; (iii) the relationship between resonant *orexis* and what Aristotle calls *pathē* in the Rhetoric's catalogue of higher-order emotions (anger, fear, pity, shame) — which operate only in rational animals capable of *doxa* — must be distinguished from the basic affective valence co-constitutive with perception at A₂ (this is the distinction between *pathos* simpliciter and *pathē* in the higher-order sense that the downstream sections on cognitive actualities and emotion will develop).

- White (1985), "The Meaning of Phantasia," p. 498: "This lingering, resonating, echoing presence of sensible forms freed from their original matter appears to be the essence of phantasia for Aristotle. The movement in the sense-power caused by the sensible object itself sets up a second movement which continues after the sensation has ceased and the object is gone."

- Caston, "Why Aristotle Needs Imagination," p. 48: "like an echo, the phantasma is only an indirect effect of the object of perception; the phantasma is directly caused by the sensory stimulation (αἴσθημα), which in turn is directly caused by the object (αἰσθητόν)."
