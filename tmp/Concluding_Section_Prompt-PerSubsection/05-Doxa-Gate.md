SUBSECTION 5 of 9 — The Content-Conditional Doxa-Gate
================================================================

(The CLI invocation passes the LaTeX heading, word target ~700, quotation count, and output format via subsection-mode flags. This delta supplies the topical content + dissertation-specific deployment guidance.)

**Subsection role.** Formal articulation of the gating condition that §4's typology exposed.

State the rule explicitly: higher-order *pathē* arise when (a) *doxa* forms (involuntary *taking-as-something-true*) AND (b) the *doxa*'s content engages evaluatively complex categories — slight, threat, undeserved suffering, disgrace, or their kin (*Rhetoric* II.1–11).

- When (a) holds without (b): habitual-rational action without higher-order *pathē* (Type 2).
- When (a) and (b) both hold: evaluatively complex action with full higher-order *pathē* (Type 3).
- When neither (a) nor (b) holds (the *phantasma* operates without *doxastic* ratification on any axis): simple appetition (Type 1).

Anchor the gate's textual warrant in *De Anima* III.3, 427b21–24: when the soul merely *imagines* fearful things "we remain unaffected"; only when *doxa* propositionally ratifies the aspectual presentation does emotion arise *euthys* (immediately). This is the *De Anima* passage that grounds the dissertation's coined term **content-conditional doxa-gate**.

Anchor the content-conditional reading in *Rhetoric* II.1, 1378a21–22 (the *Rhetoric*'s definition of *pathē* as "feelings that so change men as to affect their judgements" — i.e., requiring evaluative-judgemental content) and in *Rhetoric* II.2, 1378a31–b5 (anger as definitionally arising from perceived slight).

Develop that the gate's content-conditionality explains both the *cases* of action (the three types) and the *therapeutic* possibilities of rhetoric: a rhetor who induces an evaluatively complex *phantasma* (via *enargeia*) can move an audience from Type 2 routine into Type 3 affective engagement, OR can de-fuse an audience's evaluative complexity by reframing the *phantasma* so that *doxa* no longer ratifies the evaluative categories.

Note the structural symmetry: the gate is functionally the same whether moving *toward* or *away* from higher-order *pathē* — what matters is whether *doxa* finds evaluatively complex content to ratify.

Close with the directional claim: the doxa-gate is the chain's pivot at A_3 — it is what determines whether the chain runs through evaluative articulation (Type 3) or bypasses evaluation entirely (Types 1 and 2). The next subsection takes up the dispositional ground (the *hexis* bivalence) that conditions how the gate opens.
