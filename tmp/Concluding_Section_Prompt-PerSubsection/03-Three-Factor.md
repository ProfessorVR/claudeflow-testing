SUBSECTION 3 of 9 — The Three-Factor Schema, the Three Entry Points
================================================================

(The CLI invocation passes the LaTeX heading, word target ~600, quotation count, and output format via subsection-mode flags. This delta supplies the topical content + dissertation-specific deployment guidance.)

**Subsection role.** Deploy the three-factor schema of *De Anima* III.10 as the architectural backbone of action: every action involves (1) that which originates the movement; (2) that by means of which it originates it; (3) that which is moved. Specify each factor in the case of action: the unmoved originator is "the realizable good" (*to orekton* / *to prakton agathon*); that by means of which is the faculty of appetite (*to orektikon*), which is "moved-mover" (moves the body while being itself moved by the realizable good); that which is moved is the animal, via the bodily instrument.

Then deploy the three entry-points per *De Motu Animalium* 7, 701a32–33: sense, imagination, and thought can each initiate the chain — "I want to drink, says appetite; this is drink, says sense or imagination or thought: straightaway I drink." This is the conceptual hinge for the typology. Each of the three entry-points can fire the chain in the appetitive case (Type 1), but in the rational-animal case the same chain can also be entered through habit-grounded *doxa* (Type 2) or through evaluatively complex *doxa* (Type 3).

Emphasize: the three entry-points are NOT three parallel chains; they are three modes by which the SAME chain is entered. The three-factor schema applies in every case; what varies is which entry-point activates the unmoved originator (the realizable good) and at what level of *doxastic* articulation.

Integrate *De Anima* III.10, 433a9–12: "These two at all events appear to be sources of movement: appetite and thought (if one may venture to regard imagination as a kind of thinking; for many men follow their imaginations contrary to knowledge, and in all animals other than man there is no thinking or calculation but only imagination)." The bracketed clause about non-rational animals matters: they have *phantasia* but not the *logos*-dependent operations of *doxa*, which is why their chains never enter Type 2 or Type 3 — they remain in the pre-rational analogue of Type 1.

Close with the typological foreshadowing: which entry-point is operative, and at what level of *doxastic* articulation, determines which of three types of action results. The full typology is the load-bearing work of the next subsection.
