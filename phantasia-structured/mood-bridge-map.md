## Unique Formulations in E (Absent or Different in A)

### 1. "As If" Structure (Part I)

E frames phantastic presentation as an "as if" (*hōs*) structure — phantasia presents things "as if" they are present even when absent, "as if" they are fearful even before judgment. This phenomenological framing of phantasia's operation is absent from A, which uses "taking-as" language more aligned with Heidegger.

**Assessment**: The "as if" framing is distinctively Aristotelian and could strengthen A's Chapter 1 account of how phantasia operates pre-judgmentally.

### 2. Gonzalez Quotation on Befindlichkeit (Part II)

> "Heidegger's analysis operates at the level of fundamental ontology, not empirical psychology; Befindlichkeit names the a priori condition for the possibility of any particular mood" (Gonzalez 2006)

Present in E (p. 5). **Status in A**: Needs verification — A's sec. 2.1 covers Befindlichkeit but the Gonzalez quotation may not be present.

**Significance**: This quotation precisely articulates the ontological/ontic distinction that A's Chapter 2 relies on. If absent from A, it should be considered for inclusion.

### 3. "Both as Conditions of Possibility" Framing (Part III)

E frames phantasia and Stimmung as **parallel conditions of possibility**: phantasia is a condition of possibility for perceptual experience, Stimmung is a condition of possibility for existential disclosure. They are structurally analogous but operate in different domains (perception vs existence).

A's Chapter 2 instead uses **subsumption** language: Stimmung "subsumes" phantasia by providing its "ontological completion." This is a substantive philosophical difference:

| E Framing | A Framing |
|-----------|-----------|
| Parallel: both are conditions of possibility | Hierarchical: Stimmung subsumes/completes phantasia |
| Phantasia retains full autonomy in its domain | Phantasia is "completed" by Stimmung |
| Structural analogy | Ontological asymmetry |

**Assessment**: This is Tension #10 (subsumption vs complementarity). E's "conditions of possibility" framing represents an earlier philosophical position that A deliberately revised into the subsumption thesis. The revision represents a bolder philosophical claim.

### 4. "Key Differences: Perception versus Existence" Schema (Part III)

E includes an explicit comparative schema separating the domains:
- **Aristotle's domain**: Perception, faculty psychology, mechanistic causation, form-reception
- **Heidegger's domain**: Existence, fundamental ontology, meaning-constitution, disclosure

This explicit comparative schema is implicit in A but never formally articulated. It would make a useful table or appendix in A.
