## Pre-Execution Notes

### Book Character

This text is structurally different from B&T and BCAP:
- **No numbered sections (§).** The text is organized into named **chapters** (short, 5–12 pages each).
- **Two distinct works** bound together: *Foray* (1934, popular biology) and *Theory of Meaning* (1940, theoretical biosemiotics). They share vocabulary but differ in register and ambition.
- **Heavily illustrated.** Many chapters contain figures (drawings, diagrams, photographs). Some PDF pages are image-only with no extractable text.
- **Short chapters.** Most chapters are 5–10 book pages. Grouping into analysis units of 2–3 chapters each keeps units at ~15–25 pages, consistent with the B&T and BCAP pipelines.
- **German terminology is sparser** than B&T or BCAP, but the key terms (*Umwelt*, *Merkwelt*, *Wirkwelt*, *Funktionskreis*, *Bauplan*, *Bedeutung*) are philosophically significant and require a terminology appendix.
- **Paratext is substantial.** Sagan's Introduction (34 pp.) and Winthrop-Young's Afterword (35 pp.) provide important reception context, especially the Heidegger–Uexküll connection.

### PDF Offset

> **CORRECTED during Phase 0**: Book page + 4 = PDF page (uniform throughout).
> Confirmed by printed page numbers in text: "ENVIRONMENT SPACES 53" on PDF p.57 (53+4=57); "FOREWORD" on PDF p.45 (41+4=45); "INTRODUCTION" on PDF p.48 (44+4=48).
> Front matter uses same offset: Sagan Introduction (book p.1) = PDF p.5.
> Note: Several PDF pages are full-page figures with no extractable text.

**Total PDF pages**: 281.

## Provisional Tensions (~8 candidates)

1. **Umwelt vs. Umgebung** — subjective world vs. objective surroundings; Uexküll argues the Umwelt is primary, yet the Umgebung is needed as contrast.
2. **Mechanism vs. Planmäßigkeit** — anti-mechanistic but not straightforwardly vitalist; purposiveness without invoking élan vital.
3. **Darwinism vs. Design** — Uexküll rejects natural selection's explanatory power for functional harmony, but doesn't fully embrace creationism.
4. **Subject as perceiver vs. Subject as object** — the same entity is constituted differently in different Umwelten (ontological perspectivism).
5. **Meaning as imposed vs. Meaning as encountered** — carriers of meaning are objectively present yet only "mean" within a specific Umwelt.
6. **Simplicity vs. Completeness** — the simplest Umwelten (paramecium) are functionally complete; complexity doesn't add ontological status.
7. **Musical metaphor vs. Biological mechanism** — Uexküll's pervasive musical language (tone, score, counterpoint, composition) vs. the cybernetic/functional-cycle framework.
8. **Individual Umwelt vs. Shared environment** — how do Umwelten overlap or communicate if each organism inhabits its own world?
